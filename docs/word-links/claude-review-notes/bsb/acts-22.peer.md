# BSB Acts 22: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 30 of 30 verses (22:1-30). I checked every `g`/`s`/`t` line
against N1904, BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`. 22:2, 22:3 and
22:29 have a `partial` record. The chapter has no KEEP verses. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 30, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (3 verses, 3 links)

- 22:12: `g 12 = living there` → `g 12 = living` and `t there = English
  adds there (Damascus); κατοικούντων means only living.` The participle
  has no word for the place. This follows Acts 17:21, where "who lived
  there" has `t there` for ἐπιδημοῦντες. It also follows the brief's rule
  that an added "there" is English-only.
- 22:25: `g 9 = standing there` → `g 9 = standing` and `t there = English
  adds there; the participle ἑστῶτα means standing.` This is the same
  participle and the same English as Acts 4:14 (`t there`), and it matches
  9:7 (εἱστήκεισαν, "stood there").
- 22:20: `g 14 = giving my approval` → `g 14 = giving + approval //
  συνευδοκῶν ("approving") is rendered giving … approval.` and `t my =
  English supplies the possessive; the participle συνευδοκῶν has no person,
  and I renders αὐτός.` A participle has no person (Acts 16 peer rule, and
  the brief's person rule). The author applied that rule in this chapter
  (22:3, 22:5), so "my" cannot stay on συνευδοκῶν either. Acts 8:1
  renders the same participle "giving approval", with no possessive.

No other row changed. I agree with all the author's corrections to the
publisher rows.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 22:3 Ἐγώ = I + I + I: **agree, kept**. Ἐγώ is the nominative subject of
  εἰμι, and the participles πεπαιδευμένος and ὑπάρχων agree with it. Each
  repeated "I" therefore has the same role (subject) as Ἐγώ, which the
  same-role rule for pronouns requires (9:25, 16:3). Each participle keeps
  English of its own (was educated, am), and neither carries a person word.
  22:5 ἐπορευόμην = I + I was on my way: **agree, kept**. The first person
  comes only from the ending of the finite verb. δεξάμενος shares that
  subject and keeps "obtained".
- 22:20 "giving my approval": **changed** (see above). The other possible
  fix, αὐτός = I + my, is considered below.
- 22:11 χειραγωγούμενος … ἦλθον = led me by the hand: **agree, kept**. BSB
  merges "being led by the hand, I came into" into "led me by the hand
  into". Separate links would leave "me" alone on ἦλθον, and the meaning
  "came" is not in "me". The other option leaves "me" on the participle,
  which the person rule forbids. The joint group with its note is the more
  honest picture. Acts 9:8 is different: there the finite verb εἰσήγαγον
  ("brought in") itself means "led", so "led" goes on it and "by the hand"
  goes on the participle.
- 22:29 καί = himself, δέ = and: **agree, kept**. In καὶ … δέ, δέ is the
  connective and καί is the adverb "also, even". BSB's "the commander
  himself" (as in NIV) gives the same emphasis, "even the commander". The
  reviewed Acts 17:13 links καί ("also") the same way (κἀκεῖ = there
  themselves). The publisher's δέ = himself was wrong. The other option,
  καί = and with δέ `s` and "himself" `t`, would put "and" on the wrong
  word and split καί from "himself", which renders it.
- 22:5 καί (22) `s` + "and#2" `t`: **agree, kept**. This καί is the
  adverb "also" with τοὺς ἐκεῖσε ὄντας ("those there too"). It does not
  coordinate a participle chain. BSB's "and" joins apprehend (from the
  predicate participle δεδεμένους) to bring (ἄξων), and the Greek has no
  coordinating καί there. So the participle-"and" rule makes it `t` (Acts
  8:30, 14:22), and "and" does not mean "also", so this is not a split
  pair. καί (12) = even is right.
- 22:30 τὸ τί = reason with the second τό `s`, and τὸ ἀσφαλές = the real:
  **agree**. The second τό only turns the question into a noun.
- 22:10, 22:13 and 22:18: a preposition joined with its object where BSB
  makes the object a direct object: **agree** (as 9:11).

## Other points checked

- Parallels with the reviewed Acts 9. Identical Greek gets identical links:
  - 22:7 = 9:4: Σαοὺλ Σαούλ, τί με διώκεις = Saul#0 / Saul#1 / why / Me /
    do you persecute; φωνῆς = a voice; λεγούσης = say; μοι = to me (9:4
    has αὐτῷ = to him).
  - 22:8 = 9:5: Τίς = Who, εἶ = are You, Κύριε = Lord, Ἐγώ = I, εἰμι = am,
    Ἰησοῦς = Jesus, ὅν = whom, σύ = you, διώκεις = are persecuting.
  - 22:13 Σαοὺλ ἀδελφέ = Saul / Brother, as 9:17.
  - 22:10 σοι with the passive = you, as 9:6.

  Links differ only where the Greek differs. 22:7 has the finite ἔπεσα
  and a real καί = and, where 9:4 has the participle πεσών and a `t` "and".
  22:8 has ἀπεκρίθην = asked.
- 22:4 τε = both: **confirmed**. BSB writes "both men and women" for τε …
  καί. καί (8) between the two participles = and#1.
- EVIDENCE for 22:2, 22:3 and 22:29: the partial records give sourceWords =
  matchedSourceWords = publisherGreekWords (13/13/13, 34/34/34, 23/23/23).
  Every N1904 word is matched, so the difference is only in form. No
  reading is claimed, and no note names the publisher's Greek:
  **confirmed**.
- These supplied words are `t`: 22:2 Paul, 22:18 the people here, 22:22
  The crowd and he made, 22:28 a citizen, and 22:30 the commander and
  Paul#0. All are supplied names or complements for verb endings. Also
  `t`: 22:4 them, 22:5 them#1, 22:25 him#1, and 22:26 this and it
  (supplied objects), and 22:12 There and named (no ὄνομα, Acts 10:5).
  All **confirmed**.
- Person: "I/we/you" sits only on finite verbs or real pronouns (μοι in
  22:6 and 22:17, με in 22:17, Ἐγώ, ἐγώ, κἀγώ, αὐτός). Only third-person
  pronouns sit on participles (22:2, 22:24, 22:26, 22:29, 22:30), which is
  the convention. The chapter has no jussive "let".
- Lone articles: none carries of/to/in/at (22:3, 22:11, 22:14, 22:26).
  "of the One speaking" (22:9) and "of those who killed" (22:20) are
  article + participle units.
- Connectives: 22:17 δέ = Later follows 9:1 δέ = Meanwhile. 22:23 τε `s`
  with "As" on the genitive absolute. 22:30 καί (24) = Then. 22:29 οὖν =
  Then, with εὐθέως `s`.

## Still uncertain

- 22:20 ἐφεστώς = "stood there" is kept linked. The ἐπι- of ἐφίστημι
  ("stand by, be present") supplies the place, just as ἐπιστάς = "stood
  beside" in 22:13. With plain ἵστημι (4:14, 9:7, 22:25), "there" is `t`.
  A stricter reviewer could make this "there" `t` too.
- 22:20 "my": the alternative is αὐτός = I + my, since the approver is the
  subject that αὐτός states. I chose `t` because "my" is a possessive, not
  a repeated subject.
- 22:28 τήν = my with ταύτην `s`: the article stands there because of the
  demonstrative, so it is not a true possessive article. I kept the
  author's link: "my" fills the article's place, and neither Greek word
  means "my" exactly.
- 22:12 κατά = observer of (the publisher's split) and 22:19 ἐπίστανται =
  know very well were kept. "very well" may partly reflect the emphatic
  αὐτοί.

## Coordinator adjustment (consistency pass, 2026-11-24)

- 22:5: the first-person "I" that BSB supplies for a participle clause
  is now `t`; a participle has no person, and the finite verb keeps its own
  "I". This aligns with Acts 22:20 and 23:27–30.
