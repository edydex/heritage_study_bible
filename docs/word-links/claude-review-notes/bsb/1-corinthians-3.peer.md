# BSB 1 Corinthians 3: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 23 of 23 verses (3:1-23). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Every
`g`, `s` and `t` line was checked against the Greek, BSB's English and each
verse's EVIDENCE line. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 23,
"problems": 0}`.

## Changes

One change.

- 3:4 "follow#1": `t` → linked to εἰμι, so εἰμι = "follow#0 + follow#1"
  (note: εἰμι with the genitive is rendered I follow; BSB repeats follow
  where the Greek leaves the verb understood). "Ἐγὼ μέν εἰμι Παύλου, ἕτερος
  δέ Ἐγὼ Ἀπολλῶ" is one construction stated twice: the second clause leaves
  the same εἰμι understood, with the same role (the verb of the claim "I am
  X's") and the same meaning. That is a word understood again (John 15:4,
  12:13), not a supplied copula: "follow" is BSB's rendering of εἰμί + a
  genitive of belonging, not an English "is/are". This matches 1 Corinthians
  1:12, where the author and peer both link εἰμι to all four "follow". The
  Galatians 3:29 precedent the author cited does not apply here: that clause
  ("εἰ δὲ ὑμεῖς Χριστοῦ") has no εἰμί of its own to be understood again.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 3:14 τὸ ἔργον … ὅ = "what": kept. BSB's "what" is "that which", the
  antecedent and the relative in one English word; here the antecedent is
  "the work", so the subject of "survives" ("what he has built") maps onto
  the Greek subject τὸ ἔργον ὃ ἐποικοδόμησεν with nothing left over. The
  stricter ὅ = "what" with τὸ ἔργον `s` would leave "the work" unlinked
  although "what" carries it. Consistent with 3:15 τὸ ἔργον = "it" and with
  1 Corinthians 2:9 (Ἅ and ὅσα joined to one "what"). Moving "what" off
  ἐποικοδόμησεν (now "he#0 has built") is right: the verb does not mean
  "what". τινος `s` is right: BSB's "he" comes from the verb's person
  ending, not from the genitive "anyone's".
- 3:4 "mere" `t`: kept. ἄνθρωποι is "men/human"; the restrictive "mere" is
  English emphasis, like the added "only" (3:7, 3:15) and "already" (3:11).
  "of you" `t` is also right (τις alone is "someone").
- 3:15 οὕτως `s` + "only" `t`: kept. οὕτως means "in this way/so"; "only"
  means something else, so this is not a split pair. ὡς alone fully gives
  "as if". τὸ ἔργον = "it" (the work is the subject of "is burned up") and
  τινος `s` confirmed.
- 3:2 "for solid food" `t`: kept. Its role differs from βρῶμα (complement of
  "ready", not object of "gave"), so it is not a word understood again with
  the same role. ἔτι νῦν = "still" kept: "still now" is the sense of
  "still"; νῦν `s` would also be acceptable but is not more accurate.
- 3:13 first ἑκάστου = "his": kept. "his" is BSB's possessive for the same
  genitive possessor (the builder of 3:12); reviewed precedent links ἕκαστος
  to a pronoun rendering the same person (Romans 12:3 ἑκάστῳ = "you", Acts
  4:35 ἑκάστῳ = "to anyone"). The note explains the shift.
- 3:3 ὅπου = "since" (causal "where", BDAG sense) and 3:2 Ἀλλ’ = "In fact"
  (ascensive ἀλλά): kept.

## Points checked and confirmed

- 3:3: καί (14) `s` (BSB starts a second question; no "and"); οὐχί =
  "not#0 + not#1" (one negative governs both halves of the Greek question,
  same role and meaning in BSB's two questions); περιπατεῖτε = "Are you +
  walking" (person and auxiliary on the finite verb). "there is" `t` is
  right: "ἐν ὑμῖν ζῆλος καὶ ἔρις" has no verb, so the existential "there
  is" is a supplied copula, not the rendering of an εἰμί/γίνομαι.
- 3:13: τό (15) `s`; second ἑκάστου = "each man’s" ("man’s" belongs to the
  distributive "each one's", not to the article); "of + work" on ἔργον
  (preposition on the head noun, Hebrews 5:12; no "of" on a lone article).
  ὅτι (9), ἐστιν (18) and αὐτό (21) `s` are correct.
- 3:19: παρά = "in#0 + sight", Θεῷ = "God’s", τῷ `s`; "of + world" on
  κόσμου with "this" on τούτου; γάρ = "As" (a causal connective for "for").
  Ὁ δρασσόμενος = "He catches": "He" comes from the article, not from a
  person ending on the participle. 3:20 "of" on σοφῶν with τῶν = "the#1".
  Both quotations (Job 5:13, Psalm 94:11) are linked word by word.
- 3:22 "belong" and 3:23 "belong"/"belongs" `t` (Galatians 3:29): the
  clauses are verbless; 3:21's ἐστιν is in another verse and rendered "are",
  so no cross-verse link. ὑμῶν = "to you", Χριστοῦ = "to#0 Christ", Θεοῦ =
  "to#1 God".
- 3:12 reading: EVIDENCE shows 2 of 14 N1904 words unmatched; in the
  publisher's record the only content words with English but no group are
  χρυσίον and ἀργύριον, which fits the author's reading claim. The notes
  ("a different form of this word with the same meaning") do not name the
  publisher's Greek. No other verse claims a reading; all other EVIDENCE
  lines are word-for-word equal.
- Other rules: no "of the"/"to the" on a lone article anywhere; supplied
  copulas ("They are" 3:5, "there is" 3:3), supplied objects ("the seed",
  "it" ×2 3:6, "things" 3:7, "it" 3:10, 3:13, "his role" 3:5), "and" 3:6,
  "using"/"or" 3:12, "yourselves" 3:16 and "in purpose" 3:8 are `t`;
  jussive "Let" on ἐξαπατάτω (3:18); 3:17 οἵτινες = "and + that temple"
  (Acts 23:33, Galatians 4:26).

## Still uncertain

- 3:18 εἶναι = "he#0 is" follows Galatians 6:3. The infinitive has no person
  ending, so a stricter reading would make "he" `t`; kept for consistency
  with the registered precedent.
- 3:21 μηδείς = "stop": BSB recasts "let no one boast" as a second-person
  command. "stop" carries the negation that μηδείς supplies, so the link is
  kept (negatives go on the negator, the verb keeps "boasting"); no
  registered precedent was found either way.
- 3:8 second τόν = "his", ἴδιον = "own": registered precedent is split
  (Romans 14:4-5, 8:32 link the article to "his"; Matthew 25:15, Galatians
  6:5 put "his own" on ἴδιον). Kept as the author has it.
- 3:13 "of" could also sit with ὁποῖον ("the quality of"); "of + work"
  follows the head-noun convention and is kept.
