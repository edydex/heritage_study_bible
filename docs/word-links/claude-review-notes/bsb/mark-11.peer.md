Checked: 32 of 32 verses

That is 11:1-25 and 11:27-33; 11:26 is empty in both texts. 11:23, from an
earlier review, was checked like the others.

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 32, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB.
Textual claims were checked against the word counts in

## Changes (2 verses)

- 11:29: ἀποκρίθητε = "you answer", second καί = "if" →
  ἀποκρίθητε = "if you answer", and the second καί is `s` ("The καί that
  follows the conditional imperative has no English word; BSB uses an
  if-clause instead."). Why: "answer me, and I will tell you" is an
  imperative used as a condition. The imperative is the if-clause and the
  καί begins the result clause. So "if" belongs with ἀποκρίθητε. On the
  second καί, tapping "if" showed a Greek "and" from the result clause.
  This also matches how this review treats conditional participles
  (Matthew 21:22 πιστεύοντες = "If you believe"; 1 Timothy 6:8,
  Hebrews 2:3).
- 11:23: μὴ διακριθῇ = "has no doubt" → μή = "no", διακριθῇ = "has" +
  "doubt". Why: rule 2. "no" renders the negative and must not be folded
  into the verb's group. The parallel Matthew 21:21 already has μή = "not",
  διακριθῆτε = "do" + "doubt", and this chapter splits its other negatives
  the same way (11:16, 11:17, 11:31, 11:33). The other notes in 11:23 were
  long, several sentences each. They are now one short sentence each, and
  their links are unchanged.

Nothing else was changed. The author's corrections of the publisher rows
are right. These include When/After moved onto the καί between the clauses
(11:15, 11:18, 11:27, as in Mark 5:15 and 5:38), "As" on παραπορευόμενοι
(11:20), the genitive absolutes in 11:11 and 11:12, οὔπω = "ever" (11:2),
ἄρα `s` (11:13), "again" moved off καρπόν (11:14), and the negatives taken
out of the verbs.

## Textual claims

- 11:31 `t what they should answer`, λέγοντες `s`: **correct.** N1904 has
  16 words, all 16 matched, against 18 publisher words. So the publisher
  adds exactly two words and drops none. That fits τί εἴπωμεν ("what
  should we say"), and N1904 has nothing that "what they should answer"
  could render. λέγοντες is in both texts and has no English word except
  the colon, so `s` is right. This is not a split pair: the `t` words render
  the added τί εἴπωμεν, not λέγοντες.
- 11:32: 16 N1904 words, 15 matched, 16 publisher words. That is one
  changed word (a spelling), not an added ἐάν. So "if" has no ἐάν behind
  it in either text (see 11:32 below).
- 11:21 and 11:30: one changed word each, with the same word count. The
  author says these are the spellings of Ῥαββεί and Ἰωάνου, and the counts
  allow that. The links are unaffected.
- 11:3: 20 N1904 words, all matched, against 21 publisher words. That is
  one extra publisher word, which BSB does not translate. No note claims a
  reading.
- All other verses have publisher Greek identical to N1904, and none of
  their notes claims a different reading.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 11:13, εὑρήσει: **agree** with εὑρήσει = "there was" and `t to see`. In
  Greek the indirect question εἰ ... εὑρήσει follows ἦλθεν directly, and
  English needs "to see" to introduce it. Inside that question, "there
  was any fruit" is BSB's recast of "he would find anything". Putting
  εὑρήσει on "to see" would leave the if-clause without its Greek verb.
- 11:29, "if" on the second καί: **changed** (see above).
- 11:32, "if we say" on εἴπωμεν: **keep.** The counts show that BSB's
  Greek has no ἐάν here. The deliberative subjunctive "should we say?" is
  what BSB turns into a condition, so "if" goes with that verb. This is the
  same principle as in 11:29. A `t` on "if" would claim that nothing in the
  Greek is conditional.
- 11:11, 11:13, 11:30, "it": **keep.** In 11:11 "it was already late" =
  "the hour was already late", so "it" stands for ὥρας. In 11:13 and 11:30
  "it was" / "was it" is the English form of the clause with ἦν. A `t`
  would be defensible, but the current links are not wrong.
- 11:27, "After" on the middle καί with `t and`: **agree.** That καί joins
  "they come again" to the rest of the verse, and BSB expresses that
  relation as "After". The English "and" joins "Jesus was walking" to
  "came up". In Greek those are a genitive absolute and the main verb,
  with no conjunction. Mark 5:15 and 5:38 already put "When" on the same
  kind of καί.
- 11:9-10, supplied "is": **agree** with `t is`. Neither verse has a
  copula, and Matthew 21:9 and 23:39 use `t is` with Εὐλογημένος. A lone
  article must not carry "is" (rule 5), and the earlier publisher row had
  "is the" on ἡ.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 11:14: Μηκέτι εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα = "ever" + "again" as one group. A finer
  split would be εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα = "ever" and Μηκέτι = "again". It was kept
  because the reviewed Matthew 21:19 joins Οὐ μηκέτι ... εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα =
  "never" + "again" the same way.
- 11:3: ὧδε `s` ("here", implied by "return"). It could also be joined to
  ἀποστέλλει πάλιν = "will return". Both are defensible.
- 11:15: "those who were" is shared by τοὺς πωλοῦντας and τοὺς
  ἀγοράζοντας. It sits with the buying group, which is next to it in the
  English.
