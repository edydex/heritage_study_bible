# BSB Romans 11: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 36 of 36 verses (11:1-36), every `g`, `s` and `t` line against the
Greek, BSB's English and the publisher record. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 36, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

One change in one verse:

- 11:5: γέγονεν "is" + `t there` → γέγονεν = "there is" (note "rendered
  there is"); the `t there` line is removed. This "there" is the existential
  "there is" that renders the existence verb itself, so it stays with that
  verb (Acts 14:8, Romans 5:13). Only a place-word "there" added by English
  is `t`. No other existential "there" occurs in this chapter.

All other rows of the author's file are kept. I checked each one against the
points below.

## Points checked and confirmed

- Lone articles: no "of the"/"to the"/"in the" sits on a lone article. 11:4
  τῇ Βάαλ and 11:25 τῷ Ἰσραήλ carry "to" because the names are
  indeclinable, which is the convention. 11:2 κατὰ τοῦ Ἰσραήλ needs no case
  word ("against" is on κατά), so τοῦ is `s`. Articles used as pronouns or
  possessives: 11:15 ἡ "their", 11:18 τῶν "those" (anaphoric article, not a
  supplied demonstrative), 11:22 τῇ "His", 11:24 τῇ "their", 11:29 ἡ "His".
- 11:4: BSB's "divine reply" renders χρηματισμός, and no English word
  renders λέγει, so λέγει `s` stands. "was" is a copula supplied by BSB's
  rewording ("what was the divine reply" for "what does the divine response
  say") and is correctly `t`. It does not mean "says".
- 11:17: συνκοινωνὸς … ἐγένου = "to share in"; τῆς (17) = "the" before
  "nourishment"; πιότητος = "nourishment"; ῥίζης = "of + root" with its
  article τῆς (15) = "the"; ἐλαίας = "olive" (linked, so no split pair);
  only τῆς (19) is `s`. No English word is over-claimed. Giving "the" to τῆς
  ἐλαίας instead would also be defensible, but this is not wrong.
- 11:21: EVIDENCE shows 0 unmatched N1904 words and 2 extra publisher words.
  The publisher's own N1904 rows leave "He will certainly not" unowned, so
  BSB probably renders its extra words there. "certainly" has no N1904
  counterpart, so `t` with the reading reason is right. "He will" on φείσεται
  and "not … either" on οὐδέ belong to those N1904 words.
- Spelling and reading notes: 11:1 (two words), 11:2, 11:5 and 11:8 match
  the EVIDENCE counts (1-2 unmatched words each, and these are the unlinked
  tokens in the publisher rows). 11:8 καθάπερ = "as" uses the Romans 3:4
  wording. None of the notes names the publisher's Greek.
- Joined units: μὴ γένοιτο "Certainly not" (11:1, 11:11), εἴ πως "in the hope
  that" (11:14), ἐφ’ ὅσον "Inasmuch as" (11:13), εἰ μή "but" (11:15), ἄχρι
  οὗ "until" (11:25), διὰ παντός "forever" (11:10), εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας
  "forever" (11:36) and ὁ Ῥυόμενος "The Deliverer" (11:26) are all
  confirmed. 11:33: BSB prints "O," (not "Oh"). Ὦ alone = "O" and βάθος =
  "the depth" stay separate words, which is correct.
- Person and modal words: every "I/we/they/He" from a verb ending sits on a
  finite verb or an explicit pronoun. The participle ὤν (11:17) is `s`.
  "May" is on Γενηθήτω and σκοτισθήτωσαν, "would" on γίνεται, "may" on
  παραζηλώσω and ἐλεηθῶσιν, "could" on ἐγκεντρισθῶ, and "should" on
  ἀνταποδοθήσεται. 11:8 "could" stays on the articular infinitive because
  there is no finite verb.
- Supplied words `t`: the copulas (11:6, 11:12, 11:15, 11:16, 11:20, 11:24,
  11:27, 11:28, 11:29, 11:33, 11:36), 11:4 "was", 11:3 "and" (asyndeton)
  and "as well", 11:10 "they", 11:11 "beyond recovery" and "has come", 11:18
  "remember this", 11:24 "readily", 11:30 "who", 11:31 "shown", and 11:35
  "God" (the agent of the passive).
- Old Testament quotations, checked word by word: 11:3 (Elijah's complaint;
  "as well" has no Greek), 11:4 (Κατέλιπον ἐμαυτῷ "I have reserved for
  Myself"), 11:8, 11:9-10 (the four εἰς `s`; σύνκαμψον "be bent" under the
  same "May"), 11:26-27 (ἀποστρέψει "He will remove"; παρ’ ἐμοῦ "My"),
  11:34-35 (ἤ `s` before the new quotation; καί = "that"). The links are
  sound.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 11:5 "there": changed (see above). It follows the settled existential
  convention.
- 11:1 ἐκ = "from" with σπέρματος = "a descendant": agreed. ἐκ governs both
  genitives and BSB prints its one "from" before the tribe. Linking the real
  preposition to "from" is more exact than putting the understood ἐκ on
  φυλῆς.
- 11:14 "own" on the fronted μου: agreed. It must not sit on τήν. "my own"
  on the emphatic possessive is defensible, and σάρκα = "people" carries the
  kinship sense.
- 11:16 "of the dough" `t`: agreed. ἀπαρχή means only "first portion". The
  dough is the image the English spells out (from φύραμα). "whole batch" on
  φύραμα is kept: φύραμα is the whole lump in contrast with the first
  portion.
- 11:24 "natural branches" on κατὰ φύσιν: agreed. This is a substantival
  phrase with BSB's added head noun, the same as Luke 6:45 "evil man" and
  "good things".
- 11:17 the three τῆς: agreed (see above).
- 11:12 the third "riches" on the second πλοῦτος: agreed. It is a word
  understood again, and the nearest statement of it is the right owner.
- 11:10 "they" `t` and 11:8 "could" on the articular infinitive: agreed. In
  11:10 "cannot" holds μή, so τοῦ μὴ βλέπειν is one group. In 11:8 "not"
  stands alone, so μή is split.

## Still uncertain (not changed)

- 11:35 καί = "that": I kept it as a connective BSB renders as result
  ("and so" → "that"), like ἀλλά = "And" in 11:4. A stricter reviewer could
  make καί `s` and "that" `t`.
- 11:22 / 11:23: the same construction, ἐπιμένω + dative, places "in" on the
  verb in 11:22 ("continue in") and on the noun in 11:23 ("in unbelief").
  Both are the publisher's rows and neither is wrong, so they are left
  alone.
- 11:8 τῆς σήμερον ἡμέρας = "this very day" is joined as an idiom. Splitting
  it (σήμερον "this very", ἡμέρας "day") would also be acceptable.
