# BSB Acts 25: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 27 of 27 verses

Started from the author's final file. Ran `totext --all`, checked every line of
every verse against the Greek, BSB's English and the publisher's record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`, then ran `fromtext`
again. Final check: `{"versesOk": 27, "problems": 0}`. One verse changed
(25:19). The other 26 verses are unchanged.

## Change

- **25:19 δέ "only" → δέ `s`, "only" `t`.** In ζητήματα δέ τινα … εἶχον, δέ
  ("but") sets the disputes against the charges 25:18 says were never brought.
  BSB marks that contrast with the adverb "only" ("they only had some
  contentions"). "Only" means "merely". It is not a connective, so a reader
  who taps it would wrongly see δέ glossed as "only". This is the same case as
  reviewed Acts 10:26, where καί = "only" was rejected. It is not a split pair,
  because "only" does not render δέ's meaning. New notes: δέ `s` "BSB leaves
  the connective δέ ("but") untranslated and marks the contrast with 25:18 by
  only instead." "Only" `t` "English adds only ("merely"); no Greek word here
  means only." The connective changes kept elsewhere in the chapter are
  different: 25:2 τε = "where" (as 6:13) and 25:3 ὅπως = "by" each still join
  the clause in the connective's own position.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **25:3 αἰτούμενοι = "to grant them": agree, kept.** BSB says "They urged
  Festus to grant them a concession against Paul by summoning him". "Urged"
  belongs to παρεκάλουν (25:2), and "a concession" renders χάριν. "To grant"
  does not belong to χάριν. It is the verb of the transaction, and "them"
  carries the middle voice ("asking for themselves"). Αἰτούμενοι χάριν ("asking
  a favor for themselves") is told from the other side as "to grant them a
  concession". This is a converse rendering of the same act. Reviewed Acts
  24:27 links that kind of rendering (ἔλαβεν διάδοχον = "was succeeded by").
  No other English word renders αἰτούμενοι, so `s` plus a `t` "to grant them"
  would drop a real correspondence.
- **25:19 δέ = "only":** changed (above).
- **25:11 "truth" `t`, ὧν `s`, "to#1" `t`: agree.** BSB says "But if there is
  no truth to their accusations against me". Οὐδέν means "nothing". "Truth"
  adds the idea of validity, which οὐδέν lacks, so οὐδέν keeps "no" only.
  "To" is governed by the supplied "truth". Ὧν is a relative that BSB turns
  into the noun phrase "their accusations". Neither "to" nor any other word
  means "which", so this is not a split pair.
- **25:11 ἀδικῶ καὶ … πέπραχα = "I am guilty of": agree.** BSB fuses the two
  verbs: "If, however, I am guilty of anything worthy of death". The joined
  group says just that, and it does not over-claim. "Anything" stays on τι,
  "worthy" on ἄξιον and "of death" on θανάτου. The alternative (πέπραχα `s`
  with "of#0" `t`) would hide that πέπραχα is part of the fused verb.
  "However" on οὖν with μέν `s` matches 25:4.
- **25:2 and 25:15 "their case" `t`: agree.** This matches reviewed Acts 24:1,
  where ἐνεφάνισαν = "presented" and "their case" is `t`.
- **25:18: agree.** περὶ οὗ = "him" (BSB turns "concerning whom" into the
  object of charge), οἱ = "his" (possessive article), οὐδεμίαν = "not + any",
  αἰτίαν … ἔφερον = "they did + charge + with" ("bring a charge" → "charge …
  with"), ὧν `s`, πονηρῶν = "of the crimes". "But" and "to speak" are rightly
  `t`, because σταθέντες means only "having stood". "With" belongs to the
  English verb frame "charge … with", so it sits better in the charge group
  than as `t`.
- **25:8 "Then" `t`, Οὔτε … τι = "no": agree.** The Greek opens with a
  genitive absolute and has no connective. The first Οὔτε supplies the
  negation that BSB moves onto "no offense". The later οὔτε are "or".

## Other points checked

- **25:20 ἐγώ = I#0 + I#1: agree.** The explicit ἐγώ is the grammatical
  subject of ἔλεγον, and the participle ἀπορούμενος agrees with it. Both
  English "I"s therefore render the same word in the same role (as 22:3). The
  finite ἔλεγον keeps "asked".
- **25:17 I#0 `t`: agree.** ποιησάμενος is a participle. I#1 goes on
  ἐκέλευσα ("I#1 + ordered that"), as in 23:27.
- **25:24 ἄνδρες `s`: agree.** BSB says "King Agrippa and all who are present
  with us". "All" = πάντες, "who are present with" = οἱ συνπαρόντες, "us" =
  ἡμῖν. No English word renders "men", so there is no split pair. "That"
  before the infinitive δεῖν is `t`, and "he" is on αὐτόν, so the impersonal
  δεῖν carries no person.
- **Readings: all checked against the partial records.**
  - 25:1 has 2 unmatched words, and both are the publisher's own TODO tokens
    (ἐπαρχείῳ, Καισαρίας).
  - 25:4, 25:6 and 25:13 each have 1 unmatched word, Καισαρίαν each time.
  - 25:12 has 1 unmatched word (συνλαλήσας), and 25:24 has 1 (συνπαρόντες).
  - 25:10 has 1 unmatched word. The publisher left οὐδέν, ἠδίκηκα and "I have
    done nothing wrong" all unlinked, so the same-sense note "in this clause"
    is supported.
  - 25:17 has 0 unmatched words, 1 extra publisher word, and "they" is the
    only unlinked English word. So "they" `t` with the reading reason is
    right.
  - 25:26 has 0/0, and no reading is claimed there.
  - No note states the publisher's Greek spelling or wording.
- Lone articles: none carries of/to/in/before (25:1, 25:8, 25:14, 25:15,
  25:21, 25:23, 25:26 checked). Participle "and" `t` is correct in 25:5, 25:6,
  25:12, 25:15 and 25:17. The jussive "let" is on κατηγορείτωσαν (25:5). There
  are no supplied copulas. Added "there" (25:4, 25:5), "now" (25:25), the
  supplied object "him" (25:25), and supplied names (25:6 Festus, 25:22
  Festus#1) are all `t`.

## Still uncertain (left as the author has them)

- 25:5 ἐν = "of" in "some of your leaders". "Among you" → "of your" is a
  shifted partitive. One could argue that "of" belongs with the supplied
  "some" (`t`) instead. I kept the publisher row, because it is not clearly
  wrong.
- 25:4 μέλλειν = "am" and ἐκπορεύεσθαι = "going". A joined μέλλειν …
  ἐκπορεύεσθαι = "am going" would be equally valid. The split is not wrong.
- 25:26 κυρίῳ = "to + sovereign one". BSB's current English has "one", which
  the publisher's English lacks (matched 37 of 38 target words). I kept "one"
  inside the rendering of the noun "lord" and did not make it `t`.
