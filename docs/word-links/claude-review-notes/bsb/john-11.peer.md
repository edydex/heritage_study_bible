Checked: 57 of 57 verses (11:1-57). No verse was kept from an earlier review.
I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against N1904, the displayed BSB, the

## Changes (3 verses)

- 11:52, links: `g 2` ὑπέρ = "for#0 + for#1" (note "ὑπέρ is understood
  again with the children") → `g 2` ὑπέρ = "for#0" and `t for#1` ("BSB
  repeats for to set the children beside the nation; in the Greek they are
  the object of συναγάγῃ ('gather')."). Why: ὑπέρ does not govern the
  children, even by ellipsis. The ἀλλ’ ἵνα clause replaces the ὑπέρ phrase,
  and τὰ τέκνα is the accusative object of συναγάγῃ. This is unlike 11:50,
  where ἵνα really governs both subjunctives (ἀποθάνῃ … ἀπόληται), so the
  repeated "that" there stays linked.
- 11:29, note only: ἐγείρεται = "got up" "BSB's Greek has a different form
  of the same verb ('rose')." → "BSB's Greek has a different form of the same
  verb here." Why: the record (10/9/10) shows only that one word differs. The
  gloss "rose" described the publisher's word, and a reader could take it as
  the meaning of ἐγείρεται ("rises"). The link is kept: "got up" fits N1904's
  verb as well (rule 9, same meaning).
- 11:33, note only: ἐτάραξεν ἑαυτόν = "troubled" "is rendered was …
  troubled" → "is rendered troubled". Why: "was" belongs to the group
  ἐνεβριμήσατο = "He was deeply moved", not to this one.

Every other line of the author's file is kept.

## Points I was asked to settle, and the author's doubts

- 11:13: **agree** with Ἰησοῦς = "He", λέγει = "was#0 talking" and "Jesus"
  `t`. This is exactly the reviewed John 5:16 pattern (τὸν Ἰησοῦν = "Him",
  "Jesus" `t` because BSB names him in the reversed first clause). In the
  English, "Jesus" is the subject of "was talking", which renders λέγει
  (subject only in the verb); "He" is the subject of "was speaking", which
  renders εἰρήκει, whose subject is ὁ Ἰησοῦς. The publisher's λέγει =
  "Jesus was talking" put a name on a verb. δέ (first) = "but" with the
  second δέ `s` follows the same clause logic; a tap shows δέ either way.
- 11:31: **agree** with all three. παραμυθούμενοι αὐτήν is "consoling her",
  so αὐτήν = "Mary" (pronoun rendered by name) is the honest owner of the
  object of "consoling". The publisher had "Mary" on αὐτῆς in μετ’ αὐτῆς
  ("with her"), which BSB does not render; `s 5 6` is right. τὴν Μαριάμ is
  the proleptic object of ἰδόντες and the logical subject of ἀνέστη; BSB
  turns it into "how quickly she got up", so Μαριάμ = "she#0" fits the
  5:16 pattern (BSB names the person earlier and renders the name here as a
  pronoun). The alternative (Μαριάμ = "Mary", αὐτήν `s`) would leave the
  object of "consoling" on the wrong word.
- 11:12: **agree** with οἱ = "His" and αὐτῷ `s`. The record shows no
  reading difference, so BSB's Greek has the dative αὐτῷ ("to him"), not a
  genitive. Linking "His" to a dative mislabels the grammar; the article as
  possessive is the majority convention (also 11:16, 11:19, 11:41, 11:44,
  11:48 here).
- 11:50: **agree** with ἵνα = "that#1 + that#2" and καὶ μή = "than"; see the
  11:52 change for why the two cases differ.
- 11:52: **disagree**; changed (see above).
- 11:3: agree with λέγουσαι = "word" (publisher row). "sent word" is the
  English idiom for sending a spoken message; `s` + `t word` would be a
  split pair.
- 11:38: agree with ἐν ἑαυτῷ `s`. In 11:33 "deeply moved" renders
  ἐνεβριμήσατο alone (τῷ πνεύματι = "in spirit"), so "deeply moved" does
  not also say "within himself".

## Still uncertain

- 11:13: which δέ carries "but" is a judgment call. The second δέ (ἐκεῖνοι
  δέ) carries the contrast in the Greek; the first sits in the clause BSB
  introduces with "but". I kept the author's clause-based choice.
- 11:19 περί = "in the loss of" and 11:42 διά = "for the benefit of"
  (publisher rows) are expansive; "loss" and "benefit" have no Greek word of
  their own. Kept as dynamic renderings of the preposition.
- 11:7 ἔπειτα μετὰ τοῦτο = "and then": "and" only joins to 11:6, but "and
  then" is a common English rendering of ἔπειτα. Kept.
- 11:29: ἐκείνη = "she" with "Mary" `t` (publisher row) is kept. ἐκείνη =
  "Mary" with "she" on the main verb would also be possible.
