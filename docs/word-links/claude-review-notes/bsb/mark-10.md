## Notable decisions

- A different reading is claimed only in 10:7. "and be united to his wife" is `t`, because BSB follows καὶ προσκολληθήσεται πρὸς τὴν γυναῖκα αὐτοῦ, which N1904 lacks. That is six words, matching EVIDENCE.
- In 10:1, 10:35, 10:41, 10:46 and 10:51, EVIDENCE differs only in spelling: συνπορεύονται, Ἰωάνης/Ἰωάνου, Ἰερειχώ twice, Ῥαββουνεί. Those words are linked.
- 10:6 "God", 10:37 second "Your" and 10:43 "shall be" are treated as English choices, since EVIDENCE shows the publisher's Greek is identical to N1904 there.
- The article used as a pronoun is linked to the noun BSB names: 10:22 ὁ → the man, 10:39 οἱ → the brothers, 10:50 ὁ → Bartimaeus. This follows ὁ → Jesus in earlier chapters.
- English "the" before an anarthrous noun is kept in the noun phrase, as in the publisher's rows: the crowds (10:1), the beginning (10:6), the little children (10:13), the slave (10:44).
- 10:14: τῶν → "to" is kept, as in Matthew 19:14. 10:44: ἐν ὑμῖν is `s`, because BSB leaves out "among you".

## Doubts for the peer

- 10:32: "As" is on the καί between "they were going up" and "Jesus was walking ahead", following the When rule for καί-joined clauses. δέ is `s`.
- 10:10: καί is `s` and "When they were" is `t`. The alternative is καί → When.
- 10:16: αὐτά → "the children" only, and "them" after "blessed" is `t`. The alternative treats αὐτά as the shared object and links it to both.
- 10:30: ἐὰν μή → "will fail to". The alternative gives "will" to λάβῃ.
- 10:34: καί is `s`, and "who will mock" stays on the verb. "who" replaces "and they".
- 10:13: αὐτοῖς → "those", and "who brought them" is `t`, as in Matthew 19:13.
- In 10:32, 10:49 and 10:50, an opening καί or δέ is `s` in the same verse as a `t` "and". That "and" comes from the participle-to-finite-verb conversion, not from the connective.
- 10:21: Ἕν → "one thing", ὑστερεῖ → "lack", and "There is" is `t`.
