# BSB Luke 13: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 35 of 35 verses (13:1-35). I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line
against N1904, the displayed BSB, the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json` and the reviewed
parallels Matthew 7:13-14, 7:22-23, 8:11-12, 13:31-33, 17:20, 23:37-39,
25:10-12 and Mark 4:30-31. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 35, "problems": 0}`.

## Reading claims

The publisher record has a `partial` entry for 13:1, 13:2, 13:5, 13:7, 13:13
and 13:27 only. Every reading note in the file sits in one of these verses,
and none states the publisher's spelling as fact. For each verse I also
listed the English words that no N1904 row of the publisher covers:

- 13:1 (Pilate), 13:5 (you repent), 13:13 (she straightened up): the
  publisher has as many Greek words as N1904, one N1904 word is unmatched,
  and that word's English is the part the publisher's rows leave uncovered.
  A same-meaning note with a link is right under rule 9.
- 13:7: 28 N1904 words, all matched, 29 publisher words; all 41 English
  words are matched, and "Therefore" is the only one outside the N1904 rows.
  So the publisher links "Therefore" to its extra word (probably οὖν).
  `t Therefore` with the reading reason is right.
- 13:27: 14 N1904 words, all matched, 15 publisher words; all 21 English
  words are matched, and the "you" of "where you are from" is the only one
  outside the N1904 rows. So the publisher links that "you" to its extra word
  (probably ὑμᾶς, as in 13:25). See the change below.
- 13:2: all 18 Greek words match with no extra publisher word; only the
  English count differs. The author correctly claims no reading.
- 13:35: no `partial` entry, so the publisher's Greek equals N1904 and has
  no word for "desolate" (unlike Matthew 23:38). `t desolate` without a
  reading claim is right; the publisher had folded it into ἀφίεται.

## Changes (1 verse)

- 13:27: `ἐστέ` = "you#1 are" (with a note that the extra word "does not
  change the English") → `ἐστέ` = are, `t you#1` = "BSB follows a reading
  with an extra word here, rendered you, which N1904 lacks." The publisher's
  own alignment puts this "you" on its extra word, not on ἐστέ (see above).
  In 13:25, where N1904 itself has ὑμᾶς, the same English "I do not know
  where you are from" links "you" to ὑμᾶς and ἐστέ only to "are". The old
  link kept the English but credited it to the wrong Greek word, and it
  made 13:25 and 13:27 disagree.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 13:19 κόκκῳ = a + seed, σινάπεως = mustard: kept. It follows the
  "a goes with the noun" rule and Mark 4:31. Matthew 13:31 and 17:20 put
  "a mustard" on σινάπεως and "seed" on κόκκῳ/κόκκον. One side should be
  aligned in a later pass. Mark 4:31 and this verse follow the current
  convention, so Matthew is the better side to change.
- 13:25 τὴν θύραν (after κρούειν) = "the door" after "open": kept. The noun
  is in this verse, and BSB only moves it from the knocking to the opening.
  `s` plus `t` would be a split pair. Matthew 25:11 uses `t the door`
  because its Greek has no θύρα, so the two verses do not conflict.
- 13:11 εἰς τὸ παντελές = straight: kept. The publisher row is defensible
  ("could not stand up fully" → "stand up straight"). Moving "straight" to
  ἀνακύψαι would only change the style.
- 13:27 "you#1 are" on ἐστέ: changed to `t you#1` (see above).
- 13:34 "gathers" on ἐπισυνάξαι: kept. Luke has no second verb (Matthew
  23:37 has ἐπισυνάγει), so the hen clause understands the infinitive's
  verb again, and the conventions let such a verb link to each English
  repeat. The publisher's "a hen gathers" on ὄρνις put a verb on a noun.
- 13:35 ἥξει ὅτε `s`: kept. Unlike ἕως ἄν, ἕως ἥξει ὅτε is not a fixed
  unit. ἥξει is a full verb ("[the time] will come") that BSB leaves out,
  and the note says so. Joining it to "until" is a possible alternative,
  not a correction.
- 13:4 οὓς = who, αὐτούς = them: kept. Each English pronoun goes to a Greek
  pronoun of the same kind with the same referent. The role swap comes from
  BSB's passive recast. The alternative (ἐφ’ οὓς = on + them, αὐτούς = who)
  would show a personal pronoun under "who".
- 13:30 εἰσίν = some + are, οἵ = who: kept. οἵ is the only Greek relative,
  and existential εἰσίν ("there are [last ones]") fits "some … are". The
  publisher's "some who are" on εἰσίν over-claimed.
- 13:23 οἱ σῳζόμενοι = will + people be saved: kept. "Will" is the
  auxiliary of the verb, and the verb keeps its auxiliary. The publisher's
  εἰ = will was wrong; εἰ only marks the question.
- 13:1 τινες = "some of those": kept. It is the publisher's row and not
  wrong; "those present" can come from Παρῆσαν or from τινες.

## Points checked on request

- 13:7 "Therefore" `t` with the reading reason: confirmed (see Reading
  claims).
- 13:35 "desolate" `t` without a reading claim: confirmed. "again" and
  "that" are also `t` without a reading claim. Matthew 23:39's ἀπ’ ἄρτι and
  ὅτι are not in Luke's Greek.
- 13:34-35 match Matthew 23:37-39 row for row, except for the "gathers"
  repeat and the three English-only words above.
- 13:21 matches Matthew 13:33 row for row (ἕως οὗ = until, `t of it`,
  `t and`, ἔκρυψεν = mixed).
- 13:24 matches Matthew 7:13 (διὰ τῆς στενῆς = through the narrow).
  13:23 ὀλίγοι = only a few matches Matthew 7:14.

## Still uncertain

- 13:22: καί = "as" ("teaching as He made His way"). It follows the rule
  that a καί BSB recasts as a subordinator carries that word. The publisher
  put "as" on ποιούμενος, which fits the rule that "As" goes with the
  participle. Both rules apply here; I kept the author's choice.
- 13:10: ἐν τοῖς σάββασιν = "One Sabbath". "One" has no Greek counterpart;
  `t One` with ἐν τοῖς σάββασιν = Sabbath is the alternative.
- 13:2: ταῦτα = "this fate". "Fate" could be `t`, since "these things"
  has no noun.
- 13:19 versus Matthew 13:31 and 17:20 needs one alignment across the
  Gospels (see above).
