# BSB 2 Corinthians 10: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: all 18 (10:1-18). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 18, "problems": 0}`.

84 of the 246 publisher `g` rows changed. Many changes are the standard
splits: copula, person, negative, article. Unchanged rows were checked too,
including every lone article. In the final file a lone article carries only
"the" or a possessive ("his", "my", "our").

Readings: EVIDENCE shows a difference from N1904 only in 10:4 and 10:5:
2 words unmatched in 10:4 and 2 extra in 10:5. This is a verse division, not
a reading. BSB's 10:5 begins "We tear down arguments", which is N1904's last
two words of 10:4 (λογισμοὺς καθαιροῦντες). These two words are moved with
`m 15 16 > 10:5` and linked in 10:5 with `g @10:4` ("arguments", "tear
down"). No other reading is claimed.

Conventions followed: 1 Corinthians 1:31 for the Jeremiah quotation (10:17
ὁ καυχώμενος = "him who boasts", καυχάσθω = "Let + boast"). Romans 7:25 for
an unrendered αὐτός "myself" (10:1). Romans 14:8 for τε without "both … and"
(10:8). 2 Thessalonians 3:9 for ἵνα becoming "to" after a supplied "want"
(10:9). Titus 3:11 for ὁ τοιοῦτος (10:11). 2 Corinthians 4:8 for
participles: the auxiliary stays with the verb, and "we" is `t`. Romans 4:13
for an articular infinitive joined with its article (10:2).

## Publisher rows corrected

Supplied copulas taken off content words (`t`): 10:1 ταπεινός "am humble" →
"humble"; 10:4 οὐ "are not" → "not"; 10:10 βαρεῖαι, ἀσθενής, ἐξουθενημένος
lose "are"/"is#0"/"is#1"; 10:11 τοιοῦτοι "we#1 will be" → `s`, with "we#1
will be" as `t`.

Person removed from participles and infinitives ("I/we" `t`):
- participles: 10:2 παρών "when I come" → "when + come"; 10:3
  περιπατοῦντες; 10:5 αἰχμαλωτίζοντες; 10:14 ἐφικνούμενοι; 10:15
  καυχώμενοι and ἐλπίδα (ἔχοντες).
- infinitives: 10:2 θαρρῆσαι; 10:16 εὐαγγελίσασθαι and καυχήσασθαι.
- 10:6 had "we" on ἐν. It is now `t`. ἐν ἑτοίμῳ ἔχοντες is joined as one
  idiom = "will be ready".

Negatives split off verbs: 10:3 στρατευόμεθα, 10:8 αἰσχυνθήσομαι, 10:12
τολμῶμεν, 10:13 καυχησόμεθα, 10:14 ὑπερεκτείνομεν and ἐφικνούμενοι, 10:16
καυχήσασθαι, 10:9 δόξω. Each now has its own οὐ/μή = "not".

Lone articles and misplaced words:

- 10:4 τῷ "they have" → `s`. "they have" is `t`.

- 10:8 οἰκοδομήν/καθαίρεσιν had "you". ὑμῶν = "you#0 + you#1" (one Greek

- 10:12 αὐτοί "When they" → "they#0"; "When" goes on μετροῦντες.

- 10:18 cleft: the publisher had ἐκεῖνος = "who#1 is" and ἐστιν = "it is".

- 10:9: δόξω "I do not want to seem" → "I + seem". ἵνα = "to#0" (2 Thess

## Notable decisions

- 10:1 κατὰ πρόσωπον = "face to face"; "when#0" is `t`. μέν and εἰς ὑμᾶς
  (not rendered after "bold") are `s`.
- 10:4 σαρκικά = "of#1 the world". The repeated "weapons#1" links to ὅπλα
  (understood again); the supplied "the#0" is `t`.
- 10:8: τε is `s` and "even" is `t`, following the τε rule and Romans 14:8.
  ἡμῶν = "us", the recast possessor.
- 10:10: "his#0" was left on τοῦ (σώματος) as the publisher had it, and ἡ is
  `s`.
- 10:11: τῷ λόγῳ is `s`. BSB's "in#0 our#0 letters" renders δι’ ἐπιστολῶν
  ("our#0" `t`). In the parallel phrase τῷ = "our#1" and ἔργῳ = "in#1 +
  actions".
- Auxiliaries with non-finite verbs stay on the verb (10:6 "will be ready";
  10:14 "had + come"; 10:15 "do + boast", "will + increase"; 10:16 "will +
  be boasting"). Only person words and "Then" are `t`.

## Doubts for the peer

- 10:13: I put κατὰ τὸ μέτρον together as "within" and μέτρου = "a field".
  Another option is κατά = "within" with μέτρον `s`. The publisher linked
  μέτρον to "field of influence" and left κανόνος unlinked.
- 10:7 ἑαυτῷ = "he#0" ("in himself" folded into the subject). This could
  instead be `s`, with "he#0" `t`.
- 10:9: ὡς ἄν is `s` and "to be trying" is `t`. Linking ὡς ἄν to "to be
  trying" as the same hedging function is arguable.
- 10:2: the four-way split of θαρρῆσαι / τῇ πεποιθήσει / ᾗ / τολμῆσαι
  across "be as bold as I expect". Joining θαρρῆσαι and τολμῆσαι to the one
  "be … bold" was not done.
- 10:16: "so that" on the result infinitive εὐαγγελίσασθαι. It could
  instead be `t`.
