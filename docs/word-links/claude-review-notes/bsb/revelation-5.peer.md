## Answers to the author's doubts and the open points

- 5:6 "The Lamb" `t`: **kept**. Ἀρνίον is the object of εἶδον, and ἔχων
  only agrees with it. The Greek does not repeat the noun, and the
  understood-again rule (John 12:13, 15:2) covers a word repeated in the
  same role in a parallel clause. Here BSB starts a new sentence and gives
  the participle a subject. That is a supplied subject. Reviewed files mark
  such a subject `t` in more than 180 rows that name a participle in the
  reason (for example Acts 16:33 and Mark 5:6, "English names the subject;
  the Greek has only the participle").
- 5:6 "encircled by" on the second ἐν μέσῳ: **kept**. Being "in the midst
  of" the creatures and elders is the same picture as being "encircled by"
  them, so this English renders ἐν μέσῳ. The first ἐν μέσῳ already gives
  "in the center", so the second is the only owner left. `s` + `t` would
  split a pair. καί (10) = "and" (it joins the elders on), and καί (6) `s`
  (BSB has a comma there) are right. "who" in "a Lamb who appeared" is an
  added relative (`t`, as Revelation 4:3 "that" and 1 Corinthians 9:6
  "who"). ὡς = "appeared" is an honest link for "as though".
- 5:8 ἔχοντες = "had + were holding", "they" `t`: **kept**. The one
  participle governs both objects. BSB says it once for each object, with
  the same role and the same meaning ("have/hold"), so both verbs link to
  it. "were" is the progressive form of a present participle in a past
  narrative, not an added tense. "they" has no Greek owner (a participle has
  no person), so it is `t`.
- 5:10 "to" on Θεῷ, "serve" `t`: **kept**. There is no Greek word behind
  "serve", so it is `t`. The registered 2 Corinthians 13:4 does the same
  ("to serve you": εἰς = "to", "serve" `t`). The other way, "to serve" both
  `t` and Θεῷ = "God", is also defensible, because "to" is the English
  infinitive marker. I kept the registered pattern. "to be" `t` matches
  Revelation 1:6.
- 5:3 and 5:4 "look inside" on βλέπειν: **kept**. "inside" is the
  preposition English needs to make "look" take "it", the same way "looks
  at" sits on βλέπων in Matthew 5:28. It is not an added adverb like "now".
- 5:13 τά = "that", "is" `t`, ὃ … ἐστίν `s`: **kept**. Here τά is a
  nominalizing article ("the things in them"), and reviewed files link such
  an article to the English that stands for it (Luke 20:25 and 24:27 τά =
  "what"). The "is" belongs to the τὰ ἐν αὐτοῖς phrase, which has no verb.
  A supplied copula is never linked to an εἰμί in another clause, so "is"
  is `t`. BSB does not render ὃ … ἐστίν. "be" `t`, Ἀρνίῳ = "to + Lamb",
  "forever" / "and ever" match Revelation 1:6 and 4:9.
- 5:11 φωνήν = "the voices": **kept**. There is no Greek article. An
  English "the" with no Greek article goes with its noun, as "the center"
  does on μέσῳ in 5:6 and Revelation 4:6. Singular to plural is a
  translation choice.
- Other points checked: 5:5 Μή = "not", κλαῖε = "Do + weep" (Luke 7:13,
  23:28). 5:1 ἔσωθεν καὶ ὄπισθεν = "on both sides" as one unit, because BSB's
  phrase cannot be split. σφραγῖσιν = "with + seals", and "It" and "and" are
  `t`. 5:2 τάς = "the", αὐτοῦ `s`, and "is" `t` (no Greek verb). The titles
  (τοῦ καθημένου = "of the One seated", Τῷ καθημένῳ = "To Him who sits"),
  "twenty-four", "Worthy" + "are You" (5:9) and "Worthy" + "is" (5:12)
  match Revelation 1 and 4.
