## Publisher rows corrected

- Connectives carrying a participle's "When/On/Since": 2:4 καί = "Since" →
  μὴ δυνάμενοι = Since they were unable; 2:5 and 2:16 καί = "When" → ἰδών /
  ἰδόντες = When + saw; 2:17 καί = "On" → ἀκούσας = On hearing. The καί is
  now `s` in each verse.
- 2:13: καί = "Once" was wrong → πάλιν = Once again, καί `s`.
- Supplied subjects folded into verbs, now `t`: 2:1 "Jesus" (went back to),
  2:2 "Jesus" (spoke), 2:12 "the man" (got up), 2:13 "Jesus" (went out),
  2:14 "Levi" (got up), 2:18 "people" (came), 2:25 "Jesus" (replied), 2:27
  "Jesus" (declared).
- 2:1: ἠκούσθη = "And when the people heard" → heard; "And" and "when the
  people" are `t`. The participle εἰσελθών = "Jesus went back to" → went, with
  πάλιν = back and εἰς = to.
- Supplied "and" joining a participle to the main verb, now `t`: 2:14 "and
  followed". In 2:4 and 2:12 the "and" moved off the verb to a real καί that
  joins two main verbs (ἀπεστέγασαν … καὶ … χαλῶσι; ἠγέρθη καὶ … ἐξῆλθεν).
- Negatives folded into verbs: 2:17 "I have not come" → οὐκ = not, ἦλθον = I
  have + come; 2:25 "Have you never read" → Οὐδέποτε = never, ἀνέγνωτε = Have
  you + read.
- Auxiliary "does" on the wrong word: 2:7 οὗτος = "does this man" → this man,
  λαλεῖ = does + speak; 2:16 Ὅτι = "Why does" → Why, ἐσθίει = does He eat.
- 2:2: ὥστε = "that there was" → that; χωρεῖν = there was + room.
- 2:8: the article τῷ carried "in" alone → πνεύματι = in + spirit, article `s`.
- 2:12: πάντας = "they were all" → they + all, ἐξίστασθαι = were + astounded;
  Οὕτως = "anything like this" → like this, `t anything`.
- 2:13: αὐτούς = "them there" → them, `t there`.
- 2:16: the lone article τῶν carried "who were" (forbidden) → Φαρισαίων = who
  were Pharisees, article `s`. ὅτι = "Jesus" was wrong → ὅτι `s`, `t Jesus`.
- 2:17: Οὐ = "It is not" → not, `t It is`; ἔχουσιν = "who" → χρείαν ἔχουσιν =
  who need; κακῶς ἔχοντες joined = sick.
- 2:18: ἦσαν = "were often" → were, `t often`; νηστεύουσιν = "fast like" → both
  νηστεύουσιν = fast, `t like`.
- 2:19: νυμφῶνος (bridal chamber) = "bridegroom" was lexically wrong → οἱ υἱοὶ
  τοῦ νυμφῶνος joined as an idiom = guests of the bridegroom.
- 2:21: ἀγνάφου = "of unshrunk" → unshrunk, ῥάκους = of + cloth; πλήρωμα =
  "new piece" → piece, καινόν = new; the article τοῦ = "from" → ἀπ’ = from,
  τοῦ = the.
- 2:22: οἶνον = "wine is poured" → wine; `t is poured` (the Greek clause after
  ἀλλά has no verb).
- 2:23: ἐν = "One" → ἐν τοῖς σάββασιν = One Sabbath.
- 2:26: ἄρτους = "consecrated bread" → bread, προθέσεως = consecrated;
  ἔδωκεν = "he gave some" → he gave, `t some`.

## Notable decisions

- Joint units: ἐν οἴκῳ / εἰς τὸν οἶκόν σου = home; τὰ πρός = outside;
  ἔρχονται φέροντες = was brought; ὅπου ἦν = above Him; ὅπου … κατέκειτο = on;
  εἰ μή = but; Διὰ τί = Why; οἱ μαθηταί = those (2:18); Μὴ δύνανται = How can;
  ἐν ᾧ = while; ὁ νυμφίος = He; ὅσον χρόνον = As long as; ἔχουσιν τὸν νυμφίον
  = He is; οὐ δύνανται = they cannot; εἰ δὲ μή = If he does (2:21, 2:22);
  ὁδὸν ποιεῖν = as they walked along; οὐκ ἔξεστιν = is unlawful; χρείαν ἔσχεν
  = in need; οἱ μετ’ αὐτοῦ = his companions; εἰσῆλθεν εἰς = he entered;
  οὐκ … εἰ μή = only; τοῖς σὺν αὐτῷ οὖσιν = to his companions.
- 2:15: καὶ γίνεται (with the infinitive) joined = While; in 2:23 καὶ ἐγένετο
  has no English counterpart and is `s`.
- Pronoun ↔ name, linked with a note: αὐτῷ/αὐτόν = Jesus (2:4, 2:15, 2:18,
  2:23); αὐτοῦ = Levi’s (2:15, BSB's reading of "his house"); Ἰησοῦ = Him
  (2:15).
- 2:16: τῶν ἁμαρτωλῶν καὶ τελωνῶν joined = these people (BSB condenses the
  phrase). Ὅτι is interrogative (why).
- 2:20: ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ (on that day) is `s`: BSB does not render it.
- Dative articles that render "the" keep "to the"/"on the"/"for the" (2:5,
  2:10, 2:24, 2:26), as in earlier reviewed chapters; only 2:8, where τῷ had
  no "the", was moved.
- Textual differences: none claimed. In 2:14 and 2:18 the unmatched N1904
  words are the names Λευεὶν and Ἰωάνου (spelling), linked to Levi and John’s.
  In 2:12 two N1904 words are unmatched by the publisher Greek, but every
  English word there renders an N1904 word of the same sense (ἔμπροσθεν = in
  front of, οὐδέποτε εἴδαμεν = We have never seen), so nothing is left as a
  reading difference. The extra publisher word in 2:17 has no English.
- Kept from the publisher on purpose: πολλοί = in such large numbers (2:2);
  τεσσάρων = four men (masculine substantive); τόν = his (2:4, 2:12); τόν =
  son (2:14, the article in τὸν τοῦ Ἀλφαίου); καί = and both (2:22); οὐ =
  don’t (2:18, contraction); ἀρχιερέως = the high priesthood (2:26).

## Doubts for the peer

- 2:1: verse-opening καί `s` with `t And` (BSB's "And" joins the participle
  sentence to ἠκούσθη). Linking καί = And is the alternative; check it does
  not read as a split pair.
- 2:18: one "fast" for both νηστεύουσιν, with `t like` and δέ `s`. "often" as
  `t`, not part of a joined ἦσαν … νηστεύοντες group.
- 2:19: the idiom joined as guests of the bridegroom, while Matthew 9:15 has
  νυμφῶνος `s` and `t bridegroom`.
- 2:22: καί = "and both" (correlative with the later καί) kept from the
  publisher; καί = and with `t both` is the alternative.
- 2:2: "such" could go with ὥστε (such … that) instead of πολλοί.
- 2:15: καὶ γίνεται joined = While, or γίνεται = While with καί `s`.
