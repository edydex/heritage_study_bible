## Changes

- 8:23: εἴτε = "As#0", ὑπέρ = "for#0 + for#1", εἴτε = "As#1" → εἴτε ὑπέρ
  = "As#0 for"; second εἴτε = "As#1 for". The second clause has the
  nominative ἀδελφοί with no ὑπέρ, and ὑπέρ cannot govern a nominative, so
  linking "for#1" to it claimed a word the clause does not have. "As for" is
  BSB's topic marker for each εἴτε.
- 8:11: note on τοῦ θέλειν `s`: "it says as you began instead" → "it says
  you began instead". "as#1" is linked to καθάπερ, so the note should not
  name it.
- 8:19: note on δόξαν: "is rendered to honor" → "is rendered as the verb
  honor". "to#1" belongs to πρός.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:11: agree. τοῦ θέλειν `s` with "you#1 began" `t` is right: "began"
  recalls προενήρξασθε of 8:10 and does not mean "want", so it is a
  different-meaning case, not a split pair. καθάπερ … οὕτως joined as
  "just as + as#1" is kept. In English "as … as", the first "as" goes with
  οὕτως and the second with καθάπερ. Joining the pair avoids that fragile
  split and over-claims nothing. "you#0 may" and "it" `t` are right (the
  ὅπως clause has no finite verb). τοῦ ἔχειν = "your means" is the
  articular infinitive with its article as the possessive.
- 8:19: agree with χάριτι = "offering + gracious gift". In BSB, "with the
  offering—the gracious gift we administer" restates one Greek noun. The
  repeated attributive article τῇ διακονουμένῃ resumes χάριτι, so English
  needs a head noun for "we administer". Both English nouns have the same
  referent, the same role (the noun the participle phrase modifies) and the
  same meaning ("gracious gift" is the closer one). Neither is an added
  noun, so neither is `t`.
  ἐν = "with" under the reading note is kept. EVIDENCE shows 1 N1904 word
  unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904. ἐν is the only plausible
  candidate (the likely reading is a preposition meaning "with"). The note
  does not name the publisher's word. "with" also fits ἐν of accompaniment
  (KJV "with this grace" from an ἐν text), so rule 9 lets it link. "More
  than" on οὐ μόνον … ἀλλὰ καί with "that" `t` and δέ `s` follows Romans
  5:3, 8:23, 9:10. "this brother", "to#2 show", "to#3 help" are correctly `t`.
- 8:10: agree with δίδωμι = "is#0 my". The "is" is not a copula supplied for
  a verbless clause. It is part of BSB's recast of the finite verb ("I give
  an opinion" → "this is my opinion"). John 8:41 does the same: ἔχομεν =
  "Our + is". Person → possessive "my" on the finite verb is accepted there.
  γνώμην = "opinion" keeps the noun tappable. γάρ `s` with "about" `t` is
  right ("about" does not mean "for"). τοῦτο = "this#0" is kept. BSB's
  forward-pointing "this" and Greek τοῦτο can both refer to the advice
  that follows, and "what" `t` covers the restated subject. It is not wrong
  enough to change a publisher row.
- 8:24: agree. "the#2 reason" `t`. "reason" does not mean "proof", so it is
  not ἔνδειξις understood again.
- 8:13: agree with ἐξ `s`. 8:5 διά = "because" and 8:8 διά = "in comparison
  to" are kept as shifted renderings of the same role (cause/means; means
  of testing). They are not new meanings.
- 8:23: changed (see above).

## Points the caller asked to settle

- 8:5 ἀλλά note: EVIDENCE shows 1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher word
  not in N1904. The publisher left both ἀλλά and διά unlinked. ἀλλά (likely
  elided in BSB's Greek) is the only plausible differing word. The note
  "Same word, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text" does not state the
  spelling, and the link is right either way. Kept.
- 8:22: πολλῇ = "great" (πεποιθήσει πολλῇ "great confidence") and
  σπουδαιότερον = "more so" ("so" stands for "earnest"), with πολύ =
  "even", are correct. The publisher's πολλῇ = "more", σπουδαιότερον = "so"
  were wrong. "his" `t` is right: τῇ (19) is the attributive article of
  τῇ εἰς ὑμᾶς, not a possessive. ὅν = "whose", ἐδοκιμάσαμεν = "has been
  proven" and σπουδαῖον ὄντα = "earnestness" are all confirmed.
- 8:8 "I#1" `t` off δοκιμάζων ("am#1 testing") is confirmed, as in 2
  Corinthians 4:8 ("are hard pressed" on the participle, "We" `t`).
- 8:9 "He#0" `t` off ὤν ("though + was") is confirmed. "He#1" sits on the
  finite ἐπτώχευσεν.
- 8:15 (Exodus 16:18) word by word: Ὁ / ὁ = "He who" / "he who" (article as
  pronoun), πολύ = "much", ὀλίγον = "little", οὐκ = "no#0" / "no#1",
  ἐπλεόνασεν = "had#0 + excess", ἠλαττόνησεν = "had#1 + shortfall", τό `s`
  twice. "gathered" is supplied twice (`t`): the Greek has no verb. All
  correct.
- Supplied words made `t` (8:2–8:24 list): each was checked against the
  Greek and has no Greek owner. These include 8:3 "they gave" (ἔδωκαν
  stands only in 8:5, and no cross-verse link is made), 8:5 "only", "did
  they do", "then", "it was", 8:6 "help", "act of", "it", 8:12 "the#1 gift
  is", 8:13 "It is", "our intention", "may#0", "while", "that#1 there may
  be", 8:14 "will#0", 8:16 "be", "I have", 8:18 "for his work", 8:20 "We
  hope", "the way", 8:21 "to do", "is", and 8:23 "he is", "they are", "to".
  No supplied copula, place "there", object, possessive, noun, "that" or
  adverb sits on a Greek word. 8:12 "is#0 there" renders πρόκειται ("is
  present"), not an added place word.
- Lone articles: none carries "of the"/"to the"/"in the". "a" goes with its
  noun (8:8 "a demand", 8:10 "a desire"). No person word sits on a
  participle.

## KEEP block 8:17 (noted only, not edited)

- μέν … δέ = "not only + but" puts "not" on a pair with no negator. As a
  discourse-pair rendering this is defensible, but it is the boldest link in
  the chapter.
- τήν `s` with "our" `t`: the majority convention would let the article
  carry "our" as a possessive.
- The notes on groups 2 6 and 5 7 9 run to several sentences, against the
  one-sentence note rule.
