# BSB Luke 1: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 80 (1:1-80). No verse was kept from an earlier review (the
draft had no KEEP blocks). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 80, "problems": 0}`.

159 of the 957 publisher `g` rows changed, in 69 verses. 41 of them only
gained unlinked Greek tokens or were joined with a neighbouring row, with the
English unchanged. Examples: ἀποκριθεὶς … εἶπεν = replied (1:19, 1:35, 1:60);
εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ/αὐτῆς = home (1:23, 1:56); εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας and εἰς τὸν
αἰῶνα = forever (1:33, 1:55); ἄνδρα οὐ γινώσκω = I am a virgin (1:34); τοῦ +
infinitive (1:73, 1:77, 1:79); article + participle (1:35, 1:79).
The other 118 rows, in 56 verses, were corrected, split or moved. Luke 1 has no
Gospel parallel, so Matthew and Mark conventions were followed.

## Publisher rows corrected

"When" on the wrong word:

- 1:12: the opening καί carried "When". Now ἰδών = When + saw. The καί is `s`,
  and "him" is `t`.
- 1:22: δέ carried "When". Now ἐξελθών = When + he came out.
- 1:41: ἐγένετο carried "When". Now ὡς = When, and καὶ ἐγένετο is `s`.
- 1:57 and 1:59: δέ and ἐγένετο carried "When"/"when". BSB turns two
  καί-joined clauses into "When …, …", so "When" now goes on that καί.

Negatives and auxiliaries:

- 1:13 and 1:30: Μή = "Do not" → Μή = not, φοβοῦ = Do + be afraid.
- 1:15: πίῃ = "He shall never take" → οὐ μή = never, πίῃ = He shall + take.
- 1:20: ἐπίστευσας = "you did not believe" → οὐκ = not, verb = you did +
  believe.
- 1:37: πᾶν carried "will ever". Now οὐκ … πᾶν = no + ever, and ἀδυνατήσει =
  will + fail.

Supplied words a Greek word had claimed (now `t`):

- Copulas: 1:5 "was" (on ἐκ), 1:18 "is", 1:27 "was" (Mary), 1:28 "is",
  1:42 "are"/"is", 1:43 "am", 1:45 "is", 1:49 "is", 1:68 "be".
- Subjects, objects and relatives: 1:2 "they" (on οἱ) and "by" (on ἀπ’); 1:5
  "who"; 1:27 "who was"; 1:28 "The angel"; 1:63 "Zechariah"; 1:65 "people";
  1:12 "him"; 1:25 "me"; 1:66 "this"; 1:70 "those".
- Other words: 1:50 "extends" (on εἰς); 1:62 "to find out" (on the article τό);
  1:75 "of … lives" (on ἡμῶν); 1:17 and 1:52 "their" (children, thrones).
- An "and" that only joins a participle or infinitive to the main verb: 1:9,
  1:22, 1:25, 1:28, 1:39, 1:63, 1:66. In 1:8, "and" (on ἐν) joins two
  circumstances that the Greek leaves without a conjunction.

Over-claims split or moved:

- 1:1: "that" moved from πραγμάτων to the participle ("that have been
  fulfilled").
- 1:2: οἱ = "they", ἀπ’ = "by", ἀρχῆς = "the initial" → οἱ = the, ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς =
  initial.
- 1:5: τῶν carried "a". Now ἐκ τῶν θυγατέρων = a daughter.
- 1:6: ἦσαν carried "of them were". Now ἀμφότεροι = Both of them, ἦσαν = were.
- 1:8: τῆς carried "while" → ἐν τῷ = while. δέ = "One day" → Ἐγένετο δέ.
- 1:9: θυμιᾶσαι carried "and burn incense". Now τοῦ θυμιᾶσαι = to + burn
  incense, and εἰσελθών = enter.
- 1:16: "back" moved from ἐπί to ἐπιστρέψει.
- 1:19: εὐαγγελίσασθαι carried "to bring you this good news". Now σοι = you,
  ταῦτα = this.
- 1:25: "these" moved from ἡμέραις to the relative αἷς.
- 1:29: the verb carried "Mary". Now the article ἡ ("she") = Mary. ὁ carried
  "of"; now ποταπός = what kind of.
- 1:34: ἔσται carried "can this be". Now ἔσται = can + be, τοῦτο = this.
- 1:36: the opening καί carried "even". Now the καί of καὶ αὐτή = even, and
  αὐτῇ = she + her. τῇ καλουμένῃ is joined (who was called).
- 1:42: "In a" moved from μεγάλῃ to κραυγῇ (In a + voice).
- 1:44: ἰδού carried "as soon" → ὡς = as soon as, and ἰδού is `s`.
- 1:48: μακαριοῦσιν carried "me". Now με = me.
- 1:53: πλουτοῦντας carried "away"; it now goes with ἐξαπέστειλεν.
- 1:57: τεκεῖν carried "her". Now αὐτήν = her, and "for" moved from Τῇ to
  Elizabeth.
- 1:58: ἐμεγάλυνεν carried "her". Now μετ’ αὐτῆς = her.
- 1:63: ἐθαύμασαν carried "all". Now πάντες = all.
- 1:64: γλῶσσα carried "was released". Now ἀνεῴχθη = was opened + was
  released (the verb is understood again with tongue).
- 1:66: τοῦτο carried "will". Now τοῦτο = this, ἔσται = will + become.
- Genitive "of" moved from the pronoun to its noun: 1:23, 1:32, 1:51, 1:69,
  1:77, 1:78, 1:80 (for example "of His father": τοῦ = of, αὐτοῦ = His).
- 1:77: τῷ carried "to"; it now goes with λαῷ.
- 1:79: the second "in" moved from σκιᾷ to ἐν, which governs both nouns.

## Notable decisions

- 1:1 and 1:3: Ἐπειδήπερ ("since") makes 1:1-2 the reason for 1:3. It is
  linked across verses to "Therefore" in 1:3 (`m` in 1:1, `g @1:1` in 1:3).
  The publisher had folded "Therefore" into παρηκολουθηκότι.
- Readings: the EVIDENCE counts match spelling only. The publisher's Greek
  spells Ἐλισάβετ (1:5, 1:7, 1:13, 1:24, 1:36, 1:40, 1:41 twice, 1:57),
  Ἰωάννην/Ἰωάννης (1:13, 1:60, 1:63) and Ἠλίου (1:17). These are linked with a
  same-name note. 1:15 has one extra publisher word but no unmatched N1904
  word. "the Lord" is ordinary English there, so no reading is claimed.
- Pronoun rendered by a name or noun, linked with a note: 1:8 and 1:64
  Zechariah's, 1:11 Zechariah, 1:17 the Lord, 1:29 Mary (article ἡ), 1:56
  Elizabeth, 1:62 the child.
- Relative with its noun drawn into the clause: 1:4 ὧν … λόγων = the things,
  and 1:20 ἧς ἡμέρας = the day.
- Idioms joined: 1:10 πλῆθος τοῦ λαοῦ = congregation; 1:31 συλλήμψῃ ἐν γαστρί
  = conceive; 1:39 ἐπορεύθη … μετὰ σπουδῆς = hurried; 1:42 ἀνεφώνησεν … καὶ
  εἶπεν = exclaimed; 1:50 εἰς γενεὰς καὶ γενεάς = from generation to
  generation; 1:59 ἐπὶ τῷ ὀνόματι = after; 1:70 διὰ στόματος = through.
- Kept publisher rows: 1:38 Ἰδού = "I am" (presentational, "here is"); 1:40 καί
  = where; 1:21 καί = Meanwhile; 1:11 δέ = Just then. The genitive and dative
  articles keep "of the", "to the" and "at the", following Matthew and Mark.
- 1:14: "He" is `t`. In Greek the subject of ἔσται is "joy" ("there will be joy
  to you"); BSB makes the child the subject.
- 1:76: Καὶ … δέ are joined as the one "And".

## Doubts for the peer

- 1:22: "When" is on the participle ἐξελθών, and the καί before ἐπέγνωσαν is
  `s`. The καί convention ("When …, …") would instead put "When" on that καί,
  because BSB draws "was unable" into the When-clause.
- 1:1 and 1:3: the cross-verse link Ἐπειδήπερ = Therefore. The alternative is
  `s` in 1:1 with "Therefore" as `t`, a split pair.
- 1:36: αὐτῇ = she + her ("she … is in her sixth month"), with οὗτος as `s` and
  "in" as `t`. "her" could be `t` instead.
- 1:38: Ἰδού = "I am" is kept. It could be `s` with "I am" as `t`.
- 1:37: "ever" is joined with οὐκ … πᾶν. It could be `t`.
- 1:5: ἐξ = "belonged to", with "who" as `t`. 1:27 uses ἐξ = "of", with "who
  was" as `t`.
- 1:9: "to" is on τοῦ θυμιᾶσαι, although it stands before "enter".
