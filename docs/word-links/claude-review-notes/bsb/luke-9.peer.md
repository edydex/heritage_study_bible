## Changes (2 verses)

- 9:34: δέ "While" → `s` (connective not expressed); λέγοντος "was
  speaking" → "While + was speaking", with the note "The genitive absolute
  αὐτοῦ λέγοντος gives the time clause." ταῦτα δὲ αὐτοῦ λέγοντος is a
  genitive absolute. By the convention, "When/As/While" goes on the
  participle, not on δέ. The author did this in 9:37, 9:42, 9:43 and 9:57
  and in the author note, but the publisher row in 9:34 had been left as it
  was. Matthew 17:5 (ἔτι αὐτοῦ λαλοῦντος) links "While" to the participle
  in the same way.
- 9:41: note only. On ἀνέξομαι = "put up with", the note said it was
  rendered "must I … put up with", but "must I" belongs to the ἔσομαι
  group. It now reads: rendered "put up with", under the same "must I". The
  link is unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- 9:12, "As" on the second δέ: kept. The δέ that joins the two clauses is
  the one BSB turns into "As …, …". This extends the καί rule, which the
  reviewed files apply when the clauses are joined by καί after an opening
  δέ: Luke 1:57 (Τῇ δὲ … ἐπλήσθη …, καὶ ἐγέννησεν, "When" on καί), Mark
  10:32 and 15:25. The opening δέ only links to 9:11, so `s` is right.
- 9:4, καί = "until": kept. BSB turns "stay there and leave from there" into
  "stay there until you leave". "Until" carries the link between the two
  commands, and that is the καί's job. Luke 2:44 already links καί =
  "before" in the same kind of recast. Mark 6:10 and Matthew 10:11 have ἕως
  ἄν, so they do not apply.
- 9:18, καί = the "and" before "the disciples": kept. The verse has one
  clause-joining καί and BSB has one "and". BSB moves "the disciples were
  with Him" into the "as" clause and makes ἐπηρώτησεν the main verb. `s`
  plus `t and` would be a split pair for the same coordinator.
- 9:52, καί = "who": kept. Mark 6:27-28 ("He sent an executioner, who
  went") links the καί of Greek 6:28 to "who" in the same construction.
- 9:58 and 9:62, τήν = His/his: kept. This is the majority convention for an
  article used as a possessive, and it is consistent with Mark 9:18 τούς =
  "his". Matthew 8:20, the identical verse, still has `t His` with τήν
  `s`. It is the outlier and should be aligned with Luke 9:58 later.
  Neither file is changed here. Luke 9:58 also matches Matthew 8:20 on
  τοῦ οὐρανοῦ = "of the air" and on ἔχουσιν = have#0 + have#1.
- 9:8 and 9:19, δέ = "and still": kept. It matches Mark 8:28 and Matthew
  16:14 (ἄλλοι δέ … "and still others"). The other δέ in 9:19 is `s`
  (semicolon), as in Mark 8:28.
- 9:26, ἐν/τῇ/δόξῃ linked to the repeated "in the glory" as well: kept.
  English repeats the phrase that one ἐν τῇ δόξῃ governs, and the repeated
  "the" is the only "the" before "glory". This follows "a word understood
  again may link to each English repeat".
- 9:29, ἐγένετο … ἕτερον = "changed" with `t became`: kept. Linking
  ἐγένετο alone to "became" would leave ἕτερον = "changed", which reads
  worse. The `t` note is accurate: the clothes clause has no verb of its
  own.
- 9:36, ἐσίγησαν = "kept this to themselves" with `t with them`: kept. It
  is an idiom for "were silent"; "with them" has no Greek counterpart.
- 9:53, οὐκ = "refused" and ἐδέξαντο = "to welcome", split: kept. Where the
  reviewed files join a "refused" (2 Thessalonians 2:10 οὐκ ἐδέξαντο = "they
  refused"; Matthew 18:30, 22:3, 27:34), English has no separate word for
  the verb. Here BSB has its own "welcome" for δέχομαι, so the split is
  exact and follows the rule that negatives are split off verbs.
- 9:39, μετὰ ἀφροῦ = "so that he foams at the mouth" as one group: kept.
  BSB recasts the whole prepositional phrase as a result clause, so "so
  that" has no other owner. Mark 9:18 and 9:20 render ἀφρίζει/ἀφρίζων as
  "foams/foaming at the mouth".
