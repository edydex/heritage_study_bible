# BSB Revelation 2: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 29 of 29 verses (2:1-29). This chapter has no KEEP blocks.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 29, "problems": 0}`.

I started from the author's final file and exported every verse with
`totext --all`. I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against the Greek, the
BSB English and the publisher record. I compared the letter formulas with
registered Revelation 1 and 3 and with the settled Revelation conventions.
No lone article carries "of", "to" or "in". I changed links in four verses
(2:2, 2:9, 2:18, 2:19) and notes in two (2:27, 2:28). Everything else stays
as the author left it.

## Changes

- 2:2 (link): the article τόν (5) was "your" before "labor". Now the σου
  after ὑπομονήν (10) = "your#1 + your#2", and τόν is `s`. In τὸν κόπον καὶ
  τὴν ὑπομονήν σου the one σου covers both nouns, so both "your"s render
  that σου. Luke 2:47 is the precedent (ἐπὶ τῇ συνέσει καὶ ταῖς ἀποκρίσεσιν
  αὐτοῦ: the one αὐτοῦ = His + His, both articles `s`). An article counts as a
  possessive only when no Greek possessive covers the noun.
- 2:2 (link): κακούς "those who are evil" → "those who + evil", and "are" is
  `t`. The copula is supplied. Revelation 3:7 makes "is" `t` in "the One who is
  holy", and 1 Corinthians 10:17 and Acts 2:39 do the same.
- 2:9 (link): τήν (5) was "your" before "poverty". Now the σου placed before
  both nouns (1) = "your#0 + your#1", and τήν is `s`. This follows the same
  rule as 2:2.
- 2:18 (link): φλόγα πυρός "a blazing fire" was joined. Now φλόγα = "blazing"
  and πυρός = "a + fire". Registered Revelation 1:14 has the same Greek and the
  same English and links it this way. Each word now gets its own link.
- 2:19 (link): the articles before ἀγάπην, πίστιν and διακονίαν were "your".
  Now the σου after ὑπομονήν (16) = "your#1 + your#2 + your#3 + your#4", and
  all four articles are `s`. BSB's dashes enclose exactly that list: "your
  deeds—your love, your faith, your service, your perseverance—". σου (1)
  stays with "your deeds", as in the formula Οἶδά σου τὰ ἔργα. τῶν (24)
  stays "your" in "than your first". No σου covers τῶν πρώτων, so the
  article-as-possessive rule applies there.
- 2:27 (note only): added a note to `g @2:28 0` ("just as"). The note says that
  N1904 prints these words at the start of 2:28. Romans 3:25 and 2 Corinthians
  10:5 have the same kind of note on their cross-verse groups.
- 2:28 (note only): the `s` reason for τοῦ (4) now says it has no English word
  in BSB's "from My Father" at the end of 2:27. The old wording ("in this
  English wording") was unclear, because 2:28's English has no "from My
  Father".

## Points I was asked to settle

- **2:27/2:28 verse division: confirmed real.** The publisher's 2:27 record
  has 12 N1904 words, all matched, against 19 publisher words, so 7 are
  extra. Its 2:28 record has 14 N1904 words with only 7 matched, against 7
  publisher words. The 7 words are exactly ὡς κἀγὼ εἴληφα παρὰ τοῦ Πατρός μου.
  BSB's 2:27 English ends "just as I have received authority from My Father",
  and its 2:28 is only "And I will give him the morning star." So this is a
  real numbering difference, not a moved connective. The mechanics match
  Romans 3:25–26 and 2 Corinthians 10:4–5. The source verse has an `m … >`
  line with the reason, and the English verse has `g @` groups. One
  difference: Romans moved its article too (τῇ ἀνοχῇ joined as
  "forbearance"). Here τοῦ stays `s` in 2:28. An `s` line cannot point to
  another verse, and joining the article to Πατρός would depart from how
  this chapter treats articles, so I kept it. "Authority" is a supplied
  object (`t`), because ἐξουσίαν is in 2:26. κἀγώ = "I", because BSB has no
  "also" (conventions; 3:21).
- **2:9 καὶ οὐκ = "falsely", εἰσίν = "are": kept.** In καὶ οὐκ εἰσίν ἀλλὰ
  συναγωγὴ τοῦ Σατανᾶ, συναγωγή is the predicate nominative of εἰσίν ("they
  are not [Jews] but a synagogue"). So the "are" of "but are in fact a
  synagogue" is εἰσίν's own copula. It is not a supplied copula linked to an
  εἰμί in another clause. "Falsely" renders "and … not", the denial of their
  claim. In 2:2 BSB has no other "are", so all three words are "falsely".
  Revelation 3:9 is consistent: there the English says "but are liars
  instead", so καὶ οὐκ εἰσίν is `s` and ψεύδονται = "are liars". "In fact" is
  an added adverb (`t`).
- **2:2 and 2:19 possessives:** changed to one σου on every "your" it covers
  (see Changes). This is the same-role rule, with Luke 2:47 as precedent.
- **2:20 "By" and 2:21 "Even though": kept.** Both stand on the καί that
  joins the two clauses BSB subordinates ("When" convention). In 2:21 the καί
  is adversative ("and yet"), which is close to concessive "even though". The
  opening καί of 2:21 only links to 2:20 and is `s`.
- **2:3 οὐ = "Without": kept.** "Without" is the negative; κεκοπίακες =
  "growing weary", and the καί before it is `s`.
- **2:17 "only" = οὐδεὶς … εἰ μή: kept.** "No one … except" means "only". This
  is a real rendering, not an added "only".
- **2:24: kept.** ταύτην ("this") rendered "her" has a different meaning, so
  ταύτην is `s` and "her" is `t`. The article τήν goes with the demonstrative,
  not as a possessive. οἵτινες ("who") is not the "and" that joins the clauses,
  so it is `s` and "and" is `t`.
- **οὐ δύνῃ "cannot" (2:2), οὐ θέλει "is unwilling" (2:21): kept joined.** The
  English fuses the negative into one word, so it cannot be split off.
- **Readings: kept.** Only 2:5, 2:10 and 2:22 claim a different reading. In
  each, the record shows exactly one N1904 word unmatched against one
  publisher word. The publisher's English row for that word is left over:
  "you have fallen" (2:5), "Do not" (2:10) and "they repent" (2:22). Each
  linked word has the same meaning, and each note is neutral. 2:13, 2:19 and
  2:25 report 0/0 unmatched words and claim nothing. No note in the review
  file names the publisher's Greek.

## Answers to the author's doubts

1. 2:2 and 2:19 possessives: changed (see above). 2:9 follows the same rule.
   2:13 τήν = "your faith" stays, because no σου covers it there.
2. 2:9 εἰσίν = "are": agree (see above).
3. 2:20 "By" and 2:21 "Even though": agree.
4. 2:10 "even unto": agree. "Even" is an added adverb (`t`), and ἄχρι = "unto".
5. 2:2 κακούς: changed. "Are" is now a supplied copula (`t`), as in Revelation
   3:7.
6. 2:23: agree. ἀποκτενῶ = "I will strike" and ἐν θανάτῳ = "dead" give each
   part of "strike … dead" its own Greek owner.

## Settled Revelation conventions

I checked them all against this chapter, and all of them hold: the opening Καί
of 2:8, 12 and 18 is `s`; the address line, Τάδε λέγει and the hearing formula
are linked as settled; Τῷ νικῶντι = "To the one who overcomes" with αὐτῷ `s`
(2:7, 17); ὁ νικῶν with αὐτῷ = "to" (2:26); "the right" is `t` (2:7); and the
second substantival titles ὁ περιπατῶν (2:1) and ὁ τηρῶν (2:26) are joined
with their own article. No bare participle shares an earlier article in this
chapter. κἀγώ = "I also" in 2:6, where BSB has "also", and "I" in 2:27. I
think none of the shared formulas is wrong.

## Still uncertain

- 2:15 οὕτως `s` with ὁμοίως = "In the same way". The two words are near
  synonyms, and either could own the phrase. Joining both is also defensible.
- 2:22 "of sickness" `t`. BDAG glosses βάλλειν εἰς κλίνην here as "throw on a
  sickbed", so linking "a bed of sickness" to κλίνην could be argued. I kept
  the supplied-noun rule.
