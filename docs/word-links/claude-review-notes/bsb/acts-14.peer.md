# BSB Acts 14: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 28 of 28 verses (14:1-28), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 28, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 1 verse (14:22), one linked edit (καί `g` → `s`, with the English
"and" now `t`). The other 27 verses are kept exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 14:22 καί (before ὅτι): before καί = "and" (the "and" between
  "strengthening" and "encouraging", note "BSB … places this καί between
  strengthening and encouraging") → after καί `s` ("BSB makes the ὅτι clause
  a separate quotation, so the καί joining it to continue in the faith has
  no English word") and "and" `t` ("English joins the participles
  strengthening and encouraging, which the Greek sets side by side without
  a conjunction"). The καί joins the two things urged by παρακαλοῦντες
  (ἐμμένειν τῇ πίστει and the ὅτι clause). BSB turns its second half into a
  new sentence with "they said". The English "and" joins two participles
  that stand without a conjunction in the Greek (asyndeton). So the "and" is
  at a different level from the καί, not the same "and" moved, and `s` +
  `t` is not a split pair. This is the Acts 8:30 case: the καί belongs to a
  clause BSB recasts as a separate quotation, and the only "and" joins
  participles. Reviewed rows treat a supplied "and" between asyndetic
  participles as `t`.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- **14:19 and 14:27, shifted καί = "and"**: kept. In both verses the καί
  joins two participles of the same chain (14:19 πείσαντες καὶ λιθάσαντες,
  14:27 Παραγενόμενοι καὶ συναγαγόντες). BSB's "and" stands before the main
  verb at the end of that chain ("They stoned Paul and dragged him"; "they
  gathered the church together and reported"). Reviewed rows link this καί
  to the "and" closing the series: Matthew 26:26 and 26:27, Mark 1:26, Luke
  10:30, Matthew 27:48, John 12:29, Acts 10:4 (author and peer agreed), and
  Acts 8:35. Acts 8:30 is different: there the καί stood before εἶπεν,
  outside the participle chain, and BSB moved its clause after the
  quotation. The `t` rule for a participle "and" applies when no καί stands
  in the chain (14:6, 14:14 and 14:20 are rightly `t`). In 14:27 BSB also
  makes συναγαγόντες a finite verb, so the "and" there joins two English
  main verbs.
- **14:22 καί**: changed (above). The author rightly called it the weakest.
- **14:22 ὅτι `s` + "they said" `t`**: confirmed. ὅτι means "that" and
  introduces the content. It is not a verb of speaking (Acts 6:11: "ὅτι
  introduces the direct quotation and has no English word"). "they said" is
  BSB's frame for a direct quotation. It is not παρακαλοῦντες understood
  again either: that word is already "encouraging", and "said" is not what
  it means.
- **14:13 "temple", "city" (city gates), τοῦ ὄντος, πυλῶνας**: confirmed.
  BSB reads "The priest of Zeus, whose temple was just outside the city,
  brought bulls and wreaths to the city gates". The Greek says "of Zeus, the
  one [being] before the city": the cult title is about Zeus, and no word for
  "temple" stands in the Greek, so `t` is right. τοῦ ὄντος = "whose … was":
  the article with the participle gives the relative "who … was", and
  "whose" is only its English possessive form, which the added "temple"
  requires. ὄντος is the "was". πυλῶνας = "gates" is its plain meaning
  ("gateways, gates"). Whether these are the city's or the temple's gates is
  disputed, and the Greek does not say, so "city" is `t`.
- **14:6 εἰς = to + to**: confirmed. One εἰς governs both τὰς πόλεις … and
  τὴν περίχωρον, and BSB repeats "to" (as Acts 6:11 against + against). In
  14:21 the opposite applies: three εἰς, one "to", and the 2nd and 3rd
  are `s`.
- **14:11 ἀνθρώποις = "human", ὁμοιωθέντες = "in … form"**: confirmed. Each
  English word renders its own Greek word ("men" → "human"; "made like" →
  "in … form"). Joining them into one group is not needed.
- **14:12 second "they called" on ἐκάλουν**: confirmed. The one ἐκάλουν
  takes both double accusatives (τὸν Βαρνάβαν Δία, τὸν δὲ Παῦλον Ἑρμῆν). It
  has the same role in both clauses, so the repeat is that verb understood
  again. 14:12 λόγου = "speaker" (note) and ὁ ἡγούμενος = "the chief"
  (article + participle joined) are also confirmed.
- **14:15 moves**: confirmed. "like" belongs to ὁμοιοπαθεῖς ("of like
  nature" → "human like"), and ὑμῖν = "you". ὑμᾶς = "you" (bringing you good
  news), and εὐαγγελιζόμενοι keeps "We are bringing + good news". πάντα =
  "everything", with τά `s`. καὶ λέγοντες `s` is right: BSB's "shouting"
  (14:14) introduces the speech. "only" `t` is right.
- **14:16 move**: confirmed. αὐτῶν = "their own" and ταῖς `s`, as the
  possessive pronoun is present (majority convention). ὅς = "He" (the
  relative becomes a new sentence) is a Greek word in a new role and is
  rightly linked.
- **14:26 "just" `t`**: confirmed. Nothing in ἐπλήρωσαν or elsewhere means
  "just". κἀκεῖθεν = "From Attalia" (a pro-adverb rendered by the name) is
  kept with its note.
- **Readings (14:9, 14:14, 14:21, 14:25)**: confirmed against the
  publisher records. Each verse has a `partial` record with 1 N1904 word
  unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904. In each, the publisher left
  the noted word unlinked although BSB's English for it is there, which
  fits it being the unmatched word: 14:9 ἤκουεν, 14:14 ἑαυτῶν, 14:21
  εὐαγγελιζόμενοί, 14:25 Ἀτταλίαν. The other
  20 verses have no `partial` record, and no note claims a reading there.
  The four notes say only "a different form of this verb/pronoun" or "Same
  name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text", and none states the
  publisher's exact Greek.

## Other rows checked and kept

- Supplied words as `t`: 14:3 and 14:28 "there" (place), 14:3, 14:18 and
  14:23 "Paul and Barnabas", 14:10 "Paul" and "the man" (verb endings
  only), 14:8 "was" (copula), 14:6 "about it", 14:14 "about this", 14:19
  "him", 14:22 "them" (supplied objects), 14:20 "back", 14:21 "Then",
  14:23 "as".
- 14:8 ἐκάθητο = "there sat": the existential "there" stays with the verb,
  as reviewed rows put "there was" on ἦν. It is not a place adverb.
- Greek words in a new role, linked: 14:1 αὐτούς = "Paul and Barnabas",
  14:3 αὐτῶν = "them" (object of enabling), 14:8 ὅς = "who", 14:16 ὅς =
  "He".
- Connectives rendered by a different connective: 14:1 καί = "where", 14:18
  καί = "Even", 14:23 μετά = "and" (praying and fasting).
- τε in τε … καί `s`: 14:1, 14:5, 14:21. A lone connective τε left
  untranslated is `s`: 14:11, 14:12, 14:13.
- Lone articles: no "of the", "to the" or "in the" sits on a lone article.
  The prepositions are on the nouns (14:4, 14:6, 14:8, 14:22, 14:23, 14:26,
  14:27).

## Still uncertain

- 14:19 and 14:27: the stricter option would be καί `s` + "and" `t`, as was
  noted for Acts 8:35. I kept the link because the καί stands in the
  participle chain that BSB's "and" closes (precedents above).
- 14:22 διά = "endure" (publisher row, with a note): BSB turns "through
  many hardships" into "endure many hardships", so the preposition becomes a
  verb. I kept it as a shifted rendering of διά. The alternative is διά
  `s` with "endure" `t`.
- 14:22 "in" (continue in the faith) is on πίστει (dative). The ἐν- of
  ἐμμένειν could carry it instead ("to continue in"). Both are defensible,
  so it was not changed.
