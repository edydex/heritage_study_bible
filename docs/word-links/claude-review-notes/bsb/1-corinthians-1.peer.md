# BSB 1 Corinthians 1: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 31 of 31 verses (1:1-31). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Every
`g`, `s` and `t` line was checked against the Greek, BSB's English and the
publisher record for each verse in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/1-corinthians.json`. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 31, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

- 1:10: ἵνα = "that" → "that + that#2"; `t that#2` removed. The third clause
  ("and that you may be united", ἦτε δὲ κατηρτισμένοι) still stands under the
  one ἵνα: its verb is subjunctive like λέγητε and ᾖ. BSB's repeated "that"
  has the same role and meaning as the first one, so it is not an added
  "that". The reviewed files link this repeat to ἵνα every time: Romans
  15:31, John 11:50, Ephesians 6:3 and 6:22, Colossians 4:8 and
  2 Thessalonians 1:11. The note now reads "English repeats that for the last
  clause under the same ἵνα." The middle clause keeps καί = "so that". The
  καί joins the clauses, and BSB turns that link into result (Luke 8:23,
  Philippians 1:20).
- 1:20: the note on συνζητητής = "the philosopher" was unclear ("this
  "debater" is rendered philosopher"). It now reads: "Same word ("debater"),
  spelled differently in BSB's Greek text; BSB renders it philosopher." The
  note still does not name the publisher's spelling. The link is unchanged.

No other change. Every other verse in the peer JSON is identical to the
author's.

## Points settled

- 1:12 (εἰμι + genitive = "follow" ×4): kept as the author has it. "I am of
  Paul" is a genitive of belonging: Paul's party, Paul's man. "I follow Paul"
  renders that same allegiance, so the meaning has not changed. The reviewed
  files already link εἰμί in a belonging construction to a belonging verb:
  1 John 3:19 (ἐκ ἐσμέν = "we belong to"), 1 John 3:12, and
  1 Thessalonians 5:5 (ἐσμέν + genitive = "we do + belong"). In the last
  three slogans the verb is understood, and BSB repeats it, so all four
  "follow" link to the one εἰμι (John 15:4, 12:13). The four are treated the
  same. The names carry no "of", because "follow" absorbs the genitive
  relation. Each explicit Ἐγώ keeps its own "I".
- 1:11 (ὑπὸ τῶν Χλόης): kept as split. τῶν = "some from + household" and
  Χλόης = "Chloe’s". The article stands for "those of Chloe". This follows
  Romans 16:10-11, where τῶν = "the household" and the genitive name is
  linked separately. Because the article is used as a pronoun, it may carry
  "from". BSB's "from" expresses membership in the household, not agency.
  BSB turns the agent into the subject, so ὑπό has no English word and `s`
  is right. Joining τῶν Χλόης as one unit (as in Acts 16:33, οἱ αὐτοῦ) would
  also be acceptable. The split is chosen because "Chloe’s" renders the
  name alone exactly.
- 1:2 (Lord#1 on Κυρίου): kept. BSB takes αὐτῶν καὶ ἡμῶν with Κυρίου
  ("their Lord and ours"). The repeated "Lord" is the same head noun with the
  same role, now possessed by "their", and has the same meaning. It is the
  word understood again.
- 1:7 (second "you" on ὑμᾶς): kept. ἀπεκδεχομένους agrees with ὑμᾶς, which is
  also the subject of ὑστερεῖσθαι. Both English "you"s are subjects, so the
  role is the same, and an explicit pronoun owns them (Acts 22:3). The
  participle keeps only "as + eagerly await", with no person.
- 1:8 ("you#1" `t`): confirmed. ὑμᾶς is the object of "sustain", while BSB's
  second "you" is the subject of a result clause that BSB supplies ("so that
  you will be blameless"). The role is different, so the word is not linked.
  "so that" and "will be" stay `t`.
- 1:13: "Is + divided" and "Was + crucified" put the passive auxiliaries on
  the finite verbs. This is correct. μή, marking a question that expects
  "no", is `s`. ἤ is `s` because BSB starts a new question.
- 1:31: "Let + boast" on the imperative καυχάσθω, and Ὁ καυχώμενος = "him
  who boasts". This is correct. ἵνα = "Therefore" stays. ἵνα is linked to a
  consequence connective in Luke 18:5 ("Then"), Hebrews 6:18 ("Thus") and
  1 Thessalonians 4:12 ("Then").
- 1:30: "It is … that" is `t` as a cleft frame, and δέ is `s`. The
  publisher's δέ = "It is" over-claimed. "our" is `t`: BSB's possessive
  "our righteousness" is not the role of the dative ἡμῖν ("for us"), which is
  already linked to "for us".
- "of" on Κυρίου with "our" on ἡμῶν (1:2, 1:7, 1:8, 1:10): confirmed. "of"
  marks Κυρίου's relation to the noun before it, and ἡμῶν is the possessive
  of Κυρίου.
- Readings: all agree with the publisher counts. In 1:4, "my" is `t`: the
  publisher has 18 Greek words against N1904's 17, with all 17 matched, so
  there is one extra word. In 1:14, "God" is `t`: 12 against 10, so two extra
  words. In 1:28, "and" before "the things that are not" is `t`: 19 against
  18, so one extra word. In 1:20, 16 of 17 words are matched and the counts
  are equal, so one word differs in form. The notes say only "a reading with
  an extra word/extra words" or "spelled differently in BSB's Greek text",
  and none names the publisher's Greek. Elsewhere in the chapter, no verse
  has a partial record and none claims a reading.
- The quotations are linked word by word with no interpretive notes: 1:19
  (Isaiah 29:14), with "of + wise" and "of + intelligent" on the nouns and
  "the" on each τῶν, and 1:31 (Jeremiah 9:24).
- Brief rules re-checked across the chapter. Supplied copulas are `t`: 1:1,
  1:2, 1:8, 1:9, 1:12, 1:20 ×3, 1:24, 1:25 "is#1" and 1:26 ×3. 1:25 "is#1"
  is not linked to the ἐστίν of the first clause. No person word sits on a
  participle. Modal words are on finite verbs: 1:10 "may be", 1:15 "can
  say", 1:29 "may boast". No "of the"/"to the"/"in the" sits on a lone
  article, except τῶν in 1:11, which is used as a pronoun.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 1:2 Lord#1: keep linked (reason above).
- 1:7 ὑμᾶς = "you + you": keep (reason above).
- 1:11: keep the split with ὑπό `s`. ὑπό = "from" would claim the agent
  marker for a word that expresses membership. Joining τῶν Χλόης is a
  tolerable alternative but not needed.
- 1:12 εἰμι = "follow" ×4: keep linked. The meaning is the same
  (belonging/allegiance), so it is not a different-meaning word that would
  need `s` + `t`.
- 1:17 λόγου = "words of": keep the author's correction. The genitive case
  is on λόγου, while σοφίᾳ is dative after ἐν, which is linked to "with".
  The publisher's σοφίᾳ = "of wisdom" therefore put the English genitive
  marker on a word that is not genitive.
- 1:26 τήν `s` with "the time of" `t`: keep. BSB's "the" introduces the
  supplied "time", not κλῆσιν, whose determiner in English is "your".
- 1:31 ἵνα = "Therefore" and 1:10 καί = "so that": keep both linked.
  Each is the connective rendered by a related connective, with
  purpose/result recast as consequence/result. The reviewed files do the
  same (see above).

## Still uncertain

- 1:17: κενωθῇ = "be emptied of its power" is kept whole (publisher row).
  "of its power" spells out the verb's sense ("be made void"), as κεκένωται =
  "is useless" does in Romans 4:14 and κενῶς = "without reason" does in
  James 4:5. A stricter reading could make "of its power" `t` as a supplied
  complement.
- 1:12 δέ = "or" and 1:16 δέ = "Yes" are publisher rows, kept as
  connectives rendered by BSB's list or discourse marker (Philippians 1:18,
  ἀλλά = "Yes"). They are not wrong, though `s` + `t` would be stricter.
- 1:6 τό = "our": BSB's "our testimony" is interpretive. It is kept under
  the article-as-possessive convention, and the publisher row is not wrong.
