## Answers to the author's doubts

- 2:1, καί `s` + `t And`: **agree.** This is not a split pair. The καί
  opens the verse and links it to 1:45. BSB replaces it with "A few days
  later". BSB's "And" exists because BSB made the participle clause its own
  sentence and then joined it to ἠκούσθη. The publisher's own row put "And"
  with ἠκούσθη, not with καί. Matthew 26:22, 26:37 and 26:39 follow the same
  convention. Linking καί = And would also be defensible.
- 2:18, one "fast" for both νηστεύουσιν: **agree.** The first verb is
  gapped in English ("like John’s disciples … [fast]"), so the single "fast"
  renders both. δέ `s` + `t like` is not a split pair: "like" makes a
  comparison, and δέ ("but") is a contrast. "often" as `t`: **agree.** No
  Greek word means "often". The 2:18 counts are fully explained by the two
  Ἰωάνου spellings, so there is no πολλά reading as in Matthew 9:14. Joining
  ἦσαν … νηστεύοντες would not make "often" any less supplied.
- 2:19, οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ νυμφῶνος: **the Mark treatment is right, and Mark
  should differ from Matthew 9:15.** The publisher's νυμφῶνος = "bridegroom"
  is lexically wrong (νυμφών is the bridal chamber; νυμφίος is the
  bridegroom). Matthew 9:15's νυμφῶνος `s` + `t bridegroom` is a split pair.
  The English "bridegroom" is there only because BSB renders the idiom that
  contains νυμφῶνος. Rule 3 joins fixed idioms. So υἱοὶ τοῦ νυμφῶνος =
  guests of the bridegroom is correct, and the note tells the reader what
  νυμφῶνος means. Matthew 9:15 should be brought into line when it is next
  reviewed. It was not edited here.
- 2:22, καί = "and both": **keep.** The pair καὶ ὁ οἶνος … καὶ οἱ ἀσκοί
  can be read as the correlative "both … and", and the publisher's own row
  gives "and both". `t both` would also be defensible. It is not an error.
- 2:2, "such": **keep** it with πολλοί (publisher row). Moving it to ὥστε
  would be a matter of taste.
- 2:15, καὶ γίνεται = While: **agree.** BSB renders the narrative frame
  with "While". In 2:23 the frame καὶ ἐγένετο has no English counterpart
  ("One Sabbath"), so `s` there is consistent.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 2:26 τούς = "for the": the author note groups this with dative articles,
  but τοὺς ἱερεῖς is accusative, the subject of φαγεῖν. "for" renders that
  construction ("lawful for the priests [to eat]"), so the link was kept.
  τούς = the with `t for` is the stricter alternative.
- 2:17 "who" in χρείαν ἔχουσιν = who need. It is part of BSB's cleft ("It is
  not … who"), so `t who` beside `t It is` is the alternative. It matches
  "who followed" in 2:15.
- 2:19 and 2:11 group notes name a whole Greek phrase (οἱ υἱοὶ …; εἰς τὸν
  οἶκόν σου) whose article is linked or explained separately. This is
  harmless and was not changed.
