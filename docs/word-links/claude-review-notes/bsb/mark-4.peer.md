Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB,

## Changes (4 verses)

- 4:1: ἐν = "in", τῇ θαλάσσῃ `s`, `t it` → ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ `s` ("on the
  sea" is not rendered) and `t in it`. Why: BSB's "in it" means in the boat;
  the Greek ἐν goes with the sea. ESV ("sat in it on the sea") and NIV
  ("sat in it out on the lake") show "in it" as an English addition next to
  the phrase. Linking ἐν alone half-linked a phrase whose meaning BSB
  changed.
- 4:21: ὁ λύχνος = "a lamp", τὸν μόδιον = "a basket", τὴν κλίνην = "a bed",
  τὴν λυχνίαν = "a stand" → the nouns alone carry "a lamp", "a basket",
  "a bed" and "a stand", and the four articles are `s`. Why: rule 5. These
  articles are not rendered by "a". The same saying in reviewed Matthew 5:15
  links "a basket" and "a stand" to the nouns and gives the articles `s`.
- 4:35: ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ = "that" → ἐκείνῃ = "that" (as in the publisher
  row) and ἐν … τῇ ἡμέρᾳ `s`. Why: "that" renders only the demonstrative.
  "day" is dropped, so folding ἡμέρᾳ into "that" over-claimed (rule 1).
  Matthew 13:1 also links ἐκείνῃ alone ("That same").
- 4:40: οὕτως = "so" → οὕτως `s` and `t so`, both with the reading reason.
  Why: the counts are 11 N1904 words against 9 publisher words, with 8
  matched. The three unmatched N1904 words are οὕτως, πῶς and οὐκ, and the
  one extra publisher word is οὔπω. So BSB's Greek reads Τί δειλοί ἐστε;
  οὔπω ἔχετε πίστιν; and has no οὕτως. The publisher row δειλοί = "so
  afraid" agrees: "so" is English emphasis. Linking it to οὕτως invented a
  link for a reading BSB does not follow (rule 9).

All other rows were kept. The author's corrections of publisher rows are
right, including the `t Jesus` lines, καί = "Once"/"As soon as"/"Others"/
"When"/"After" → `s`, εἶτα = "When" → `s` with γενομένης = When + comes
(as in Matthew 13:21), αὐτῷ = "more" → `s` with `t more` (as in Matthew
13:12), and the split negatives (οὐκ = not, verb = did + have) in 4:5, 4:13,
4:25 and 4:34.

## Textual claims

- 4:40: see above. οὐκ = "no" is kept with its note. οὔπω contains the same
  negation in the same place, and reviewed Matthew 20:26 likewise links the
  N1904 word for a variant form with a reading note. πῶς `s` and `t still`
  correctly name the οὔπω reading.
- 4:19 `t this`: 23 against 23, 22 matched. The one difference is
  συνπνίγουσιν/συμπνίγουσιν, so BSB's Greek has no τούτου. The reason is
  correct.
- 4:28 (εἶτεν twice, σῖτος) and 4:32 (κατασκηνοῖν): the counts allow only
  these spelling differences. They are linked normally, with notes.
- 4:8 `t another`: the counts cannot show this, because the comparison ignores
  accents (ἐν/ἕν). Both "one" and "another" stay `t`, so no link depends on the
  claim.
- The other verses have no `partial` key and make no reading claim.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 4:40 οὕτως = so: **disagree**, changed (see above).
- 4:1 ἐν = in: **disagree**, changed. The phrase-level link ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ =
  "in it" would bring back the publisher's θαλάσσῃ = "it" error. Leaving the
  whole phrase unlinked is the honest choice.
- 4:10 σύν = with, `t and`: **agree.** BSB keeps "with the Twelve" and only
  moves it after alone. σύν means with, and "and" is the new English joint.
- 4:16 ὅταν `s`, `t and`: **agree.** "and" replaces the subordinating clause
  but does not mean "when". This is not a split pair.
- 4:19 `t this`: **agree** (see Textual claims; same as Matthew 13:22).
- 4:21 Μήτι = Does: **agree.** Μήτι is what makes the clause a question.
- 4:22 ἐὰν μή = not, ἀλλ’ = not: **agree.** "nothing hidden except to be
  disclosed" and "nothing hidden that will not be disclosed" say the same
  thing. The exceptive (ἐὰν μή; ἀλλά after οὐδέ) is what BSB's "not"
  expresses. Making them `s` with `t not` would create split pairs.
- 4:25 αὐτῷ `s`, `t more`: **agree.** "whoever" as the passive subject absorbs
  the dative. No Greek word means "more" (as in Matthew 13:12).
- 4:35 ἐν ἐκείνῃ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ = that: **disagree**, changed (see above).
- 4:31 second ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς = upon the earth: **agree.** In BSB, "upon the
  earth" describes "seeds", and so does the second phrase (τῶν σπερμάτων τῶν
  ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς). Only "sown" comes from the first clause.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 4:15 and 4:18 εἰσιν = "are like": "like" may be English supplied for the
  simile (`t like`). It is kept for consistency with the reviewed 4:20 and the
  publisher rows. In 4:16, ὁμοίως carries "like".
- 4:8 καὶ ἀναβαίνοντα = "where it sprouted" and 4:20 καὶ ἐν ἑκατόν = "or a
  hundredfold" (earlier review): coarse joint groups that could be split
  (ἀναβαίνοντα = sprouted; καί = or). They do not mislead.
- 4:20 παραδέχονται = "receive it": "it" has no Greek word. Matthew 13:23 puts
  that kind of "it" with the shared object λόγον instead.
- 4:10 τὰς = "about the" and 4:37 καί = "Soon" (publisher rows): defensible
  as the case/connective slot, but loose.
