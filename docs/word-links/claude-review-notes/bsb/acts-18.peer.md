# BSB Acts 18: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 28 of 28 verses (18:1-28), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 28, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 1 verse (18:21), one linked edit (καί `s` → `g` "And", with the
English "And" no longer `t`). The other 27 verses are kept exactly as the
author left them.

## Changes

- 18:21 καί (between ἀποταξάμενος and εἰπών): before καί `s` ("BSB
  subordinates the first participle (as he left), so καί has no English
  word") and "And" `t` ("English starts a new sentence for the main verb
  ἀνήχθη") → after καί = "And" (note "BSB subordinates the first participle
  (as he left) and places the καί of the participle chain before the main
  verb set sail"). The Greek is a participle chain ἀποταξάμενος καὶ εἰπών …
  ἀνήχθη, and BSB's only coordinator is the "And" that closes it before the
  main verb. This is the Matthew 26:26 / Acts 14:19 pattern (the καί of the
  chain linked to the "and" BSB places before the main verb). The `t` rule
  for a participle "and" applies only when no καί stands in the chain, so
  `s` + `t` here was a split pair.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- **18:19 "When" on κἀκείνους**: confirmed. The verse has two finite
  clauses (κατήντησαν … κἀκείνους κατέλιπεν); the opening δέ links to 18:18,
  and the καί inside the crasis κἀκείνους is what joins the two clauses BSB
  turns into "When …, …". Acts 11:22 puts "When" on that καί and leaves δέ
  `s`. ἐκείνους = "Priscilla and Aquila" (pronoun given a name by BSB) is
  linked in the same group, and the note explains the crasis. No reading
  is claimed (EVIDENCE 0/0).
- **18:3 "because"**: kept on διὰ τό. BSB's "because" gives the reason Paul
  "stayed and worked with them", which is the job of διὰ τὸ … εἶναι
  (because of being of the same trade he stayed); ὁμότεχνον εἶναι ("just
  as" / "he was") sits inside that same because-clause. The γάρ clause only
  explains the trade; BSB merges its content (ἦσαν = "they were",
  σκηνοποιοί = "tentmakers", τέχνῃ = "by trade", τῇ `s`) into the same
  clause. γάρ `s` is not a split pair: no English word that renders it is
  left `t`. διὰ τό joined follows Acts 8:11 and 12:20.
- **18:10 "you" on σοι**: confirmed. BSB reads "no one will lay a hand on
  you"; there is no "to harm you" or any other rendering of κακῶσαι in the
  English. "lay a hand on you" is ἐπιθήσεταί σοι (σοι is the dative object of
  ἐπιτίθεμαι), so "you" belongs on σοι and τοῦ κακῶσαί σε is rightly `s`.
- **18:13 "in ways contrary to" on παρά**: kept. "in ways" does not render
  any other Greek word; it is BSB's way of making παρὰ τὸν νόμον adverbial,
  so the whole phrase renders παρά (publisher row).
- **18:28 "in public debate" on δημοσίᾳ**: kept (publisher row). "debate"
  could reflect the sense of διακατηλέγχετο ("refute in debate"), but the
  phrase reads naturally as BSB's rendering of δημοσίᾳ and over-claims no
  other word. "he" moved onto διακατηλέγχετο (verb ending) is right.
- **18:18**: confirmed. ἡμέρας ἱκανάς = "for quite some time" (the
  publisher's ἔτι = "some" was wrong; ἔτι `s`); κειράμενος = "He had …
  shaved", τήν = "his". "to keep" renders nothing in the Greek: εἶχεν γὰρ
  εὐχήν is "for he had a vow", and BSB recasts the reason as purpose. γάρ
  means "for/because", "to keep" adds a purpose and a verb, so they differ
  in meaning and γάρ `s` + "to keep" `t` is not a split pair. εἶχεν = "he
  had made", εὐχήν = "a vow". "and then" `t` is right: the καί before σὺν
  αὐτῷ does not join the shaving to the sailing.
- **18:27**: confirmed. ὅς = "he" (relative pronoun given a new role in a
  new sentence), συνεβάλετο = "was a … help", πολύ = "great";
  τοῖς πεπιστευκόσιν joined.
- **18:16 "away" on ἀπήλασεν**: confirmed (ἀπελαύνω = "drive away").
- **Token-only joins**: all confirmed. διὰ τό = "because" (18:2, 18:3);
  κατὰ πᾶν = "Every" (distributive); ἐστί μοι = "I have"; ἀνοίγειν τὸ
  στόμα = "to speak" (idiom); τοῦ καθ’ ὑμᾶς = "your own"; οὐ βούλομαι =
  "refuse" and οὐκ ἐπένευσεν = "he declined" (one English verb carries the
  negation, so it cannot be split). μῆνας ἕξ = "a half" is honest:
  ἐνιαυτόν = "for a year", καί = "and", and "a half" (of a year) is exactly
  "six months"; leaving μῆνας `s` would hide part of what "a half" renders.
- **18:4 τε = "alike"**: kept. BSB's "Jews and Greeks alike" renders the
  "both … and" of τε … καί, as "both" is linked to τε in Acts 5:14 and 8:12.
  In 18:5, where BSB has only "Silas and Timothy", τε is rightly `s`.
- **Readings**: all match the EVIDENCE lines and partial records. 18:3 (1
  word differs) "worked" linked with a generic reading note; 18:7 (2 words
  differ) "went" and "Titus" linked with notes; 18:22 "Caesarea" and 18:25
  "John" linked with spelling notes (1 word each). 18:19 and 18:26 show 0/0
  Greek differences, and no reading is claimed there. No note states the
  publisher's Greek spelling or wording.
- **Other rules checked**: no lone article carries "of the"/"to the"/"in the"
  (18:5, 18:8, 18:12, 18:18, 18:19, 18:25, 18:26, 18:27 all keep only "the"
  or `s` on the article); supplied copulas (18:6 "am", "be"; 18:14 "be"),
  supplied objects ("him", "it", "the message", "complaint") and places
  ("There", "in Corinth", "at Jerusalem", "in Antioch", "there") are `t`;
  supplied "Paul" for a verb or participle ending is `t`, while pronouns BSB
  turns into names (18:11 αὐτοῖς = "the Corinthians", 18:19 ἐκείνους, 18:27
  αὐτοῦ = "Apollos") are linked; "and" joining a participle to the main verb
  with no καί there is `t` (18:1, 18:6, 18:7, 18:17, 18:19, 18:27).

## Still uncertain

- 18:7 τινός = "of" (publisher row) is kept. "of" could instead sit on
  Τιτίου with τινός `s` (BSB leaves "a certain man" unrendered); both treat
  the genitive honestly, so it was not changed.
- 18:7 τῇ συναγωγῇ = "the synagogue" with ἐκεῖθεν `s`: BSB's "left the
  synagogue" names what ἐκεῖθεν refers to. Linking the word to συναγωγή
  (publisher) is kept; ἐκεῖθεν = "the synagogue" would also be defensible.
- 18:21 πάλιν = "back", ἀνακάμψω = "I will come" (author's split of the
  publisher's "I will come back") is kept: πάλιν means "back" with verbs of
  returning, and the split avoids leaving πάλιν `s` beside a "back" that
  renders it.
