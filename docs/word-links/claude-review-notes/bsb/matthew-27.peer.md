## Changes (2 verses)

- 27:64: οἱ = "His" → οἱ `s` (article), `t His` (reading difference). The
  publisher row is partial: all 32 N1904 words are matched, but the
  publisher's Greek has 33 words. Its exported groups leave "His" out, and
  "His" is the only BSB word here with no N1904 counterpart. BSB's text reads
  οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ (no νυκτός, since BSB has no "by night"). "His" renders
  that αὐτοῦ, not the article, so the link to οἱ was invented (rule 9). Same
  case as 25:3 (τὰς λαμπάδας αὐτῶν). This also corrects the author's
  statement that no textual difference needed special handling.
- 27:7: the ἐξ = "used" note said "rendered used … to", but "to" belongs to
  ἠγόρασαν ("they + to buy"). The note now says "rendered used". The link is
  unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 27:9 (τοῦ τετιμημένου = "on Him", ὃν ἐτιμήσαντο = "set"): kept. "set … by
  the people of Israel" matches ὃν ἐτιμήσαντο ἀπὸ υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ ("whom they
  priced, from the sons of Israel"), so "set" goes with the finite verb that
  has the agent phrase. τοῦ τετιμημένου ("of the one priced") names the person:
  "on Him". The other split (Him = ὅν, set = τετιμημένου) would separate "set"
  from its "by" phrase. Both splits are defensible, so this is not an error.
  λέγοντος and the quotation's καί as `s` are correct.
- 27:24 (τούτου = "this man's"; one ὅτι for both "that"s): both correct. The
  publisher's Greek has the same 28 words as N1904. Its only dropped groups are
  Pilate and "before", which are spelling or synonym differences. So there is
  no τοῦ δικαίου, and "man's" comes from reading τούτου as masculine. One ὅτι
  governs both clauses (οὐδὲν ὠφελεῖ ἀλλὰ μᾶλλον θόρυβος γίνεται), and English
  repeats it. This is the same treatment as ἵνα = to + to in 27:20.
- 27:29 (ἐπέθηκαν = "set + They put"): correct. One verb governs both
  objects (the crown on the head, the reed in the right hand). BSB repeats the
  verb in a new sentence, and the καί before κάλαμον is rightly `s`.
- 27:64 (οἱ = His): changed (see above).
- 27:48 (καί = "and" before held): kept. The English "and" closes the series
  that the Greek τε … καί structure joins. Making καί `s` and "and" `t` would
  create a split pair.
- 27:60 (τῇ = "across the"), 27:7 (τοῖς = "for"), 27:66 (μετά = "and
  posting"): kept. The dative-article anchors follow the chapter's convention
  (τοῖς = "to the" in 27:3, τῷ = "to the" in 27:15). μετά is the only Greek
  basis for "and posting".
- 27:11 (ὁ ἡγεμών = "who", καί `s`): kept. "who" stands for the subject named
  again. Joining καί would also be acceptable, but it is not needed.
- 27:23 (γάρ = "Why"): kept. γάρ in a retort question ("Why, what evil …?")
  is what BSB turns into the separate "Why?".

## Still uncertain (no change made)

- 27:54: one N1904 word is unmatched, and the publisher drops only the
  γινόμενα group. Its Greek probably has the aorist γενόμενα, which fits "had
  happened". The link τὰ γινόμενα = "all that had happened" is right either
  way, so no claim was added. The same probably holds in 27:3 (παραδιδούς for
  παραδούς) and 27:49 (ἔλεγον for εἶπαν), where the meaning and links are
  the same.
- 27:17 and 27:41: the publisher's Greek has one word that N1904 lacks
  (probably καί after ὁμοίως in 27:41). No BSB English word renders it, so
  there is nothing to mark.
- 27:43: `t Him` (before now) versus linking αὐτόν to both Him, as in 27:2 and
  27:26. Kept as `t`: here the publisher's Greek equals N1904, and αὐτόν
  stands as the object of θέλει.
