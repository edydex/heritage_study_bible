# BSB Luke 19: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 48 of 48 verses (19:1-48, including Codex's earlier 19:17). I
checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against N1904, the displayed BSB, the
publisher record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json`
and the reviewed parallels Mark 11:1-10, Matthew 21:1-13 and Matthew
25:14-30. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 48, "problems": 0}`.

## Reading claims

The publisher record has a `partial` entry for 19:1, 19:8, 19:15, 19:40 and
19:48 only. No other verse claims a reading.

- 19:1, 19:8, 19:15, 19:48: each has 1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher
  word not in N1904, with the same word count. In each verse the only English
  the draft could not place from the publisher's links is exactly the English
  for the claimed word ("Jericho", "half", "had earned", "hung"). So the
  publisher linked that English to the differing Greek word, and the
  links Ἱερειχώ = Jericho, ἡμίσειά = half, διεπραγματεύσατο = had earned and
  ἐξεκρέμετο = hung on are right, with soft notes ("spelled differently in
  BSB's Greek text"; "a different form of this verb, with the same
  meaning"). No note states the publisher's spelling as fact.
- 19:40: 11 of 11 N1904 words matched and 12 publisher words, so BSB's text
  has one extra word. Every English word is accounted for, and none renders
  an extra word, so the author rightly claims no reading. "very" stays `t`.

## Changes (4 verses: 2 link fixes, 2 note-only fixes)

- 19:22: πονηρέ "wicked" + δοῦλε "You + servant" → πονηρέ = "You wicked",
  δοῦλε = "servant". Why: the reviewed Matthew 25:26 has the same Greek order
  (Πονηρὲ δοῦλε → "You wicked … servant") and links "You wicked" to πονηρέ.
  The vocative "You" goes with the vocative word it stands next to. Here
  that word is πονηρέ, which is itself vocative. This restores the
  publisher's row.
- 19:7: ἁμαρτωλῷ "a sinful" + ἀνδρί "man" → ἁμαρτωλῷ = "sinful", ἀνδρί =
  "a + man". Why: English "a" goes with the noun. The author already moved
  "a" this way in 19:12 (χώραν = a + country), 19:21 and 19:22 (ἄνθρωπος = a
  + man), and 19:37 has φωνῇ = "in a + voice". 19:7 was the one row left
  unchanged.
- 19:17 (Codex's verse): notes only; the links are right and unchanged. I
  rewrote two garbled notes as one sentence each: ἐλαχίστῳ = "a very small
  matter" and ἴσθι … ἔχων = "you shall have". I also brought the reasons for
  καί `s`, αὐτῷ `s` and `t His master` into line with 19:22.
- 19:2: note only. The `s` reason for tokens 6-7 described "καὶ αὐτὸς ἦν" as
  having no counterpart, although ἦν is linked to was#1. It now names only
  καὶ αὐτός.

## Points I was asked to settle, and the author's doubts

- 19:22, vocative "You": **changed** (see above). The Greek order matches
  Matthew 25:26, so the reviewed precedent applies.
- 19:44, ἐν σοί = "within your walls", σου `s`: **agree.** ἐν σοί ("in you",
  the city) is what BSB renders "within your walls", so "your" belongs to
  σοί. BSB says "the children" with no possessive, so τέκνα σου's σου has no
  word of its own. The publisher also linked σοί to "your walls". The
  alternative (σου = your, σοί = walls) would link a possessive to the
  wrong phrase. τά = "the#1" (moved from σου) is right.
- 19:2, ἦν = was#1 and καὶ αὐτός = "who": **agree.** The last clause, καὶ
  αὐτὸς πλούσιος, is verbless with ἦν understood. BSB renders that
  understood verb as "who was", and drops the verb of the ἀρχιτελώνης
  clause (now an apposition). A verb understood again may link to its
  English repeat. The first καὶ αὐτός `s` and "very" `t` are right.
  ἰδού = "there was" matches the reviewed Matthew 20:30 (ἰδού = "there were").
- 19:42, σύ = "you#0" (subject of "had known"), "you" in "bring you peace"
  `t`: **agree.** σύ is nominative, the emphatic subject of ἔγνως ("if you,
  even you, had known"). It cannot be the person peace comes to.
  τὰ πρὸς εἰρήνην has no pronoun, so the second "you" is English-only.
- "servant" `t` in 19:16, 19:18, 19:20: **agree.** ὁ πρῶτος, ὁ δεύτερος and
  ὁ ἕτερος are substantival ("the first/second/other one"). "servant" is
  supplied, as "talents" is `t` in the reviewed Matthew 25:17. The publisher's
  "first servant" on πρῶτος linked a noun the word does not mean. Moving
  "servant" to the article (as Matthew 25:22 does with ὁ τὰ δύο τάλαντα)
  would break the rule that a lone article never carries a noun.
- Readings 19:1, 19:8, 19:15, 19:48: **agree** (see Reading claims).
- 19:26, "him" `t`: **agree.** N1904 has no ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ here (Matthew 25:29
  has it, and there "him" is linked). ἀπό governs τοῦ μὴ ἔχοντος directly,
  and English adds the resumptive "him". ἀπό = "from" alone is right.
- Parallels: 19:29-35 agree with the reviewed Mark 11:1-7 (ἤγγισεν εἰς = He
  approached; χρείαν ἔχει = needs; αὐτοῦ = it; "him" and "there"/"here"
  `t`). Luke has no ὑμῶν, so `t you#0` in 19:30 is right. 19:38 agrees with
  Mark 11:9 and Matthew 21:9 (`t is`; ὁ ἐρχόμενος = who comes). 19:46
  agrees with Matthew 21:13. 19:24-26 agree with Matthew 25:28-29.
  19:45 `t there` is right because Luke has no ἐν τῷ ἱερῷ (Matthew 21:12
  links "there" to that phrase).

## Still uncertain (kept as the author left them)

- 19:8: τι = "it" (the amount taken, resumed as the object of "repay").
  It is defensible, because τι is understood again with ἀποδίδωμι.
  `s` τι + `t it` is the alternative.
- 19:19: γίνου = "shall have authority", with a note. "authority" is part of
  BSB's idiom for γίνου ἐπάνω ("be over"). `t authority` (supplied from
  19:17) is the alternative.
- 19:20: εἶχον = "I have", ἀποκειμένην = "laid away". Joining both as one
  periphrastic unit "I have laid away" would also be right.
- 19:30: ἐν ᾗ = "it#0" with `t and#0`. BSB turns the relative clause into
  "and as you enter it". The "and" could also be seen as part of the
  relative's rendering.
- 19:48: ἀκούων = "words" (the idiom "hung on His words"), with a note.
