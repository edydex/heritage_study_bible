# BSB Romans 15: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 33 of 33 verses (15:1-33, including the KEEP verse 15:27, which
was checked but not edited). Started from the author's final file, rendered
with `totext --all`, and checked every line against the Greek, the BSB
English, the brief rules and the publisher's per-verse record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/romans.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 33, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 3 verses (2 link changes, 1 note). All other author decisions are
kept.

## Changes

- 15:9: δέ "so that" → `s` ("The connective δέ ('and') is not separately
  expressed; BSB opens the clause with so that"); δοξάσαι "may glorify" →
  "so that + may glorify" (note: the infinitive continues the purpose of
  εἰς τό in 15:8). By the connective rule, δέ is linked only where English
  renders it (and, but, for, then, so). δέ here only adds a second item
  ("and"); the purpose that BSB's "so that" expresses comes from the
  construction: δοξάσαι is the second infinitive under εἰς τό in 15:8.
  A cross-verse link to that εἰς τό is not allowed just to move a
  connective, so the purpose phrase goes on the infinitive that carries it.
  Precedent: Ephesians 3:17 (κατοικῆσαι = "so that + may dwell", a second
  infinitive continuing an earlier purpose) and Colossians 4:6 (εἰδέναι =
  "so that + may know"). Luke 8:23 (καί = "so that") does not apply: there
  καί itself introduces the result.
- 15:17: τά "in#1 my service" → "in#1 + service"; "my" `t` ("English
  supplies the possessive; the Greek has none"). τὰ πρὸς τὸν Θεόν means
  "the things toward God". "in … service" renders the article used as a
  pronoun, but nothing in the Greek means "my". Supplied possessives are
  `t` everywhere else in this chapter (15:9 "His", 15:24 "my", 15:30 "my").
- 15:1 (note only): οἱ = "who" kept, note "rendered who are; are is a
  supplied copula" → "rendered who". The old note named "who are" as the
  rendering although "are" is `t`.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 15:9: changed, see above.
- 15:23 "there are" / "for me": kept as the author had it. ἔχων = "there
  are" is an honest recast and not an existential frame added by English.
  BSB turns "no longer having a place" into "there are no further
  opportunities for me". "there are … for me" is the same statement as
  "having", in English form. Acts 23:29 links ἔχοντα ("having") to "there
  was" in the same way. The reverse recast is linked in the same way too
  (ἐστί μοι "there is to me" = "I have"). The "there" rule keeps an
  existential "there is" on an existence verb when one renders it. It does
  not make a recast of "having" `t`. Making ἔχων `s` and "there are" `t`
  would be a split pair. "for me" stays `t`, because the participle has no
  person and only the participle could own it. The same principle covers
  the second ἔχων: ἐπιποθίαν … ἔχων ("having a longing") is joined and
  linked to its English recast "since + have longed", and the person word
  "I" stays `t`. Each ἔχων goes to the English predicate that recasts it,
  and never to a person word.
- 15:24: kept. "on my way" renders both ὡς ἂν πορεύωμαι ("whenever I go")
  and διαπορευόμενος ("passing through"). BSB has no separate "in passing",
  so one join is honest, and making either verb `s` would hide a rendered
  word. "my" has a finite owner in that group (πορεύωμαι, first person
  singular). "equip … for … journey" is not over-claimed. προπέμπω in the
  NT means "help someone on a journey with provisions", and BSB's "equip
  me for my journey" is that sense. "journey" belongs to that verb and not
  to ἐκεῖ, so ἐκεῖ `s` is right. "can", "me" and "my#1" are rightly `t`.
  ὑμῶν (agent) = "you", with ὑπό `s`, matches 15:15. ὑμῶν = "your company"
  with ἐμπλησθῶ = "I have enjoyed" follows the lexical idiom (ἐμπίμπλημι
  with a genitive of person means "enjoy someone's company"), so it is
  kept.
- 15:30 "my" (my struggle): kept as `t`. μοι is an explicit pronoun and
  could be linked to a repeated first person (Acts 22:3). But "my" is a
  possessive that English adds in the recast "join me in my struggle"
  (Greek: "strive together with me"). The supplied-possessive convention
  (`t`) is the safer reading.
- 15:27 (KEEP, unchanged): agreed that the verse breaks this chapter's
  conventions. "to do it" is folded into ηὐδόκησαν, "it" into ὀφειλέται
  εἰσίν ("they owe it"), and the supplied "blessings" twice into
  πνευματικοῖς and σαρκικοῖς. By the supplied-object rule each of these
  would be `t`. Its notes are also long and multi-sentence. Not edited (it
  is Codex's verse), and left for the merge check.

## Points checked and kept

- Readings, all checked against the verse records. 15:7 has 1 N1904 word
  unmatched: ἡμᾶς `s` and "you" `t`, both with the reading reason.
  15:19 has 1 unmatched: Ἁγίου `s` and "of God" `t`. 15:15 has 2
  unmatched: "bold" is linked to τολμηροτέρως with a hedged same-meaning
  note, and the second ἀπό is `s` for the active recast, so either
  preposition fits. 15:26 has 1 unmatched: ηὐδόκησαν = "were pleased",
  with the softened "same word, spelled differently" note. No review note
  names the publisher's Greek. 15:27 has no `partial` record only because
  its row was rebuilt from the Codex review, so it says nothing about
  15:26's spelling.
- 15:4: the record shows 0 N1904 words unmatched and 1 extra publisher
  word. The author's note guesses that the second διά is missing in BSB's
  Greek, but that would show as an unmatched N1904 word. The extra word is
  an addition elsewhere. The review file claims no reading here, and the
  `s` reason for the second διά ("one through covers both phrases") is
  right either way.
- Optative "may" is on the verb (15:5 δῴη, 15:13 πληρώσαι). The wishing
  "be" in 15:33 is `t`. 15:32 "I may" is on the finite συναναπαύσωμαι, and
  "and" is `t`. 15:12 "of" is on τοῦ before the indeclinable Ἰεσσαί.
- Person words on participles are `t`: 15:20 "I", 15:23 "I" and "for me",
  15:28 "I", 15:29 "I". "When/after/since" stay on the participles
  (15:28, 15:29, 15:23).
- Old Testament quotations (15:3, 15:9-12, 15:21) were checked word by
  word. Negatives are split, article + participle units are joined (15:3,
  15:12), "to + name" is on the noun (15:9), and the address articles are
  "you" (15:11).
- Lone articles: no "of the/to the/in the/among the" sits on a lone
  article. τά in 15:17 is an article used as a pronoun ("the things"), and
  the exception in the brief covers it.

## Still uncertain

- 15:9: if a later reviewer reads δοξάσαι as depending on λέγω (15:8)
  rather than on εἰς τό, the purpose "so that" would better be `t`. BSB's
  "so that … may" shows that it reads purpose, so the link follows BSB.
- 15:24: ὑμῶν = "your company" vs "your" + "company" on the verb. Both are
  defensible; the author's version is kept.
