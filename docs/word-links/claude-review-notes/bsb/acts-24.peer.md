# BSB Acts 24: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 26 of 26 verses (24:1-6, 24:8-27; 24:7 is absent from both N1904
and BSB's main text and stays out), every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 26, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 2 verses (24:18 links; 24:4 one note). The other 24 verses are kept
exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 24:18 οὐ μετὰ ὄχλου οὐδὲ μετὰ θορύβου ("and was not inciting a crowd or
  an uproar"): before both μετά joined = "inciting" → after both μετά `s`
  ("BSB recasts 'not with a crowd nor with an uproar' with the verb
  inciting, so μετά has no English word") and "inciting" `t` ("English
  supplies the verb inciting; the Greek has only μετά"). μετά means "with",
  "in company with"; "inciting" is a verb of a different meaning, so it is
  not a shifted rendering of μετά and `s` + `t` is not a split pair (the
  author brief's ὅταν/"and" case). No other Greek word carries "inciting"
  either. The "and was" `t` note is reworded to match ("English adds and
  was to make a clause; the Greek phrase has no verb or conjunction").
- 24:4 note on the second σε (`s`) only: before "BSB's your indulgence
  stands for this second σε" → after "BSB recasts 'I beg you to hear' as I
  beg your indulgence to hear, so this second σε, the subject of ἀκοῦσαι,
  has no English word". "your indulgence" is linked to σῇ ἐπιεικείᾳ, so the
  old note suggested a link to σε that the row does not hold. Links
  unchanged.

## Points checked and confirmed

- **24:18-19 verse division**: confirmed. The publisher record for 24:18
  has 14 of 14 N1904 words matched and 20 publisher Greek words (6 extra);
  24:19 has 12 of 18 N1904 words matched and 12 publisher Greek words (6
  unmatched). Six N1904 words of 24:19 are therefore counted in BSB's
  24:18, which fits τινὲς δὲ ἀπὸ τῆς Ἀσίας Ἰουδαῖοι = "But there are some
  Jews from the province of Asia" closing BSB's 24:18. The mechanics match
  the reviewed Acts 5:39-40 and 19:40-41: the `m … > 24:18` line sits in
  the N1904 verse (24:19) and the `g @24:19` lines in the BSB verse (24:18).
  τινές = some, δέ = But, ἀπό = from, τῆς = the, Ἀσίας = Asia, Ἰουδαῖοι =
  Jews; "there are" `t` (no verb) and "province of" `t` (as Acts 16:6,
  19:10, 20:16, 20:18).
- **Readings**: no different reading is claimed anywhere. Only 24:4 (0
  unmatched, 0 extra: form or order only), 24:18 and 24:19 (the verse
  division above) have a `partial` record; every other verse is identical
  to N1904. No note names the publisher's Greek.
- **24:20 μου = "I", "in me" `t`**: confirmed. στάντος μου is a genitive
  absolute ("when I stood"), so μου is the subject of στάντος. εὗρον
  ἀδίκημα has no "in me"; BSB supplies it. The publisher row put "in me" on
  μου and "I" inside "when I stood" on στάντος, which mismatches the roles.
- **24:17 ποιήσων = "to bring" + "to present"**: confirmed. ποιήσων governs
  both ἐλεημοσύνας and προσφοράς ("to make alms … and offerings"); BSB
  writes the verb out again for the second object. Same role (purpose verb
  of that object) and same meaning (ποιέω "make, perform" an offering =
  present it), so it is the understood-again pattern, not added English.
  προσφοράς = "offerings" only.
- **24:15**: αὐτοί = "themselves" (intensive) and οὗτοι = "they"
  (demonstrative) confirmed; the publisher had them swapped. καί = "the
  same" (the hope they also hold) and τε = "both" kept; "and" before "I
  have" `t` (ἔχων has no καί).
- **24:20**: εἰπάτωσαν = "let … state" (jussive on the finite verb), οὗτοι
  = "these men", αὐτοί = "for themselves" confirmed.
- **24:21**: φωνῆς = "thing" (the noun BSB renders), relative ἧς `s`
  confirmed; "it was", "It is", cleft "that" `t`.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **24:20 μου**: confirmed (above).
- **24:18 "inciting"**: changed (above).
- **24:17 "to present"**: confirmed (above).
- **24:5 πᾶσιν = "all", κατά = "over"**: kept. πᾶσιν ("all [the Jews]") is
  the only Greek source of BSB's "all"; BSB moves it into "all over the
  world", and κατά ("throughout") gives "over". Same split as Luke 23:5
  ("all over Judea"). Joining πᾶσιν with κατά would pair two words that are
  not a unit in the Greek.
- **24:1 "their case" `t` vs 24:22 "adjourned the hearing"**: both kept;
  they follow the same rule. In 24:1 ἐνεφάνισαν has no object word, so
  "their case" is a supplied object (`t`, as Acts 10:10). In 24:22 the
  Greek has an object, αὐτούς, which BSB recasts as "the hearing"
  (BDAG: ἀνεβάλετο αὐτούς, "adjourned their case"); a Greek pronoun moved
  into a new role is linked, and joining the two words as the legal idiom
  is acceptable.
- **24:23 τε = "but"**: kept. τε joins ἔχειν to τηρεῖσθαι at exactly the
  point where BSB writes "but"; BSB renders the connective with contrast,
  as δέ or καί are linked to "but" elsewhere. Even read as τε … καί, BSB's
  only word at the τε is "but", so `s` + `t` would be a split pair.
- **24:6 ὅς = "and he"**: kept. A relative opening a new clause is a
  connecting relative, normally rendered "and he"; the "and" carries the
  link ὅς makes to 24:5, so it is not added English.

## Other verses checked (no change)

- 24:2 "to this" on τούτῳ (publisher row) is kept: τούτῳ is a
  demonstrative, not a lone article. 24:10 puts "over" on ἔθνει in the
  same phrase because there the publisher's word sat on the lone article.
- 24:8 "against him" on αὐτοῦ and 24:13 "their charges against" on
  κατηγοροῦσιν are both publisher rows and both defensible (genitive object
  of κατηγορέω); left as they are.
- 24:10 Παῦλος = "he" and αὐτῷ = "for Paul" (BSB swaps name and pronoun);
  24:23 αὐτόν = "Paul" + "him" (subject of both infinitives); 24:26 αὐτόν
  = "Paul": all Greek words moved into a new role, linked.
- 24:12 "my accusers", 24:24 "speak", 24:23 "needs", 24:8 "the truth",
  24:3 "this", 24:11 "for yourself", 24:17 "to Jerusalem" `t` confirmed.
- Participle "and" `t`: 24:15, 24:22, 24:25, 24:26 confirmed (no καί).

## Still uncertain

- 24:22 ἀνεβάλετο αὐτούς could also be split (ἀνεβάλετο = "adjourned",
  αὐτούς = "the hearing"); the joined idiom is not wrong, so it is kept.
- 24:1 "their case" could be read as part of the legal sense of
  ἐνεφάνισαν ("brought formal charges"), as κατηγορεῖν = "bring charges" in
  24:19; `t` is kept under the supplied-object rule.
