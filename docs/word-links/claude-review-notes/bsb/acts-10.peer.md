# BSB Acts 10: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 48 of 48 verses. The chapter has no KEEP blocks. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 48, "problems": 0}`.

I made 3 changes in 3 verses (10:5, 10:10, 10:25). All three change links. I
confirmed everything else in the author's file, including all five reading
claims (10:1, 10:19, 10:24, 10:37, 10:39).

## Changes (verse: before → after, why)

- 10:5: τινα = "a man named" → τινα = "a man", with "named" `t`. The verse
  has no ὄνομα/ὀνόματι, so "named" is English that introduces the name.
  Reviewed rows treat it this way: "named" is `t` where the Greek gives no
  word for "name", as with Lazarus, Bartimaeus and Anna. The ὀνόματι of 10:1
  stays linked to its "named".
- 10:10: γεύσασθαι = "something to eat" → "to eat", with "something" `t`.
  παρασκευαζόντων = "while + the meal was being prepared" → "while + was
  being prepared", with "the meal" `t`. Both are objects that English
  supplies, and the Greek has no word for them. Reviewed rows make
  "something" `t` in the same wording ("given something to eat" and "give
  them something to eat", with φαγεῖν). "The meal" is BSB's supplied object,
  which BSB turns into the subject of the passive. The participle means
  "while they were preparing". It does not mean "the meal".
- 10:25: ἐγένετο τοῦ = "was about", εἰσελθεῖν = "to enter" → ἐγένετο = "was
  about", τοῦ εἰσελθεῖν = "to enter". τοῦ belongs to the articular infinitive
  (τοῦ εἰσελθεῖν works as the subject of ἐγένετο), not to ἐγένετο. Reviewed
  rows join the article to its infinitive (τοῦ πιστεύειν = "to believe",
  τοῦ μεῖναι = "to stay"). The author's own note said "was about to", but
  "to" was in the infinitive's group.

## Points I was asked to settle

- **10:1 "there was" `t`, δέ `s`**: confirmed. The Greek clause has no verb
  until εἶδεν in 10:3, so "there was" is an English existential frame and
  does not render δέ. This matches 9:10 and 9:36. In both, δέ is `s` ("BSB
  opens the new scene without rendering δέ"), and "there was" is linked only
  because those verses have ἦν. Acts 3:1 is a different case: there δέ =
  "One afternoon", where BSB uses a temporal phrase in the connective's
  place, not a frame for a missing verb. τις = "a" matches 9:10.
- **10:19 δύο `s`, "three" `t`, "to him" `t`**: confirmed against EVIDENCE.
  It shows 1 N1904 word unmatched and 2 publisher words not in N1904. The
  publisher's own row leaves exactly "to him" and "three" unlinked. "Two"
  and "three" differ in meaning, so `s` + `t` is right (rule 9). Neither
  note states the publisher's Greek.
- **10:26 καί `s`, "only" `t`**: confirmed. In καὶ ἐγὼ αὐτὸς ἄνθρωπός εἰμι
  the καί means "also/too", and "myself" renders αὐτός. "Only" means
  something else ("a mere man"), so it cannot carry καί.
- **10:39 "in#1" `t`**: confirmed against EVIDENCE. It shows 0 N1904 words
  unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904. The publisher's row leaves
  "in#1" as its only unlinked English word. So BSB's text has an extra word
  here, and the understood-again link (ἔν = in + in, as κατά in 6:13) would
  claim the wrong Greek word. The note gives no publisher spelling.
- **10:28 κἀμοί = "But + me"**: confirmed. Crasis of καὶ ἐμοί: BSB renders
  its καί as an adversative "But", and ἐμοί as "me". The publisher's ὁ = "But"
  was wrong.
- **10:47 ἔλαβον = "have received + have"**: confirmed. In ὡς καὶ ἡμεῖς the
  verb is understood again, so BSB's "have" repeats ἔλαβον (as in John 15:4).
  καί is `s`. Most reviewed rows leave the καί of ὡς/καθὼς καί `s` when BSB
  says "just as" (Heb 3:2, 2 Pet 2:1, Col 1:6, Eph 4:4).
- **10:3 τῆς ἡμέρας = "One day"**: confirmed as a phrase unit, like the
  reviewed ἐν μιᾷ τῶν ἡμερῶν = "One day". There is no connective in 10:3.
- **10:22 τε / καί**: confirmed. This τε is a plain connective and BSB has
  no "and" for it, so it is `s`. The second καί is `s` because BSB turns the
  infinitive into "so he could hear".
- **10:11–16 vs Acts 11:5–10**: they match the committed reviewed Acts 11:
  καταβαῖνον `s`, σκεῦός τι = "something", τέσσαρσιν = "four", ἀρχαῖς =
  "by + corners" with "its" `t`, Ἃ = "anything + that", μή = "not", κοίνου =
  "Do + call + impure", σύ `s`, and ἐπὶ τρίς = "three times". 10:16
  ἀνελήμφθη = "was taken back up" is fine without πάλιν: ἀνα- covers "back"
  ("take up/back").
- **One object linked to two English pronouns**: confirmed in all three
  verses. In 10:28 the dative ἀλλοφύλῳ is the shared object of κολλᾶσθαι ἢ
  προσέρχεσθαι. In 10:25 συναντάω and προσκυνέω both take the dative, and
  προσεκύνησεν has no object of its own. In 10:39 ὅν is the accusative object
  of ἀνεῖλαν, and κρεμάσαντες shares it. This follows 6:12 (αὐτόν = "him +
  him"). Acts 5:30 is different: there BSB renders ὅν as "whom", so its
  "Him" after "hanging" is not a repeat and stays `t`.
- **τε … καί in 10:39**: τε = "both" is right. BSB writes "both … and", and
  reviewed rows link τε = "both" when BSB has it (Acts 2:11, 5:14). They make
  it `s` only when BSB renders just "and".
- **Spelling notes (10:1, 10:24 Caesarea; 10:37 John)**: confirmed. Each
  EVIDENCE line shows 1 word unmatched and 1 extra, and each note only says
  "Same name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text."
- **Author doubts**: 10:4 second σου `s` is accurate as written (BSB's one
  "Your" serves both nouns). 10:30 Ἀπό = "ago" and μέχρι = "at" are kept,
  because together they render the idiom "from the fourth day until this
  hour", which BSB gives as "four days ago … at this hour".

## Still uncertain

- 10:24 δέ = "where" and 10:20 ἀλλά = "So" are connective shifts. They have
  reviewed precedent (6:13 τε = "where"; Eph 5:24 ἀλλά = "Now"), so I kept
  them.
- 10:47 τοῦ μή stays `s` as one unit, as Luke 4:42 and 24:16 join τοῦ μή.
  Under the 10:25 logic, τοῦ could instead join βαπτισθῆναι ("to baptize").
- 10:48 "he ordered that" keeps "that" on the verb, as several reviewed rows
  do ("ordered that", "directed that"). In 10:28 and 10:43 "that" is `t`.
  Both patterns have reviewed precedent.
