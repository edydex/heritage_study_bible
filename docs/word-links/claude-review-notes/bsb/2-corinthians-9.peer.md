Checked: 15 of 15 verses (9:1-15), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against

Changes: 1 verse (9:14), two lines.

## Changes

- 9:14 ἐπιποθούντων: "express + affection" → "affection"; "will" `t` →
  "will express" `t`. ἐπιποθέω means "long for". BSB turns the participle
  into the noun "affection" and adds "express" as a new main verb, so that
  "their prayers" becomes the subject. "express" does not render ἐπιποθέω.
  It is the same kind of supplied verb as "provides" in 9:13 and "has given"
  in 9:14, which the author already marked `t`. This is not a split pair:
  ἐπιποθούντων stays linked to "affection".

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 9:14 αὐτῶν = "their#0 + their#1": kept. αὐτῶν does both jobs in the
  Greek: it stands with δεήσει ("their prayer") and it is the subject the
  participle ἐπιποθούντων agrees with. BSB writes each job as its own
  "their". The two words have the same meaning (the same people) and the
  same role (the person praying or longing, written as a possessive). This
  is an explicit pronoun, not a person ending, so the person rule does not
  apply. The precedent is Hebrews 13:7, where one genitive relative carries
  both "their" words.
- 9:14 ἐφ’ `s` + "has given" `t`: kept. A verb does not mean "upon", so
  linking ἐφ’ to "has given" would give it a different meaning.
- 9:5 ὑμῶν = "you#1" and ταύτην = "your gift#1": kept. The promiser "you"
  can only come from ὑμῶν, since the participle has no person. ταύτην points
  back to "your promised gift", and the Greek names both the gift (εὐλογίαν)
  and its owner (ὑμῶν). BSB writes that referent out in full. Philippians
  1:19 (τοῦτο = "my distress") is the precedent.
- 9:5 also confirmed: προέλθωσιν = "visit + beforehand" with εἰς `s`. Joining
  εἰς to the verb, as 2 Corinthians 1:15 does for "to visit", would also be
  acceptable; the difference is only taste, so I left the author's choice.
  εὐλογίαν = "bountiful gift" is an attested sense ("generous gift") and has
  a note. The "for" in "make arrangements for" belongs to the English verb
  (the Greek verb takes a direct object), so it rightly comes off the lone
  article τήν.
- 9:11 ἥτις = "your giving", "so that" `t`: kept. The feminine relative
  refers to ἁπλότητα. It is the subject of κατεργάζεται, as "your giving" is
  the subject of "will produce". "so that" means something different from
  ἥτις ("which"), so it is `t`. Philippians 1:19 is again the precedent for
  a pronoun carrying a possessive with its referent. The person rule holds:
  "You will#0" is `t`, and the participle πλουτιζόμενοι carries only "be#0
  enriched". πᾶσαν = "on every#1 occasion" stays one unit. BSB makes the
  adjective "all" into an adverb phrase because "generous" is an adjective;
  this matches "every#0 way" for παντί in the same verse.
- 9:8 αὐτάρκειαν = "need" with "that#1 you#1" `t`: kept. The added "that"
  and the "you" with no Greek owner must be `t` under the brief rules. The
  note explains the change of polarity: "all sufficiency" becomes "all that
  you need".
- 9:12 ἐστίν = "is#0 + is#1": kept. The one periphrastic ἐστίν governs both
  participles in the οὐ μόνον … ἀλλὰ καί pair. BSB's second "is" repeats that
  verb in the same sentence. It is not a supplied copula and not an εἰμί
  from another clause.
- 9:7 καθώς = "what": kept, with its note. BSB changes "give as he has
  decided" into "give what he has decided to give". "what" is the word that
  opens the recast clause, and the measure of the giving is kept. "should
  give" and "to give" are rightly `t`.
- 9:9 ἔδωκεν = "His#0 gifts": kept, after checking the Psalm 112:9 quote word
  by word. Psalm 111:9 in the Greek Old Testament reads ἐσκόρπισεν, ἔδωκεν τοῖς
  πένησιν, ἡ δικαιοσύνη αὐτοῦ μένει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα (τοῦ αἰῶνος). BSB's Psalm and
  this verse match it word for word: "He has scattered abroad" =
  ἐσκόρπισεν, "His gifts" = ἔδωκεν ("he gave"), "to the poor" = τοῖς
  πένησιν. The verb becomes a noun phrase. "His" comes from the third-person
  ending of the finite verb ἔδωκεν, and person words belong on the finite
  verb, so "His" is not a supplied possessor. "gifts" is what the giving
  produced. Marking "His" `t` would leave ἔδωκεν's person without a word.
