## Textual claims (counts are N1904 words / matched / publisher words)

- 8:9–10: εὐθύς of 8:10 = "As soon as" in 8:9 (`g @8:10 1` with
  `m 1 > 8:9`). **Confirmed.** The Greek counts are 7/7/7 and 15/15/15, so
  the Greek is identical and divided the same way. The English counts are
  15/12/14 and 15/15/16. The only 8:9 words without a publisher match are
  "As soon as": every other word is in a transferred group. In 8:10 the
  publisher has one English word more than BSB, and εὐθύς is the only
  content word there with no transferred group. The publisher's English
  for the two verses has 30 words, the same as BSB's. So the difference is
  at the verse break, not in the Greek. No 8:9 Greek word can give "As soon as". The link matches
  Mark 1:10, 1:29 and 6:54, which link εὐθύς alone to "As soon as" and
  leave καί `s`.
- 8:3 (18/17/18): one word is replaced. No group came over for εἰσίν,
  although "have come" is matched in English. That fits ἥκασιν in that
  place. `s` εἰσίν + `t have come` is right (rule 9), not a split pair.
- 8:7 (11/10/11): εἶχον for εἶχαν, linked normally. The opening καί is
  matched in the Greek, and all 21 English words are matched, yet no group
  came over for it. So the publisher's καί row had no English, and "also"
  most likely sat with εἶχον, which means only "they had". Linking καί =
  "also" is better than that row, and better than `s` καί + `t also`.
- 8:20 (14/14/16): the publisher's Greek has two extra words, ‹καὶ› after
  ὅτε and ‹αὐτῷ› after λέγουσιν (BibleHub interlinear). "And" is matched in
  English but has no group, and N1904's καί before λέγουσιν has no
  publisher group either. So BSB's "And" renders the extra καί. `t And`
  with `s` for N1904's καί is right.
- 8:11 (17/16/17), 8:27 (30/29/30) and 8:28 (18/16/18) differ only in
  spelling (συνζητεῖν, Καισαρίας, Ἰωάνην, Ἡλείαν). The number of unmatched
  words equals the number of respelled words, and the respelled words are
  linked normally.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:2 and 8:32, τόν = "this": **agree.** BSB is rendering the
  article's pointing force. `s` τόν + `t this` would be a split pair.
  Mark 6:2 links ἡ = "this" in the same way. Matthew 15:32 uses `t this`;
  both are accurate, and neither misleads.
- 8:22, `t some people`: **agree.** This chapter treats every supplied
  noun subject this way ("The people" and "the disciples" in 8:8, and
  "the disciples" in 8:14). Mark 6:14 keeps "people" with the verb. Both
  are accurate.
- 8:13, ἐμβάς = got + into, πάλιν = "back", `t the boat`: **agree.** The
  absolute ἐμβαίνω means "embark", so ἐμβάς = "got into the boat" would
  also be accurate. But neither N1904 nor the publisher's Greek has a noun
  here (8:13 has no `partial` record; some manuscripts add εἰς τὸ πλοῖον).
  The chapter also treats other implied objects as `t`. 8:25 `t them` is
  right for the same reason, because διέβλεψεν has no object.
- 8:9 τετρακισχίλιοι = "four thousand men" and 8:4 τούτους = "these
  people": **agree.** A Greek word used as a noun keeps the head noun that
  English adds, as τυφλόν = "a blind man" does in 8:22. "men" follows the
  masculine numeral; unlike Matthew 15:38, there is no ἄνδρες here.
- 8:17 πεπωρωμένην = "such hard": **agree.** "such" only intensifies the
  participle's rendering.
- 8:7 καί = "also": **agree.** See the counts above.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 8:9–10: the counts do not show the publisher's exact wording. They fit a
  one-word rendering of εὐθύς in 8:10 and two other words in 8:9.
  "As soon as" could also be linked to καί (8:9) together with εὐθύς. The
  established εὐθύς-only link is kept.
- 8:24: ὅτι = "but" and ὁρῶ = "they look" (publisher rows) follow BSB's
  recast "I see them like trees". Leaving ὁρῶ `s` would only create a
  split pair.
- 8:14: οὐκ is joined to εἰ μή as "except for", because English "had"
  stays positive. `s` οὐκ would be the stricter alternative.
