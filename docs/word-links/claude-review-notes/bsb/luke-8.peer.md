Checked: 56 of 56 verses (8:1-56), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
N1904, the BSB English and the publisher's per-verse record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json`. Parallels read:
Mark 4:1-25, 4:35-41, 5:1-43; Matthew 8:23-34, 9:18-26, 13:1-23; Luke 4:43,
5:13, 7:34 for conventions.

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 56, "problems": 0}`. Only 8:7 and
8:29 differ from the author's file.

## Changes

- 8:7 καί (before συνφυεῖσαι): `g and` → `s` ("The καί is not expressed;
  English recasts the thorns clause as a relative clause."), and "and"
  (grew up with it **and** choked) → `t`, joining the participle to
  ἀπέπνιξαν. Why: in BSB the καί's work is taken over by "which". The
  remaining "and" only joins the participle to the main verb. This matches
  the same sentence in Mark 4:7 and Matthew 13:7 (καί `s`, which = αἱ
  ἄκανθαι) and Luke 5:13 (καί `s`, participle "and" `t`).
- 8:29 καί (before διαρήσσων): `g and#2` → `g though`. "and#2" (broken the
  chains **and** been driven) → `t`, joining the participle to ἠλαύνετο.
  The old `t though` is removed. Why: this καί joins "he was bound…" and
  "breaking the chains he was driven". BSB turns those two clauses into
  "though he was bound …, he had broken …". By the "When …, …" convention,
  the subordinating word goes on the καί between the clauses. The "and"
  before "been driven" stands where the Greek has a participle and no
  conjunction.
- 8:29 note on διαρήσσων: "Same verb, spelled differently in BSB's Greek
  text" → "N1904 spells διαρήσσων with one ρ; it is the same verb, διαρρήσσω
  ('break apart')." Why: the record shows only that 2 of 32 N1904 words are
  unmatched, not which two. The new note states only a fact about N1904.
- 8:29 note on ἀπό = by: "BSB's Greek has a different word here" → "ἀπό
  with the passive ἠλαύνετο marks the demon as the agent, by; BSB's Greek
  text may have a different preposition here." Why: same reason. The note
  must not state the publisher's wording as fact. The link itself is
  right, because ἀπό can mark the agent after a passive (as in Luke 6:18).

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:7 and 8:29 (καί linked to the "and" after a participle): neither καί
  is the coordinator that this "and" renders, so I changed both (above).
  This is not a true split pair. In 8:7 the καί is absorbed into the
  relative clause, as in the Mark/Matthew parallels. In 8:29 the καί now
  carries "though", so nothing is split.
- 8:22: kept as the author has it. αὐτούς = His disciples and οἱ μαθηταὶ
  αὐτοῦ = them each link the Greek phrase in the same role (said to …;
  with …), with καί = with. That is the publisher's reading and it is not
  wrong. For the two καί: καί (between boarding and speaking) = So, because
  "So" joins the same two clauses in BSB's new order. καί before ἀνήχθησαν =
  and, the "and" before "set out". The publisher's opposite assignment was
  also defensible. The author's version is clearer to a reader, since "and
  set out" now lights up καὶ ἀνήχθησαν, so I did not revert it.
- 8:42: kept ἦν = was, αὐτῷ = his, `t who`. ἦν with a genitive of age is
  the normal "was … years old" (cf. Mark 5:42), so the English "was" has a
  real Greek "was". The dative of possession gives "his".
- 8:19: kept πρός = "to see" with its note. `s` + `t to see` would split
  BSB's paraphrase of "came to Him". `g to` + `t see` would attach the
  infinitive "to" to a preposition.
- 8:41: kept ἀνήρ = "a" with its note. BSB folds "a man … this one was
  ruler of the synagogue" into "a synagogue leader". The indefinite "a" is
  what is left of ἀνήρ. Merging ἀνήρ into "leader" (as Luke 7:34 does with
  ἄνθρωπος φάγος) would also work. That is a choice of style, not a fix.
  8:12 εἶτα = "but" kept: BSB's "but" stands at εἶτα and renders it.
- 8:27: kept αὐτῷ = Jesus + He. The one dative is both the person stepping
  ashore and the person met. Mark 5:2 and Matthew 8:28 have two pronouns
  for these two English words.
