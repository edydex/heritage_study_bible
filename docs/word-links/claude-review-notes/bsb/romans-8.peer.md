# BSB Romans 8: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 39 of 39 verses (8:1-39), every `g`/`s`/`t` line of the author's
final file, against N1904, the BSB English, the publisher rows in
`drafts/romans-8.txt`, the EVIDENCE lines and the `partial` records in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/romans.json`. No KEEP blocks.

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 39, "problems": 0}`. Three verses
differ from the author's JSON: two link changes (8:3, 8:24) and one note
correction (8:28).

## Changes

- 8:3 περί: "as an offering for" → "for"; "as an offering" `t` ("English
  adds as an offering; περὶ ἁμαρτίας ("for sin") is the Greek Old Testament
  phrase for a sin offering."). The preposition on its own means "for,
  concerning". The offering idea comes from the phrase περὶ ἁμαρτίας as a
  whole, and BSB's added noun phrase spells it out. Hebrews 10:6 and 10:8
  (reviewed) set the precedent: the "offerings" in "sin offerings" is `t`
  and περί is not given the offering noun. ἁμαρτίας = "sin" stays.
- 8:24 τί: "Who" → `s` ("τί ("why") has no English word; BSB asks the
  question with Who."), and "Who" `t` ("English asks Who hopes, where the
  Greek asks why (τί) he hopes."). In N1904, ὃ γὰρ βλέπει τις, τί ἐλπίζει
  has the unaccented, enclitic τις as the indefinite subject of βλέπει. BSB
  renders it "he" ("what he can already see"), so the author's τις = "he"
  stays. τί is the adverbial "why" of ἐλπίζει. "Who" is the subject of
  "hopes", so it differs from τί in both meaning and role. That rules out
  the kind of question-word shift that John 1:21 and Acts 13:25 allow
  (τί "what?" → "who", same identity question). A word that BSB renders
  with a word of different meaning stays `s` with the English `t`. The
  EVIDENCE line shows the publisher's Greek equals N1904 here, ignoring
  accents. So no different reading may be claimed, and the notes describe
  only the English recast.
- 8:28 note on "God" `t`: "BSB follows a reading with two extra words here,
  naming God as the subject." → "BSB follows a reading with two extra words
  here; its English names God as the subject." The `partial` record shows
  only that two publisher words are not in N1904. The old note stated as
  fact what those words are. The new note states only the count and what
  BSB's English does.

## Points checked and confirmed (no change)

- 8:21 διότι `s` and "that" `t`, both with "BSB follows a reading with a
  different word here.": the `partial` record has 1 N1904 word unmatched
  and 1 publisher word not in N1904. The publisher row for "that" did not
  transfer, and διότι is the unmatched token. N1904's διότι means
  "because", but BSB's "in hope that" introduces the content of the hope,
  so linking would show a reader the wrong meaning. Neither note names the
  publisher's word.
- 8:28 Θεόν = "Him" (τόν `s`) with the note that BSB names God as the
  subject. This is right: the two extra publisher words (0 N1904 words
  unmatched) carry BSB's "God", so "God" has no N1904 owner, and the
  object τὸν Θεόν is what BSB renders "Him".
- 8:8-9 ἐν = "controlled by" (publisher row, kept with a note). ἐν with
  σαρκί / πνεύματι has the sense "in the power of, under the control of",
  so "controlled by" renders ἐν itself. Putting "controlled" on ὄντες /
  ἐστέ would claim that a form of εἰμί ("being") means "controlled". The
  8:9 second ἐν = "by" with "controlled" understood is consistent with
  this.
- 8:8 οὐ δύνανται joined = "cannot": "cannot" is one English word, and
  this is the reviewed convention (1 Timothy 5:25, Acts 4:20, Acts 27:31,
  Mark 3:24, Luke 14:27 and others).
- 8:35 "Shall" linked to χωρίσει, understood again in the second question:
  χωρίσει is future, so "Shall" adds no tense that the Greek lacks. The
  publisher's "Shall trouble" on θλῖψις was wrong.
- 8:23 ἐν ἑαυτοῖς = "inwardly"; the second καὶ αὐτοί `s` (BSB renders
  "we ourselves" once, with ἡμεῖς = "we" and the first αὐτοί =
  "ourselves"); the first καί `s`; "that" (Not only that) `t`;
  ἀπεκδεχόμενοι = "as + wait eagerly for", with "we" `t` because a
  participle has no person; "our" before "adoption as sons" `t`.
- 8:26 "we" is on προσευξώμεθα (1st plural subjunctive), not on the
  impersonal δεῖ, which keeps "ought". τί … καθό joined = "how" is the most
  honest form of BSB's collapse of "what … as is fitting" into "how". The
  alternative, τί = "how" with καθό unlinked, would hide καθό's manner sense
  behind τί ("what").
- 8:15 Ἀββᾶ = "Abba", ὁ `s`, Πατήρ = "Father", word by word as in
  Galatians 4:6. EVIDENCE shows a form/order difference only (0 words), so
  no reading is claimed. πάλιν = "returns", with "that" and "you" `t`.
- 8:35 and 8:38-39: every list noun and every ἤ / οὔτε has its own "or" /
  "neither" / "nor". The N1904 order of 8:38 matches BSB's. "any" (8:38)
  and "in all" (8:39) are `t`.
- 8:24 βλεπομένη = "that is seen": an attributive passive participle
  rendered as a relative clause, voice preserved. This is the same pattern
  as μέλλουσαν = "that will" (8:18) and τοῦ ἐνοικοῦντος = "who lives"
  (8:11). βλέπει = "can + see" (modal on the finite verb); "already" and
  "at all" are `t`; τῇ = "this", with "in" on ἐλπίδι.
- Spelling notes 8:16 συνμαρτυρεῖ, 8:17 συνπάσχομεν and 8:22 συνστενάζει
  each read "Same word, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text." Each
  verse's `partial` record has exactly 1 N1904 word unmatched, and the
  draft TODO shows that token was the untransferred one. None of the notes
  names the publisher's spelling.
- 8:34: one extra publisher word (0 N1904 words unmatched) that BSB does
  not render. No reading is claimed, and "For" stays `t`.
- Lone articles: no "of the"/"to the"/"in the" sits on a lone article. The
  only articles with more than "the" are pronoun, relative or possessive
  uses (8:1 τοῖς "for those who", 8:39 τῆς "that", 8:10/8:18/8:21/8:27/8:32
  possessives, 8:24 "this").

## Author doubts answered

- 8:24 τις / τί: neither the author's τί = "Who" nor the publisher's τις =
  "Who"; τις = "he", τί `s`, "Who" `t` (see Changes).
- 8:21 διότι `s` / "that" `t`: kept. Even where διότι can mean "that", the
  reading evidence and the "because" reading in N1904 make a link
  misleading.
- 8:26 τί καθό = "how": kept (see above).
- 8:8-9 "controlled" on ἐν: kept (see above).
- 8:3 περί = "as an offering for": changed to "for" with "as an offering"
  `t` (see Changes).
- 8:5 second "live" and 8:35 "Shall" understood again: both kept. In 8:5
  the second clause elides ὄντες, and BSB repeats its own rendering "live".
  That is a lexical verb, not a supplied copula "is/are", so the
  understood-again rule applies.
- 8:15 πάλιν = "returns" with "that" and "you" `t`: kept. "returns"
  recasts "again (back) to" as a verb, with the same sense.
- 8:18 τοῦ νῦν καιροῦ = "present" and τά = "our": kept. The whole phrase
  "of the present time" becomes the one adjective, and the article is
  used as a possessive.
- 8:33 and 8:38 "any" `t`: kept. These are added determiners with no
  Greek word.

## Still uncertain

- 8:24: a later reviewer could prefer the publisher's τις = "Who". That
  reading takes τις as the interrogative τίς, since accents are editorial.
  It would also need "he" `t` or on βλέπει. I chose the N1904
  accentuation and syntax.
- 8:3: joining περὶ ἁμαρτίας = "as an offering for sin" as a fixed idiom
  is defensible too. The `t` form follows the Hebrews 10 precedent.
