# BSB Romans 16: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 26 of 26 verses (16:1-23, 16:25-27). 16:24 is absent from both
N1904 and BSB's main text and stays out. No KEEP blocks. Every `g`, `s` and
`t` line of the author's file was checked against the Greek. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 26, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 1 verse (16:19), one link and one note. The other 25 verses are
unchanged.

## Changes

- **16:19 εἰς πάντας = "Everyone" → εἰς `s`, πάντας = "Everyone".** In BSB's
  recast, πάντας becomes the subject. The preposition εἰς ("to") has no
  English word, so folding it into "Everyone" over-claimed. This also matches
  the author's own handling of the second εἰς in 16:26 (`s`, "BSB makes all
  nations the object of lead").
- **16:19 note on ἀφίκετο** reworded: it now says the clause is told from
  the other side ("your obedience has reached all" → "Everyone has heard
  about your obedience"). The link itself is kept (see the doubts below).

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **16:26 second εἰς `s`, "in order to lead" `t`: agree.** εἰς ὑπακοὴν
  πίστεως is the purpose phrase. Its εἰς already owns "to" before "the
  obedience", as in Romans 1:5, so it cannot also take "in order to". The
  second εἰς (εἰς πάντα τὰ ἔθνη, "to all the nations") goes with
  γνωρισθέντος. BSB makes "all nations" the object of a supplied "lead", so
  no English word renders it. Linking "in order to" to that εἰς would claim a
  purpose sense it does not have there. τε = "and" (it joins φανερωθέντος and
  γνωρισθέντος) and διά = "through": agree. The publisher had "through" on
  τε, which was wrong.
- **16:19 ἀφίκετο = "has heard + about": agree; εἰς changed.** "Has heard"
  is not a dictionary meaning of ἀφικνέομαι ("arrive, reach"). But BSB
  describes the same event (the report reaching everyone) from the
  receivers' side. Reviewed chapters link this kind of converse rendering:
  Acts 24:27 ἔλαβεν διάδοχον = "was succeeded by", and Acts 25:3 αἰτούμενοι
  = "to grant them". There the role-shifting preposition also stays with the
  verb, as "about" does here. No other English word renders ἀφίκετο, so `s`
  plus `t` would drop a real correspondence. εἰς is now `s` (above).
- **16:4 ψυχῆς μου = "me", τράχηλον = "lives": agree, not cross-wired.**
  "Lives" is possessed by "their" (ἑαυτῶν), and in Greek the head of
  ἑαυτῶν is τράχηλον. τὸν τράχηλον ὑποτιθέναι is the idiom "risk one's neck",
  i.e. "risk one's life", so "lives" renders τράχηλον. It does not take over
  ψυχή's meaning. ψυχῆς μου ("my life") is compressed to "me", the person
  whose life was at stake. Moving "lives" to ψυχῆς would wire "my life" to
  "their lives", so the pair stays as the author linked it. Joining ψυχῆς
  with μου (rather than ψυχῆς `s`) is acceptable: ψυχή often stands for the
  self.
- **16:5 "Greet" `t`: agree.** The Greek continues the 16:3 sentence with
  καί. The verse division is the same in both texts, so a cross-verse link
  is not warranted. Newer reviews make such a repeated verb `t` (Romans 2:7,
  4:12). "That meets" `t` (supplied relative verb) and "province of" `t`
  (Acts 16:6): agree. The genitive Ἀσίας owns "in + Asia". The "of" in
  "province of Asia" is part of the supplied appositive frame, not the Greek
  genitive.

## Points checked at the caller's request

- **16:7 γέγοναν = "were + was": agree.** πρὸ ἐμοῦ ("before me") becomes
  "before I was" (the elided "was in Christ"). "I" is on ἐμοῦ. "Was" repeats
  the same predicate (coming to be in Christ) for the compared person. That
  is the same role and meaning, like Acts 15:11, where σωθῆναι links to
  "are" in "just as they are". This is not the John 14:10 / 15:5 case: there
  a pure copula is supplied in a verbless Greek clause beside an εἰμί.
  γέγοναν is not εἰμί, and the Greek has no second clause here. The
  publisher's "I was" on ἐμοῦ (verb on a pronoun) was rightly removed.
- **16:13 μητέρα = "mother + a mother", "who has been" `t`: agree.**
  μητέρα αὐτοῦ καὶ ἐμοῦ is "his mother and mine". The noun is understood
  with ἐμοῦ, and BSB's "a mother to me" spells out that understood noun.
  Second καί = "as well", ἐμοῦ = "to me".
- **"send(s) you greetings" (16:16, 16:21, 16:23 twice): agree.** "Send(s)
  + greetings" goes on ἀσπάζεται/ἀσπάζονται and "you" on ὑμᾶς. "As do" /
  "as does" puts "as" on καί and "do/does" on the single Greek verb, which
  governs the added subjects. That is the same role and meaning
  (Colossians 4:10).
- **Greeting list:** each name is linked to its Greek name. In 16:10-11,
  τοὺς ἐκ τῶν + genitive name gives τούς = "those (who)", ἐκ = "belong
  to"/"from" and τῶν = "the household". τῶν is an article used as a pronoun
  for the household, and "of" sits on the declinable name: agree.
- **Doxology 16:25-27:** linked word by word, and the notes give only
  literal glosses. κατά = "by + by" (one preposition governing both nouns),
  χρόνοις αἰωνίοις = "for ages past", ᾧ and ἡ `s`, "be" `t`, and εἰς τοὺς
  αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων = "forever": agree.
- **Readings:** 16:1 EVIDENCE shows one extra publisher word. All 14 BSB
  English words are matched to N1904, so nothing is claimed: agree. 16:20
  EVIDENCE shows one extra publisher word with all 22 N1904 words matched.
  "Christ" has no N1904 owner, so "Christ" `t` with "BSB follows a reading
  with an extra word here" is correct. No review note names the publisher's
  Greek.
- **Brief rules swept:** no lone article carries "of the"/"to the" or a
  verb. Articles used as pronouns, possessives or demonstratives carry only
  that sense: τούς/τῶν in 16:10-11, τό "what" in 16:19, τήν "my" in 16:12,
  τήν "this" in 16:22 and ὁ "our" in 16:23. Supplied copulas, "that"
  frames and "be" are `t` in 16:5, 16:10, 16:13, 16:17, 16:19, 16:20, 16:26
  and 16:27. No person is attached to a participle (16:1, 16:12, 16:17,
  16:22, 16:25, 16:26). "May" in 16:2 is on the finite subjunctive χρῄζῃ.
  "A" goes with its noun.

## Still uncertain (left as the author linked them)

- 16:7 "was": `t` (an English-added comparative verb, as "are" is in
  Matthew 23:15) would also be defensible. I kept the link for the reasons
  above.
- 16:6 and 16:12b πολλά = "very hard" and the verb = "has worked", but in
  16:12a κοπιώσας = "have worked hard". The split of "hard" is not uniform,
  though both splits are defensible (κοπιάω = "work hard", πολλά = "much").
  These are publisher rows and not clearly wrong, so they were not changed.
- 16:2 προστάτις = "a great help": "great" conveys the patron sense of the
  noun. It is kept as the publisher's row.
