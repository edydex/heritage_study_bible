## Changes (5 verses: 4 links, 1 note)

- **14:62** τῆς δυνάμεως, τοῦ οὐρανοῦ: τῆς = "of" + δυνάμεως = "Power" →
  τῆς `s`, δυνάμεως = "of Power"; τοῦ (22) = "of" + οὐρανοῦ = "heaven" →
  τοῦ `s`, οὐρανοῦ = "of heaven". A lone article should not carry a
  preposition. The same verse already links τοῦ `s` + ἀνθρώπου = "of Man", and
  Matthew 26:64 (same Greek, same English) links all three phrases this way.
- **14:35** προελθὼν μικρόν: προελθών = "Going + farther", μικρόν = "a little"
  → προελθών = "Going", μικρόν = "a little farther" (with a note). The Greek
  καὶ προελθὼν μικρόν and the English "Going a little farther" are identical
  in Matthew 26:39, which links it this way. The author's split was
  defensible, but the parallel should match.
- **14:34** ἕως θανάτου: ἕως = "to the point of", θανάτου = "death" → ἕως =
  "to the point", θανάτου = "of death". The Greek and English are identical to
  Matthew 26:38. I changed it only so that the parallel matches.
- **14:4** τοῦ μύρου: τοῦ = "of", μύρου = "perfume" → τοῦ `s`, μύρου = "of
  perfume". This removes a lone article that carried only a preposition.
- **14:2** note only. γάρ = "But" stays linked. The note now names the sense
  shift: "BSB renders the connective γάρ ("for") as But: the Greek explains the
  covert plan of v. 1, while the English contrasts with it."

## Points raised for this review

- **14:67** τὸν Πέτρον / αὐτῷ: I kept the author's (and publisher's) swap.
  BSB's EN line reads "and saw **him** warming himself there. She looked at
  **Peter** and said". So in BSB the one seen warming himself is "him", and
  the one she looks at is "Peter". BSB did move the name. It used the pronoun
  in the ἰδοῦσα clause and put the name in the ἐμβλέψασα clause. Each English
  word belongs to the Greek word in the same clause: τὸν Πέτρον = "him" and
  αὐτῷ = "Peter". This is the usual way a pronoun that BSB renders as a name
  is linked (for example "BSB names Jesus for αὐτόν").
- **14:2** γάρ = "But": kept as a link with the clearer note above. "But" is
  BSB's only connective at that point. It joins this sentence to v. 1, which
  is γάρ's role, though with the opposite logic. Making γάρ `s` and "But" `t`
  would split the pair. Matthew 26:5 has δέ = "But" in the same English.
- **14:30** σήμερον = "very": agree. BSB compresses σήμερον ταύτῃ τῇ νυκτί
  ("today, this night") into "this very night". "very" is the only trace of
  σήμερον, and the note says so. Matthew 26:34 has `t very` because its Greek
  has no σήμερον, so the difference is real, not an inconsistency.
- **14:4** ἦσαν = "those present": agree. ἦσαν δέ τινες ἀγανακτοῦντες can be
  read as existential ("there were some who were indignant"), as the KJV and
  ESV take it. BSB's "Some of those present" follows that reading. The
  periphrastic alternative is also possible, but the publisher row is not
  wrong.
- **14:11** πῶς + εὐκαίρως = "an opportunity": agree. BSB turns the indirect
  question "how he might betray him conveniently" into the noun object "an
  opportunity". πῶς has no other English word, so joining it is better than
  `s`.
- **14:25** οὐκέτι οὐ μή = "no longer": agree. The triple negative is one
  emphatic unit. "no longer" is its whole English form, and πίω keeps "I will +
  drink", as Matthew 26:29 does with οὐ μή = "not".
- **14:68** `t and the rooster crowed`: confirmed. The publisher record has
  sourceWords 17, matchedSourceWords 17 and publisherGreekWords 20. Every
  N1904 word is matched, and the publisher has exactly 3 extra words. That
  fits καὶ ἀλέκτωρ ἐφώνησεν and the four English words "and the rooster
  crowed". Its targetWords and matchedTargetWords are both 29: the publisher
  links those English words to its own extra Greek words.

## Answers to the author's other doubts

- **14:45** ἐλθών `s`: agree. BSB has one participle ("Going") where the
  Greek has two. Joining ἐλθών to προσελθών would also be defensible, but the
  `s` reason states the fact correctly. Matthew 26:49 has only προσελθών =
  "Going".
- **14:66** `t down` and **14:31** `t Even`: agree. ἔρχεται has no "down",
  and Mark has plain Ἐάν, not Matthew 26:35's Κἄν ("Even if").
- **14:21** ἐκείνῳ = "to that", τῷ `s`: kept. Matthew 26:24 has the same Greek
  and English but puts "to" on the lone article τῷ. The conventions say a lone
  article does not carry a preposition, so Matthew is the outlier here. A
  later pass on Matthew 26:24 could move "to" to ἐκείνῳ or ἀνθρώπῳ.
- **14:8** εἰς = "of" (in "in advance of") and τόν = "My": kept. Both are
  loose but not wrong. προέλαβεν carries "in advance", and εἰς is the relation
  word to "burial". The possessive article follows reviewed precedent (Mark
  12:33, Matthew 27:24).

## Still uncertain

- 14:13 τῶν = "of" (two of His disciples) is still a lone article with "of".
  I kept it because Mark 13:1 links the same pattern this way. The noun cannot
  take "of" without a split across "His".
- 14:6 καλὸν ἔργον: "a" goes with ἔργον ("a + deed"), following the
  convention. Matthew 26:10 has "a beautiful" on καλόν, with a different word
  order.
- 14:4: the periphrastic reading, with ἦσαν ἀγανακτοῦντες = "expressed their
  indignation" and `t of those present`, remains a reasonable alternative.
