# BSB Acts 11: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 30 of 30 verses (11:1-30), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
N1904, BSB's English and each verse's EVIDENCE record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`. No KEEP blocks.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 30, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

One change, a note only; no link was moved.

- 11:28 note on ἔσεσθαι (= sweep): `ἔσεσθαι ("would be") with ἐπί is
  rendered would sweep across.` → `ἔσεσθαι ("to be") with ἐπί ("upon") is
  rendered sweep across; would belongs to μέλλειν.` Why: the old note
  glossed ἔσεσθαι as "would be" and put "would" in its rendering, but
  "would" is linked to μέλλειν in the same verse.

## Points checked

- 11:15 and 11:17, the repeated verbs: ὥσπερ alone means "just as", and the
  publisher already had "just as" on ὥσπερ. So καί ("also") has no English
  word in 11:15, and "just" is not its split-off rendering. In 11:17 ὡς καί
  has no English word ("the same gift He gave us"). καί `s` (11:15) and
  ὡς καί `s` (11:17) are correct, and neither forms a split pair with "He".
  The links for the repeated verbs are right: ἐπέπεσεν = fell + had fallen,
  ἔδωκεν = gave + gave. "He" is `t` because it is a pronoun for a subject
  the Greek states once (τὸ Πνεῦμα τὸ Ἅγιον, ὁ Θεός). It does not repeat
  that word.
- 11:15 "had fallen": I agree that "had" is not an added tense. The verb
  understood again is the aorist ἐπέπεσεν, and English pluperfect is a
  normal rendering of a past aorist about an earlier event. The "will"
  rule does not apply.
- 11:19: in "speaking the message only to Jews", "to" belongs to the
  dative Ἰουδαίοις (`g 25 = to Jews`). No English word belongs to μηδενί
  alone. The four tokens μηδενὶ … εἰ μὴ μόνον ("to no one … except only")
  together mean "only", so the joined group is honest. Giving "only" to
  μόνον alone with μηδενί and εἰ μή `s` would leave a split pair.
- 11:6: ἀτενίσας κατενόουν = "I looked + closely" is right. Each Greek word
  has some "look intently" and some "observe" in it, and the English pair
  renders the two together, so neither split is clearly better. ἥν = "it"
  and εἰς = "at" are both linked. καί = and, εἶδον = saw.
- 11:23: "in" on Κυρίῳ uses the same note as the precedent in John 12, John 6
  and Acts 7 ("BSB's Greek has an extra word here with the same meaning …").
  This verse's EVIDENCE line shows one extra publisher word and no
  unmatched N1904 word, and the publisher's own rows leave "in" unlinked,
  so the reading note is supported. τῇ προθέσει τῆς καρδίας follows the
  lone-article rule: τῇ `s`, προθέσει = with all, τῆς = their (possessive
  article), καρδίας = hearts. No lone article carries a preposition.
- Acts 10 parallel (11:5-10 against 10:11-16): the same Greek gets the same
  links. καταβαῖνον `s`, σκεῦός τι = something (11:5 adds "of", as in "city
  of Joppa"), ὡς = like, μεγάλην = a large, τέσσαρσιν = four, ἀρχαῖς = by +
  corners with "its" `t`, καθιεμ- = being let down. Also τῆς = the, γῆς =
  of + earth and τοῦ = the, οὐρανοῦ = of + air, as in 10:12. Ἅ = anything +
  that, ὁ `s`, σύ `s`, μή = not, κοίνου = Do + call + impure, ἐπὶ τρίς
  joined. 11:16 Ἁγίῳ = "the Holy" matches Acts 1:5 (the same saying).
  11:24 Πνεύματος = "of the + Spirit" matches Acts 6:5 and Luke 4:1.
- Readings: every reading note sits in a verse whose EVIDENCE shows a
  difference, and the publisher's own rows agree. In 11:11 the unlinked
  word is Καισαρίας and ἦμεν is matched, so no reading is claimed for "I
  was staying". In 11:16 the unlinked word is Ἰωάνης. In 11:20 it is
  Ἕλληνας: "Greeks" correctly renders the N1904 word, so the link and note
  are right. 11:22 and 11:23 each have one extra publisher word. In 11:28
  the unlinked word is ἐσήμαινεν. 11:18 is 0/0 and no reading is claimed.
  None of the notes states the publisher's exact Greek.
- Brief rules: no supplied copula in this chapter. Supplied names "Barnabas"
  (11:24, 11:25) are `t`. The relatives ὅς = He (11:14) and ὅς = he (11:23)
  are linked as Greek pronouns. 11:21 τε stands alone and is rendered "and".
  11:26 τε is not paired with καί and starts a new English sentence, so it
  is `s`. No lone article carries "of the", "to the" or "in the" (11:1, 6,
  16, 21, 22, 23, 24, 28, 29 checked).

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 11:6 joined group: keep it (see above).
- 11:19 four-token group: keep it (see above).
- 11:29 τις = his: keep it. τις is the subject of εὐπορεῖτο ("as anyone had
  means"), and BSB makes that person the owner in "his ability". "his" is
  how the English refers to τις, so `s` would leave a split pair.
- 11:23 "in" on Κυρίῳ under a same-meaning reading note: keep it. It
  matches the John 12 and Acts 7 precedent and is supported by the
  EVIDENCE record.
- 11:17 δυνατός `s`: keep it. "to" is the infinitive marker of κωλῦσαι,
  and no English word renders "able". The publisher had "to" on δυνατός,
  which was wrong.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 11:21 ἀριθμός = "number of people": "of people" is English filling in
  what the number consists of. It is a publisher row and not clearly wrong,
  since ἀριθμός here means a body of believers. Strictly, "of people" could
  be `t`. 11:24 and 11:26 have "number(s) of people" on ὄχλος ("crowd"),
  where it is a real rendering.
- 11:1 δέ = soon and 11:19 οὖν = Meanwhile follow the convention for scene
  openers. Neither English word strictly translates the connective.
- 11:12 τόν = the for "the man's home" (as in Matthew 13:55). τοῦ ἀνδρός =
  "the man's" would also be defensible.
