# BSB Acts 1: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 26 of 26 verses (1:1-26), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, against N1904,
BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json` (1:5, 1:7, 1:8,
1:13, 1:16 and 1:22 have a `partial` record). The chapter has no KEEP
verses. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 26, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (3 verses)

- 1:10: `g 9 = when` (καί before ἰδού) + `s 1` (ὡς) → `g 1 = when //
  Temporal ὡς ("as", "while") is rendered when; BSB makes its clause the
  main clause and puts when before the next clause.` + `s 9 = BSB leaves
  untranslated the καί that brings in the main clause after the ὡς clause.`
  Reasons:
  - ὡς is the Greek word that means "when/as" here. BSB turns "As they were
    gazing …, behold, two men stood" into "They were looking … when
    suddenly two men stood". The time relation moves to the other clause,
    but its meaning is the same. "when" is therefore ὡς's rendering moved
    to the other clause ("same meaning, even shifted"). With ὡς `s`, the
    word lost its English and καί received it.
  - The καί before ἰδού is the Hebrew-style καί that brings in the main
    clause after a time clause. The reviewed Luke 24:4 has the same
    structure (ἐν τῷ ἀπορεῖσθαι … καὶ ἰδοὺ ἄνδρες δύο). There ἐν τῷ =
    "While", καί is `s` with this reason, and ἰδού = "suddenly". The
    When-on-καί convention applies only when no Greek word carries the time
    relation (Luke 1:59).
  - "as" stays on the genitive absolute πορευομένου ("as + was going",
    αὐτοῦ = He). That is the convention, and "as" introduces "He was going",
    not the ὡς clause, so it is not ὡς's word. This matches 1:9, where
    "watched as" stays on the genitive absolute βλεπόντων.
- 1:25: `g 14 = his` (article τόν "used as a possessive") + `g 17 =
  rightful` + `s 16` → `g 17 = his rightful // ἴδιον ("his own") is
  rendered his rightful.` + `s 14 16` (article reason). In τὸν τόπον τὸν
  ἴδιον the possessive idea comes from ἴδιον ("his own"). The first article
  only goes with τόπον and is not used as a possessive. Reviewed precedent
  for the same structure: John 1:41 τὸν ἀδελφὸν τὸν ἴδιον (ἴδιον = his, both
  articles `s`), Matthew 22:5 (ἴδιον = his), Jude 1:6 τὸ ἴδιον (ἴδιον =
  "their proper"). In this chapter 1:7 also gives "His own" to ἰδίᾳ (τῇ
  `s`). With "his" on the article, a reader who taps "his" saw "the" and
  not the Greek word for "own".
- 1:12 (note only): "The second Ἰερουσαλήμ" → "The second Ἱερουσαλήμ", to
  match the token (rough breathing). No link changed.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 1:21-22 δεῖ / "must", "one", "For": **agree, kept**. δεῖ is linked
  in-verse to "it is necessary". The Greek and English verse divisions
  are the same (the sentence runs across both verses in each), so a
  cross-verse link is not allowed. "must" (1:22) is a second English
  rendering of δεῖ, which stands in 1:21. ἕνα (1:22) is already linked to
  1:22's "one", so 1:21's "one" is an anticipation of it. Neither is a
  split pair. All four `t` reasons are factual: "to choose" (no Greek verb
  of choosing), "one" (ἕνα is in 1:22), "For" (BSB starts a new sentence
  where the δεῖ construction continues), and "must" (δεῖ's necessity is
  repeated for γενέσθαι). The publisher's "must become" on γενέσθαι and
  "For one" on ἕνα over-claimed, so the author's split was right.
- 1:11 "will come back" on ἐλεύσεται, "here" `t`; 1:12 "away" on ἔχον:
  **agree, kept**. "come back" is a phrasal verb for the coming that the
  verb states. "here" is a separate place adverb with no Greek source.
  "a Sabbath day's journey away" expresses the distance that ἔχον ("being
  [at] a distance of") carries.
- 1:15 ὄχλος + ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό = "a gathering": **agree, kept**. "gathering"
  means a crowd gathered together, so it covers both ὄχλος and ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό
  ("together"). The publisher had put "a gathering of" on ἐπί and "and"
  (of "and said") on τό. Both were wrong.
- 1:9 "watched as" on βλεπόντων: **agree, kept**. BSB makes the genitive
  absolute the main clause, and "as" carries its time relation over to
  ἐπήρθη. This is the same reasoning as for ὡς in 1:10.

## Points from the brief

- 1:5 οὐ … πολλάς = "a few" (litotes), μετά = "in", ταύτας `s`:
  **confirmed**. "a few" = "not many". The "from now" sense is partly
  ταύτας's ("after these [present] days"). Joining μετὰ … ταύτας = "in"
  would also be honest. But the `s` reason states correctly that BSB has no
  word for "these", so I left it (rule 10).
- 1:1 τόν = "my" (possessive article), "In" `t`, ὦ = "O", Θεόφιλε =
  "Theophilus": **confirmed**. The explicit ὦ owns "O". The vocative "You"
  rule does not apply because no "You" is involved.
- 1:13 "son" `t` twice, as in the reviewed Luke 6:15-16 (bare genitive,
  no article; Matthew 10:3 links "son" only because it has ὁ τοῦ):
  **confirmed**. Every name and patronymic is linked (Ἀλφαίου = "of
  Alphaeus", Ἰακώβου = "of James"). The καί between items map correctly:
  15 and 27 are commas (`s`), 13/17/20/23/31 = and#0-#4. τε and ὅ are `s`.
- 1:15 against BSB's exact wording ("In those days Peter stood up among
  the brothers (a gathering of about a hundred and twenty) and said,"):
  **confirmed**. Every English word is covered once. ὀνομάτων = "of" (the
  genitive, with "persons" absorbed into the number). ἦν `s` (verbless
  parenthesis), τε `s`, "and" before "said" `t` (participle joined to the
  main verb).
- 1:24 καρδιογνῶστα = know + heart, πάντων = everyone's, Σύ = You, "us"
  `t`: **confirmed**. 1:17 ἔλαχεν τὸν κλῆρον = "shared in" (τῆς `s`):
  **confirmed**.

## Coordinator adjustment at registration

- 1:2, 1:8, 1:21: "to"/"of" moved off the lone article onto its noun
  (τοῖς = the, ἀποστόλοις = to + apostles; τῆς = the, γῆς = of + earth;
