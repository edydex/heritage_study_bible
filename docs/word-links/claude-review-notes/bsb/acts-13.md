## Readings and verse division

- Names spelled differently in the publisher's Greek, linked with a note:
  13:4 Seleucia, 13:5 and 13:13 and 13:24 and 13:25 John (probably
  Ἰωάννης), 13:6 Bar-Jesus, 13:16 "of Israel" (Ἰσραηλεῖται, as Acts 2:22),
  13:21 Kish and Benjamin, 13:28 Pilate.
- 13:20 "the" before prophet: the publisher's Greek has an extra word
  (probably τοῦ); `t` with the reading reason.
- 13:33 "their": the publisher's Greek has an extra word (probably αὐτῶν,
  "for their children, us"); "their" is `t`, and ἡμῶν stays linked to "us"
  with a note, since the evidence matches every N1904 word.
- 13:44 "of the Lord": the publisher's Greek has a different word (probably
  Κυρίου); Θεοῦ is `s`, "of" and "Lord" are `t`, the shared article τοῦ =
  the.
- 13:38-39: N1904 ends 13:38 with καὶ ἀπὸ πάντων … δικαιωθῆναι, which BSB
  renders in 13:39. Tokens 14-22 are `m` to 13:39 and linked there with
  `g @13:38`; the καί is `s` in 13:38 (BSB ends the sentence). No reading is
  claimed for "the law"; it is linked to νόμῳ.
- 13:14 differs by one word (probably Πισιδίας, "of Pisidia"); "Pisidian"
  still renders N1904 Πισιδίαν, so no reading is claimed.
- 13:39's counts include the ten moved words; "Through" renders N1904 ἐν,
  so no other reading is claimed there.

## Notable decisions

- Matthew 26:27 precedent for a καί between participles that BSB turns into
  the "and" of an English series: 13:16 καί = and (stood up, motioned, and
  began), and 13:3 καί before ἐπιθέντες = the "and" that joins laid and sent.
- Vocatives: 13:10 Ὦ = O, πλήρης = you + full ("are" `t`); 13:41 οἱ
  καταφρονηταί = you scoffers; 13:16 οἱ φοβούμενοι = you + who fear; in 13:26
  ἐν ὑμῖν = you and οἱ … φοβούμενοι = who fear.
- Pronouns BSB renders as names are linked: 13:4 Αὐτοί = Barnabas and Saul,
  13:7 οὗτος = The proconsul, 13:9 αὐτόν = Elymas, 13:22 αὐτόν = Saul, 13:24
  αὐτοῦ = of Jesus, 13:27 τοῦτον = Jesus, 13:38 τούτου = Jesus, 13:42 αὐτῶν
  = Paul and Barnabas; 13:33 ὁ Θεός = He (God is named in 13:32, where it is
  `t`).
- Psalm 2:7 in 13:33 follows Hebrews 1:5 and 5:5 (γεγέννηκα = have become +
  Father, σε = Your); Psalm 16:10 in 13:35 follows Acts 2:27.
- 13:37 ἤγειρεν = raised from the dead, as Acts 2:24.
- οὕτως = "that is what" (13:8) and "this is what" (13:47) kept whole, as
  Matthew 18:35 and 1 Peter 3:5 ("That is how").
- 13:46 τε = Then: it follows the participle as the sentence connective, not
  as τε … καί with Paul and Barnabas (contrast 13:1, where both τε are `s`).

## Doubts for the peer

- 13:34 μέλλοντα ὑποστρέφειν εἰς ("about to return to") = "to see", one
  group with a note: BSB borrows "see decay" from the psalm. The other
  option is to leave the verbs `s` and "see" `t`.
- 13:42 αὐτοῖς ("to them", the hearers) is `s` and English "them" (Paul
  and Barnabas, object of urged) is `t`, because they name different
  people.
- 13:14 διελθόντες … παρεγένοντο jointly = traveled inland; παρεγένοντο could
  instead be `s`.
- 13:17 τὸν λαόν = them + people, with ὕψωσεν = made + into a great.
- 13:26 ἐν ὑμῖν = you (rather than `s` with "you" on the vocative article as
  in 13:16).
- 13:15 "please" `t` and 13:16 "began to speak" kept on εἶπεν.
