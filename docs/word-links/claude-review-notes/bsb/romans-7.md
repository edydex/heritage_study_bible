# BSB Romans 7: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: all 25 (7:1-25). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`.

105 of the 391 publisher `g` rows changed, spread over all 25 verses. 11 of
those only gained or merged a token with the English unchanged (joined units
listed below). Every changed row is listed by kind; unchanged rows were checked too.

Readings: EVIDENCE shows real differences only in 7:9/7:10, 7:16, 7:17 and
7:25.

- 7:9/7:10: N1904 starts 7:10 with ἐγὼ δὲ ἀπέθανον; BSB ends 7:9 with "and I
  died". These three tokens are moved (`m … > 7:9`) and linked from 7:9 with
  `g @7:10`: ἐγώ = "I", δέ = "and", ἀπέθανον = "died". This is the only
  cross-verse link.
- 7:16 σύνφημι = "I admit", noted as the same word spelled differently in
  BSB's Greek text (probably σύμφημι).
- 7:17 ἡ ἐνοικοῦσα = "living", noted as a slightly different form of the
  verb with the same meaning in BSB's Greek text (probably οἰκοῦσα, as in
  7:20).
- 7:25: BSB's Greek has one extra word that BSB's English does not render
  (probably δέ after χάρις). Nothing is claimed for it.
- 7:3 shows a difference with no unmatched words (order only). No reading is
  claimed.

Conventions followed: Romans 1-2 (lone articles, "the"/"a" on the noun,
supplied copulas and possessives `t`, connectives rendered by another
connective stay linked, article as possessive, εἰς τό + infinitive joined);
Galatians 2:17 and 3:21 for μὴ γένοιτο = "Certainly not" as one unit; Romans
2:13 for BSB's cleft "it is … who/that" (`t`); Acts 10:25 for an articular
infinitive's τοῦ joined to its infinitive; Acts 19:40-41 for the verse-split
`m`/`g @` pattern; John 15:2/15:4 for words understood again.

## Publisher rows corrected

"the", a possessive, or `s`; the case word goes to the noun):

- 7:4 τῷ "to the" → "the", "to" on νόμῳ.
- 7:7 ὁ "Is the" → "the", "Is" `t`.

  "the", resumptive αὕτη = "very".
- 7:13 τό "Did that which" → "that which", "Did" on ἐγένετο ("Did +
  become"); τοῦ "what was" → "what", "was" `t`.
- 7:18 τό "what is" → "what", "is" `t`.
- 7:21 τόν "this is the" → "the", "this is" `t`.
- 7:22 τῷ "in" → `s`, "in" on νόμῳ; τόν "in my" → "my", "in" on κατά
  (which had no link).
- 7:25 τῷ "to" → `s`, "to" on Θεῷ; τῷ/τῇ "with my" → "my", "with" on νοΐ /

- Related: 7:3 "while" moved off ἀνδρός to the genitive absolute ζῶντος, and
  "to" off ἑτέρῳ to ἀνδρί; 7:14 ὁ (no link) now "the", taken from νόμος;

Negatives split from verbs (the verb keeps do/would/had): 7:7 three times
(οὐκ … ἔγνων, οὐκ ᾔδειν, Οὐκ ἐπιθυμήσεις = "not" + "Do … covet") and μή =

## Notable decisions

- Theological terms are linked word by word to BSB's English with no
  interpretive notes: νόμος = "law" (and "principle" in 7:21), ἁμαρτία =
  "sin" (7:5 genitive = "sinful", 7:13 ἁμαρτωλός = "sinful"), ἐντολή =
  "commandment", σάρξ = "flesh" (7:14 σάρκινος = "unspiritual"), πνεῦμα =
  "the Spirit", ἐπιθυμία = "coveting" / "covetous desire", νοῦς = "mind".
- First person: "I" sits on the finite verb ending (7:7 "I would", 7:15
  "I do", 7:18 "I know"). Each explicit ἐγώ is linked on its own (7:9 twice,
  7:14, 7:17, 7:20 twice, 7:24, 7:25). Only 7:25 links ἐγώ to a repeated "I",
  where both are the subject of "serve". Datives μοι/ἐμοί that BSB turns
  into the subject "I" stay linked (7:10, 7:18, 7:21).
- μὴ γένοιτο = "Certainly not" as one unit (7:7, 7:13).
- Connectives rendered with another connective stay linked: 7:2 γάρ = "For
  instance", 7:7 ἀλλά = "Indeed", 7:9 δέ (from 7:10) = "and", 7:10 καί =
  "So", 7:19 ἀλλά = "Instead". Untranslated γάρ (7:14, 7:15, 7:18), δέ (7:9,
  7:17), μέν (7:12, 7:25), τε (7:7) and ἤ (7:1) are `s`.
- Untranslated resumptive τοῦτο (7:15 twice, 7:16, 7:19, 7:20) and the
  relative ὅ of 7:19 ("the good I want") are `s`.
- Kept publisher renderings: 7:5 ἐν = "according to", ἦμεν = "we lived",
  μέλεσιν = "bodies"; 7:6 the second "in" on παλαιότητι (dative under the
  same ἐν); 7:7 εἰ / μή / διά = "if" / "not" / "for"; 7:14 πεπραμένος = "sold
  as a slave", ὑπό = "to"; 7:23 ἐν = "to" (captive to); 7:25 ἄρα = "then",
  οὖν = "So" (as in 7:3).

## Doubts for the peer

- 7:18: τὸ κατεργάζεσθαι linked to both "to do" and "carry … out". "to do"
  `t` (like 7:15 and 7:19), with only "carry … out" linked, is the
  alternative. "I" in "I cannot" is `t`; linking it to μοι (understood again)
  is possible.
- 7:3 τοῦ … εἶναι = "and + is": "and" `t` with τοῦ … εἶναι = "is" is the
  alternative.
- 7:6 "to" `t` in "having died to what bound us"; putting it on the
  participle ("having died to") is the alternative.
- 7:13 ἡ ἁμαρτία = "sin" (in BSB's purpose clause) with "it produced" →
  "it" `t`. Grammatically ἡ ἁμαρτία is the subject of κατεργαζομένη, so
  linking it to "it" (or to both) is arguable.
- 7:11 με linked to the second "me" (shared object); `t` is the
  alternative. Same question for αὐτό / κατεργάζομαι understood again in
  7:17 and 7:20.
- 7:15, 7:16, 7:19: "to do" after θέλω `t`; linking it to the main verb
  (πράσσω / ποιῶ) understood again is the alternative.
- 7:20: ἐγώ (after θέλω in N1904) kept on the first "I" with ποιῶ, as the
  publisher had it; it could instead go with θέλω ("I do not want").
- 7:10: εἰς = "meant to bring" with "was" `t`, and εἰς = "brought" with
  "actually" `t`.
- 7:23: μου = "me" with μέλεσιν `s`, versus joining τοῖς μέλεσίν μου = "me".
