## Points settled

- 2:1: κἀγώ = "I + I + I" stays. κἀγώ is the explicit subject of the whole
  sentence (of ἐλθών, ἦλθον and καταγγέλλων alike), so each "I" has the
  same role and meaning, as in Acts 22:3. ἦλθον = "did + come",
  ἐλθών = "When + came" and καταγγέλλων = "as + proclaimed" keep the
  person off the participles and "When/as" on them. Correct.
- 2:8: ἂν ἐσταύρωσαν = "they would + have crucified" (Luke 12:39); οὐκ =
  "not". 2:6 and 2:8 "of + age" on αἰῶνος with "this" on τούτου (Luke
  16:8). Correct.
- 2:9: BSB has no "man" or "heart of man" ("no heart has imagined"), so
  ἀνθρώπου `s` is right. Ἅ and ὅσα joined to the one "what" is an honest
  link: BSB's "what" is both the object of "seen/heard/imagined" (the role
  of Ἅ) and the relative of "has prepared" (the role of ὅσα), and it means
  what both words mean. ἐπὶ … ἀνέβη = "has imagined" (the idiom "come up
  upon the heart") with καρδίαν = "heart". The quotation is linked word by
  word with no interpretive notes.
- 2:13: ἅ = "what" with "this is" `t` (supplied demonstrative and copula);
  "us" `t` (supplied object); "by + wisdom" on σοφίας (genitive of agent
  after διδακτοῖς, "human" on ἀνθρωπίνης); the second "words" linked to
  λόγοις as understood again in the parallel clause. Correct. The third
  "words" stays inside πνευματικοῖς ("in spiritual words"), as "truths" stays
  inside πνευματικά: both are heads BSB supplies for substantival adjectives.
- 2:15: EVIDENCE shows one N1904 word unmatched and one publisher word not in
  N1904. Of the words the publisher left unlinked, δέ, ὑπ’ and οὐδενός stand
  in every major edition, so the unmatched word is almost certainly μέν; the
  `s` reason ("BSB’s Greek text differs here") does not name the publisher's
  word. ὑπ’ οὐδενός = "not + anyone’s" and ἀνακρίνεται = "is + subject to +
  judgment" fit BSB's "is not subject to anyone’s judgment": οὐδενός carries
  the negative and the agent (rendered as the possessive), and "is subject to
  … judgment" paraphrases the passive "is judged".
- 2:16: ὅς = "so as" stays linked. The relative with a future indicative
  (ὃς συμβιβάσει, from Isaiah 40:13) is the Greek construction for
  purpose/result ("who would instruct him" = "so as to instruct him"), and
  ὅς is the word that introduces that clause; "so as" is BSB's marker for the
  same clause. This is a recast of the same word's function, like ὅς =
  "and" (Luke 23:51) and ὃς ἔχει = "with" (John 6:9), not an unrelated
  English word, and the note states the literal "who will". Publisher row,
  not wrong.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 2:1 (κἀγώ on all three "I"s vs. first "I" only): keep the author's
  choice; the alternative would leave two "I"s `t` or on ἦλθον although an
  explicit pronoun owns them.
- 2:9 (Ἅ + ὅσα joined vs. Ἅ `s`): keep joined (reason above).
- 2:15 (ὑπ’ = "subject to"): no. ὑπό with the genitive marks the agent
  ("by"), not "under"; "subject to" belongs to BSB's rendering of the
  passive verb. Keep the author's split.
- 2:16 (ὅς = "so as" vs. `s` + `t`): keep linked (reason above).
- 2:14 ("The natural" on anarthrous ψυχικός): keep. A generic English "The"
  with no Greek article goes with the word it introduces; not wrong.

## Still uncertain

- 2:4: "of the Spirit’s" sits on Πνεύματος (publisher row). Because BSB
  joins the two genitives as "the Spirit’s power", "of" could equally sit on
  δυνάμεως. Both genitives depend on ἀποδείξει, so the current row is not
  wrong; left unchanged.
- 2:10: γάρ = "But". EVIDENCE shows BSB's Greek equals N1904 here, so no
  reading is claimed; linking a connective to BSB's shifted connective
  follows Romans 9:11.
