## Publisher rows corrected

Names and words supplied by English that sat on a Greek word (now `t`):

- 1:23 "John replied" → replied; 1:29 "John saw" → saw; 1:42 "Andrew brought" → brought. The Greek gives the subject only in the verb ending.
- 1:43 "Jesus decided" → decided. The Greek Ἰησοῦς (subject of λέγει, publisher "He") is now linked to Jesus, and λέγει = He told.
- 1:23 Ἐγώ "I am" → I, `t am` (verbless clause).
- 1:8 "he came to testify" → testify, ἵνα = to, `t he came` (the verb is understood from 1:7).
- 1:15 ἦν "is He", 1:30 οὗτός "He" + ἐστιν "This is" → This / is, `t He` (antecedent of the relative "of whom").
- 1:18 "but the one and only Son" → `t but` (asyndeton); "who is Himself God" → Θεός = God, `t who is Himself`; "has made Him known" → has made + known, `t Him`.
- 1:21 Τί "who are you" → who, `t are you`.
- 1:26 μέσος "but among" → among, `t but` (no connective in the Greek).
- 1:27 "He is the One who" → ὁ … ἐρχόμενος = the One who comes, `t He is`.
- 1:31 ἵνα "was that" → that, `t was`.
- 1:37 "say this" → say, `t this`; 1:42 "and said" → said, `t and` (joins the participle clause).
- 1:44 πόλεως "same town" → town, `t same`.
- 1:51 "you will all see" → you will + see, `t all` (marks the plural).

Negatives split from their verbs (verb keeps did/has/do):

- 1:5 has + overcome / not; 1:10, 1:11, 1:31, 1:33 did + recognize/receive/know / not; 1:26 do + know / not; 1:20 He did + refuse / not (see below).

Words on the wrong Greek word:

- 1:20: ὡμολόγησεν carried the whole "He did not refuse to confess". Now ὡμολόγησεν = to confess, οὐκ = not, ἠρνήσατο = He did + refuse; both inner καί and ὅτι are `s`.
- 1:10: καί "and though" → and; the καί between the last two clauses = though (BSB subordinates the clause before it; same convention as "When").
- 1:13: οἵ "children born" → children; ἐγεννήθησαν = born + born (one verb, said twice in English); the repeated ἐκ is `s`.
- 1:14: ὡς carried "of" → `s` (BSB leaves "as" out); μονογενοῦς = of the one and only Son.
- 1:15, 1:30: ἔμπροσθεν "surpassed" + γέγονεν "has" → ἔμπροσθεν … γέγονεν = has surpassed. 1:30 ὅς (`s` in the draft) = who, since BSB moves the relative onto "comes after me"; ἔρχεται = comes.
- 1:16: πάντες "have all" → all; ἐλάβομεν = have + received.
- 1:18: πώποτε (`s` in the draft) = ever, ἑώρακεν = has + seen; the article ὁ carried "and" → `t and`, ὁ ὤν = is; τόν "the" → `s`, τοῦ = the (of "the Father’s").
- 1:23: καθώς "in" + εἶπεν "the words of" → καθὼς εἶπεν = in the words of.
- 1:27: οὗ "of whose" → οὗ … αὐτοῦ = whose (as Mark 1:7, Luke 3:16); ὑποδήματος = of + sandals.
- 1:29: τοῦ "of the" → the; κόσμου = of + world.
- 1:31: "to Israel" → τῷ = to, Ἰσραήλ = Israel; 1:45 τοῦ = of, Ἰωσήφ = Joseph; 1:49 τοῦ = of, Ἰσραήλ = Israel (indeclinable names: the article shows the case).
- 1:33: Ἐφ’ "The man on" → on; the resumptive οὗτός = The man (the subject of ἐστιν); ὁ βαπτίζων = He who will baptize.
- 1:36: καί "When" → `s`; ἐμβλέψας = When he saw. 1:37: "And when" → καί = And, the καί between the clauses = when. 1:47: Ἰησοῦς "When Jesus" → Jesus; the καί between the clauses = When.
- 1:38: εἶπαν "They said" → οἱ (in οἱ δέ) = They, εἶπαν = said.
- 1:41: the second τόν "his" → ἴδιον ("his own") = his.
- 1:45: καί carried "the One" (second time), προφῆται carried "foretold". Now Ὅν = the One + the One, ἔγραψεν = wrote about + foretold (one relative and one verb serve both subjects), καί `s`.
- 1:47: ἀληθῶς "is a true" → true; Ἴδε = Here is; Ἰσραηλείτης = a + Israelite.
- Article + participle joined (rule 3): 1:12 to those who believed, 1:15 He who comes, 1:22 for those who sent, 1:29 who takes away, 1:33 the One who sent, 1:40 who heard. 1:28 ἦν … βαπτίζων = was baptizing.

## Notable decisions

- 1:1: every word linked one to one; τόν with Θεόν is `s`. The one note, on Θεός, states only the grammar: it stands first, without the article, as the predicate, and ὁ Λόγος is the subject. No theological gloss on πρός or on the anarthrous Θεός.
- 1:14 and 1:18: μονογενής is used as a noun; "the one and only Son" is linked to it (as Hebrews 11:17 links "his one and only son" to τὸν μονογενῆ), with a note that the Greek has no separate word for Son. In 1:18 BSB's Greek = N1904 (μονογενὴς Θεός); Θεός = God, with BSB's apposition "who is Himself God" as `t`.
- 1:9: the publisher's separate links ἦν = was, ἐρχόμενον = coming are kept (not joined), because whether ἐρχόμενον goes with ἦν or with ἄνθρωπον is disputed; a note says BSB takes them together.
- 1:11: notes distinguish neuter τὰ ἴδια (his own things/home) from masculine οἱ ἴδιοι (his own people); the article = His is kept (majority convention).
- 1:13: θελήματος σαρκός ("will of the flesh") = the desire, joined; BSB's "the desire or will of man" puts both under one "of".
- 1:22: ἵνα … δῶμεν = We need (BSB recasts the purpose clause).
- 1:24-25: one English sentence; 1:25 opening καί and the doubled verb καὶ εἶπαν αὐτῷ are `s`. 1:24 ἐκ is `s` (BSB makes the Pharisees the subject).
- Readings: every EVIDENCE difference is a spelling or form only, linked with a note: Ἰωάνης/Ἰωάνου = John (1:6, 1:15, 1:19, 1:26, 1:28, 1:32, 1:35, 1:40, 1:42), Λευείτας = Levites (1:19), Ἡλείας = Elijah (1:21, 1:25), Ῥαββεί = Rabbi (1:38, 1:49), Ἰσραηλείτης = Israelite (1:47), and στήκει = stands (1:26: BSB's Greek has a different form of the verb, probably ἕστηκεν; same meaning). 1:47 counts one more publisher word, probably the article before Ἰησοῦς, which has no English word, so nothing is claimed. The 1:9 EVIDENCE line counts no unmatched words.
- Pronoun or noun rendered by another referent: αὐτῷ = Jesus (1:40), the second Ἰησοῦς = who (1:42), οἵ = children (1:13), ὅν = One (1:26), αὐτοῖς = to (1:12, resumptive).
- Kept publisher rows that are free but not wrong: καί = with (1:35), εἰς = as (1:7), Ὅτι = just because (1:50), Ἀνδρέου = as Andrew (1:44, with a note), ἐσκήνωσεν = made His dwelling (1:14), εἶναι = come (1:46, with a note), ἐστίν = was (1:19, with a note).

## Doubts for the peer

- 1:33: οὗτός = "The man" (BSB's sentence subject) instead of `s` for οὗτός + `t The man`. I think it is the same subject moved to the front; the alternative is defensible.
- 1:15 and 1:30: "He" in "This is He of whom" is `t` (antecedent of the relative). It could instead be joined to ὅν/οὗ as a headless relative.
- 1:45: linking "foretold" to ἔγραψεν (understood again for the prophets) rather than `t foretold`.
- 1:14 and 1:18: "Son" kept inside the μονογενής group with a note, rather than `t Son`.
- 1:22: ἵνα joined into "We need" rather than `s`.
