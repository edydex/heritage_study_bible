# BSB John 17: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 26 of 26 verses (17:1-26), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, against N1904,
BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/john.json` (only 17:21, 17:24
and 17:25 have a `partial` record). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 26, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (1 verse)

- 17:24: `g 9 = those` (κἀκεῖνοι, note) + `s 1` (ὅ) → `g 1 = those` with
  the note "The headless relative ὅ ("what") stands for the group and is
  rendered those." + `s 9` ("The resumptive κἀκεῖνοι ("and those") picks
  up those You have given Me; BSB names the group only once."). This
  restores the publisher's row. Reasons:
  - Evidence: the `partial` record has exactly one N1904 word unmatched and
    one publisher word extra. The publisher's own groups pair N1904 ὃ with
    its own word and link it to "those". So BSB's Greek has this ὅ, the one
    differing word is the vocative "Father" word, and there is no reading
    with another relative here. No reading note is needed.
  - Consistency: in 17:2 the same structure (pendant πᾶν ὅ … resumptive
    αὐτοῖς) has ὅ = "those" and gives the resumptive only "to". The
    reviewed John 6:39 does the same (πᾶν ὅ = "those", resumptive ἐξ αὐτοῦ
    = "of" only), and 17:9 has ὧν = "those" in "those You have given Me".
    "those You have given Me" heads a relative clause that is headless in
    Greek, so ὅ is its source. κἀκεῖνοι only resumes it.
  - Rule 10: the publisher row was not wrong.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 17:24 "those": changed back to ὅ (see above).
- 17:21 ἵνα + ὦσιν = "May + be": **agree, kept**. In this verse "may"
  always stays in a group with its subjunctive verb (ὦσιν "may be",
  πιστεύῃ "may believe"). The join keeps the wish's "May" with ὦσιν too and
  still owns ἵνα, as the reviewed 1 John 4:21 (ἵνα … ἀγαπᾷ = "must love")
  and John 1:22 (ἵνα … δῶμεν = "We need") do. The reviewed Gospel requests
  (Matthew 20:33, Mark 10:51, Luke 18:41 ἵνα = "let"; Mark 5:23 "Please")
  put the request word on ἵνα alone. But "let" and "Please" are not the
  verb's own modal, while "May" here is. The publisher's split (ἵνα = May,
  ὦσιν = be) would also be acceptable. That is a matter of style, so I did
  not change it.
- 17:13 "while I am" `t`, ἐν = in: **agree**. "in the world" is still
  rendered word for word ("in" = ἐν). "while I am" turns the phrase into a
  clause. The Greek has no verb or temporal word for it.
- 17:10 second "all" `t`: **agree**. πάντα modifies τὰ ἐμά only. It is not
  understood again the way ἐστιν is (ἐστιν = is + is is right). Putting
  "all" on the article τά (the publisher's row) would make a lone article
  carry a quantifier.
- 17:2 αὐτοῖς = "to", πᾶν = all: **agree**. Only αὐτοῖς is dative. This
  matches the reviewed John 6:39, where the resumptive ἐξ αὐτοῦ carries
  "of" and πᾶν ὅ carries "those".
- 17:26 ἠγάπησας = "You have for", με = Me: **agree, kept**. με is an
  accusative object, so it supplies no case word. "for" comes from BSB's
  noun recast ("love You have for"), so it stays with the verb.

## Points from the brief

- 17:5: παρά = in + presence and σεαυτῷ = Your are correct (precedent:
  "in + presence" on the preposition). δόξῃ = with#0 + glory is honest:
  with#0 renders the instrumental dative τῇ δόξῃ ("glorify Me with the
  glory"). with#1 is the second παρά (παρὰ σοί = "with You"), and the first
  παρά is fully used by "in … presence". Nothing to move.
- 17:21, 17:24, 17:25 vocative: in all three the `partial` record has 1
  N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher word extra, with the publisher's
  English fully matched. Yet "Father" is the only English word missing from
  the published groups, so the publisher linked it to its extra word, and
  Πατήρ/Πατὴρ (never in a publisher group) is the N1904 word left
  unmatched. In 17:24 the other words without a group (ἵνα, κἀκεῖνοι, τήν,
  τήν, ἥν) are blank publisher rows of the usual kind. The note "Same
  word, in a different form in BSB's Greek text." follows the reviewed
  wording ("Same negative, in a different form …"). It does not spell the
  publisher's form. Kept.
- 17:24 ἐμήν `s` ("BSB leaves ἐμήν ("My") unexpressed."): correct. The
  publisher's groups pair N1904 ἐμήν with its own word (its row linked it
  to "the"), so BSB's Greek has it. The English has no "My", so this is a
  translation choice, not a reading. τήν = the and the second τήν `s` are
  right.
- 17:1: "When" on the καί between Ταῦτα ἐλάλησεν Ἰησοῦς and the εἶπεν
  clause follows the When-rule. "and" `t` only joins "He lifted up" (from
  the participle ἐπάρας) to "said". Correct.

## Other points checked

- Negative splits (17:9, 11, 15, 20, 25), the κἀγώ links (17:11, 18, 21,
  22, 26), the 17:6 τοῖς ἀνθρώποις = "to those" join and the 17:23 εἰς ἕν =
  "united" join are correct.
- 17:11/12 ᾧ = "the name" (BSB repeats the noun for the relative) is
  correct.
- Supplied words are `t` with clear reasons: copulas in 17:11, 21 and 22,
  "them" in 17:8 and "it" in 17:26.
- No verse other than 17:21, 24 and 25 makes a reading claim, and none
  quotes the publisher's Greek.

## Still uncertain

- 17:21: joined ἵνα + ὦσιν vs. the publisher's ἵνα = May / ὦσιν = be. Both
  are accurate.
- 17:11 "are one" `t`: ἕν could also link to one#0 + one#1 as understood
  again (as ἐστιν does in 17:10). The current `t` is not wrong.

## Coordinator adjustment at registration

- 17:11: ἕν now links to both "one"s (understood again in καθὼς ἡμεῖς, the
  rule used in John 15:2 and 15:4), and only the supplied copula "are" is
  `t` (as in John 14:10 and 15:5). This settles the open point above.
