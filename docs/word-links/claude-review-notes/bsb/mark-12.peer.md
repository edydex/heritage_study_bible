# BSB Mark 12: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 44 of 44 verses (12:1-44).

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 44, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB,
including the `#n` anchors of repeated words (the, to, and, he, they, will,
God, of, Him). Textual claims were checked against the publisher-row counts
in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/mark.json`. Conventions
were compared with the reviewed parallels in Matthew 21:33-46, 22:15-46 and
23:5-7, and with earlier reviewed chapters of Mark.

## Changes (2 verses)

- 12:28: εἰδώς `s` + "Noticing" `t` → εἰδώς = "Noticing", with the note
  "N1904 has εἰδώς ("knowing, perceiving") where BSB's Greek reads ἰδών
  ("seeing"); Noticing fits both." Why: the counts confirm that BSB's
  Greek differs by two words here (συζητούντων and ἰδών). But εἰδὼς ὅτι
  ("perceiving that") and ἰδὼν ὅτι ("seeing that") say the same thing in
  this clause: the scribe saw that Jesus had answered well. "Noticing"
  renders either one. Under rule 9, a different word with the same meaning
  is linked with a note, as in Mark 6:39. Before the change, the two halves
  of one rendering were split into `s` and `t`.
- 12:19: τήν = "his brother’s", γυναῖκα = "widow" → τὴν γυναῖκα = "his
  brother’s widow" ("the wife" already mentioned, spelled out as whose wife
  she was). Why: the article cannot carry the noun "brother’s"; that
  over-claims, like the lone-article rows the author corrected elsewhere in
  this chapter. But the anaphoric τήν is what tells the reader whose wife
  she is, so making "his brother’s" `t` with τήν `s` would leave a split
  pair. Joining the article with its noun keeps every English word linked,
  and a tap on "brother’s" now shows τὴν γυναῖκα, "the wife". ὁ ἀδελφὸς
  αὐτοῦ = "the man" is unchanged and correct: it is the same person, the
  surviving brother.

Nothing else was changed. The author's corrections of the publisher rows
are right. These include καί = "He sent" removed in 12:5, the negatives
taken out of the verbs, the copulas and names made `t`, ἔσχον … γυναῖκα =
"were married" (12:23), ἐκ νεκρῶν = "the dead" (12:25), πολύ = "badly"
(12:27), the proleptic αὐτόν = "the man" (12:34) and ἀποκριθεὶς … ἔλεγεν =
"He asked" (12:35).

## Textual claims

- 12:9 `t then`: **correct.** N1904 has 16 words, all 16 matched, against
  17 publisher words. The one extra word fits τί οὖν, and the publisher's
  "then" is the one English word with no transferred row.
- 12:26 `t the#4`, `t the#5`: **correct.** N1904 has 31 words, all matched,
  against 33 publisher words. The two unlinked English words are exactly the
  "the" before "God of Isaac" and before "God of Jacob". That fits ὁ θεὸς
  Ἰσαὰκ and ὁ θεὸς Ἰακώβ.
- 12:28: N1904 has 20 words, 18 matched, against 20 publisher words. That
  is two changed words. They are the spelling of συνζητούντων (linked as the
  same verb) and εἰδώς/ἰδών (now linked, see above).
- 12:33: the Greek is identical in count (32/32/32). Only one English word
  differs, so no reading is claimed, and none should be.
- 12:20 "Now", 12:23 "then", 12:32 "God", 12:41 "Jesus": these are `t`
  without a reading claim. That is correct, because the publisher's Greek
  equals N1904 in those verses. Other editions add οὖν (12:20, 12:23), θεός
  (12:32) and ὁ Ἰησοῦς (12:41), but this publisher does not. 12:23's
  ὅταν ἀναστῶσιν is `s` as a BSB omission, not as a reading, which is also
  right.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 12:19, "his brother’s": **changed** (see above). This joins τήν with
  γυναῖκα rather than using a `t`.
- 12:5, second "He sent" on the one ἀπέστειλεν: **agree.** πολλοὺς ἄλλους
  continues the object of the same verb, and English repeats it. This is the
  reviewed practice for a verb that is understood again: Matthew 19:24
  εἰσελθεῖν, 19:30 ἔσονται, Mark 6 ἦν. The publisher's καί = "He sent" was
  wrong, and the author's fix is right.
- 12:13 "Later" and 12:22 "In this way" on καί: **agree, kept.** In both
  verses the narrative καί is the only connective, and BSB renders it with a
  transition: "Later" for the move to a new scene, and "In this way" for the
  consecutive "and so" that sums up the series. This is the same treatment
  as "At this" (12:12, Mark 1:26, Mark 3:6). A `t` would claim that BSB adds
  a connective where the Greek has one.
- 12:21, καί = "but" with "also" `t`: **agree.** The καί joins "married"
  and "died", which is the contrast BSB writes as "but". "also" compares
  this brother with the first one, and N1904 has no word for that. The
  publisher's Greek equals N1904 here, so no reading lies behind it.
- 12:28, εἰδώς/"Noticing": **changed** to a link (see above).
- 12:41, πῶς … βάλλει = "putting": **agree.** "watched the crowd putting"
  turns the indirect question "how the crowd puts" into one participle.
  Splitting off πῶς as `s` would also be defensible. The joined group is not
  wrong.

## Also checked and kept

- 12:12 and 12:43: the opening καί is `s` and the participle "and" is `t`.
  This is not a split pair under this project's convention. The same pattern
  is used in Mark 1:20, 7:14 (καὶ προσκαλεσάμενος … ἔλεγεν, as in 12:43),
  8:12, Matthew 21:39 and many other verses.
- One Greek word rendered more than once: αὐτόν in 12:3 and 12:8 follows
  Matthew 21:39. κἀκεῖνον = "and" + "him" + "him" (12:4) and Τίνος = "Whose"
  + "whose" (12:16, as Matthew 22:20) are also kept.
- Articles rendered as possessives or demonstratives: τῆς/τόν = "your"
  (12:33), τάς = "their" (12:40), τήν = "this" (12:12), τούς = "those"
  (12:9).
- 12:14 follows Matthew 22:16: οὐ … οὐδενός = "no one", and μέλει σοι =
  "seek favor". οὐ βλέπεις εἰς πρόσωπον ἀνθρώπων = "You are impartial" is
  kept as one idiom.
- No note mentions Russian.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 12:28 ὅτι καλῶς = "how well" is one group. Splitting it (ὅτι = "how",
  καλῶς = "well") would be finer, but the joined group does not mislead.
- 12:2 τῷ is `s`, and only καιρῷ = "At harvest time". Matthew 21:34 links
  its whole phrase to "harvest time". Both are defensible.
- 12:7: "the" sits on οἱ, and ἐκεῖνοι ("those") is `s`. Putting "the" on
  ἐκεῖνοι would also be defensible.
