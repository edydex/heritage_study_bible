# BSB Acts 20: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 38 of 38 verses (20:1-38), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 38, "problems": 0}` (also `check
--target BSB`).

Changes: 4 verses (20:6, 20:13, 20:35 links; 20:23 one note). The other 34
verses are kept exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 20:6 μετὰ τὰς ἡμέρας τῶν ἀζύμων ("after the Feast of Unleavened Bread"):
  before τάς = "the", ἡμέρας = "Feast", τῶν `s`, ἀζύμων = "of Unleavened
  Bread" → after τῶν = "the", ἀζύμων = "Feast of Unleavened Bread" (note
  "as in 12:3"), τὰς ἡμέρας `s` ("BSB names the festival the Feast of
  Unleavened Bread, so τὰς ἡμέρας has no separate English word"). ἡμέραι
  means "days", not "Feast"; the reviewed Acts 12:3 puts the whole name on
  ἀζύμων with its article τῶν = "the", as Mark 14:1 (τὰ ἄζυμα), Mark 14:12
  and Matthew 26:17 do. "after" stays on μετά alone (μετά already means
  "after"; unlike 12:3 ἦσαν … ἡμέραι = "during", no English word here
  carries the "days").
- 20:13 μέλλων αὐτὸς πεζεύειν ("because he was going there on foot"):
  before μέλλων = "because" + "was going", πεζεύειν = "on foot" → after
  μέλλων = "because" + "was", πεζεύειν = "going" + "on foot". μέλλων gives
  the intention ("was …", as μέλλοντι = "was about" in 20:3 and μέλλων =
  "was ready" in 20:7); the travel verb "going" renders πεζεύειν ("to travel
  on foot"), which otherwise had only its manner linked. ἦν joined with
  διατεταγμένος = "He had arranged", αὐτός = "he" and "there" `t` are
  confirmed.
- 20:35 δεῖ: before "we must" → after δεῖ = "must", "we" `t` ("English
  supplies a subject; δεῖ is impersonal"). Same as the reviewed Acts 5:29
  ("We must obey God": δεῖ = must, "We" `t`). δεῖ is third-person
  impersonal; the implied subject of the infinitive (κοπιῶντας) is not a
  first-person word.
- 20:23 note on πλήν only: before "πλὴν ὅτι ('except that') is rendered only
  … that" → after "πλήν ('except') is rendered only". ὅτι is linked
  separately to "that", so the old note claimed a pair the row does not
  hold. Links unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- **20:6 "Feast" on ἡμέρας**: changed (see above), consistent with 12:3.
- **20:21 τε = "alike"**: confirmed. The reviewed Acts 18:4 has exactly
  this rendering (τε of τε … καί = "alike"), and 2:11, 5:14 and 10:39 link τε
  to "both". "alike" renders the "both" of the correlative, so `s` + `t`
  would be a split pair. **"Christ" `t`**: confirmed by EVIDENCE (16 of 16
  N1904 words matched, 17 publisher words: one extra publisher word), and
  the note does not name the word.
- **20:24 "complete" and the second "the ministry"**: confirmed. τελειώσω
  governs both τὸν δρόμον and τὴν διακονίαν; BSB writes the verb out again
  ("complete") for the second object, with the same role (verb of that
  object) and the same meaning (τελειόω = finish, complete), so it is the
  understood-again pattern (John 15:2, 15:4), not added English. The
  second "the ministry" is an appositive naming the same ministry (same
  referent, same slot) to carry the epexegetical infinitive; linking it to
  τήν/διακονίαν follows John 15:2 κλῆμα. **Form note**: EVIDENCE shows 1
  N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904, and the publisher
  row leaves τελειώσω and "I may finish" unpaired, so a form difference on
  this verb is supported. The note does not state the publisher's form.
- **20:15 day words vs 20:7 μιᾷ**: both confirmed as applied in their
  precedents. ἐπιούσῃ, ἑτέρᾳ, ἐχομένῃ = "next day" / "day after that" /
  "following day" as Acts 16:11 (ἐπιούσῃ = following day), with "on" moved
  from τῇ to ἐχομένῃ (lone-article rule; John 20:1 and Luke 24:1 likewise
  put the dative-of-time "On" on the noun-word). 20:7 μιᾷ = "first", "day"
  `t`, exactly as John 20:1, Mark 16:2 and Luke 24:1 (the numeral "one"
  implies ἡμέρᾳ). Here Ἐν supplies "On", so μιᾷ carries only "first".
- **20:13**: see Changes.
- **20:32 Κυρίῳ `s`, "to God" `t`**: confirmed. EVIDENCE: 1 N1904 word
  unmatched, 1 publisher word not in N1904; the publisher row leaves Κυρίῳ
  unlinked and "God" unowned. Lord and God differ in meaning, so rule 9
  forbids a link. τῷ `s` is right: its English "to" belongs to the noun
  BSB reads, not to the lone article.
- **20:22 "to me" form note**: confirmed. EVIDENCE: 1 unmatched, 1 extra;
  the publisher row leaves ἐμοί and "to me" unpaired, consistent with a
  different form of the same pronoun (same meaning, so linked with a note).
  The note does not state the form.
- **20:37 ἱκανός = "openly"**: kept (publisher row). "openly" is BSB's only
  word for the degree of the weeping that ἱκανός ("considerable") states; it
  is a shifted rendering in the same slot, not a different idea.
- **20:19 καί = "especially"**: kept. BSB uses "especially" in the place
  of the καί to join the third item to the list, as the reviewed Acts 6:9
  links καί = "including".
- **20:34 "of mine" `t`, αἱ `s`**: kept. With the demonstrative (αἱ χεῖρες
  αὗται) the article is grammatically required, so it is not a possessive
  article; "of mine" is English addition.
- **20:38 ᾧ εἰρήκει = "his"**: kept. The possessive comes from "which he
  had spoken"; τῷ is the antecedent's article, not a possessive.
- **20:10 "still" `t`, ἐν αὐτῷ `s`**: kept. "still" does not mean "in him";
  it is BSB's inference, so this is not a split pair.
- **20:18 ἐγενόμην linked twice**: kept. "I was with you" is the literal
  sense of μεθ’ ὑμῶν … ἐγενόμην and "how I lived" renders πῶς … ἐγενόμην, so
  both English verbs render the one Greek verb; "was" is not a supplied
  copula here.
- **Participle subjects (20:1 "he said goodbye to", 20:14 "we took",
  20:37 "they embraced")**: kept with the participle BSB turns into a finite
  verb, as the reviewed Acts 9:17, 9:40 and 14:19 do.

## Still uncertain

- 20:25 πάντες = "none of" and οὐκέτι = "again" (publisher rows, with
  notes): the negative of "none" comes from οὐκέτι. Kept, because joining
  them would lose the separate "all" and "no longer".
- 20:11 "went back upstairs" and 20:14 "went on" keep "back" / "on" inside
  the verb phrase as idiomatic English; a stricter reading of "added
  adverb" could make "back" `t`.
