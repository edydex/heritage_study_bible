# BSB Luke 5: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 39 of 39 verses (5:1-39). I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line
against N1904, the displayed BSB, the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json` and the reviewed
parallels Mark 1:40-45 and 2:1-22 and Matthew 8:2-4 and 9:1-17. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 39, "problems": 0}`.

Reading claims: the publisher record has a `partial` entry for 5:2, 5:6,
5:7, 5:9, 5:10, 5:18, 5:27, 5:29 and 5:33 only. I mapped the publisher's
groups onto N1904 to see which English words are left unmapped:

- 5:2 "fishermen", 5:6 "began to tear", 5:7 "they came", 5:10 "John",
  5:27 "Levi", 5:29 "Levi" and 5:33 "John’s". Each matches one unmatched
  N1904 word out of an equal word count. That fits the author's "spelled
  differently" / "different form" notes. None of these notes states the
  publisher's spelling.
- 5:18 "him" (after "set") is the only unmapped English word. All 18 N1904
  words are matched, and the publisher has 19 words. So the publisher linked
  this "him" to its extra word, and the author's `t him#1` with a reading
  reason is right.
- 5:9: every English word maps onto N1904. The one differing publisher word
  has no English, and no link depends on it, so the author correctly
  claims no reading there.

## Changes (2 verses: 1 link change, 1 note-only)

- 5:6: πολύ "large" → "such + large". δέ "such + that" → "that", with the
  note "BSB turns the δέ clause into a result clause". δέ is a plain
  connective with no sense of degree. "such" intensifies the size that πολύ
  ("great") expresses, and the publisher's own row put it on πολύ. Only "a"
  had to move, to πλῆθος. This differs from 5:7, where ὥστε itself means
  "so that", so "so + that" stays on ὥστε.
- 5:17: the note on ἐκ = "from#0 + from#1" said the one ἐκ "governs both
  places". It governs the whole list (every village of Galilee, Judea and
  Jerusalem), so the note now says "governs the whole list of places". The
  link is unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 5:1 ἐν τῷ = "with", καί (14) = "while": kept. ἐν τῷ + infinitive is the
  crowd clause, which BSB renders "with the crowd pressing in on Him". The
  καί joins that clause to Jesus' standing, and BSB subordinates the
  standing with "while". This matches the convention that a subordinator
  BSB creates between καί-joined clauses goes on the καί. The publisher's ἐν
  = "while" would point "while" at the wrong clause. Its τόν = "with the"
  also put a preposition on a lone article.
- 5:14 καθώς `s` + `t the offering`: kept. No Greek word in Luke renders
  "the offering". προσένεγκε is the bare verb "offer" with no object, so
  "the offering" is a supplied object, like "Him" (5:15) and "the sick"
  (5:17). καθώς means "just as". Unlike Mark 1:44's relative ἅ ("what [Moses
  prescribed]"), it is not a pronoun for the thing offered, so linking "the
  offering" to it would be lexically wrong. Since `t the offering` is not
  καθώς's rendering, the two lines are not a split pair. The publisher's
  Greek equals N1904 in 5:14, so no ἅ reading can be claimed.
- 5:14 τῷ = "the", ἱερεῖ = "to + priest": kept. A lone article cannot carry
  "to", and the dative supplies it, as in 5:7 (τοῖς = their, μετόχοις = to
  + partners). The parallels differ: Mark 1:44 puts "to the" on τῷ, and
  Matthew 8:4 joins τῷ ἱερεῖ. A later pass may want to harmonize Mark 1:44.
- 5:31 οἱ ὑγιαίνοντες / οἱ κακῶς ἔχοντες joined: kept (rule 3, article +
  participle). The "who" of the cleft stays on χρείαν ἔχουσιν, and `t It
  is` stays, exactly as in Mark 2:17 and Matthew 9:12.
- 5:13: the opening καί is `s`, "and" is `t`, and τήν = "His": kept. This
  follows the current convention ("and" joining a participle to the main
  verb is `t`) and the chapter's possessive-article rows. Matthew 8:3 is
  older and follows a different scheme.
- 5:3 ἓν τῶν = "the": kept. A tap on "the" then shows "one of the", which
  explains BSB's definite "the boat". Making ἕν `s` would also be
  acceptable, but changing it would only be taste.
- 5:10 οἳ ἦσαν κοινωνοί = "partners" with `t were`: kept. James and John
  are accusative objects of περιέσχεν in 5:9. The English "so were" stands
  for "were astonished", not for ἦσαν. ἦσαν belongs to the relative clause
  that BSB recasts as "his partners", so joining it into "partners" is
  right. It is the same treatment as ὃς ἦν παραλελυμένος = "paralyzed"
  (5:18).
- 5:12 καὶ ἰδού = "came along": kept (presentational). It matches 5:18
  καὶ ἰδού = "Just then".

## Other points checked and kept

- 5:16 ἦν ὑποχωρῶν = "frequently withdrew": kept joined. The iterative
  sense comes from the periphrastic as a whole. The publisher's ἦν =
  "frequently" gave the auxiliary an adverb's meaning.
- 5:34: same as Mark 2:19. τούς = "the", υἱοὺς τοῦ νυμφῶνος = "guests of
  the bridegroom" (idiom), Μή + δύνασθε = "Can you", ἐν ᾧ = "while", ὁ
  νυμφίος = "He".
- 5:22 `t these things` (Luke has no ταῦτα, unlike Mark 2:8) and 5:17
  `t the sick` (αὐτόν is the infinitive's subject, and the publisher's
  Greek equals N1904 here).
- 5:17: ἦσαν ἐληλυθότες = "had come" is joined, but ἦσαν καθήμενοι =
  "were" + "sitting" is split. The difference follows the English: ἦσαν
  maps onto "were" but not onto "had".
- 5:39 χρηστός = "better" (publisher Greek = N1904, so no comparative
  reading is claimed).

## Still uncertain

- 5:6 "such" could also be `t` (English sets up the result clause). I chose
  πολύ because the publisher row and the meaning both point there. It
  should not stay on δέ.
- 5:7 "full" is `t`. It could instead join ἔπλησαν ("filled … full"),
  because it repeats that verb's meaning. Either is defensible.
- 5:18 φέροντες = "came carrying": "came" may be read as BSB's supplied
  main verb, not as part of φέρω ("bring"). I kept the publisher row with
  the author's note.
