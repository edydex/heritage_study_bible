# BSB Acts 8: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 39 of 39 verses (8:1-36, 8:38-40). 8:37 is in neither N1904 nor
BSB's main text; it has no block and none was added. There are no KEEP
blocks. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 39, "problems": 0}`.

I made 4 changes in 4 verses: 2 change links and 2 only reword a note. I
confirmed everything else in the author's file, including every reading
claim.

## Changes (verse: before → after, why)

- 8:36: κωλύει = "is there to prevent" → κωλύει = "to prevent", with "is
  there" `t`. τί κωλύει με means "what prevents me". "What is there to …"
  is an English frame around the verb, like the "here is" that the author
  already made `t` in "Look, here is water". Both frames are now treated
  the same way. This follows the reviewed rule for an English existential
  frame whose verb meaning sits in another word (Mark 5:4, "there was" `t`
  where ἴσχυεν = "with the strength").
- 8:39: γάρ `s` + "but" `t` → γάρ = "but", with the note "BSB renders γάρ
  ("for") here as but". This restores the publisher row. γάρ is the only
  connective in the clause, and BSB's "but" stands where it stands. It is
  BSB's rendering of a loosely used γάρ, not a different Greek word.
  Reviewed chapters link γάρ to the English connective BSB gives it (Luke
  20:40 "And", John 19:31 "and", Mark 16:4 "even though", Mark 8:37 "Or").
  `s` + `t` made a split pair. That is right only when the English word
  means something unrelated (e.g. "and" for ὅταν), not when BSB recasts the
  same connective.
- 8:1: note only, on ἦν = "was there". The old note did not say why "there"
  belongs to ἦν. It now reads: "BSB takes ἦν as "was present" (was there)
  rather than as part of a periphrastic "was approving"; συνευδοκῶν is
  rendered giving approval."
- 8:29: note only, on ἅρματι = "to + chariot + it". The old note called
  ἅρματι only the object of κολλήθητι, which did not explain why "to" (with
  "Go over") sits on it. It now says that the dative, the complement of
  κολλήθητι, is understood with Πρόσελθε too. (προσέρχομαι also takes a
  dative.) BSB names the chariot there and repeats it as "it".

## Points I was asked to settle

- **8:1 (ἦν = "was there")**: kept. BSB does not render ἦν συνευδοκῶν as a
  periphrastic "was approving". It reads ἦν as "was present" (a normal sense
  of εἰμί) and gives the participle its own phrase. "There" is part of that
  rendering of ἦν. It is not an added place word. A reviewed row does the
  same: Luke 5:29 ἦν = "was there" (also John 1:35 εἱστήκει = "was there").
  The reviewed "there" `t` rows in John, Luke and Mark are all "standing
  there", "sitting there" or "lying there". In those, the verb's meaning is
  already given by the participle, so "there" is extra. That is not the case
  here. I reworded the note (above).
- **8:36 "is there to prevent"**: changed to `t` (above).
- **8:36 καί = "and" in "and came"**: confirmed. BSB pulls "came to some
  water" into the "As" clause and moves the καί into that clause. This is
  the same as Luke 9:18 (reviewed): "BSB subordinates "were with Him" and
  moves the καί "and" into the "as" clause". 8:30 is different. There the
  only "and" ("ran up and heard") joins the participle προσδραμών to the
  main verb, so the participle convention makes it `t`. The καί before
  εἶπεν is `s`, because BSB turns "and said" into "Philip asked" after the
  quotation. In 8:36 no participle is involved: the "and" joins two finite
  verbs of the Greek.
- **8:29**: confirmed (note reworded). τούτῳ = "that" (a demonstrative
  rendered with English distance) owns only "that". "To" no longer sits on
  the demonstrative, as it did in the publisher row. "Stay by" is on
  κολλήθητι. "It" is on ἅρματι, as the object of κολλήθητι, which is the
  Greek word's own position. Every English word in the quotation has one
  owner. 8:3 "them" `t` is consistent with this. There the pronoun would
  have to stand for two separately linked nouns (ἄνδρας, γυναῖκας), so it
  cannot be linked.
- **8:34 "is" on λέγει**: confirmed. "is … talking" renders the present
  λέγει. ὁ keeps only "the". τοῦτο and the second and third περί are
  rightly `s`.
- **8:39 γάρ**: changed back to γάρ = "but" (above).
- **Readings**: every claim matches acts.json. The partial records are 8:1,
  8:5, 8:6, 8:9, 8:11, 8:14, 8:25, 8:28, 8:32, 8:33 and 8:40.
  - Spelling, each 1 word unmatched (2 in 8:14): Samaria (8:1, 8:5, 8:9,
    8:14), John (8:14), Samaritan (8:25), sorcery (8:11), Caesarea (8:40).
  - 8:32 "shearer": 1 unmatched, which is the participle. It is linked as
    "same verb, in another form".
  - 8:28 "and" on δέ: 1 unmatched and 1 extra. The publisher row leaves
    "and" unlinked, which fits a different connective with the same sense.
    It is linked with the soft same-sense note (rule 9).
  - 8:33: 0 unmatched and 1 extra (21 publisher words against 20). The
    publisher row leaves the first "His" unlinked and links αὐτοῦ (after
    κρίσις) to "He". So "In His#0 humiliation" `t`, with the reading
    reason, and αὐτοῦ = "He" are both right.
  - 8:6: 0/0 Greek (only English counts differ), so rightly no reading is
    claimed there.

  No note states the publisher's exact Greek. The notes say only "spelled
  differently", "another form", "differs slightly … same sense" or "a
  reading with an extra word".
- **Isaiah 53:7-8 in 8:32-33**: confirmed word by word. Ὡς/ὡς = like/as;
  πρόβατον = "a sheep"; ἐπὶ σφαγήν = to + "the slaughter"; ἤχθη = "He was
  led". ἀμνός = "a lamb"; τοῦ κείροντος = "the shearer"; αὐτόν `s`; ἄφωνος
  = "silent", with the supplied "is" `t`. οὕτως = so; οὐκ = not; ἀνοίγει =
  "He did + open"; τό `s`; στόμα αὐτοῦ = His mouth. In 8:33: Ἐν = In,
  ταπεινώσει = humiliation, κρίσις = "of justice", ἤρθη = "was deprived",
  τὴν γενεὰν αὐτοῦ = His descendants, τίς διηγήσεται = Who can recount, ὅτι
  = For, αἴρεται = was removed, ἀπὸ τῆς γῆς = from the earth, ἡ ζωὴ αὐτοῦ =
  His life.
- **Brief rules**:
  - Supplied copulas are `t`: 8:32 "is", 8:36 "here is" and now "is there".
    8:23 "are" is linked to the real participle ὄντα.
  - Supplied names for a verb ending are `t`: 8:16 "the Holy Spirit", 8:17
    "Peter and John", 8:32 "The eunuch", 8:38 the second "Philip".
  - Greek pronouns or articles rendered by a name or noun are linked: 8:1
    αὐτοῦ = Stephen's, 8:25 Οἱ … διαμαρτυράμενοι = "Peter and John had
    testified" (as Acts 5:41 Οἱ = "The apostles"), 8:30 αὐτοῦ = "the man",
    8:33 αὐτοῦ = He.
  - Words understood again link to the stated word: 8:3 οἴκους = house +
    house, 8:10 δύναμις = power + Power, 8:29 ἅρματι = chariot + it, 8:30
    Φίλιππος = Philip + Philip. In 8:30 ὁ Φίλιππος is the stated subject of
    both ἤκουσεν and εἶπεν. In 8:38 Φίλιππος is in apposition to the plural
    subject of κατέβησαν, not the subject of ἐβάπτισεν, so `t` is right
    there.
  - Lone articles: I scanned by script. No group of articles alone carries
    more than "the", except the article as possessive (8:11 ταῖς = his,
    8:17 τάς = their, 8:19 τάς = my) and as pronoun (8:31 ὁ = he). Every
    "of/to/in/for" sits on a noun or verb.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:1 "was there": kept (above).
- 8:36 "is there to prevent": changed to `t` (above).
- 8:36 καί = "and": kept (Luke 9:18).
- 8:3 τε `s` and "and#1" `t`: kept. τε joins the σύρων … παρεδίδου clause to
  ἐλυμαίνετο … εἰσπορευόμενος. BSB breaks the sentence before "Going", so
  that join has no English word. The "and" before "put" only joins the
  participle σύρων to the main verb, so the convention applies.
- 8:27 ἀναστάς = "he started out", ἐπορεύθη = "on his way": kept. Each
  English phrase renders its own Greek word ("rising" = setting out, "went"
  = on his way). Joining them would leave "on his way" with no owner for no
  gain.
- 8:10 πάντες = "all the people": kept. It is substantival masculine plural
  "all [people]", and "the people" only makes that explicit.

## Still uncertain

- 8:35 καί = "and" (the publisher row, kept by the author): the καί joins
  the two participles ἀνοίξας and ἀρξάμενος, but BSB drops the first. Its
  "and" now joins "began" (ἀρξάμενος) to "told" (εὐηγγελίσατο). That looks
  like the participle "and" that the convention makes `t` (as in 8:30). On
  the other hand, Matthew 8:33 (reviewed) keeps a καί that stands before a
  participle on the "and" BSB moves to the finite verb. I left the
  publisher row as it is. The stricter option is καί `s` and "and" `t`.
- 8:21 τῷ … τούτῳ = "our" ("this matter" → "our ministry"): kept as BSB's
  rendering of the determiner. The alternative is τούτῳ `s` with "our" on τῷ
  alone.
- 8:22 "He will forgive" for the passive ἀφεθήσεται: "He" is the implied
  agent and was kept in the verb group, like other reviewed passive-to-
  active rows (e.g. "would stone them"). A stricter reading would make "He"
  `t`.
