## Publisher rows corrected

"We" off participles (the brief and Acts 16:11, 21:1-10): 27:2 "We
boarded", 27:5 "we had sailed across", 27:7 "we arrived", 27:8 "we had moved
along the coast". The participle keeps its verb; this first "we" is `t`
because the finite verb's ending already gives the later we. 27:15 "So we
gave way": we moved to the ending of ἐφερόμεθα, ἐπιδόντες = gave way. 27:20
"we abandoned": we = ἡμᾶς (the pronoun BSB moves from being saved to
abandoned), περιῃρεῖτο = abandoned, τοῦ σῴζεσθαι = of being saved (ἡμᾶς had
"of"). 27:37 "of us" moved from ψυχαί to the ending of ἤμεθα (were … of us),
ψυχαί `s`.

Participle "and" (no καί in the Greek; now `t`): 27:2 and we put out, 27:3
and receive, 27:6 and put, 27:13 and sailed along, 27:15 and let ourselves,
27:21 and said, 27:24 and said (λέγων continues 27:23), 27:28 and found,
27:29 and prayed, 27:35 and began, 27:36 and took, 27:40 and made, 27:41 and
ran aground and and would not move, 27:43 and get. 27:17 "and" had been on
οὕτως: the "and" is `t`, οὕτως `s`. 27:35 the first "and" moved from
εὐχαρίστησεν to the καί inside the participle chain (εἴπας … καὶ λαβών), as
Acts 14:19. 27:36 the καί of καὶ αὐτοί means "also": joined to αὐτοί =
themselves, "and" `t`.

"When/After/Since" moved from δέ/τε to the participle or genitive absolute:
27:5 when (τε keeps And), 27:7 After (δέ) and When (had been on τοῦ), 27:8
After (τε), 27:12 Since (δέ), 27:13 When (δέ), 27:20 When (δέ), 27:21
After (τε), 27:35 After (δέ), 27:38 After (δέ).

Supplied words that had been folded into a Greek word (now `t`):

- Subjects: 27:17 the crew (ἐχρῶντο), 27:18 the men (ἐποιοῦντο), 27:21
  the men (ὑπαρχούσης), 27:35 Paul (λαβών), 27:38 the men (κορεσθέντες),
  27:25 He (passive λελάληται = told), 27:21 you twice (impersonal Ἔδει
  and the infinitive κερδῆσαι), 27:13 they#1 (infinitive κεκρατηκέναι).
- Objects: 27:4 us (ἐναντίους), 27:9 them (advised), 27:20 us (continued
  to batter), 27:35 it (broke), 27:40 them (left).
- Other additions: 27:9 By now (had been on δέ) and So (had been on
  Παῦλος); 27:13 So; 27:31 But (no connective in the Greek); 27:10 and
  27:34 own (had been on τῶν and τῆς; as Acts 13 "own" `t`); 27:12 there,
  Phoenix was (new sentence for the apposition λιμένα) and both (had been on
  κατά); 27:14 it was (had been on οὐ, now = not); 27:18 so … that (that
  had been on τῇ); 27:19 overboard (had been on σκευήν); 27:24 the lives of
  (πάντας = all); 27:27 still; 27:28 that the water was; 27:33 Right (had
  been on δέ) and is; 27:37 there; 27:44 were to follow (no verb; the
  command of 27:43 continues); 27:3 their (care).
- "coast of" in 27:2 (along the coast of Asia) and 27:5 (off the coast of
  Cilicia) is `t` like "province of"; τήν = the, κατά = along/off.

Lone articles and prepositions (the article keeps "the", the preposition goes
on the noun): 27:10 τοῦ "to" → πλοίου = to ship (BSB names the ship first);
ψυχῶν = to + lives, ἡμῶν = our; 27:11 τῷ "by the" twice → κυβερνήτῃ = by +
pilot, ναυκλήρῳ = by + owner of the ship; 27:18 τῇ "that the" → the; 27:19
τῇ "On the" → τρίτῃ = On + third day; 27:22 τοῦ "of the" → πλοίου = of +
ship; 27:31 τῷ "to the" → ἑκατοντάρχῃ = to + centurion; 27:32 τῆς "to the"
→ σκάφης = to + lifeboat; 27:40 τῶν "that held the" → πηδαλίων = that held +
rudders; 27:40 τῇ πνεούσῃ joined = to the wind (article + participle).

- 27:39 οὐκ = not, ἐπεγίνωσκον = they did + recognize; "aground" moved

## Notable decisions

- Places and names all linked: Italy, Julius, Adramyttian, Asia,
  Aristarchus, Macedonian, Thessalonica, Sidon, Cyprus, Cilicia, Pamphylia,
  Myra, Lycia, Alexandrian, Cnidus, Crete, Salmone, Fair Havens, Lasea,
  Phoenix, Northeaster (Εὐρακύλων), Cauda, Syrtis, the Fast, Adriatic Sea,
  Caesar. Nautical words link to BSB's English as rendered (sounding,
  fathoms, anchors, sea anchor = σκεῦος, ropes = βοηθείαις, foresail,
  rudders, lifeboat).
- "They/he" on participles BSB makes finite are kept (27:13 they thought,
  they weighed anchor; 27:17 they lowered; 27:28 They took soundings; 27:29
  they dropped; 27:30 they let; 27:35 he had said, he broke; 27:40 they
  loosened, they hoisted), as Acts 21:16 and 21:32. Only we/us follow the
  stricter rule. A subject on an infinitive is `t` (27:13 they#1, 27:21
  you#1).
- Vocatives: 27:10 and 27:25 ἄνδρες = Men/men; 27:21 ὦ ἄνδρες joined =
  Men; 27:24 Παῦλε = Paul. No "O"/"You" in BSB.
- Pronouns BSB renames are linked: 27:8 αὐτήν = the coast, 27:14 αὐτῆς =
  the island, 27:17 ἥν = it, 27:27 αὐτοῖς = they, 27:43 αὐτούς = their.
- Connectives BSB renders with another connective are linked: 27:3 τε =
  and, 27:21 τε = Then, 27:22 καί = But, 27:30 δέ = Meanwhile, 27:32 τότε
  = So, 27:35 καί = Then, 27:40 καί = Then.
- `s` for words BSB does not render: 27:7 μόλις, 27:9 καί and the second
  ἤδη, 27:12 κατά twice (absorbed by facing), 27:17 οὕτως, 27:20 λοιπόν,
  27:21 τότε and μέν, 27:27 τινά, 27:29 που, 27:34 τοῦτο γὰρ … ὑπάρχει
  (BSB makes the clause a phrase), 27:41 μέν, 27:44 οὓς μὲν … οὓς, the
  second ἐπί and ἐγένετο.
- 27:4 and 27:7 "of Cyprus"/"of Crete" stay on the noun (publisher rows);
  27:16 "of" follows on νησίον for consistency.

## Doubts for the peer

- 27:9-10: "them" in "So Paul advised them" renders αὐτοῖς of 27:10
  (λέγων αὐτοῖς). This is BSB's restructuring, not a versification
  difference, so I kept "them" `t` and λέγων αὐτοῖς `s` rather than a
  cross-verse link.
- 27:9, 27:13, 27:15: the sentence δέ is `s` and BSB's later "So" `t`
  (inferential, added where BSB splits off the genitive absolute); the
  alternative is δέ = So.
- 27:2, 5, 7, 8: the first "we" is `t`; the alternative gives both we's to
  the finite verb's ending.
- 27:3 "him" linked to the dative participle πορευθέντι; the alternative is
  `t` (supplied object).
- 27:33 διατελεῖτε = your + constant; the alternative is "your" `t`.
- 27:37 ψυχαί `s` with "of us" on ἤμεθα; the alternative links ψυχαί to
  "of us".
- 27:2 and 27:5 "coast of" `t`; the alternative keeps the publisher's κατά
  = along/off the coast of.
