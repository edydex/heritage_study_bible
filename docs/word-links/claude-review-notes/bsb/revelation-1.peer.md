# BSB Revelation 1: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 20 of 20 verses (1:1-20). This chapter has no KEEP blocks.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 20, "problems": 0}`.

I started from the author's final file and exported every verse with
`totext --all`. I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against the Greek, the
BSB English and the publisher record. I also checked every lone article: none
carries "of", "to", "from" or "in". I found one wrong link and changed it. I
also changed two notes to make them clearer. Everything else stays as the
author left it.

## Changes

- 1:1 (link): ἀποστείλας "sending" → "by sending". διά "by" → `s` ("BSB
  makes the angel the one sent, so διά ("through") has no English word").
  In BSB's "He made it known by sending His angel", "by" introduces the
  gerund "sending". It marks how the revelation was made known. That is
  the job of the participle of means ἀποστείλας, not of διά ("through the
  angel"). BSB turns the angel from the channel into the one sent. Matthew
  11:2 (πέμψας διὰ τῶν μαθητῶν → "he sent his disciples") is the same
  recast, and there the reviewed file leaves διά `s`. "When/As/After"
  already goes with the participle, and an English "by" + gerund follows the
  same rule.
- 1:8 (note only): ὁ (9) "The article of Θεός is rendered by the the before
  Lord God" → "The article belongs to Θεός; BSB places its the before Lord
  God." The words "the the" were hard to read. The link is unchanged.
- 1:13 (note only): the gloss for περιεζωσμένον was ("wrapped around"). It
  is now ("girded"). The "around" sense in the English belongs to πρός. The
  old gloss suggested that the participle owned it.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the open points

- 1:13 περιεζωσμένον = "with", πρός = "around": **kept**. In BSB's "with a
  golden sash around His chest", "with … around" is the English way of
  saying "girded … at the chest". Only "with" carries the predication that
  the sash is worn, so it is an honest rendering of the participle. If the
  participle were `s` and "with" `t`, the pair would be split. Reviewed
  files link participles to "with" elsewhere: 1 Corinthians 11:4 ἔχων →
  "with", and 1 Peter 3:22 has "with + subject". πρός ("at") is the
  location word, and "around" renders it. One correction to the author
  note: Revelation 15:6 (not yet reviewed) reads "and girded with golden
  sashes around their chests". The publisher row there puts "girded with"
  on περιεζωσμένοι and "around" on περί. So 15:6 does not have a bare
  "with". It does support the same division: "with" goes with the
  participle and "around" with the preposition.
- 1:1 διά = "by": **changed** (see above).
- 1:6 καί `s`, ἐποίησεν = "who has made": **kept**. "who" stands for the
  subject ending of the finite verb ("and he made" → "who has made"). It
  does not mean "and", so it cannot sit on καί. The rule is that a person
  word goes on the finite verb. Reviewed precedents follow it: 1 John 5:16
  "who will give" and Acts 21:36 "that followed" sit on the finite verbs.
  This is consistent with 1:5. There the "who" of "To Him who loves" comes
  from the article Τῷ with the participle, and καί between the two
  participles is linked to the English "and". 1:6 has no English "and"
  for its καί.
- 1:14 λευκόν = "white#1", ὡς (11) = "as#0 + as#1": **kept**. The Greek has
  two λευκ- words and BSB has two "white". BSB's "as white as snow" renders
  λευκὸν ὡς χιών directly, applied to the hair rather than the wool. If
  λευκόν were `s`, it would be an unlinked word whose exact English
  rendering is printed beside it. The correlative "as … as" together
  renders the one ὡς.
- 1:9 "of#0 Patmos" `t`: **kept**. Πάτμῳ is dative in apposition, so "of"
  does not come from its case. The reviewed Acts 1:12 leaves τοῦ καλουμένου
  `s` when BSB drops "called". "of" is the English naming construction.
- "forever and ever": **confirmed**. Philippians 4:20 and 1 Timothy 1:17
  (registered) both have εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας = "forever" and τῶν αἰώνων = "and
  ever". 1:6 and 1:18 match this split. Some other registered files link the
  whole idiom as one group (Galatians 1:5, 2 Timothy 4:18, 1 Peter 5:11),
  but the split used here follows the precedents the author cited.
- 1:8 ὁ = "the", Κύριος = "Lord": **confirmed** against Luke 1:32 (Κύριος =
  "Lord", ὁ = "The"). The titles are handled the same way in 1:4 and 1:8.
  ὁ ὤν = "Him who is" in 1:4 and "who is" in 1:8. ὁ ἦν = "was" in both.
  ὁ ἐρχόμενος = "is to come" in both, as in John 6:14 ("who is to come").
  καί = "and" in both.
- 1:3 Μακάριος = "Blessed + blessed": **confirmed**. Greek gives the
  beatitude word once. BSB repeats it for the second subject, with the
  same role (predicate) and the same meaning, so this follows the
  understood-again rule (John 12:13). "is#0", "are" and "is#2" are `t`
  copulas. "what is written" on τὰ … γεγραμμένα matches 1 Corinthians
  15:54 ("that is written").
- 1:17 Μή = "not", φοβοῦ = "Do + be afraid", λέγων = "said", "and#0" `t`:
  **confirmed**. These follow the rules that negatives split off verbs and
  that a participle's "and" with no καί is `t`. νεκρός = "a dead man"
  matches Luke 7:12 and 7:15.
- Name spellings: **confirmed**. EVIDENCE shows a difference only in 1:1,
  1:4, 1:9 and 1:18 (1 word each) and in 1:11 (2 words). In each of these
  verses, the N1904 words the publisher record leaves unmatched are the
  names: John three times, Philadelphia and Laodicea, and Hades. The only
  unmatched English words are those same names. Each note says only "Same
  name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text". No other note in the
  chapter claims a different reading. 1:19 (0/0) has no reading note, and
  καί (4) = "and#0" is right.

## Still uncertain

- 1:13: a third option would join καί (10) with περιεζωσμένον as "with".
  BSB's ", with" replaces "and girded", much as Mark 1:6 puts "with" on καί.
  I kept the author's split, because καί itself means only "and".
- 1:16 δυνάμει = "brightest" and 1:7 ναί = "So shall it be" are free
  renderings kept from the publisher with notes. I judged them the same
  meaning in context (the sun at full strength; an affirmation), not words
  of different meaning.
- 1:20: the second αἱ (28) carries "the#5" and αἱ (26) is `s`. Linking the
  noun's own article would also be correct. I left the publisher's row as
  it is.
