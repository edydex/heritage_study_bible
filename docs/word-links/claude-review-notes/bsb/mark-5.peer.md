Checked: 43 of 43 verses (5:1–43, including the earlier reviewer's 5:5).

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 43, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB.
Textual claims were checked against the publisher rows and the `partial`
word counts in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/mark.json`.

## Changes (4 verses: 3 with link fixes, 1 note only)

- 5:15: καί (opening) = When, καί (middle) `s` → opening καί `s`, middle
  καί = When. Why: BSB's "When" turns the link between the two clauses
  ("they come … and they see") into subordination, and that link is the
  middle καί. The opening καί joins the verse to 5:14 and is unrendered,
  like every other opening καί in this chapter. Mark 1:37 (author and
  peer) puts "when" on the καί between the clauses in the same way.
- 5:38: the same change for "When" (opening καί `s`, the καί before θεωρεῖ
  = When). Also τοῦ = "of" and ἀρχισυναγώγου = "the synagogue leader" →
  τοῦ = "of the", ἀρχισυναγώγου = "synagogue leader". Why: rule 5; the
  article renders "the" (compare τῆς = "of the" in 5:1).
- 5:43: αὐτοῖς `s` + `t them` → αὐτοῖς = them. Why: this was a split pair.
  αὐτοῖς names the people Jesus gave orders to. BSB's "them" in "He told
  them" is the object of "told", the people given orders, which is the same
  role and the same people. BSB only moves the pronoun from "gave strict
  orders" to "told". Their also being the doers of "give" does not make
  "them" English-only, since N1904 has no other word for these people.
  δοθῆναι = "to give" now has a note that BSB makes the passive active.
- 5:5 (earlier reviewer's verse): links unchanged. The note on διὰ παντός +
  ἦν κράζων = "he kept crying out" was two unclear sentences ("through-all
  duration phrase", "copula/crying participle"). It is now one short
  sentence: διὰ παντός ("continually") with the periphrastic ἦν κράζων
  ("was crying out") is rendered "he kept crying out".

No other link was changed. The author's corrections of publisher rows are
right. They include: "When/As/After/While" moved from καί/δέ to the
participle or genitive absolute (5:6, 5:18, 5:21, 5:27, 5:35, 5:40), the
supplied subjects and objects moved to `t`, negatives taken out of verbs
(5:10, 5:19, 5:36, 5:37, 5:39), "He was met by" split into αὐτῷ = He
(5:2), the article no longer carrying "on the"/"by the"/"that" (5:11, 5:15,
5:30), and ἐκστάσει μεγάλῃ = utterly (5:42).

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 5:15, 5:38: **changed** (see above). The middle καί = When.
- 5:2, "As soon as" on εὐθύς alone: **agree.** "soon" comes from εὐθύς.
  Splitting the one English conjunction would be worse, and the genitive
  absolute keeps "got".
- 5:40, ὅπου ἦν = "to see": **agree.** BSB replaces "where [the child] was"
  with "to see [the child]". An `s` on ὅπου ἦν and a `t` on "to see" would be
  a split pair. The note says the rendering is free.
- 5:24, one "Him" for αὐτῷ and αὐτόν: **agree.** In "followed and pressed
  around Him" the one "Him" is the object of both verbs. This matches 5:14
  ("in the" for both εἰς … τήν/τούς) and Mark 1:5.
- 5:43: **changed** (see above).
- 5:4, διὰ τό = "Though", `t there was`, ἴσχυεν = "with the strength":
  **agree.** διὰ τό introduces the clause in the same place as "Though". The
  note says BSB recasts the causal clause as a concession. "there was" is
  BSB's existential frame.
- 5:36, τόν = "their", λόγον λαλούμενον = "conversation": **agree.** This is
  the article used as a possessive, as with τὰς χεῖρας = "Your hands" in 5:23.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 5:4: BSB's concession is really between "was bound" and "had broken". In
  Greek these are joined by καί (token 8). So καί = Though, with διὰ τό `s`
  ("BSB drops the causal link to 5:3"), is a defensible alternative. The
  publisher row and the author both chose διὰ τό. That choice is not wrong.
- 5:15: λεγιῶνα = "legion of demons". "of demons" has no word of its own. It
  explains the name and may echo δαιμονιζόμενον. It was kept with the noun
  (publisher row). `t of demons` is the stricter alternative.
- 5:30: ἐξ = "out from", ἐξελθοῦσαν = "had gone". "out" could go with the
  verb (ἐξ-ελθεῖν, "go out"), leaving ἐξ = "from". Both are accurate.
- 5:8: ἔλεγεν = "had already declared". "already" expresses BSB's reading
  of the explanatory γάρ + imperfect. It was kept with the verb.
