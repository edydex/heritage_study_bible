# BSB 1 Corinthians 13: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 13 of 13 verses (13:1-13). Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was checked
against the Greek, BSB's English and the publisher record for each verse in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/1-corinthians.json`. The
chapter has no KEEP blocks. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 13, "problems": 0}`. `totext` of the final JSON gives the
peer text back unchanged.

Changes: none. The peer JSON is byte-identical to the author's final file.
Each author correction of a publisher row was checked on its own merits
(below), and no remaining row was found wrong or over-claiming.

## Points checked in detail

- 13:3 reading. Only this verse has a `partial` record: 21 N1904 words, 20
  matched, 21 publisher words. So one N1904 word has no match and one
  publisher word is not in N1904. In the publisher rows, καυθήσομαι is the
  only content word with no link, and "exult" the only English content word
  with no link. "exult in" fits a "boast" reading, not "I will be burned".
  The meaning differs, so καυθήσομαι `s` and "exult in" `t`, both with the
  reading reason, is correct (rule 9). Neither note names or spells the
  publisher's word. "the#1 surrender" on παραδῶ is confirmed: "the" is the
  English article of the noun BSB makes from the verb. τό (10) belongs to
  σῶμα and is absorbed by "my". "of + body" on σῶμα, with μου = "my", is
  confirmed: "of" marks the body as the thing surrendered. "to the poor"
  `t` is confirmed. ψωμίζω has no recipient word, and a supplied object is
  `t`. The publisher row "possess to the poor" over-claimed. τὰ ὑπάρχοντα
  = "possess" as one unit, μου = "I#1", second ἐάν `s` and ἵνα `s` are
  confirmed. The ἵνα is in both readings, and BSB recasts the clause.
- 13:12. BSB reads "Now we see but a dim reflection as in a mirror; then
  we shall see face to face." δι’ = "in" with "as#0" `t` is confirmed. διά
  never means "as"; "as" only marks the image as a comparison, so the
  publisher's "as in" over-claimed. ἐν before αἰνίγματι `s` is confirmed
  (BSB makes "a dim reflection" the object of "see", as with ἐν in
  1 Corinthians 1:7). "but" (= "only") `t` is confirmed as an added adverb
  (1 Corinthians 8:6). βλέπομεν = "we#0 see + we#1 + see#1" with "shall#0"
  `t` follows the understood-again rule: the person goes with the repeated
  verb, as in 1 Corinthians 4:15 ("you#1 do + have#1"). The future the
  Greek clause lacks stays `t`, as with "will" in John 15:4. γάρ and both δέ
  `s`, "shall" on ἐπιγνώσομαι, and "even" = καί / "as#1" = καθώς are
  confirmed.
- 13:4-5. ἀγάπη is linked correctly: (1) "Love", (5) "love", (9) "it#0".
  N1904 punctuates οὐ ζηλοῖ, ἡ ἀγάπη οὐ περπερεύεται, so the displayed
  third ἡ ἀγάπη is the subject of περπερεύεται. "It" in "It does not envy"
  is ζηλοῖ's verb ending. The publisher's "It" = ἀγάπη contradicted the
  displayed comma, so the author's correction stands. Each "is" sits on the
  Greek verb it renders, not as a supplied copula: μακροθυμεῖ,
  χρηστεύεται, φυσιοῦται, ἀσχημονεῖ, ζητεῖ, παροξύνεται (2 Peter 3:9 "is
  patient"). ἑαυτῆς = "self", ζητεῖ = "it#0 is + seeking", τά `s` (no
  English noun for "the things"), "easily" `t` (added adverb) and every
  οὐ/οὐκ = "not"/"no" are confirmed.
- 13:6. "Love" `t` is confirmed. The Greek has only the verb ending, which
  supplies "it", not the noun. A noun subject that English supplies is `t`,
  like a supplied "Jesus". A pronoun from the ending stays on the verb, as
  "It bears" in 13:7. The lone-article fix (τῇ = "the", ἀληθείᾳ = "in#1 +
  truth") is confirmed.
- 13:8. The three "there are/there is" `t` are confirmed. The εἴτε clauses
  have no verb, so this is a supplied existence verb, not one that renders
  εἰμί/γίνομαι (Acts 14:8, Romans 5:13 do not apply). εἴτε = "where" stays
  linked: it is the same conditional connective, not a word of different
  meaning.
- 13:10-11. ἐκ μέρους = "partial" joined, τό = "the#1": confirmed. In
  τὰ τοῦ νηπίου, τά = "ways", τοῦ `s`, νηπίου = "childish". This applies the
  article-as-pronoun allowance the same way as the registered 1 Corinthians
  chapters: 2:11 "the thoughts", 2:14 "the things", 7:32 and 7:34 "the
  work", 7:33 and 7:34 "the affairs". In each of these τά carries BSB's
  noun, and the genitive noun carries its own English.
- 13:13. The list-final "and" `t` is confirmed. The Greek is asyndetic,
  as with the list "and" in 1 Thessalonians 5:14. "is" `t` is confirmed
  (supplied copula; τούτων = "of these" no longer carries it). Both ἀγάπη =
  "love", with τά and ἡ `s`, are confirmed.
- Lone articles: none carries "of/to/in the" (13:1 ταῖς, 13:6 τῇ fixed by
  the author; all other articles are "the" or `s`).
- Person and modal words: 13:2 "can" on εἰδῶ; "will/shall" on 13:8 and
  13:12 finite futures; no person on the participles ἠχῶν, ἀλαλάζον or
  τὰ ὑπάρχοντα. Added "only" (13:1) and "but" = only (13:12) are `t`.
- Notes: all are lexical or grammatical one-liners. None is interpretive,
  and none mentions Russian.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 13:3 "the#1" and "of" around "surrender": kept as linked. "the" belongs
  to the noun BSB makes from παραδῶ. "of" expresses the body's role as
  object and goes on σῶμα, the same way a case word goes on its noun
  elsewhere. "of" on παραδῶ would also be readable, but the current split
  is not wrong, so it is not changed for taste.
- 13:12 "as#0" `t` vs. the publisher's "as in" on δι’: `t` is right (see
  above). "as" adds a comparison that διά does not express.
- 13:5 τά `s` vs. joining τὰ ἑαυτῆς on "self": `s` kept. "self-" renders
  only the reflexive. The 1 Corinthians chapters link τά only when BSB has
  a noun for it ("thoughts", "work", "affairs", "ways"). Philippians 2:4
  joins τὰ ἑαυτῶν because BSB has "interests" there.
- 13:2 "the gift of prophecy" on προφητείαν: kept. In 1 Corinthians 12-14
  προφητεία can itself mean the gift of prophesying, so the expansion
  renders the one noun. This differs from Romans 12:6,
  where "one's gift is" is a frame BSB adds around a separate χαρίσματα
  and is `t`.

## Still uncertain

- 13:3: some lexicons gloss ψωμίζω as "give away (to feed the poor)". A
  reviewer could argue that "to the poor" is part of the verb's sense. I
  kept `t` because the Greek names no recipient and supplied objects are
  `t` in reviewed chapters.
- 13:4: if BSB's translators read οὐ ζηλοῖ ἡ ἀγάπη as one clause (other
  editions punctuate differently), their own alignment would put ἀγάπη on
  "It". The file follows the displayed N1904 punctuation, which is the
  Greek readers see.
