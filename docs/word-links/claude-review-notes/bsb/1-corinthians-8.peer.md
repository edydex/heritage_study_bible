# BSB 1 Corinthians 8: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 13 of 13 verses (8:1-13), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, starting
from the author's final file. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 13, "problems": 0}`; `totext` of the final JSON round-trips to
the peer text unchanged.

Changes: 4 link changes in 3 verses (8:5, 8:7, 8:10), plus 3 note-only
edits (8:2, 8:5, 8:10).

## Changes

- 8:5 πολλοί … πολλοί joined to "many" → first πολλοί (token 15) = "many";
  second πολλοί (token 18) `s` ("BSB's one many covers both nouns").
  Where the Greek says a word twice and BSB says it once, reviewed chapters
  link the first and leave the second `s` (Acts 10:4 σου, Acts 13:10,
  Acts 2:18, 1 Corinthians 2:3). "many" stands before "gods", where the
  first πολλοί stands.
- 8:5 note only: the `t` reason for the second "so-called" said the Greek
  "says only 'many gods'" (it also says "many lords"). It now says the
  Greek clause is complete without λεγόμενοι. The link is unchanged.
- 8:7 ἐν = "has" → ἐν `s` ("BSB recasts ἐν πᾶσιν 'in all' as everyone
  has"); "has" `t` ("English supplies a verb; the Greek clause has none").
  ἐν means "in", not "have". The clause has no verb, and a supplied "has"
  is `t` elsewhere (Luke 23:15, Romans 13:11, Hebrews 5:11 with a dative
  of possession). πᾶσιν = "everyone" stays.
- 8:10 αὐτοῦ = "with" → αὐτοῦ = "he"; "with" `t`; οἰκοδομηθήσεται
  "will he + be encouraged" → "will + be encouraged". BSB recasts "his
  conscience will be built up" as "someone with a weak conscience … will he
  … be encouraged". The English subject "he" is the person αὐτοῦ refers to,
  not the verb's Greek subject (the conscience). So "he" renders αὐτοῦ.
  This is the same shape as Acts 8:33, where αὐτοῦ ("his") is linked to
  "He" when BSB makes the person the subject. "with" only expresses
  English possession ("someone with …"). It does not mean "his", so it is
  `t`.
- 8:10 note only: the note on ἀσθενοῦς glossed it as "being weak" (that
  is ἀσθενοῦς ὄντος). It now says ἀσθενοῦς ("weak") agrees with αὐτοῦ, and
  that BSB applies "weak" to the conscience.
- 8:2 note only: "he knows" on the infinitive ἐγνωκέναι gets a note ("with
  the thinker as its subject"), worded as in 1 Corinthians 3:18 (εἶναι =
  "he is" under δοκεῖ). The link is unchanged: an infinitive under δοκεῖ
  carrying "he" follows 1 Corinthians 3:18 and Galatians 6:3. The
  participle "no person" rule does not touch it.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:5 two πολλοί → one "many": changed to first = "many", second `s`
  (precedent above). The second "so-called" stays `t`. λεγόμενοι belongs to
  the εἴπερ clause. The ὥσπερ clause has its own εἰσίν and its own
  complete predicate (θεοὶ πολλοὶ καὶ κύριοι πολλοί), so nothing is
  elided. The "words understood again" rule (John 15:2, 15:4, 12:13) covers
  Greek ellipsis. BSB's repeated "so-called" is an interpretive addition,
  not a repeat of an understood word.
- 8:6 τὰ πάντα = "all things": kept (Romans 11:36 joins τὰ πάντα the same
  way; τά `s` would also be defensible, so this is not changed for taste).
  The rest of 8:6 is confirmed. "there is", "but" (= "only", an added
  adverb), "came" and "exist" are `t` in both halves, because the Greek has
  no verbs (1 Timothy 2:5, Romans 11:36). ἡμῖν = "for us". The notes on
  αὐτόν/αὐτοῦ = "whom" are only grammatical.
- 8:7 ἐν = "has": changed (see above). "Some people", "accustomed" for
  συνηθείᾳ, "still" for ἕως ἄρτι, "to idols" for τοῦ εἰδώλου (τοῦ `s`) and
  "since + is" on οὖσα are confirmed. "are", "so", "that", "such food" and
  "it were" are confirmed `t` (supplied copula, result frame, supplied
  object, supplied subject and verb).
- 8:10 αὐτοῦ = "with": changed to αὐτοῦ = "he", "with" `t` (see above).
  ὄντος `s` is confirmed: "be" in "be encouraged" belongs to the future
  passive, not to ὄντος. "weak" on ἀσθενοῦς is confirmed, with its note
  corrected. "a + conscience" is confirmed (English "a" goes with the
  noun). τὸν ἔχοντα = "who are", γνῶσιν = "well informed" is kept.
- 8:13 ἀδελφόν μου = "him", τόν `s`: confirmed. BSB replaces the repeated
  "my brother" with a pronoun. Leaving the Greek `s` and "him" `t` would be
  a split pair. οὐ μή = "never" with εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα = "again" is confirmed.
  John 10:28 joins all of them into "never" only because BSB has no
  "again" there. "I" in "what I eat" stays `t` (Romans 14:15), and
  σκανδαλίζω carries "cause(s) + to stumble".

## Other points checked

- 8:1, 8:4, 8:10 εἰδωλόθυτον = "food sacrificed to idols", with the article
  `s`, as in Acts 15:29 and 21:25.
- Lone articles: 8:8 τῷ `s` with Θεῷ = "to God"; 8:9 τοῖς = "the" with
  ἀσθενέσιν = "to + weak"; 8:7 τοῦ `s`. In 8:12 τούς = "your" is a
  possessive article (allowed).
- Person and modal words: 8:2 "he#1" `t` for impersonal δεῖ. In 8:8, 8:9
  and 8:13 do/will sit on the finite verbs, with the negatives split off.
  In 8:12 "By" goes on the participle.
- 8:11 γάρ = "So": kept. Reviewed chapters consistently link γάρ to the
  connective BSB uses (But/And/Now/Yet).
- Readings. 8:10: EVIDENCE shows 1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher
  word not in N1904. In the publisher's own rows εἰδωλίῳ is the only
  N1904 token left without a link, and "an idol's temple" the only English
  phrase without one. So the note "Same word, spelled differently in BSB's
  Greek text" is supported and makes no claim about the publisher's
  spelling. 8:8: EVIDENCE shows 0 N1904 words unmatched and 1 extra
  publisher word. All 24 English words have a match. The file claims no
  reading there, which is correct.

## Still uncertain

- 8:10 τὸν ἔχοντα = "who are" / γνῶσιν = "well informed" could also be
  linked as τόν = "who", ἔχοντα γνῶσιν = "are well informed". Both are
  honest, so it is left as the author linked it.
- 8:5 second "so-called": a reader who takes Paul's ὥσπερ clause as still
  meaning "so-called" could argue for linking it to λεγόμενοι. I kept `t`
  because the Greek clause is complete.
