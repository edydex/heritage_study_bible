# BSB Revelation 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 11 of 11 verses (4:1-11). This chapter has no KEEP blocks.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 11, "problems": 0}`.

I started from the author's final file and exported every verse with
`totext --all`. I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against the Greek, the
BSB English and the publisher record. I also checked these points:

- No lone article carries "of", "to", "from" or "in".
- No supplied copula, "there", "that", "other" or "something" sits on a
  Greek word.
- No person word sits on a participle (καθημένους "sat", ἔχων "had",
  καιόμεναι "burned", λέγων "was saying").
- Every "a/an" goes with its noun.
- Revelation 1 is followed for the titles, for "forever and ever" and for
  ὁ before Θεός.

I changed one link and two notes. Everything else stays as the author left
it.

## Changes

- 4:8 (link): ἀνάπαυσιν "stop" and ἔχουσιν "they" → joined as ἀνάπαυσιν …
  ἔχουσιν = "they + stop". The note now reads: "ἀνάπαυσιν … ἔχουσιν ("they
  have rest") is rendered they … stop." οὐκ = "never" is unchanged.
  The English verb "stop" renders the noun + ἔχω idiom "have rest", not the
  noun alone. Linking the bare ἔχουσιν to "they" would show "have" under a
  pronoun. Reviewed files join this kind of idiom and keep the negative
  separate:
  - 1 Corinthians 12:21: Χρείαν … ἔχω = "I do + need", οὐκ = "not";
  - Mark 3:29: ἔχει ἄφεσιν = "will + be forgiven", with οὐκ … = "never";
  - John 13:35: ἀγάπην ἔχητε = "you love".

  "they" still sits on the finite verb.
- 4:7 (note only): ἀνθρώπου "a man". The old note, "… is rendered like a
  man", is now "The genitive ἀνθρώπου ("of a man", that is, a man's face)
  is rendered a man." The old note claimed "like", but "like" belongs to ὡς.
  The link is unchanged.
- 4:8 (note only): ὁ (29) "the". The old note, "The article of Θεός is
  rendered by the the before Lord God", is now "The article belongs to Θεός;
  BSB places its the before Lord God." This is the wording of the registered
  Revelation 1:8. The link is unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the open points

- **4:8 ἀνάπαυσιν οὐκ ἔχουσιν:** changed to the joined option (see above).
  The joined group is the honest division. "They never stop" = "they have
  no rest": "never" is the negative, and "they … stop" is the having-rest
  idiom.
- **4:8 ἀνά:** kept `s`. "each" renders ἓν καθ’ ἕν, the distributive on
  the subject ("one by one of them"). ἀνά is a second, redundant
  distributive on "six wings" ("apiece"), and English does not repeat it.
  Where ἀνά is the only distributive, reviewed files link it to "each"
  (Matthew 20:9-10, Luke 9:14, John 2:6). Here "each" already has its owner.
  Folding ἀνά in would make one non-contiguous group out of two separate
  constructions. Luke 9:3 leaves an unrendered ἀνά `s`.
- **4:6 the first τοῦ θρόνου:** kept `s`, and καί (14) kept `s`. BSB's
  "In the center" has no "of the throne". "the throne" stands after
  "around" and renders κύκλῳ τοῦ θρόνου. Linking both θρόνου to one
  "throne" would make one English word stand for two Greek phrases.
- **4:6 "something":** kept `t`. "like" fully renders ὡς. "something" is a
  supplied subject for the verbless clause, like the supplied "something"
  of Acts 10:10.
- **4:6 copulas:** both clauses are verbless, so the "there was" rule (an
  existential verb kept on εἰμί/γίνομαι) does not apply. "was" and "were"
  stay `t`.
- **4:6 "clear":** kept `t`. ὁμοία = "as + as" follows Revelation 1:14.
  There "white" has its own Greek word, but "clear" has none here.
- **4:4 "other":** kept `t`. The Greek only repeats θρόνους. "other" is an
  English adjective with no Greek word. The publisher's "other thrones"
  over-claimed.
- **4:2 τὸν θρόνον = "it":** kept. "it" is on θρόνον and τόν is `s` with
  the standard reason. Both options are defensible, so I made no change for
  taste.
- **4:1 "previously" on πρώτη:** confirmed. ἡ φωνὴ ἡ πρώτη is "the first
  voice", the one John heard first in 1:10, and "previously" renders that
  sense as an adverb. "had" stays on ἤκουσα as the English tense. The
  repeated ἡ (13) is `s` (Revelation 1:5). The relative ἥν is `s`
  (Revelation 1:4 ἅ). Nothing is over-claimed.
- **4:2 ἰδού = "I saw":** kept. This matches the reviewed ἰδού renderings
  that include a person word: Acts 8:27 "he met", Luke 1:38 "I am" and Luke
  11:41 "you will see that".
- **Readings in 4:4, 4:6 and 4:8:** each EVIDENCE line shows 1 unmatched
  N1904 word and 1 publisher word not in N1904. The word counts are equal,
  so each is a one-word substitution.
  - 4:6 and 4:8: the publisher record leaves exactly τέσσερα and the
    English "four" unlinked. That is the pattern when the publisher's word
    for "four" has no exact N1904 pair, so the number is the word that
    differs.
  - 4:4: the publisher's "twenty-four" sits on εἴκοσι in both places, so
    the record does not show which τέσσαρας differs. The other unlinked
    tokens (καί, ἱματίοις, τάς) are not likely substitutions. The note on
    the first number (5 6) fits the probable form. That spot is the one
    small uncertainty that remains.
- **4:5 and 4:11 (0/0):** the Greek is identical, and both verses rightly
  claim nothing. The count gaps are in the English only. In 4:5 the current
  text has one extra "and", which is correctly linked to καί (6). In 4:11
  the unlinked words are "came to be".
- **4:11:** Ἄξιος εἶ = "Worthy" + "are You" is correct. εἶ is a real
  copula with a second-person ending, so "are You" stays linked. It is not
  a supplied copula. ἐκτίσθησαν = "came to be" renders the same Greek verb
  as BSB's translation choice: coming into being by God's will, with the
  agent given by "by Your will". The link and its passive note stay.
  ἦσαν = "they exist" (tense note) carries "they". ἐκτίσθησαν shares that
  subject, and καί (28) = "and".
- **4:7:** ὅμοιον = "like", with "was" `t` twice, is confirmed. The Greek
  has no verb.
- **4:3:** "there" (an added adverb) and "that" (an added relative) stay
  `t`. ὁράσει = "looked" and ὁράσει = "gleamed" are two separate tokens,
  and each is linked to its own appearance verb.

## Still uncertain

  confirm it.
- 4:8 αὐτῶν = "of" ("each of the four living creatures"): kept from the
  publisher with its note. `s` would also be defensible.
