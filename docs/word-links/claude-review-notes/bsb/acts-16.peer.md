# BSB Acts 16: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 40 of 40 verses (16:1-40), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, against N1904,
BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json` (16:9, 16:14,
16:23, 16:28, 16:29, 16:32 and 16:33 have a `partial` record). The chapter
has no KEEP verses. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 40, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (10 verses: 8 with link changes, 2 notes only)

- 16:11: `g 0 = We sailed` and `g 4 = straight` → `g 0 = sailed` and
  `g 4 = We + straight // εὐθυδρομήσαμεν ("we ran a straight course") is
  rendered We … straight.` A participle has no person. Only the ending of
  εὐθυδρομήσαμεν says "we", and this is the first "we" of the passage. BSB's
  wording still allows the link, because a group may hold two separate
  phrases.
- 16:37: `g 24 = Let them come` and `g 27 = escort + out` → `g 24 = come`
  and `g 27 = Let them + escort + out`. The jussive "let them" is the
  third-person imperative of ἐξαγαγέτωσαν. ἐλθόντες only supplies "come".
- 16:39: `g 3 = them#0 + them#1` → `g 3 = them#0` and `t them#1 = English
  supplies the object of led out; after καί the new clause leaves it
  understood.` αὐτούς is the object of παρεκάλεσαν in the first clause.
  ἐξαγαγόντες belongs to a new clause, after καί, with its own main verb
  ἠρώτων. Its object is simply left out as obvious. This is not one object
  shared by a participle and its main verb (Acts 9:25, 16:3), and the Acts
  9:39 peer rule links a pronoun twice only in that case. Compare 16:19,
  where the author already made the "them" after dragged `t`.
- 16:32: `s 5 6` (τοῦ Θεοῦ, different reading) and `t of the Lord` →
  `g 5 = the#1`, `s 6` (Θεοῦ only), `t of` and `t Lord`, all three with the
  reading reason. The EVIDENCE line shows only one unmatched N1904 word. The
  publisher's own row links τοῦ ("of the"), so the article is the same in
  both texts, and only the noun differs. Under rule 9 the reading can be
  claimed only for Θεοῦ. "of" stays off the lone article and goes `t` with
  the noun it belongs to. John 4:1 and Acts 12:24 leave their article `s`
  only because their English ("Jesus", "of God") has no "the".
- 16:23 (note only): `g 1 = And` gains the note `BSB's Greek text has a different
  connective here, with the same sense.` The author note guessed that the
  difference was an accent on πολλάς. But the publisher rows link πολλάς and
  every other N1904 word except δέ, so the unmatched word is the
  connective, probably τε (as in NA28). "And" renders δέ just as well, so it
  stays linked with a same-meaning note (rule 9).
- 16:13: `g 19 = who had gathered there` → `g 19 = who had gathered` and
  `t there = English adds there; …`. συνελθούσαις has no word for the place.
  An added "there" is English-only (Acts 9:7 peer, Acts 4:14).
- 16:36: `g 11 = have sent orders` → `g 11 = have sent` and `t orders =
  English supplies what was sent; Ἀπέσταλκαν has no object.` Ἀπέσταλκαν has
  no object; the ἵνα clause gives what the message said. "orders" is a
  supplied object, like "the city" in 16:40 and "her" in 16:18.
- 16:18: `g 6 = grew + aggravated` and `g 9 = so + that#0` → `g 6 = grew so
  aggravated` and `g 9 = that#0`, which restores the publisher's division.
  "so" is the degree adverb on aggravated and reflects the intensive
  διαπονηθείς ("greatly annoyed"). καί has no sense of degree. It only joins
  the two participles, and BSB renders that join with "that", which stays
  linked as a changed connective.
- 16:6: `g 17 = province of Asia` → `g 17 = Asia` and `t province of = BSB
  adds that Asia was a Roman province.` This follows the reviewed Acts 6:9
  (`t the provinces of`). τῇ = the#2 stays linked.
- 16:12 (note only): the reason on `t we#0 went` now says that English
  supplies a verb and its subject, while the Greek continues the sentence of
  16:11 (we sailed straight …). Before, it did not say where "we" comes
  from.

No other row changed. I checked each correction the author made to the
publisher rows and agree with all the others.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 16:11 "We sailed" and 16:37 "Let them come": **disagree, moved** (see
  above). The comparison with "they seized" in 16:19 does not hold. A
  third-person subject pronoun on a participle that is rendered as a finite
  verb is the corpus convention (Acts 8:27 "he started out", 16:3 "he took",
  16:30 "he brought"), but neither first person nor the imperative mood can
  come from a participle.
- 16:39 αὐτούς = them + them: **changed to `t` for the second "them"**.
  16:40 ἀδελφούς = brothers + them: **agree, kept**. In ἰδόντες παρεκάλεσαν
  τοὺς ἀδελφούς a participle and its main verb share one accusative object
  in one clause, as λαβὼν περιέτεμεν αὐτόν in 16:3 and Acts 9:25. 16:3 αὐτόν
  = him + him and 16:37 ἡμᾶς (7) = us + us are the same pattern and stay.
- 16:38 δέ = who: **agree, kept**. BSB turns the next sentence into a
  relative clause, so the linking job of δέ passes to "who". Acts 9:27 links
  καὶ ὅτι = who the same way. ἐφοβήθησαν keeps "were alarmed".
- 16:17 "the rest of" `t` and 16:40 "house" `t`: **agree**. 16:6
  "province": **changed to `t`** (Acts 6:9). 16:12 "Roman" stays inside the
  κολωνία row: a colonia was a Roman colony by definition, so the word is
  part of the meaning, not an addition.
- 16:1 ἰδού + ἦν = he found: **agree, kept**. In Acts 8:27 ἦν sits in the
  relative clause rendered "in charge of", so ἰδού alone took "he met". Here
  ἦν is the main verb of the presentational clause ("behold, there was")
  that "he found" replaces, so both words belong in the group. The καί
  before it stays `s`, because "where" renders ἐκεῖ.

## Other points checked

- 16:33 οἱ αὐτοῦ = his household: correct. No lone article carries the
  noun any more. ἅπαντες = all with the same-meaning note is supported by
  the record: the publisher rows leave ἅπαντες unmatched and "all"
  unlinked, and the note does not name the publisher's word.
- "We" passages: every "we/us" has a Greek owner or an honest `t`.
  ἐζητήσαμεν/ἡμᾶς (16:10); εὐθυδρομήσαμεν (16:11, now); 16:12 "we went"
  `t`, then Ἦμεν … διατρίβοντες; ἐξήλθομεν/ἐλαλοῦμεν (16:13); 16:15 "us#0"
  `t` (παρεκάλεσεν has no object), ἡμᾶς = us#1; ἡμῶν, ἡμῖν (16:16); ἡμῖν
  (16:17).
- Lone articles: none carries of/to/in. The possessive articles (16:27
  τήν, 16:33 τῶν, 16:34 τόν, 16:35 τούς) follow the majority convention.
- Connectives: 16:21 καί = by, 16:3 καί = so, 16:18 δέ = Eventually and
  16:34 τε = Then are linked. 16:13 τε is `s`.

## Still uncertain

- 16:9: the publisher's extra word is probably an article before νυκτός.
  "the night" stays on νυκτός because English needs "the" anyway, as Acts
  5:19 does for the same διὰ νυκτός.
- 16:34 τε = Then: I read it as Luke's sentence-linking τε, which BSB
  renders. If it were τε … καί with the following καί, τε would be `s` and
  "Then" `t`.
- 16:36 "orders" `t`: ἀποστέλλω used alone can mean "send word". A reviewer
  who takes "sent orders" as that lexical sense could put "orders" back on
  the verb.
- Third-person pronouns on participles (16:3 "he took", 16:19 "they seized",
  16:20 "They brought", 16:37 "They beat") were kept by convention. They
  were not moved to the finite verbs.
