# BSB 2 Corinthians 2: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: all 17 (2:1-17). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 17, "problems": 0}`.

77 of the 226 publisher `g` rows changed, in all 17 verses. About fifteen of
these only absorb a TODO token into a joined unit (εἰ μή, μή πως, ἐν παντὶ
τόπῳ, article + participle, an articular infinitive's article). Every
publisher row was checked, including the unchanged ones. No "of the"/"to
the"/"in the"/"for"/"by" sits on a lone article: the six lone-article rows
left carry only "the". Articles with no English "the" are `s` with the
standard reason (README rule 5). λύπη, ἐπιτιμία, χαρίζομαι, νόημα,
θριαμβεύω, ὀσμή, εὐωδία and καπηλεύω are linked word by word without
interpretive notes. Names (Troas, Titus, Macedonia, Satan) are linked.

Conventions followed: Acts 26:9 (ἔδοξα ἐμαυτῷ joined to its verb; here
ἔκρινα ἐμαυτῷ = "I made up my mind"), Acts 10:25 (an articular
infinitive's article joins the infinitive: 2:1 τὸ … ἐλθεῖν, 2:13 τῷ …
εὑρεῖν), Matthew 22:5 (ὃς μέν … ὃς δέ joined as the "one … another"
idiom; here οἷς μέν/οἷς δέ), Acts 27:44 (a repeated preposition BSB lets
serve both phrases is `s`), Romans 6:17 and 7:25 ("thanks be": "be" `t`,
"to" on Θεῷ), 1 Corinthians 5:5 and 5:11 (article of τοιοῦτος `s`), 1
Corinthians 5:8 (same-word spelling note), Acts 18:18 (an "and" joining a
participle to the main verb is `t`).

## Publisher rows corrected

Lone articles carrying a preposition, verb or relative:

- 2:2 "is left" on ὁ → ὁ εὐφραίνων = "to cheer"; "is left" `t`.
- 2:6 "for" on τῷ → "for him" on τοιούτῳ; τῷ `s`. "imposed on him" on the
  attributive ἡ → `t`; ἡ `s`.
- 2:7 "by" on τῇ → "by + sorrow" on λύπῃ; τῇ `s`.
- 2:13 "in" on τῷ → "in + spirit" on πνεύματι; τῷ `s`.
- 2:14 "to" on Τῷ → "to God" on Θεῷ; Τῷ `s`. "of" on τῆς → τῆς = "the";
  γνώσεως = "of + knowledge" (it had "the knowledge").
- 2:15 "those who are" on each τοῖς → joined with its participle ("those
  who are being saved", "those who are perishing").
- 2:17 "who" on οἱ (which belongs to πολλοί, not to the participle) →
  καπηλεύοντες = "who peddle"; οἱ `s`.

Negatives and person words split off:

- 2:3 "I would not be" on σχῶ → "I would + be"; μή = "not".
- 2:5 "he has not grieved" → "he has + grieved"; οὐκ = "not".
- 2:7 "will not be overwhelmed" → "will + be overwhelmed"; μή πως = "so
  that + not" (πως had "so that").
- 2:11 "should not outwit us" → "should + outwit us"; μή = "not". "we are
  not unaware" → "we are + unaware of"; οὐ = "not".
- 2:13 "did not find" → τῷ … εὑρεῖν = "because + did + find"; μή = "not";
  με "because I" → "I".
- Participles carrying a person: 2:3 πεποιθώς "I had confidence" → "had
  confidence", "I" `t`; 2:12 Ἐλθών "when I went" → "when + went", "I" `t`;
  2:13 ἀποταξάμενος "I said goodbye" → "said goodbye", and the "I" moves
  to ἐξῆλθον ("I + went on"); "and" `t`.

Supplied words folded into Greek words (now `t`):

- 2:3 ἐλθών "on my arrival" → "on + arrival", "my" `t`; ἔδει "ought to
  make" → "ought to", "make" `t` (causative; in the Greek it is I who ought
  to rejoice); ἀφ’ "by those" → "by", with "those who" on ὧν.
- 2:5 "overstate it" → "overstate"; "it" `t`.
- 2:6 ἱκανόν "is sufficient" → "sufficient"; "is" `t`.
- 2:7 "ought to forgive" → "to forgive", "ought" `t`; "comfort him" →
  "comfort", "him" `t`.
- 2:8 "your love" → "love"; "your" `t`.
- 2:9 "My purpose" → "purpose" (εἰς τοῦτο), "My" `t`; "in writing you" →
  "writing", "in" and "you" `t`; ἵνα "was to" → "to", "was" `t`;
  "would stand the test" → τὴν δοκιμήν = "stand the test", "would" `t`;
  "and" on ἐστε → ἐστε = "be", "and" `t`; "be obedient" → "obedient".
- 2:12 "to preach" on εἰς → "to"; "preach" `t`.
- 2:13 "Titus there" → "Titus"; "there" `t`.
- 2:14 "thanks be" → "thanks"; "be" `t`.
- 2:16 "we are" on μέν → `t` (no verb in the clause; ἐσμέν is in 2:15);
  "who is" on τίς → "who", "is" `t`.
- 2:17 "men sent from" on ἐκ → "from"; "men sent" `t`.

Over-claims and wrong owners:

- 2:1 "painful visit" on λύπῃ → ἐν λύπῃ = "painful"; "visit" goes with τὸ
  … ἐλθεῖν = "to make + visit" (the coming is the visit).
- 2:4 "I love you" on ἀγάπην → τὴν ἀγάπην … ἣν ἔχω = "I love"; εἰς ὑμᾶς =
  "you".
- 2:10 "If you forgive" on χαρίζεσθε → ᾧ = "If + anyone"; χαρίζεσθε =
  "you forgive + forgive" (understood again with κἀγώ); κἀγώ "I also
  forgive him" → "I also", "him" `t`. διά "for your sake" → "for + sake";
  ὑμᾶς = "your".
- 2:11 αὐτοῦ "of his" → "his"; "of" is the complement of "unaware"
  (ἀγνοέω takes a direct object).
- 2:17 Θεοῦ "of God for profit" → "of God"; "for profit" goes with
  καπηλεύοντες ("who peddle + for profit"). ὡς "with" → ὡς `s`; ἐξ =
  "with".

## Notable decisions

- 2:1 δέ = "So": EVIDENCE shows BSB's Greek differs by one word here, and
  BSB's "So" lost its publisher row, so BSB's text probably reads γάρ. A
  connective BSB renders with a different connective is linked; the note
  says only that BSB's Greek has a different connective. τοῦτο `s`.
- 2:2 εἰ μή = "but"; ἐξ ἐμοῦ = "I" (the agent becomes the subject of the
  active "have grieved"); καί `s`.
- 2:3 τοῦτο αὐτό = "as I did" (publisher row kept, with αὐτό added);
  ἐστιν = "would share" kept (a finite verb with the genitive "is of you");
  πάντων `s`.
- 2:7 τοὐναντίον μᾶλλον joined to "instead" (both mean "rather").
- 2:11 ὑπὸ … Σατανᾶ = "Satan" (the agent becomes the subject); τοῦ `s`.
- 2:13 ἀλλά = "So" (a connective BSB renders with a different connective).
- 2:16 death clause word by word: ἐκ = "of", θανάτου = "death", θάνατον =
  "demise", εἰς `s` with "and" `t` ("and" does not mean "to"). Life clause:
  ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν = "that brings life" as one unit, since BSB gives the
  two ζωή one "life".
- 2:17 εἰλικρινίας = "sincerity": same word, spelled differently in BSB's
  Greek (probably εἰλικρινείας); this is the one-word difference EVIDENCE
  shows. The second ἀλλά and the first ὡς are `s`.

## Doubts for the peer

- 2:10: "If" on ᾧ (the indefinite relative read as a condition) or `t`.
  The publisher had it on χαρίζεσθε, which does not mean "if".
- 2:16: joining all of ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν to "that brings life", or εἰς
  ζωήν = "that brings life" with ἐκ ζωῆς `s`.
- 2:3: "as I did" on τοῦτο αὐτό, where "I did" is an English pro-verb for
  "wrote"; the "I" could be `t`.
- 2:9: "would" `t` because "stand the test" renders the noun δοκιμή; it
  could instead go with ἐστε ("would … be obedient") as the only finite
  verb in the clause.
- 2:2 and 2:3: "those whom"/"those who" on ὁ λυπούμενος and ὧν, although
  the Greek of 2:2 is singular.
