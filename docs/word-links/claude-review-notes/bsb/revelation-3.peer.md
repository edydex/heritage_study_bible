## Changes

- 3:7 ὁ ἀνοίγων = "He opens" (link unchanged). The note before was
  "ὁ ἀνοίγων ("the one who opens") is rendered What He opens." It is now
  "The article with the participle ἀνοίγων ("the one who opens") is
  rendered He opens." Why: "What" is `t` (a supplied object), so the note
  must not claim it for this group.
- 3:7 ἀνοίγει = "can open" (link unchanged). It had no note. I added "The
  present ἀνοίγει ("opens") is rendered can open." Why: this matches the
  κλείσει note in the same verse. The modal "can" is not obvious for a
  present verb.
- 3:14 ἀρχή = "Originator" (link unchanged). It had no note. I added
  "ἀρχή ("beginning, origin") is rendered Originator." Why: the English
  word is not the usual gloss. The link stays, because "first cause,
  origin" is within the word's meaning.

I thought about one more change and dropped it. In 3:7 I would have split
ὁ ἀληθινός into ὁ `s` + ἀληθινός = "true". I kept the author's join. This
is a second title, made with a substantive and its own article, and BSB
folds it under the first "the One who". So it works as one unit, like an
article and participle, and it matches the Revelation 2 draft (2:1 ὁ
περιπατῶν = "walks"). The repeated article in 3:14 (ὁ μάρτυς ὁ πιστός)
is attributive and stays `s`, as in 1:5.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **Τάδε λέγει: keep the split.** Τάδε = "These", λέγει = "the words of",
  and "are" `t`. Each Greek word has its own English counterpart with the
  same meaning. Τάδε ("these things") gives "These". BSB turns the verb
  "says" into a noun phrase: "words" names what λέγει does, and "of" marks
  the speaker, who is the verb's subject. So "of" belongs to λέγει, not to
  a genitive. That keeps "of the" off a lone article in every letter
  (3:14 ὁ Ἀμήν). Joining the two words would hide this, and it is not
  needed, because the phrase is not an idiom whose parts lose their
  meaning (unlike ἐὰν μή). Acts 21:11 also treats Τάδε and λέγει
  separately. "are" is a supplied copula.
- **3:7 κλείων: keep "shuts" with "He#1" `t`.** The article ὁ governs
  both participles, but its "He" is already on "He#0" (ὁ ἀνοίγων).
  κλείων itself is a bare participle and has no person. If "He#1" were
  joined to the first group, it would claim that ἀνοίγων renders a second
  "He". Revelation 1 does the same: 1:16 ἔχων has "He" `t`, and 1:5
  λύσαντι shares Τῷ but gets only "has released".
- **3:21 "the right" `t`: keep.** δίδωμι with an infinitive means "grant
  to (sit)". "the right" is a noun that English supplies; compare John
  1:12, where ἐξουσίαν is the Greek word for "the right". "grant" on δώσω
  is fine.
- **3:9 καὶ οὐκ εἰσίν `s`: keep.** This καί means "and", not "but". BSB
  drops "and they are not", so it has no English. The contrast is ἀλλά.
  After a denial, ἀλλά means "but rather", and BSB writes that sense out
  as "but … instead". So "instead" is ἀλλά's own corrective force, not an
  added adverb. ψεύδονται = "are liars": "are" is part of the verb turned
  into a noun, not a supplied copula. διδῶ `s` is right too. It breaks off
  and is taken up again by ἰδοὺ ποιήσω αὐτούς, which BSB renders once as
  "I will make". ἐκ = "those who belong to" is the partitive ("some of").
  John 16:17, John 7:40 and 2 John 4 link "some of" to ἐκ in the same way.
- **3:12 "it" `t`: keep.** ἔξω is the "out(side)" in ἔξω … ἐξέλθῃ, and
  it is absorbed into "leave". It does not mean "it". The object (the
  temple) is supplied.

## Points the lead asked me to confirm

- Address line: τῷ = "the", ἀγγέλῳ = "To + angel", τῆς = "the",
  ἐκκλησίας = "of + church", opening Καί `s`. Confirmed. This is the same
  pattern as 1:4 and 1:11 (ταῖς = "the", ἐκκλησίαις = "To/to + churches").
- Ὁ ἔχων οὖς ἀκουσάτω … (3:6, 3:13, 3:22): ὁ ἔχων = "He who has",
  ἀκουσάτω = "let him hear" (the jussive and its person sit on the
  imperative), ταῖς = "the", ἐκκλησίαις = "to + churches". Confirmed.
- Ὁ νικῶν is joined in 3:5, 3:12 and 3:21. In 3:21 the resumptive αὐτῷ =
  "To", as αὐτοῖς = "to" in John 1:12. In 3:12 the resumptive αὐτόν has
  no English and is `s`. Confirmed.
- 3:7: ὁ ἀνοίγων = "He opens" (the article carries "He"). κλείων =
  "shuts" with "He" `t`. "What" and "what" are `t`. Confirmed.
- 3:10 κἀγώ = "I + also", τηρήσω = "will + keep". 3:21 κἀγώ = "I" with a
  note, because BSB has no "also" there. Confirmed.
- 3:14 Θεοῦ = "God’s" and κτίσεως = "of + creation". Each genitive keeps
  its own case word. Confirmed.
- 3:19 ἐγώ = "I#1", the subject of ἐλέγχω. φιλῶ has its own person
  ("I#0 love"). Confirmed.
- 3:2 οὐ … πεπληρωμένα = "incomplete": BSB moves the negative of εὕρηκα
  into the adjective. 3:17 χρείαν ἔχω = "need". Confirmed.
- 3:3 πῶς = "what" has precedent: Mark 5:16 (πῶς = "what") and Mark 4:30.
  3:15 ὄφελον = "How I wish" has precedent: 1 Corinthians 4:8, and
  Galatians 5:12 ("I wish").
- Titles match Revelation 1. ὁ Ἀμήν is split as ὁ = "the" + "Amen", like
  1:17 ὁ πρῶτος. ἡ ἀρχή is split the same way. ὁ μάρτυς ὁ πιστός matches
  1:5, with the second article `s`.
