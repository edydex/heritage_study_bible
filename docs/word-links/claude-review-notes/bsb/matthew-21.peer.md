13 changes in 11 verses.

## Changes

Supplied "and" between a participle and a finite verb. The Greek has no
conjunction there, so the English "and" is now `t`, not part of the verb.
This is how Matthew 15, 16, 18, 19 and 20 treat it; this chapter was the
only one that bundled it.

- 21:2: ἀγάγετε "and#1 bring" → bring, plus `t and#1` (after λύσαντες).
- 21:10: λέγουσα "and asked" → asked, plus `t and`.
- 21:17: ἐξῆλθεν "and went" → went, plus `t and` (after καταλιπών).
- 21:20: λέγοντες "and asked" → asked, plus `t and`.
- 21:23: διδάσκοντι "and began to teach" → began to teach, plus `t and#0`.
- 21:25: λέγοντες "and said" → said, plus `t and`.
- 21:28: εἶπεν "and#0 said" → said, plus `t and#0` (after προσελθών).
- 21:28: ἐργάζου "and#1 work" → work, plus `t and#1` (ὕπαγε … ἐργάζου has
  no conjunction).
- 21:29: ἀπῆλθεν "and went" → went, plus `t and`. In the reading BSB follows,
  "and" joins the participle μεταμεληθείς to the verb. N1904's καί belongs
  to its other wording ("and did not go"), so it stays `s`, as the author had it.
- 21:30: εἶπεν "and told" → told, plus `t and` (after προσελθών).
- 21:39: ἐξέβαλον "and#0 threw" → threw, plus `t and#0` (after λαβόντες).

Other link changes:

- 21:19: Οὐ μηκέτι = "never + again", with εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα as `s` →
  Οὐ μηκέτι … εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα together = "never + again". The `s` reason
  itself said that "never … again" carries the phrase. So it was translated,
  not left out: "ever" in "never" is its English counterpart.
- 21:28: εἶχεν "who had" → had, plus `t who`. The Greek has a plain finite
  verb ("a man had two children"). "who" belongs to BSB's "There was …"
  frame, which the author already marks `t`. In 21:33, by contrast, ὅστις
  really does give "who".

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 21:29-31 (N1904 reverses the sons' answers): **agree, keep `s`/`t`.** I
  checked the GR lines. N1904 21:29 reads Ἐγὼ κύριε, καὶ οὐκ ἀπῆλθεν.
  21:30 reads Οὐ θέλω, ὕστερον μεταμεληθεὶς ἀπῆλθεν, with no δέ. 21:31 reads
  Ὁ ὕστερος. BSB has the other order, and its answer is "The first". The
  shared frame, ἀπῆλθεν, and the article Ὁ → "The" are linked correctly.
  Each reading reason is accurate. Cross-verse `g @21:30` links would point
  readers to words in another verse of the displayed text. They would also
  have to share ἀπῆλθεν, which both verses use. Rule 9 favors `s`/`t`. The
  one change here is the supplied "and" in 21:29 (above).
- 21:19 Οὐ μηκέτι / εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα: **changed**. The three are now one
  "never … again" group (above).
- 21:12 "who were" with τοὺς πωλοῦντας: **agree.** One article governs both
  participles, and a lone article may not carry "were". Either participle is
  a defensible anchor.
- 21:23 "began to" with διδάσκοντι: **agree**, but the "and" is now `t`.
  "began to teach" is BSB's aspectual rendering of the present participle.
  It is like the "began to" that imperfects receive elsewhere.
- 21:24 ὃν ἐάν → "and if": **agree.** A clause-opening relative commonly has
  the force "and … it". Here BSB's "and" is that relative's only English
  trace, so it is not a supplied coordinator.

## Still uncertain

- 21:32: τοῦ πιστεῦσαι → "and believe" is kept. The "and" is BSB's rendering
  of the τοῦ-infinitive's result sense ("so as to believe"). An alternative
  is τοῦ `s` and "and" `t`.
- 21:39: one αὐτόν linked to all three "him"s is kept. The Greek object is
  shared by the participle and both finite verbs. The stricter alternative
  is `t` for the second and third "him".
- 21:32: ὕστερον → "after" ("even after you saw this") is kept. The "after"
  may equally reflect the aorist participle ἰδόντες.
- 21:27: καί (of καὶ αὐτός, "he also") → "And", and αὐτός → "Jesus", are kept.
  BSB drops τῷ Ἰησοῦ from the first clause and names Jesus in the second.
