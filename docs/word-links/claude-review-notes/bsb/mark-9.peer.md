# BSB Mark 9: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 48 of 48 verses

These are 9:1–50 without the empty 9:44 and 9:46, including the earlier
reviewer's 9:19.

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 48, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB,
including the `#n` occurrence of repeated English words. Textual claims were
checked against the publisher's rows and word counts in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/mark.json`. Parallels were

## Changes (7 links in 6 verses, plus notes in 2 verses)

- 9:38: `g 13 14 15 16 = else` → `s 13 14 15 16` ("BSB renders the
  repeated clause only once, in the ὅτι clause") and `t else`. Why: "else"
  does not mean ὃς οὐκ ἀκολουθεῖ ἡμῖν ("who does not follow us"). Four Greek
  words were linked to one English word that does not render them. The
  publisher's own row was only ὅς = else, and it put "he does not accompany
  us" on the ὅτι clause, as here. "else" is the English idiom "someone
  else", as `t else` in 9:6.
- 9:3: `g 8 15 = brighter than` + `t them` ("λευκᾶναι has none") →
  `g 8 15 = brighter than + them`. Why: the reason was wrong. οἷα is the
  accusative relative ("such [garments] as"), the object of λευκᾶναι. "them"
  renders that object, so it is not English-only.
- 9:2: `g 23 = There` (καί) → `s 23` ("BSB starts a new sentence here") and
  `t There`. Why: "There" is a place word pointing back to the mountain;
  καί has no locative sense. The identical clause καὶ μετεμορφώθη ἔμπροσθεν
  αὐτῶν = "There He was transfigured before them" is `s` καί + `t There` in
  the reviewed Matthew 17:2. This is not a split pair, since "There" does not
  render καί.
- 9:39: `g 17 = and` (καί) and `g 19 = turn around` → `s 17` and
  `g 19 = turn around and`. Why: the Greek καί joins ποιήσει and δυνήσεται
  ("who will do a miracle and be able to …"). BSB makes "can" the main verb
  of "no one", so that coordination disappears. The English "and" belongs to
  the idiom "turn around and speak evil", which renders ταχύ ("soon"). This
  answers the author's doubt. Mark 6:34 and 6:55 likewise leave καί `s` when
  the English "and" joins different verbs.
- 9:25: `g 18 = You + spirit#1` with τό(14) in the article `s` →
  `g 14 18 = You + spirit#1`. Why: the note already said "τὸ … πνεῦμα is
  used as a vocative", but τό was listed as an unrendered article. Mark 5:8
  (τὸ πνεῦμα = you + spirit) and 5:41 (Τὸ κοράσιον = Little girl) link the
  article with the vocative noun.
- 9:19 (earlier reviewer's verse), two links:
  - `s 18` αὐτόν + `t the boy` → `g 18 = the boy`. Why: a split pair. The
    boy is the referent of αὐτόν. This matches 9:20 (αὐτόν = the boy), 9:36
    (αὐτό = the child) and the reviewed parallel Matthew 17:17 (αὐτόν =
    the boy).
  - `g 0 2 4 = Jesus replied` → `g 0 = Jesus` and
    `g 2 4 = replied`. Why: the article ὁ (with δέ) is the pronoun BSB names
    as Jesus; the participle and verb are "replied". This is how 9:12, 9:21,
    Mark 5:34, 6:37, 7:6 and Matthew 19:4 link ὁ δέ. One coarse group had
    shown ὁ ἀποκριθεὶς λέγει under "Jesus".
- 9:19, notes only: four long or unclear notes ("one-answering-says
  subject/predicate constituent", "contextual continuation predicate …",
  "putting-up-with predicate …", "narrative connective and/but") are now one
  short sentence each: ἔσομαι → must I remain, ἀνέξομαι → must I put up,
  "BSB leaves the connective δέ untranslated", "BSB does not render the
  addressee αὐτοῖς".
- 9:34, note only: the note now quotes the whole linked phrase "they had
  been arguing about" (it omitted "about").

Nothing else was changed. The author's corrections of publisher rows are
right. They include "When/As/After/While" moved from καί/δέ to the participle
or genitive absolute (9:9, 9:14, 9:25, 9:28, 9:33); participle "and" made `t`;
negatives taken out of verbs (9:1, 9:6, 9:9, 9:30, 9:38, 9:39, 9:41, 9:48);
οὐκέτι οὐδένα = no one (9:8); μηδενί = not + anyone (9:9); τούς = his (9:18);
τῆς = the and χειρός = by + hand with αὐτοῦ = him (9:27, as Mark 5:41);
πολλάκις = often and πολλά = violently split off their verbs; μηκέτι = never +
again (9:25); and the subjects and objects moved to `t` or to their own
pronoun.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 9:38: **changed** (see above). The relative clause is `s`, "else" is `t`.
  Linking the relative clause to "he does not accompany us" instead would
  leave "because" alone on ὅτι and the ὅτι clause unlinked. The publisher
  also anchored that English on the ὅτι clause. The present "does …
  accompany" for the imperfect ἠκολούθει is ordinary English; it is not a
  reason to move the link.
- 9:2 καί → There: **changed** to follow Matthew 17:2 (see above).
- 9:34 πρὸς ἀλλήλους joined to "had been arguing" with `t of them`:
  **agree**. "of them" is the partitive set for τίς ("which of them"), not
  the reciprocal "with one another". "Arguing" carries the reciprocal sense,
  so joining is honest. `s` for πρὸς ἀλλήλους would also be acceptable.
- 9:3: **agree** with οἷα … οὕτως = brighter than, οὐ = any and στίλβοντα
  … λίαν = radiantly. "Than any launderer could" is the comparative form of
  "such as no launderer can", so "any" is where the negative goes in
  English. "radiantly" is the adverb on "white" (λίαν's place) with the
  meaning of στίλβοντα. Only "them" was changed (see above).
- 9:6, two γάρ: **agree**. The first γάρ explains Peter's speech in 9:5, as
  the opening "For" does. The second gives the cause of his not knowing,
  which BSB turns into "so … that".
- 9:23 τό `s`: **agree**. τό quotes the father's words and belongs inside
  the quotation; joining it with εἶπεν would make a non-adjacent group for
  no gain. "echoed" stays on εἶπεν.
- 9:39 καί → and: **changed** (see above).
- 9:17 ἄλαλον = "that makes him mute": **agree**. The whole relative clause
  is BSB's rendering of the one adjective ("a mute spirit" = a spirit that
  makes him mute). No other Greek word stands behind "that makes him".

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 9:22 ἵνα = trying, ἀπολέσῃ = to kill. Elsewhere in this chapter ἵνα = to
  (9:9, 9:18, 9:30). ἵνα = "trying to", ἀπολέσῃ = "kill" would be more
  uniform, but the current link is honest.
- 9:16 πρὸς αὑτούς: N1904 prints the rough breathing ("among yourselves");
  BSB's "with them" reads αὐτούς. The counts ignore breathings, so they
  cannot show this. The link πρὸς αὑτούς = with them is kept.
- 9:24 ὁ = "the" in "the boy's father": the English "the" grammatically
  goes with "boy's" (τοῦ). Either article could carry it.
- 9:42 ὀνικός = "a large" against Matthew 18:6 μύλος = "a + millstone":
  different placement of "a", both acceptable.
- 9:39 (my change): the author's καί = and is also defensible if "turn
  around and" is read as keeping the Greek sequence "and (then)".
