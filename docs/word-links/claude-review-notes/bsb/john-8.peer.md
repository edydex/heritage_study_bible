## Changes (3 verses)

- 8:35, links: `g 4` μένει = "is + a#0 + member" → "is" (note: μένει
  "remains" is rendered is; BSB recasts "does not remain … forever" as "is
  not a permanent member"), and `g 5 7` ἐν … οἰκίᾳ = "of + family" →
  "a#0 + member of + family" (note: ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ "in the household" is
  rendered a member of the family). Why: "member" over-claimed μένει. What
  makes the slave a "member of the family" is being "in the household";
  μένει ("remains") gives only the verb "is", and its lasting sense is in
  "permanent" (εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα, unchanged). English "a" goes with its noun
  "member". The rest of the author's from-scratch links are honest and
  kept: οὐ = not, τῇ = the, εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα = permanent / forever, second
  μένει = "belongs to" (no locative in that clause, so the verb takes the
  whole phrasal verb), `t it`, and `t but` (the record shows BSB's Greek
  equals N1904 here, and N1904 has no connective between the two clauses).
- 8:41 (Codex KEEP), links: `g 10 11 12 13` ἐκ πορνείας οὐκ ἐγεννήθημεν =
  "are#1 not illegitimate children" → `g 12` οὐκ = "not" and `g 10 11 13`
  = "are#1 + illegitimate children". Why: negatives are split off the verb
  in this project, and the old three-sentence note is now one sentence.
- 8:41 (Codex KEEP), links: `g 14 15 16 17 18` ἕνα Πατέρα ἔχομεν τὸν Θεόν
  = "Our only Father is God" → ἕνα = "only" (noted), Πατέρα = "Father",
  ἔχομεν = "Our + is" (noted as a recast of "we have"), Θεόν = "God", τόν
  `s`. Why: the one group linked "God" to "one", "Father" and "have", and
  "Father" to "God", though three of the Greek words have plain one-word
  English renderings. The old two-sentence note is gone. `s 8` and
  `t Himself` are kept.
- 8:7, reason only: `t and` now says "English joins He straightened up to
  said; N1904 has the participle ἀνάκυψας and no καί, and BSB's Greek
  differs in this clause." Why: the record (18 N1904 / 14 matched / 18
  publisher words) puts all 4 unmatched N1904 words in this clause, so the
  English "and" may render a word in BSB's Greek. The reason now says that
  without naming the word.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 8:7 "and": **keep `t`**, with the reading information added to the reason
  (above). It cannot be linked to an N1904 word.
- 8:10: **agree.** The six-word clause has no English counterpart, and 6 of
  the 7 unmatched words fit it. The seventh is most likely γυνή: the
  publisher did not link it although "Woman" is in the English. ἐκεῖνοι `s`
  without a reading claim is right, because the counts do not show whether
  BSB's Greek has it.
- 8:39: **keep the link** ποιεῖτε = "you would do". It is the same verb with
  the same person and number, and "do" renders it. Only the mood differs, and
  the note says so honestly ("BSB's Greek has another form of it here …
  N1904's present means 'you do' or 'do'") without claiming the same
  meaning. BSB renders the matched present ἐστε as "you were" in the same
  sentence, so the unreal frame is BSB's whole sentence, not one word. An
  `s` + `t` split would hide that "do" is ποιέω.
- 8:9 ἑστῶσα = "standing": **agree.** The counts (8 unmatched) fit the
  conscience clause (5), εἷς καθεῖς (2) and this participle (1), and
  "standing" renders N1904's word exactly.
- 8:11 "Then" `t`: **agree.** It means "in that case" inside the quotation;
  the narrative δέ before εἶπε is `s`. "Now" `t` with the reading reason
  matches the record (1 unmatched, 4 extra publisher words).
- 8:35: **changed** (above). The alternative of joining μένει … εἰς τὸν
  αἰῶνα is not needed once "member" goes with ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ.
- 8:50 κρίνων = "Judge" with `t He is the`: **agree.** "He is" is a supplied
  subject and copula; the publisher row put them on the participle.
- 8:43 ἐμήν = "I": **agree.** λαλιάν = "what … am saying" and the possessive
  turned subject is honest and keeps "I" on the word that names the speaker.
- 8:26 κἀγώ = "and + I": **agree.** The crasis contains ἐγώ, an explicit
  pronoun, which is the subject of "tell".
- 8:16 καί = "with", `t am#1`: **agree.** The Greek clause has no verb, and
  a compound subject would not take εἰμί, so `t` is safer than linking εἰμί
  again.
- 8:6 / 8:59 ἵνα = "in order" / "to": **agree** (publisher rows, both honest).

## Still uncertain

- 8:35: the author's μένει = "is + a + member" can be defended by the
  parallel with "belongs to" in the second clause. I moved "member" because
  "in the household" → "member of the family" is the closer match, and a
  reader tapping "member" now sees ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ, not "remains".
- 8:10: one unmatched word cannot be placed with certainty. The note on
  Ἡ γυνή ("BSB's Greek has another form of this word") is the most likely
  reading, but the counts do not prove it.
- 8:44 οὐκ = "refusing", ἔστηκεν = "to uphold" (publisher rows) are kept.
  Joining οὐκ ἔστηκεν = "refusing to uphold" would also be defensible,
  because "refusing" adds will to the plain negative; the note explains it.
- 8:41 ἔχομεν = "Our + is": "is" could also be `t` (from the apposition)
  with ἔχομεν = "Our" only.
