# BSB Mark 15: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 46 of 46 verses (15:1-47; 15:28 is absent from both N1904 and BSB).
Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against the Greek, the BSB English, the
publisher's own rows and the publisher's word counts in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/mark.json`. Matthew 27 (the
reviewed parallel) was compared verse by verse.

Final check: `{"versesOk": 46, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (3 verses)

- 15:44: `g πάλαι ἀπέθανεν → this was so` → `s πάλαι ἀπέθανεν` plus
  `t this was so`. "If this was so" is an English pro-form that points back to
  "Jesus was already dead". It does not mean "he had died some time ago", and
  BSB does not render that repeated content. This follows Mark 9:38, where a
  repeated Greek clause that BSB renders only once is `s`. It is not a split
  pair, because "this was so" does not translate the Greek words.
- 15:46: the note on μνήματι said N1904's μνήματι and the publisher's μνημείῳ
  are "the same word". They are two different words with the same meaning. The
  note now calls them synonyms that both mean tomb. The link is unchanged.
- 15:47: Ἰωσῆτος → "of Joseph" now has a note. Ἰωσῆτος is Joses, the same
  name that 15:40 renders "Joses", and it is a form of Joseph. Without the
  note, readers see two different names. The note on ἡ → "the mother" no
  longer says "as in 15:40", because 15:40 writes μήτηρ out.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the review points

- 15:20 "own" `t` (reading): **kept**. The counts show N1904 20 words, all 20
  matched, and 21 publisher words. So the publisher's Greek has exactly one
  extra word. The publisher's own rows matched all 26 English words, but none
  of its N1904-projected rows carries "own": αὐτοῦ → "His" only. So the
  publisher tied "own" to its extra word. Matthew 27:31 is different. There
  the publisher's Greek equals N1904, and its rows put "own" on τά, which is
  why Matthew links "His own" to αὐτοῦ. The Greek differs, so the two
  chapters can link differently. The note does not name the extra word
  because we cannot see it.
- 15:21: **agree**. The verse-opening καί → "Now" is the narrative connective.
  BSB's "and" joins its two clauses only because it turned the participle
  παράγοντα into "was passing by", so "and" is `t`. τινα is `s`: the
  publisher's τινα → "Now" was wrong. Σίμωνα → "Simon" + "him" because Simon
  is the object of ἀγγαρεύουσιν and "him" resumes him, like τὸν Ἰησοῦν →
  "Jesus" + "Him" in 15:1 and 15:15.
- 15:43 ἐλθών … εἰσῆλθεν → "went": **kept**. It is a pleonastic participle +
  finite verb that BSB renders with one verb. This chapter (15:2, 15:9, 15:12)
  and Matthew 27:21 join ἀποκριθείς … εἶπεν/λέγει → "replied"/"asked" the
  same way. Mark 12:14 makes καὶ ἐλθόντες `s`, but there BSB has no motion
  verb at all. Here "went" renders both verbs.
- 15:12 "do you want" `t`: **agree**. The counts are 16 / 15 / 17. One
  unmatched word is the Πιλᾶτος spelling, and the second publisher-only word
  (θέλετε) carries "do you want".
- Same-meaning readings, linked with notes: **agree** in all four. 15:1:
  ἑτοιμάσαντες is unmatched and the publisher left it unlinked, so its
  ποιήσαντες carries "devised". 15:22: μεθερμηνευόμενος is unmatched
  (12 / 11 / 12). 15:34: λαμά and σαβαχθανεί are the two unmatched words.
  15:46: κατέθηκεν and μνήματι are the two unmatched words (note reworded, see
  above). In each, the counts match what the note claims.
- 15:23 "There" `t`, καί `s`: **agree**. Mark 9:2 does the same, and the Greek
  has no locative word.
- 15:8 καθὼς ἐποίει → "to keep his custom", αὐτοῖς `s`: **agree**.
- 15:17 αὐτῷ → "His", "head" `t`: **agree**. The Greek never says "head".
  Matthew 27:29 has a real κεφαλῆς, and the "it" link differs from Matthew
  27:29 (`t` there) for a reason. In Mark, στέφανον is the object of the main
  verb περιτιθέασιν, so "it" resumes the Greek word.
- 15:31 μετά → "and": **agree**. No καί stands between "chief priests" and
  "scribes", and μετά is what makes the scribes a second subject. 15:1 and
  Matthew 27:41 differ because their "and" belongs to a real καί.
- 15:32 one καί → "And even": **agree**. It is a single καί that both links
  the clause and means "even/also".

## Checked and left as the author had them

- 15:1 εὐθύς `s` and πρωΐ → "Early in the morning" (the publisher had εὐθύς →
  "Early"). I also considered joining εὐθὺς πρωΐ. The author's version is
  accurate: πρωΐ alone means early in the morning (Mark 1:35), and the English
  has no word for "immediately".
- 15:33 γενομένης → "From". With no ἀπό in Mark, the genitive absolute is what
  marks the starting point. Matthew 27:45 has a real ἀπό.
- 15:27 αὐτοῦ → both "His" (the one αὐτοῦ covers both sides). In Matthew
  27:38 both are `t` because the Greek has no αὐτοῦ.
- 15:36 keeps the same ἐπότιζεν note as Matthew 27:48.

## Still uncertain

- 15:44 could also be argued the other way. One can say a pro-form "renders"
  the clause it replaces, as the publisher thought. I followed the Mark 9:38
  rule.
- 15:20: the exact publisher word behind "own" is not visible. ἴδια is likely,
  but the note does not claim it.
