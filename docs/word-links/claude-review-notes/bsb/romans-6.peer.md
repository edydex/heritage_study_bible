# BSB Romans 6: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 23 of 23 verses (6:1-23), every `g`/`s`/`t` line of the author's
final file, against N1904, the BSB English, the publisher rows in
`drafts/romans-6.txt`, the EVIDENCE lines and the `partial` records in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/romans.json`. No KEEP blocks.

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 23, "problems": 0}`. Six verses
differ from the author's JSON: three link changes (6:5, 6:16, 6:18) and
three note corrections (6:6, 6:17, 6:20).

## Changes

- 6:5 ὁμοιώματι: "like this" → "like"; "this" `t` ("English adds a
  demonstrative pointing back to burial through baptism (6:4); the Greek has
  none."). BSB has no "likeness" here: it turns the noun into the
  preposition "like", and "this" is a supplied demonstrative object of that
  preposition. Supplied demonstratives are `t`, and Romans 5:14 made the
  added "that" in the ὁμοιώματι rendering `t` in the same way.
- 6:16 ἐστε: "you#2 are + you#4 are" → "you#2 are"; the repeated "you are"
  in the whether-clause is `t` ("English repeats the subject and copula in
  the whether-clause, which has no verb in Greek (as Galatians 4:7)."). A
  supplied copula is always English-only (README rule 7; John 14:10, 15:5
  and the John 15 coordinator ruling), and Galatians 4:7 makes exactly this
  repeated "you are" `t`. δοῦλοι = "slaves#1 + slaves#2" stays linked: a
  content word understood again in the same role and meaning.
- 6:18 "You": `t` → linked to the finite verb, ἐδουλώθητε = "You + have#1
  become slaves" (note: "The ending of ἐδουλώθητε ("you were enslaved")
  gives You, the subject of both verbs."). BSB's one "You" is the subject
  of the compound predicate "have been set free … and have become slaves",
  so it comes from the finite verb's person ending, not only from the
  participle. Acts 21:2 and 21:15 put the shared "we" on the finite verb in
  the same participle + "and" + finite pattern. "and" stays `t`, δέ `s`.
- 6:6 note on τοῦ δουλεύειν: gloss "to be enslaved" → "to serve as slaves".
  δουλεύω is active ("be a slave, serve"); "enslaved" would be δουλόω
  (6:18, 6:22).
- 6:17 note on δέ = "though": "before obeyed" → "of the obeyed clause". The
  postpositive δέ stands after ὑπηκούσατε.
- 6:20 note on ἐλεύθεροι: no longer says "free of obligation to", since "to"
  is on δικαιοσύνῃ, not in this group.

## Points checked and confirmed (no change)

- 6:5 "in": BSB's "in" does not render the dative τῷ ὁμοιώματι. With the
  likeness noun rendered "like", "in" introduces "His death", which BSB has
  detached from the likeness. In the second clause the Greek has no
  ὁμοιώματι at all, so "in" can only go on ἀναστάσεως. Both clauses now
  read "in + death" and "in + resurrection" on the genitive noun, with τῷ
  and τοῦ `s`. ἀλλά = "certainly" is right: ἀλλά opening the apodosis of a
  conditional means "certainly, surely". καί = "also"; τῆς = "His" (article
  as possessive); ἐσόμεθα = "we will + be"; "Him" twice `t` (σύμφυτοι takes
  the dative τῷ ὁμοιώματι, not a pronoun).
- Words understood again, same role and meaning: 6:5 σύμφυτοι = "united
  with + united with" (predicate adjective in both clauses); 6:13
  παραστήσατε = "present#1 + present#2" (one imperative governing both
  objects; the publisher's "and present" on καί over-claimed); 6:16 δοῦλοι
  (see Changes for ἐστε).
- 6:16 first ᾧ `s` + "when" `t`: no split pair. ᾧ means "to whom" (the
  master one serves); "when" is a temporal conjunction BSB uses after
  dropping the indirect object, so it does not render ᾧ. The second ᾧ keeps
  "to the one". δούλους = "as + slaves" (predicate accusative, as ὅπλα = "as
  instruments" in 6:13) and εἰς ὑπακοήν = "obedient" (joined) leave no
  unpaired `s` or `t`.
- 6:17 δέ = "though": within one verse, BSB moves the contrast that δέ marks
  from the second clause into a concessive first clause. Same relation,
  shifted, so linking avoids a split pair; the "no moved connectives" rule
  is about cross-verse links. "once" (added adverb) and "be" (supplied
  verb) `t`; ἐκ καρδίας = "wholeheartedly" joined.
- 6:13 and 6:19 μέλη = "parts of + body", ὑμῶν = "your": the "of" is part of
  BSB's expansion of "members", not a genitive case word, so it belongs to
  μέλη. 6:19 second μέλη = "them" with ὑμῶν `s`.
- 6:21 ἐφ’ = "of" (ἐπαισχύνομαι ἐπί "ashamed of"), οἷς = "the things +
  which" (relative with its implied antecedent), "from" `t` (links the
  fruit to its source). Right.
- μὴ γένοιτο = "Certainly not" as one unit in 6:2 and 6:15, as in Romans 3
  and Galatians 2:17, 3:21.
- Negatives split: 6:6 μηκέτι = "no longer"; 6:9 οὐκέτι = "cannot + again"
  ("cannot" cannot be split, and its negative belongs to οὐκέτι; the verb
  keeps "He + die"), second οὐκέτι = "no longer"; 6:12 Μή = "not",
  βασιλευέτω = "do + let + reign"; 6:13 μηδέ = "not", "Do + present"; 6:14
  οὐ = "not", "shall + be + master"; 6:16 οὐκ = "not", "Do you + know".
- Person words: participles and infinitives carry none. 6:6 "We", 6:9 "we"
  (no first-plural finite verb in either verse), 6:12 "you" (infinitive),
  6:22 "you#0" (both verbs of that clause are participles; ἔχετε has its
  own "you") are `t`. 6:18 is the one change (see above).
- Supplied copulas `t`: 6:17 "be", 6:21 "is", 6:22 "is", 6:23 "is" twice,
  and now 6:16 "you#4 are".
- Lone articles: none carries "of the", "to the" or "in the" (6:4 "of +
  Father", 6:19 "of + flesh", "to + wickedness").
- 6:8 reading: the `partial` record shows 10 source words, 9 matched, 10
  publisher words, so one N1904 word differs. The publisher dropped the
  group holding καί and the verb, so the difference is at συνζήσομεν. The
  review note ("Same word, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text") names
  no publisher spelling, and linking is right for a spelling-only
  difference. No other verse has a `partial` record, and no other reading
  is claimed.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 6:5 "in" on the genitive nouns: agreed (see above). "this" in "like this":
  changed to `t` (see Changes).
- 6:5 and 6:6 "Him" `t`: agreed. σύμφυτοι and συνεσταυρώθη have no pronoun;
  6:4 and 6:8 have αὐτῷ and keep "Him".
- 6:9 "For" on the causal participle εἰδότες: agreed; it is the same
  structure as Romans 5:3 (εἰδότες = "because + know").
- 6:20 "free of obligation" on ἐλεύθεροι: kept as the publisher's row. It
  renders ἐλεύθεροι with the dative of respect and is not an over-claim.
- 6:13 ζῶντας = "those who + to life", "have been brought" `t`: agreed.
  "those who" renders the participle made into a relative clause.
- 6:22 "that" on the participle, "now" on νυνί: agreed. "that" does the
  subordinating work of the participle, like "when/as"; νυνί means only
  "now".

## Still uncertain

- 6:4 συνετάφημεν … αὐτῷ has "with Him" on αὐτῷ (publisher row, Colossians
  2:12), while 6:8 συνζήσομεν αὐτῷ has "live with" on the verb and "Him" on
  αὐτῷ (publisher's αὐτῷ row). Both are acceptable homes for "with" after a
  συν- verb; they are left as the publisher rows have them.
- 6:9 "cannot … again" on οὐκέτι: older reviewed chapters sometimes join the
  negative and verb under one "cannot" (1 John 5:18, 2 Timothy 2:9). The
  split keeps the current negative rule and is kept.
- 6:19 εἰς τὴν ἀνομίαν = "escalating" with ἀνομίᾳ = "to + wickedness": the
  publisher had put "wickedness" on the second ἀνομίαν. The author's move
  keeps "to" with the dative parallel to "to impurity" and is kept; joining
  ἀνομίᾳ εἰς τὴν ἀνομίαν = "escalating wickedness" is the alternative.
