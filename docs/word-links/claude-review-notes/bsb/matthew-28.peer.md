Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB.
Textual claims were checked against the publisher's own Greek, using the

## Changes (2 verses)

- 28:15: ἀργύρια = "the money" → ἀργύρια = "money", plus
  `t the` = "BSB follows the reading τὰ ἀργύρια; N1904 has ἀργύρια without
  the article." Why: the publisher's Greek has 19 words against 18 in N1904,
  and all 18 N1904 words match. The extra word is the article τά (ἡμέρας is
  in both texts). The publisher row also linked ἀργύρια to "money" alone, so
  BSB's "the" renders τά. Rule 9: say so, don't fold it into the noun.
- 28:14: the `t him` reason "English supplies the object; N1904 has none
  after πείσομεν (many manuscripts read πείσομεν αὐτόν)" → "BSB follows the
  reading πείσομεν αὐτόν; N1904 has no object after πείσομεν." Why: the
  publisher's Greek has 14 words against 13, all 13 N1904 words match, and
  the extra word is αὐτόν. So "him" renders a word in BSB's text. It is not
  an English addition. The link decision (`t`) is unchanged.

Nothing else was changed. The author's other corrections of publisher rows
are right. These include Μή = not with "Do" on φοβεῖσθε (as in 17:7), the
genitive absolutes in 28:11 and 28:13, the `t and` lines after participles,
ὑμεῖς `s` in 28:5, the article + participle joins, and "of" moving to
μαθητεύσατε in 28:19.

## Textual claims

- 28:14 αὐτόν and 28:15 τά are the only differences between the publisher's
  Greek and N1904 in this chapter. The other 18 verses have equal word
  counts and no edition difference.
- So 28:2 καὶ προσελθών `s` is a BSB translation choice, as the author says.
  It is not a reading. The second "Him" in 28:17 is not a reading either:
  BSB's Greek has no προσεκύνησαν αὐτῷ.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 28:17, one αὐτόν = Him + Him: **agree.** Neither BSB's Greek nor N1904
  has a second pronoun, so a reading reason would be false. The one αὐτόν
  is the object of ἰδόντες and the understood object of προσεκύνησαν. This
  is the same pattern as 21:39 (one αὐτόν = him + him + him).
- 28:1, `t day`: **agree.** μίαν is a feminine numeral that implies ἡμέραν
  and has no Greek noun. This matches `t son` for δευτέρῳ in 21:30.
  "first" stays linked to μίαν.
- 28:14, "him" and ἀκουσθῇ … ἐπί = reaches: **agree with both links**, but
  the "him" reason was reworded (see Changes). Joining ἐπί to "reaches" is
  fair: "is heard before the governor" becomes a transitive English verb.
  This is like "make disciples of" absorbing the object relation in 28:19.
- 28:3, ἦν = was + were: **agree.** ἦν is gapped over the verbless second
  clause, so "were" renders it. This is the same treatment as εἰσιν =
  are + are in 22:14.
- 28:15, τῆς σήμερον: **agree with the current split.** The "this very"
  sense comes from σήμερον ("today's"). τῆς is the attributive article of
  τῆς … ἡμέρας and has no distinct English word. The publisher's τῆς =
  "this" was wrong.
- 28:18, αὐτοῖς = them: **agree.** It is the only "them" in the English,
  and it names the people addressed. BSB moved it from "spoke to them" to
  "came to them", but it still renders αὐτοῖς.
- 28:20, ἕως = even to: **keep.** "even unto/to" is a stock English
  rendering of ἕως (compare the KJV) and only adds emphasis. Moving "even"
  to `t` would be a matter of taste.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 28:14 "report": BSB may have turned the passive ἀκουσθῇ ("be heard") into
  the noun "report" (a thing heard) plus "reaches". Then "report" would
  belong with ἀκουσθῇ … ἐπί. `t` (a noun supplied for τοῦτο) is also
  defensible, so it was kept.
- 28:20 "even" (see above); `t even` would also be defensible.
