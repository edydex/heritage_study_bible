# BSB Romans 1: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 32 of 32 verses

Started from the author's final file. Ran `totext --all`, checked every line of
every verse against the Greek, BSB's English and the publisher's record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/romans.json`, then ran
`fromtext` again. Final check: `{"versesOk": 32, "problems": 0}`. One verse
changed (1:17). The other 31 verses are unchanged.

## Change

- **1:17 ἐκ = "by", πίστεως = "faith", εἰς πίστιν = "from start to finish" →
  ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν = "by faith from start to finish" (one group).**
  The Greek says "from faith to faith". BSB states "faith" once and turns the
  whole "from … to …" phrase into the English idiom "from start to finish".
  The "from … to" frame of that idiom comes from ἐκ … εἰς together, and
  "start" answers to the first member as much as "finish" answers to the
  second. Εἰς πίστιν alone means "to faith". Linked by itself to "from start
  to finish", it would show a reader BSB's reading of the whole phrase as
  the meaning of two words. This is the same case as reviewed Luke 1:50,
  where εἰς γενεὰς καὶ γενεάς is joined as "from generation to generation".
  New note (literal only, no interpretation): "ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν ("from
  faith to faith") is rendered by faith from start to finish." "That comes"
  stays `t`. The quotation at the end of the verse keeps ἐκ = "by" and
  πίστεως = "faith" word by word, because there BSB renders each word
  (as in reviewed Galatians 3:11).

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **1:17 εἰς πίστιν = "from start to finish":** changed (above). The
  publisher's split ("that comes by" / "faith from start" / "to" / "finish")
  was clearly wrong, and the author was right to replace it.
- **1:5 εἰς = "to" before "the obedience", "to call" `t`: agree, kept.** Εἰς
  ("into, for") is rendered by the "to" of "to the obedience". No Greek word
  means "call". The publisher's εἰς = "to call" over-claimed, and its
  ὑπακοήν = "to the obedience" put a preposition on the noun. "Those" (`t`)
  and "that comes" (`t`) are supplied words, as in 1 Timothy 1:5.
- **1:26 "Even" `t`, τε and γάρ `s`: agree, kept.** "Even" is a focusing
  adverb, not a connective. It does not mean "for" (γάρ), and it does not mean
  "and/both" (τε). This τε opens the pair τε … τε καί (1:27, "both the women
  … and likewise the men"). BSB has no "both", so the brief's τε rule gives
  `s`. This is the same case as Acts 25:19, where δέ = "only" was rejected.
  Since "Even" does not render either word, `s` plus `t` is not a split pair.
- **1:7 τοῖς οὖσιν = "who are": agree, kept.** Τοῖς οὖσιν means "who are", and
  BSB has exactly one "who are". It moved after "in Rome" when BSB merged the
  location and "loved". `s` for τοῖς οὖσιν with `t` for "who are" would be a
  split pair. The publisher's ἀγαπητοῖς = "who are loved" put a copula on an
  adjective.
- **1:6 οἷς = "those who": agree, kept.** Οἷς is the only relative pronoun in
  the clause. BSB renders it as "those who", a normal rendering of a relative
  (as οἵ = "those who"). Its referent (the Gentiles of 1:5) is the "those" of
  BSB's English. "Who" does not claim any meaning beyond the relative.
  "Are#1" (`t`) and sentence-initial "And" (`t`) are correct.
- **1:12 ὑμῶν = "you", ἐν ὑμῖν `s`: agree, kept.** BSB's "you and I" follows
  the pair ὑμῶν τε καὶ ἐμοῦ ("yours and mine"), so καί = "and" and ἐμοῦ = "I"
  line up with it. Ἐν ὑμῖν ("among you") is folded into that subject. Τε is
  `s` (no "both"), and "that#1" before the infinitive is `t`.
- **1:13 "I might have + I + had", "have#2" `t`: agree, kept.** The comparison
  καθὼς καὶ ἐν τοῖς λοιποῖς ἔθνεσιν has no verb. BSB repeats σχῶ in the same
  role and meaning ("have [a harvest]" → "had"). The understood-again rule
  therefore links the repeat. "I" belongs to the repeated finite verb, as in
  reviewed John 18:23 (ἐλάλησα = "I said + I spoke"). It is not a supplied
  subject for a participle. The perfect auxiliary "have" adds a tense the
  Greek does not state, so it stays `t`. Making all of "I have had" `t`
  would hide that BSB repeats σχῶ.
- **1:15 οὕτως = "That is why", "so" `t`: agree, kept.** Here οὕτως is
  inferential ("thus, accordingly"), and "That is why" carries that sense.
  "So" in "so eager" marks degree, which οὕτως does not mean in this
  sentence. Τὸ κατ’ ἐμὲ πρόθυμον has no degree word.
- **1:23 ὁμοιώματι εἰκόνος = "images" (joined): agree, kept.** The two nouns
  are near-synonyms ("likeness of an image"), and "images" means both. The
  note gives the literal Greek, so nothing is over-claimed.
- **1:30 "new" `t`: agree, kept.** No Greek word means "new". BSB adds it to
  bring out ἐφευρετάς ("inventors").

## Other points checked

- **1:21 οὐχ = "neither": agree.** Οὐ … ἤ is rendered "neither … nor". The
  negative keeps "neither", and the verb keeps "they + glorified".
- **"They" with participles `t` (1:21 γνόντες, 1:22 φάσκοντες, 1:29
  πεπληρωμένους): agree.** A participle has no person. In 1:21 and 1:22 the
  "they" after "although" is `t`, and the finite verbs carry their own "they".
  In 1:32 the first "they" goes on οἵτινες, the explicit subject, and not on
  the participle ἐπιγνόντες. The second "they" goes on ποιοῦσιν.
- **1:21 "in" on καρδία ("in + hearts"): kept.** BSB turns the subject "their
  foolish heart was darkened" into "darkened in their foolish hearts". The
  note says so. "In" marks the noun's role in BSB's new sentence and sits on
  that noun, as "of" does on σώματα in 1:24. A stricter reading could make it
  `t` (see below).
- **Theological terms:** εὐαγγέλιον, χάρις, πίστις, δικαιοσύνη, ὀργή and
  δικαίωμα are linked word by word to BSB's English. The notes give only
  literal meaning, never interpretation. The new 1:17 note does the same.
- **Readings:** none of the 32 verses in romans.json has a `partial` record,
  so the publisher's Greek equals N1904 throughout. No different reading is
  claimed anywhere, and there are no cross-verse links.
- **Brief rules:** supplied copulas (1:1, 1:4, 1:6, 1:7, 1:15, 1:29, 1:31),
  "it" (1:19), objects "Him"/"to Him" (1:21), "them" (1:32), "preaching"
  (1:9), "from visiting" (1:13) and the added adverb "so" (1:15) are all `t`.
  The "and" with no καί is `t` in 1:1, 1:4, 1:5, 1:7, 1:27 (joining ἀφέντες),
  1:29 and 1:30. The participle-chain καί in 1:27 (κατεργαζόμενοι καὶ …
  ἀπολαμβάνοντες) is linked to BSB's "and". Connectives rendered by other
  connectives stay linked: 1:13 καί = "but", 1:16 γάρ = "because" and καί =
  "then", 1:19 διότι = "For" and γάρ = "because", 1:28 καί = "Furthermore".
  Τε is linked only in 1:14, where BSB says "both … and". No lone article
  carries "of the"/"to the"/"in the" (1:9, 1:19, 1:20, 1:23, 1:24, 1:25,
  1:27 checked). The articular infinitive τοῦ ἀτιμάζεσθαι (1:24) is joined.

## Still uncertain

- 1:21 "in" on καρδία: `t` ("English supplies in when it turns the subject
  καρδία into a phrase") would also be defensible, because καρδία is
  nominative and no Greek case gives "in". I kept the author's link because
  the note makes the restructuring plain.
- 1:6 "who": `t` would also be defensible, because BSB's "who are called"
  attaches κλητοί to "those" while οἷς is the object of ἐν.
- 1:10 ἤδη = "now", ποτέ = "at last" (kept from the publisher). Ποτέ alone
  means "at some time". Joining ἤδη ποτέ = "now at last" would be an
  equally honest alternative.
