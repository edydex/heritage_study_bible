## Changes

- 10:7 ἑαυτῷ: `g 7 = he#0` → `s 7` ("BSB leaves ἑαυτῷ ('in himself')
  unexpressed after is confident"), and "he#0" is now `t` (the Greek
  infinitive has no person). In πέποιθεν ἑαυτῷ Χριστοῦ εἶναι the reflexive
  dative means "in himself". BSB's "he" is the subject of the infinitive
  εἶναι, a different word and role. The brief's rule puts person words only
  on finite verbs. Reviewed Acts 10:17, Mark 5:30 and John 11:38 make an
  unrendered ἐν ἑαυτῷ `s` the same way.
- 10:5 `g @10:4 15 = arguments`: I added the note "N1904 prints λογισμοὺς
  καθαιροῦντες at the end of 10:4; BSB renders them at the start of 10:5."
  This matches the noted cross-verse rows in Romans 3:25 and Acts 5:40.
  Only the note changed.
- 10:7 Χριστοῦ (token 18): the note "the Greek states it once with αὐτός" is
  now "The Greek has Χριστοῦ in the clause with αὐτός ('he'); BSB moves it
  to the we-clause." The old wording was unclear. The link is unchanged.

## Points checked

- 10:4/10:5 verse division: confirmed. In the publisher record for 10:4,
  2 of N1904's 17 words are unmatched, and BSB's Greek has 15 words. In the
  record for 10:5, all 18 N1904 words are matched, and BSB's Greek has 20
  words. So 2 words move from 10:4 to 10:5. BSB's 10:5 begins "We tear down
  arguments", which is λογισμοὺς καθαιροῦντες. The mechanics match the
  reviewed Romans 3:25–26 and Acts 5:39–40: `m 15 16 > 10:5` in the source
  verse, `g @10:4 15` and `g @10:4 16` in the target verse. "We" is `t`
  (participle). No other verse claims a different reading, and every other
  verse's record has no `partial` key.
- 10:2: BSB reads "I beg you that when I come I may not need to be as bold
  as I expect toward those who presume that we live according to the
  flesh". I kept the author's split:
  - τὸ … θαρρῆσαι = "that + may + be + bold" (τό joined to its infinitive);
    "I#2" and "need to" are `t`.
  - "may" on the infinitive has precedent. The clause has no Greek finite
    verb, and reviewed Romans 15:9 and 15:13 put "may" on an infinitive
    that English turns into a that-clause, with the person `t`. Here δέομαι
    is "I beg" and λογίζομαι is "I expect", so neither can own "may".
  - τῇ πεποιθήσει stays `s` and "as#0" `t`. Linking the noun "confidence"
    to "as" would be an over-claim.
  - ᾗ = "as#1" (the relative of the comparison); τολμῆσαι `s` (a repeated
    infinitive that BSB does not render).
- 10:9: BSB has no "as if". In the Greek, ὡς ἄν with the infinitive means
  "as if, as it were". "to be trying" adds an attempt that ὡς ἄν does not
  mean. So ὡς ἄν `s` with "to#1 be trying" `t` is right, even though the
  phrase stands where ὡς ἄν stands. ἵνα = "to#0" with "do"/"want" `t`
  matches 2 Thessalonians 3:9, and δόξω = "I + seem".
- 10:13: κανών/μέτρον are consistent. κανών is BSB's "field of
  influence" (10:13), "area of influence" (10:15) and "territory"
  (10:16). κατὰ τὸ μέτρον = "within" folds the noun into the preposition.
  μέτρου is the measure that ἐφικέσθαι ("to reach") explains, so it takes
  the appositive "a field". ἐφικέσθαι = "reaches", and "that#1" is `t`.
- 10:7: οὕτως καί = "just as much", καθώς = "as#1", αὐτός = "he#2"; "belong
  to#1" and "does" are `t` (supplied verbs in a clause without a verb).
  ἑαυτῷ changed, as above.
- 10:16: "so that" on the result infinitive εὐαγγελίσασθαι is kept. It
  renders that infinitive's function, as in Romans 15:9. "regions" and
  "work" are supplied nouns for τὰ ὑπερέκεινα and τὰ ἕτοιμα (`t`). The
  author's swap of "in#1" and "in#2" is correct: ἐν goes with "another man's
  territory" and εἰς with "the work already done".
- 10:18 cleft: confirmed. ἐκεῖνος = "who#1" (resumptive pronoun ↔ cleft
  relative), ἐστιν = "is#1", "it is#0" `t`.
- 10:17: identical word by word to the reviewed 1 Corinthians 1:31.
  Ὁ … καυχώμενος = "him who boasts", ἐν = "in", Κυρίῳ = "the Lord",
  καυχάσθω = "Let + boast". δέ = "Rather".
- Brief rules: I checked every row against them, and no further rows needed
  changes:
  - supplied copulas, place words, objects, possessives and nouns are `t`
    (10:4 "are", "they have"; 10:5 "make it"; 10:6 "act of"; 10:8 "even";
    10:13 "our", "only");
  - a participle carries no person;
  - no lone article carries "of the" or "to the";
  - person and modal words sit on finite verbs, or on an infinitive only
    where precedent allows it.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 10:13 κατὰ τὸ μέτρον = "within": agree. The noun is folded into BSB's
  preposition. Leaving μέτρον `s` would suggest that BSB ignores it.
- 10:7 ἑαυτῷ = "he#0": disagree. Changed to `s` + `t` (see Changes).
- 10:9 ὡς ἄν: agree with `s` + `t`. "trying" means something different
  from "as if".
- 10:2 four-way split: agree. Joining τολμῆσαι to the same "bold" would link
  two Greek infinitives to one English word. The repeated infinitive that
  BSB leaves out is `s`.
- 10:16 "so that" on εὐαγγελίσασθαι: agree (Romans 15:9).

## Still uncertain

- 10:10 "his#0" sits on τοῦ (σώματος), as the publisher had it. ἡ (with
  παρουσία) would be an equally good owner. I left it, because neither is
  wrong.
- 10:2 τινας = "those": the indefinite "some" is rendered as the
  demonstrative. The other option, τινας `s` with τοὺς λογιζομένους = "those
  who presume", is also defensible. I kept the publisher/author row.
