## Changes (2 verses)

- **2:19**: the author joined τῶν ἐν σκότει as "for those in darkness". I
  split it: τῶν = "for those", ἐν = "in", σκότει = "darkness". Here τῶν is an
  article used as a pronoun ("those"). Such an article is a real word and may
  carry its case word. Precedents: the Acts 7 peer note (articles used as
  pronouns); Galatians 2:12, where τοὺς ἐκ περιτομῆς = "those" + "in" + "the
  circumcision group"; Acts 6:9, where τῶν ἀπὸ Κιλικίας = "men" + "from" +
  "Cilicia"; Hebrews 10:33, where τῶν = "with those who". Joining
  the phrase had taken away the links for ἐν and σκότει.
- **2:8**: the author joined τοῖς ἐξ ἐριθείας as "for those who" +
  "self-seeking". I split it: τοῖς = "for those who" and ἐξ ἐριθείας =
  "self-seeking". The reason is the same as in 2:19. τοῖς is the pronoun
  "those" for all three descriptions in the verse. BSB has no English word for
  ἐξ alone, so ἐξ ἐριθείας stays one unit: a prepositional phrase rendered by
  an adjective, as with 2:27 ἐκ φύσεως = "physically". "are" stays `t`.

## Checked and confirmed

- **2:3**: confirmed. BSB reads "pass judgment on others, yet do the same
  things". "the same things" is the object of "do", so it belongs to αὐτά, the
  object of ποιῶν. It does not belong to τοιαῦτα, which is the object of the
  participle that BSB shortens to "others". τοὺς … πράσσοντας is an article +
  participle unit (rule 3) naming the people, so it carries "others". τὰ
  τοιαῦτα is `s`, and its note is accurate. ἄνθρωπε = "you" + "man" follows
  the vocative rule. The "you" does not come from a person ending, and
  κρίνων is a participle, so the vocative is the right owner. ὁ κρίνων =
  "when" + "pass judgment on" follows the convention that "when" belongs to
  the participle, not to δέ, and δέ = "So" is linked.
- **2:16**: confirmed. The EVIDENCE line shows 0 N1904 words unmatched and 1
  extra publisher word. In the draft, "when" had no transferred owner. N1904's
  ᾗ is a relative ("on which day" = "on the day when"), not an article, so
  "when" renders it with the same meaning (rule 9), and ἡμέρᾳ = "the day". The
  review-file note says only "BSB's Greek text has an extra word here". It
  does not name the publisher's word.
- **2:15**: confirmed. καί = "and" joins the two genitive absolutes. "also"
  renders the συν- of συνμαρτυρούσης. τῶν = "their" is an article used as a
  possessive. ἤ = "either … or" is one English correlative for one
  disjunction, as in Mark 16:13, where οὐδέ = "but … not … either". μεταξὺ
  ἀλλήλων and the second καί are `s`. "that", "is" and "them" are `t`. The
  EVIDENCE line shows 1 word unmatched and 1 extra, and the draft left
  "bearing witness" without an owner, which fits a spelling difference. The
  note ("Same word, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text") does not state
  the publisher's spelling.
- **Person rule**: confirmed. "you" sits on the finite verbs in 2:17
  (ἐπονομάζῃ, ἐπαναπαύῃ), 2:18 (γινώσκεις), 2:19 (πέποιθας), 2:21 and 2:22
  (the questions) and 2:23 (καυχᾶσαι, ἀτιμάζεις). The "you"/"they" of a
  participle clause is `t` in 2:14 (ἔχοντες), 2:18 (κατηχούμενος) and 2:20
  (ἔχοντα). 2:27 "you#1" has no verb and is `t`. In 2:21 and 2:22, "You who …"
  on ὁ + participle matches KEEP 2:1.
- **2:10**: the swap is right: Ἰουδαίῳ = "for the Jew" and πρῶτον =
  "first". **2:12**: "also" belongs to the adverbial καί, so ἀπολοῦνται
  keeps "will + perish". **2:27/2:29**: ἡ = "The" and ὁ = "a man"; neither
  article carries a copula now. 2:29 "is" is `t`, and 2:27 "are" is `t`.
- **Other brief rules**: these supplied words are `t`:
  - copulas and "there": 2:8 and 2:9 "there will be", 2:13 the cleft "it is
    … who", 2:15 "is", 2:28 and 2:29 "is";
  - supplied verbs and objects: 2:7 "He will give", 2:14 "requires", 2:15
    "them", 2:16 "proclaimed by", 2:29 "does … come";
  - added adverbs: 2:28 "only", 2:27 "yet".

  In 2:9 and 2:10, BSB says "first …, then", so τε is `s` and καί = "then".
  Connectives rendered by a different connective stay linked (2:3 δέ "So",
  2:5 καί "when", 2:14 γάρ "Indeed", 2:17-19 καί/τε "if", 2:29 ἀλλά "No").
- **Lone articles**: I scanned the chapter by script. Apart from "the", lone
  articles carry only allowed things: a pronoun (2:8 τοῖς, 2:19 τῶν, 2:14 τά
  "what", 2:28 and 2:29 ὁ "A man"/"a man"), a possessive (2:15 τῶν "their",
  2:18 τό "His"), the relative "who" (2:27 τόν), and KEEP 2:1. No "of the" or
  "to the" sits on a lone article.
- **Readings**: there are only two reading claims, 2:15 and 2:16. They are
  the only verses whose EVIDENCE line shows a difference.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **2:3** αὐτά / τοὺς … πράσσοντας = "others": I agree (see above). τοὺς
  alone = "others" would break up the rule 3 unit for no gain.
- **2:3** "you" on ἄνθρωπε: I agree. The vocative rule applies, and 2:3 has
  no ὁ = "you who" wording like 2:1.
- **2:16** ᾗ = "when": I agree. Keeping the publisher's ᾗ = "the" would label
  a relative as an article.
- **2:19** τῶν ἐν σκότει joined: changed, so each word now has its own link
  (see above). I did the same in 2:8.
- **2:5** καί = "when": I keep the publisher row. καί is the word that joins
  "wrath" and "revelation", and BSB turns that join into "when …". This
  matches the convention that "when" can stand for a καί between two clauses.
- **2:15** οἵτινες = "So they": I keep it. ὅστις here has its explanatory
  sense ("in that they"), and BSB's "So" carries that link. This is not a
  case of "So" added with no Greek link-word (John 4, 13, 21). ἤ = "either …
  or": I keep it (see above).
- **2:16** κατά = "as" with "proclaimed by" `t`: I agree. "proclaimed" is a
  supplied verb, treated like "requires" in 2:14 and "come" in 2:29.
- **2:27** "yet" `t`: I agree. τελοῦσα is attributive. The contrast comes
  from setting it next to ἀκροβυστία, not from an adverbial use of the
  participle, unlike the concessive ἔχοντες in 2:14.
- **2:28/2:29** ὁ = "A man"/"a man": I agree. An article that stands as a
  noun may carry its English (Acts 6:9).
- **2:11** ἐστιν = "does + show": I agree. The existential verb takes the
  verb slot in BSB's recast. οὐ is split off as "not", and παρά is `s`.

## KEEP verses (not edited)

- **2:1**: the meaning is right. Two small points only. First, each ὁ κρίνων
  is linked as article and participle separately; rule 3 would join them.
  Second, the final ὁ carries "you#3 who", although that "you" is also the
  subject of πράσσεις ("you … do"). Separately, "on#0 another" is `t`, while
  2:3 puts "on" on κρίνων ("pass judgment on"). This is harmless, because the
  object "another" is supplied.
- **2:25**: no problem found.

## Still uncertain

- 2:26 "a man who is not circumcised" and 2:27 "one who is + uncircumcised"
  keep "is" inside the noun's group, while 2:8 "are" is `t`. I left both. In
  2:26 and 2:27, one noun is unpacked into a whole relative clause, and the
  copula is part of that rendering, as in John 18:16, where τοῦ γνωστοῦ =
  "who was known". In 2:8, the article and the phrase are linked separately,
  so "are" is a free supplied copula. A strict reading of "a supplied copula
  is always `t`" would move "is" to `t` in 2:26 and 2:27 as well.
- 2:15 "So" on οἵτινες: this is defensible, but `t` is a reasonable
  alternative.
