Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 34, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB.
Textual claims were checked against the publisher's own Greek, as recorded
in the publisher-row statistics of `bsb-word-links/matthew.json` (verses

## Changes (4 verses)

- 20:6: ἑνδεκάτην = "eleventh" plus `t hour` ("N1904 has no ὥραν … the
  Byzantine text adds it") → ἑνδεκάτην = "eleventh hour", with a note that
  the feminine ordinal stands without ὥραν. Why: the publisher's Greek
  matches N1904 word for word in this verse, so BSB follows no reading with
  ὥραν; "hour" is the noun the elliptical ordinal implies. This restores the
  publisher row and matches Hebrews 4:4 (ἑβδόμης = "seventh day").
- 20:8: `t them` reason "English supplies the recipients of the pay" →
  "BSB follows the reading ἀπόδος αὐτοῖς ("pay them"); N1904 has no αὐτοῖς."
  Why: the publisher's Greek has one more word than N1904 here, and all 25
  N1904 words match. "them" renders that αὐτοῖς (rule 9).
- 20:26: note on ἐστίν = "It shall + be" changed from "fits the reading
  ἔσται or a prescriptive present" → "BSB follows the reading ἔσται ("it
  shall be")". Why: in the publisher's Greek, ἐστίν is the one N1904 word
  with no match. The link is unchanged.
- 20:10: note on αὐτοί = "of them": gloss "they too" → "they". Why: "too"
  comes from καί, which is linked separately to "also".

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 20:17 Μέλλων ἀναβαίνειν = As + was going up: **agree, kept.** The
  publisher's Greek has 18 words and lacks Μέλλων, δέ and ἀναβαίνειν. That
  fits Καὶ ἀναβαίνων ὁ Ἰησοῦς … τοὺς δώδεκα μαθητάς. In N1904 the
  participle Μέλλων gives the temporal frame ("As") and ἀναβαίνειν the
  "going up". The joint group with the reading note is the honest link.
  Making Μέλλων `s` would leave "As" with no Greek counterpart.
- 20:2 "to pay" as `t`, ἐκ δηναρίου = a denarius: **agree.** ἐκ marks the
  price. It is a preposition, not a verb of paying. The publisher's
  μετά = "to pay" and ἐκ = "for" were both wrong.
- 20:9 οἱ = "The workers": **agree.** The article makes "about the eleventh
  hour" a noun phrase ("those"), and English names the group "The workers".
  "who were hired" stays `t`.
- 20:21 middle "Your" as `t`: **agree.** The publisher's Greek has exactly
  one more word than N1904 here, and all 28 N1904 words match. That word is
  the σου after δεξιῶν.
- 20:6 "still" and 20:12 "only" as `t`: **agree, kept.** Both are English
  emphasis with no Greek word. ἄλλους and μίαν are linked to "others" and
  "one". The publisher rows were not wrong, but the author's split is
  accurate, and reverting it would be taste.

## Also checked and kept

- Split pairs: none found. In 20:3, 20:5, 20:6, 20:9, 20:20, 20:25 and 20:32
  the `t and` comes from a participle turned into a finite verb. The `s`
  καί/δέ in the same verse joins different clauses.
- 20:20 αὐτῷ = "to Jesus" follows the Matthew 19 convention (BSB names
  Jesus for αὐτῷ). τῶν = "of" with Ζεβεδαίου = "Zebedee’s" correctly
  removes the publisher's over-claimed "of Zebedee’s".
- 20:5 ὥραν = hour + hour and 20:16 ἔσονται = will be + will be are correct
  one-word, two-copy links.
- 20:23 ἀλλά `s` plus `t These seats belong`: correct. ἀλλά does not mean
  "These seats belong", and the clause is an ellipsis.
- No note mentions Russian.

## Still uncertain

- 20:2 μετὰ τῶν ἐργατῶν = "them": acceptable, but the English recasts "agreed
  with the workers" as "agreed to pay them".
- 20:30 ἰδού = "there were" and 20:10 αὐτοί = "of them" are publisher rows.
  They are kept as functional renderings, not literal ones.
- 20:18: the publisher's Greek differs from N1904 in one word, probably the
  form of θάνατον. "death" still renders the same noun, so the link is
  unaffected.
- The author note lists 20:6 "hour" under textual differences. Per the
  publisher's Greek, BSB has no ὥραν there either; it is ellipsis.
