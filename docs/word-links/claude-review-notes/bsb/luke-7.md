# BSB Luke 7: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 50 (7:1-50). No verse was kept from an earlier review (the
draft had no KEEP blocks). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 50, "problems": 0}`.

172 of the 731 publisher `g` rows changed. 17 of them only gained unlinked
Greek tokens, with the English unchanged (for example κακῶς ἔχων = sick 7:2,
καὶ ἰδού = He saw 7:12, ἀποκριθεὶς … εἶπεν = He replied / answered / replied
7:22, 7:40, 7:43, ὃς ἐάν = the one who 7:23, ἄνθρωπος φάγος = glutton 7:34,
ἀφ’ ἧς = since 7:45, οὗ χάριν = Therefore 7:47). The other 155 rows, in 44
verses, were corrected, split or moved. Matthew 8:5-13 was followed for the
centurion (7:1-10), Matthew 11:2-19 for John's question and Jesus' testimony
(7:18-35), and Mark 1:2 for the Malachi quotation (7:27).

- 7:4: ἐστιν "This man is" → is, `t This man`. 7:27: ἐστιν "is the one" → is, `t the one` (as Matthew 11:10).

Negatives and auxiliaries split off verbs:

"When", "As", "After" moved from καί/δέ/ἤδη to the participle or genitive absolute:

- 7:3, 7:9, 7:20, 7:39: δέ "When" → `s`; the participle = When + heard/came/saw.

## Notable decisions

- Verse division: N1904 ends 7:18 with καὶ προσκαλεσάμενος δύο τινὰς τῶν μαθητῶν αὐτοῦ ὁ Ἰωάνης, which BSB prints as the start of 7:19 ("So John called two of his disciples"). EVIDENCE confirms the difference. These tokens are linked across with `m 9 10 11 14 15 17 > 7:19` in 7:18 and `g @7:18 …` in 7:19; τινάς and the articles stay `s` in 7:18. This is the only cross-verse link.
- 7:18: BSB swaps name and pronoun: Ἰωάνει ("to John") = him, αὐτοῦ ("his") = John’s.
- Readings: the "differs" EVIDENCE lines in 7:20, 7:22, 7:24, 7:28, 7:29, 7:33 and 7:18-19 are the spelling of John (linked under rule 8 with "Same name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text"). 7:45 differs in one word: διέλειπεν is linked to "has … stopped" with the note "same verb, in a slightly different form" (probably the aorist διέλιπεν for N1904's imperfect). 7:47 shows 0 unmatched words, so no reading is claimed.
- 7:47: ὅτι = because, with a note that BSB moves "because" to the forgiveness clause while the Greek ὅτι stands before "she loved much". Leaving it `s` with `t because` would be a split pair.
- 7:2: the relative clause ὃς ἦν αὐτῷ is `s`; ἔντιμος = highly valued; `t There`.
- 7:11: καί = "accompanied by" kept (publisher row): it adds the crowd as a second subject of "went with".
- 7:12: καὶ ἰδού = He saw (joined, as καὶ ἰδού = came along in Luke 5:12).
- Pronouns rendered by nouns are linked with a note: αὐτόν = Jesus (7:20, 7:36, 7:39), αὐτοῦ = Jesus (7:17), αὐτοῦ = John (7:30), αὐτόν = the centurion (7:9).

## Doubts for the peer

- 7:19-20: ὁ ἐρχόμενος is joined (rule 3) as "the One who was to come"; Matthew 11:3 splits it ("the One who" on the article, "was to come" on the participle).
- 7:25: οἱ … ὑπάρχοντες ("those being [in]") = those who + wear + live, and ἐν = in#1 only. Linking ἐν = wear + in#1 and ὑπάρχοντες = live is the alternative.
- 7:22: the one καί of the Greek list (before κωφοί) is linked to BSB's list-final "and" (before "the good news"). The alternative is `s` + `t and`, a split pair.
- 7:4: ᾧ ("for whom") = "to#1" and παρέξῃ = "have You grant". Leaving ᾧ `s` and putting "to have You grant" on the verb is the alternative.
- 7:37: "there" = ἐν … οἰκίᾳ, with τοῦ Φαρισαίου `s`. Joining the whole phrase to "there" would put a content word on a word that does not mean it.
- 7:8: τασσόμενος ("placed") is `s`; BSB's "a man under authority" has no word for it.
