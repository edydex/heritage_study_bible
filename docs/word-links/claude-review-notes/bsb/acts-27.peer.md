# BSB Acts 27: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 44 of 44 verses

Started from the author's final file. Ran `totext --all` and checked every line
of every verse against the Greek, BSB's English and the publisher's record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`. Worked in four
sections (27:1-10, 11-20, 21-30, 31-44), then ran `fromtext` again. Final
check: `{"versesOk": 44, "problems": 0}`. One note changed (27:7). No link
changed, and the other 43 verses are unchanged.

## Change

- **27:7 καί `s`: reason corrected.** Before: "BSB starts a new sentence and
  leaves καί untranslated." After: "The καί joins the participles
  βραδυπλοοῦντες and γενόμενοι; BSB makes the first an After-phrase and leaves
  καί untranslated." BSB's new sentence starts later, at "When the wind
  impeded us". The καί stands between the two participles, and BSB drops it
  by making the first one "After sailing slowly…". The `s` itself is right,
  because BSB has no "and" in that clause.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **27:9-10 "them" `t`, λέγων αὐτοῖς `s`: agree.** The EVIDENCE lines for
  27:9 and 27:10 both say the publisher's Greek matches N1904 word for word,
  and both editions start 27:10 with λέγων. So this is BSB rewording the
  sentence, not a difference in where the verses divide. Under the convention,
  links that cross a verse are used only for real verse-division differences
  (Acts 13:38-39). The two notes already tell readers where αὐτοῖς stands.
- **27:9, 13, 15 δέ `s`, "So" `t`: agree, and the three verses are
  consistent.** In each verse δέ opens the Greek sentence and links it to the
  verse before. BSB's "So" comes later. It opens BSB's second sentence and
  marks a result: from the genitive absolutes (27:9, 15), or from δόξαντες
  (27:13), to the main verb. That is an added inference in a different slot,
  not δέ rendered where δέ stands. The publisher never linked "So" to δέ
  either. It had "So" on Παῦλος (27:9), ἄραντες (27:13) and ἐπιδόντες
  (27:15). Compare 27:30 δέ = "Meanwhile" and 27:26 δέ = "However", where
  BSB's connective does stand in δέ's slot and is rightly linked.
- **27:2, 5, 7, 8 first "we" `t`: agree.** Each first "we" is the subject BSB
  supplies for a participle clause (ἐπιβάντες, διαπλεύσαντες, γενόμενοι,
  παραλεγόμενοι). The rule (Acts 10:29, 17:23, 22:5, 23:27-30) makes that
  `t` even when a finite verb in the verse is first person. Each finite verb
  (ἀνήχθημεν, κατήλθαμεν, ὑπεπλεύσαμεν, ἤλθομεν) keeps its own later "we".
  None of the four verses has ἡμεῖς, so no pronoun can take both. Other "we"
  links checked: 27:15 "we" on the ending of ἐφερόμεθα (BSB's only "we" there,
  as in Acts 21:2 "we + set sail"); 27:20 "we" = ἡμᾶς, the explicit subject of
  τοῦ σῴζεσθαι; 27:18, 26, 27 "we" = ἡμῶν/ἡμᾶς; 27:29 on the ending of
  ἐκπέσωμεν. "They/he" left on participles (27:13, 17, 28, 29, 30, 35, 40)
  follow the corpus convention and were not changed.
- **27:3 "him" on πορευθέντι: agree.** This is not a free supplied object like
  27:4 "us" or 27:40 "them". The dative participle is itself the Greek word
  for the person ἐπέτρεψεν allows (masculine singular, "for him, having
  gone"). The Greek leaves out only αὐτῷ, and the participle's case and
  gender carry it. So "him to visit" renders that word, and πρός `s` (folded
  into visit) is right.
- **27:33 διατελεῖτε = "your + constant": agree.** BSB: "Today is your
  fourteenth day in constant suspense, without taking any food." The only
  second-person marker is the ending of διατελεῖτε, and "constant" carries its
  sense "continue". Under the person rule, the "your" from that ending
  belongs on the finite verb. The rest of the quotation is also correct:
  σήμερον = "Today", "is" `t` (supplied copula), προσδοκῶντες = "in +
  suspense", ἄσιτοι = "without + food", μηθέν = "any", προσλαβόμενοι =
  "taking". "Right" `t` is correct too, since ἄχρι οὗ is "up to" and δέ is
  `s`. The publisher had put "Right" on δέ.
- **27:37 ψυχαί `s`, "of us" on ἤμεθα: agree.** BSB: "In all, there were 276
  of us on board." BSB has no "people", "persons" or "souls", so nothing in
  the English renders ψυχαί. "Of us" comes from the person of the finite
  ἤμεθα, so linking it to ψυχαί would gloss "persons" as "us". "There" `t`
  (added existential) and the joined 276 are correct.
- **27:2 and 27:5 "coast of" `t`: agree.** This is the same pattern as "province
  of" `t` in reviewed Acts 16:6 and 20:16, where the article still keeps
  "the" (τῇ = the). No Greek word means coast. The publisher had also split
  it two different ways: on Ἀσίαν in 27:2 and on κατά in 27:5.

## Other points checked

- **27:35:** καί between εἴπας and λαβών = the "and" before "gave thanks"
  (the "and" that closes the chain, as Acts 14:19 and Matthew 26:26). The
  second καί = "Then". "And began" `t`, "Paul" `t` and "it" `t` are correct.
  27:40 καί = "Then" is the same pattern. BSB attaches ἀνέντες to "left them
  … as they loosened", so καί opens the new sentence, and the later "and made
  for" is `t`.
- **27:36:** καὶ αὐτοί = "themselves", "and" `t`. Here καί is adverbial
  ("they too", as Paul had done), not a link between the participle and the
  verb.
- **Readings:** the EVIDENCE lines and the publisher's own groups agree with
  the author. In 27:5 exactly κατήλθαμεν and Μύρρα are left unmatched. In
  27:16 it is Κλαῦδα, and in 27:19 it is ἔριψαν. BSB's "they threw" matches
  N1904's third person. 27:41 has 0 unmatched words and 2 extra publisher
  words, so "of the waves" `t` with a reading note is right. 27:23 has 1
  extra publisher word and no N1904 word missing. Εἰμι = "I am" covers the
  English, so no reading is claimed. 27:12 differs only in the English word
  count. No note in the review file states the publisher's Greek wording.
- **Nautical terms and numbers:** these link to BSB's English as BSB renders
  them: soundings, fathoms, sea anchor = σκεῦος, ropes = βοηθείαις and
  ζευκτηρίας, foresail, rudders, lifeboat, sandbar = τόπον διθάλασσον, and
  sandbars of Syrtis (Σύρτις itself means the shoals). 276 is joined.
- **Lone articles:** checked in all 44 verses. No "of the", "to the" or "by
  the" sits on a lone article: 27:10, 11, 19, 22, 31, 32 and 40 now have the
  preposition on the noun. 27:40 τῇ πνεούσῃ = "to the wind" is an article +
  participle unit. 27:19 τήν = "the" with τοῦ `s` in "the ship's tackle"
  matches reviewed Acts 11:12 "the man's home".

## Still uncertain (left as is)

- 27:28 "fathoms#0 deep" and 27:39 "a sandy beach" are publisher rows that
  fold a small English measure or descriptor word into the noun. They do not
  mislead, so I left them.
- 27:12 ἐκεῖθεν = "on" ("to sail on") is a loose publisher link. I kept it
  because "on" carries the onward departure from that harbor. The other
  option is ἐκεῖθεν `s` with "on" `t`.
- 27:43 Παῦλον = "Paul's life" (publisher). "Life" is BSB's expansion of
  διασῶσαι ("bring safely through"). The other option is "life" `t`.
