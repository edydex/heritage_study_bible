# BSB Luke 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 44 of 44 verses (4:1-44), every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line, against
N1904, the displayed BSB, the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json`, and the reviewed
parallels Matthew 4 and Mark 1. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 44, "problems": 0}`.

Reading claims: the publisher record has a `partial` entry only for 4:17,
4:25 and 4:26, each with one unmatched word out of an equal word count
(17/16/17, 33/32/33, 15/14/15). That fits a single-word difference in each
verse, so the author's reading notes are justified, but two of them stated
the publisher's spelling or word as fact and are softened below.

## Changes (7 verses: 3 link changes, 4 note-only)

- 4:16-17: `g 21 = And` + `g @4:17 0 = when` (with `m 0 > 4:16` in 4:17) →
  `g 21 = And when` in 4:16 (the publisher's row) and `s 0` for the καί
  opening 4:17. BSB's 4:16/4:17 boundary is the same as N1904's (both put
  "and He stood up to read" in 4:16 and "the scroll was handed" in 4:17), so
  there is no verse-division difference to justify a cross-verse link for a
  connective, and 4:16 has its own καί to carry "And when". Cross-verse
  links are kept for words BSB actually moves across a verse boundary.
- 4:17: note on "Unrolling" no longer states the publisher's word
  (ἀναπτύξας) as fact; it now says BSB follows a different verb with the
  same meaning, since opening a scroll is unrolling it. The link itself
  stays (rule 9, same meaning).
- 4:19: Κυρίου "of the Lord’s" → "the Lord’s"; δεκτόν "favor" →
  "of + favor". The genitive of Κυρίου is already rendered by "’s"; the
  "of" joins "year" to "favor", which renders the adjective δεκτόν
  ("acceptable"). This was the author's open doubt, and leaving "of" on
  Κυρίου gave it two English words for one genitive.
- 4:22: note on ἐμαρτύρουν said "rendered spoke well of", but "of" is
  linked to αὐτῷ ("of Him"); the note now says "spoke well".
- 4:25: note on Ἡλείου → "Same name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek
  text." (it had named the publisher's spelling as fact).
- 4:26: same softening for Ἡλείας.
- 4:35: ἔξελθε "Come" → "Come out"; ἀπ’ "out of" → "of". In Mark 1:25 the
  preposition is ἐξ, which itself means "out of"; Luke has ἀπ’ ("from"), so
  "out" comes from the verb ἐξ-έρχομαι. This also matches 4:41 in this
  chapter (ἐξήρχετο ἀπό = "came out" + "of") and 4:35b (ἐξῆλθεν = "came
  out").

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 4:16-17 "when" across the verse break: not kept (see above). "And when"
  on the καί of 4:16 is the publisher's row and avoids both a cross-verse
  link and a split pair (a `t when` plus an `s` καί in the next verse).
- 4:6 ταύτην = "these", ἅπασαν = "all", `t over`, `t kingdoms`,
  `t all#1`: kept. "these" and "all" do come from ταύτην and ἅπασαν, which
  BSB moves from "authority" to "kingdoms"; making ταύτην `s` with
  `t these` would be a split pair. "over" and "kingdoms" have no Greek
  word, and the second "all" (before "their glory") is BSB's repetition.
- 4:26 εἰς Σάρεπτα = "of Zarephath": kept. The whole εἰς-phrase is recast
  as the widow's town; splitting εἰς off as `s` would leave "of" with no
  owner.
- 4:19 Κυρίου "of the Lord’s": changed (see above). 4:35 ἀπ’ "out of":
  changed (see above).
- 4:40 "with various" on ποικίλαις: kept, as Mark 1:34. In 4:33 "in a" on
  φωνῇ also follows Mark 1:26 ("with a" on φωνῇ), so both verses follow
  their parallel. Also confirmed in 4:40: ὅσοι = "who", ἀσθενοῦντας =
  "were ill", εἶχον `s` and αὐτούς `s`. BSB makes the sick the subject of
  a passive ("all who were ill … were brought"), so εἶχον ("had") has no
  English word and αὐτούς ("them", the sick) is the passive's subject,
  not a separate word.

## Checked and kept (non-obvious)

- Temptation (4:1-13) matches Matthew 4 where the Greek matches: shall +
  live / not (4:4), they will lift + up, so that + not, You will + strike,
  `t their` (4:11), Do + put + to the test (4:12).
- 4:5 `t to a high place` and 4:9 `t Him`: the publisher's Greek equals
  N1904 there, so no reading is claimed.
- 4:6 ὁ διάβολος rendered "he"; 4:7 ἐνώπιον `s` ("worship me").
- 4:10 τοῦ διαφυλάξαι = "to guard" + "carefully".
- 4:18 ἀποστεῖλαι … ἐν ἀφέσει = "to release" (joined unit).
- 4:25 ὡς = "and", μῆνας ἕξ = "a half".
- 4:34 identical to Mark 1:24.

## Still uncertain

- 4:16 "And when" on one καί is the least bad option, not a perfect one:
  the "when" really reflects the parataxis with the καί of 4:17. A later
  reviewer may prefer `t when` with the 4:17 καί `s`.
- 4:35 now differs in pattern from Mark 1:25 ("Come" + "out of"). That is
  deliberate because the Greek prepositions differ (ἐξ vs ἀπ’).
