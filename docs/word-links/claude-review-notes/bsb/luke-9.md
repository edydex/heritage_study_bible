# BSB Luke 9: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 62 (9:1-62). No verse was kept from an earlier review (the
draft had no KEEP blocks). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 62, "problems": 0}`.

The draft had 907 publisher `g` rows. 710 are unchanged. 43 only gained
unlinked Greek tokens, with the English unchanged (for example ὃς ἐάν / ὃς ἄν
= whoever 9:24, 9:48; ὅσοι ἄν = If anyone 9:5; καὶ ἰδού = Suddenly 9:30,
9:38; ἐβόησεν λέγων = cried out 9:38; ἕως πότε = How long 9:41; οἱ σὺν αὐτῷ
= his companions 9:32; πρὸ προσώπου αὐτοῦ = on ahead 9:52; τὸ πρόσωπον
αὐτοῦ = He 9:53). The other 154 rows, in 51 verses, were corrected, split or
moved.

Parallels followed: Mark 6:7-16, 6:30-44 and Matthew 14 for 9:1-17; Mark
8:27-9:1 and Matthew 16:13-28 for 9:18-27; Mark 9:2-10 and Matthew 17:1-8 for
9:28-36; Mark 9:17-40 and Matthew 17:14-23 for 9:37-50; Matthew 8:19-22 for
9:57-60.

## Readings

- Only the EVIDENCE lines of 9:2, 9:7-9, 9:19, 9:24, 9:27-28, 9:30, 9:32-33, 9:39, 9:49-50, 9:52, 9:54 and 9:59 show publisher Greek different from N1904.
- Name spellings (rule 8): John 9:7, 9:9, 9:19, 9:28, 9:49, 9:54; Elijah 9:8, 9:19, 9:30, 9:33; Samaritans 9:52.
- Longer readings (`t`): 9:2 "the sick" (two extra publisher words, probably τοὺς ἀσθενεῖς); 9:59 "Lord" (one extra word, probably κύριε).
- Same meaning, linked with a note (rule 9): 9:32 εἶδαν = they saw (probably εἶδον in BSB's Greek); 9:39 μόλις = rarely (probably μόγις); 9:52 ὥστε = to (probably ὡς).
- 9:9, 9:24, 9:27 and 9:50 differ without changing the English (probably an added article ὁ in 9:9 and 9:50, a verb spelling in 9:24, word order in 9:27); no reading is claimed. 9:27 ἀληθῶς = truthfully is linked.

## Notable decisions

- The "When …, …" convention for καί is applied to δέ in 9:12 (see doubts). Elsewhere "When/As/While" sits on the participle or genitive absolute (9:37, 9:42, 9:43, 9:54, 9:57) or on ἐν τῷ (9:18, 9:29, 9:33, 9:34, 9:36, 9:51).
- Pronouns rendered by nouns are linked with a note: αὐτόν = Jesus (9:9, 9:18, 9:57), αὐτῷ = Jesus (9:10, 9:12, 9:30, 9:37), αὐτοῦ = Jesus (9:32), αὐτός = Jesus (9:51), αὐτούς = Moses and Elijah (9:33), αὐτοῦ = Peter (9:34), αὐτοῦ = the boy (9:42), αὐτοί = The disciples (9:36), αὐτοῖς = the disciples (9:46).
- Repeated English for one Greek word: δύναμιν = power#0 + power#1 (9:1), δεῖ = must ×3 (9:22), ἐν/δόξῃ repeated "in the glory" (9:26), ἔχουσιν = have ×2 (9:58).
- Comparatives rendered as superlatives are noted (μείζων 9:46, μικρότερος and μέγας 9:48).
- The narrative formula ἐγένετο is `s` in 9:28, 9:33, 9:37, 9:51 and "One day" in 9:18, following Luke 1-5. In 9:29 ἐγένετο has its own subject (τὸ εἶδος … ἕτερον) and is linked with ἕτερον = changed.
- Kept for consistency with the direct parallels: δέ = "and still", ἄλλοι = others (9:8, 9:19; as Mark 8:28, Matthew 16:14); εἰσίν = are (9:27; as Matthew 16:28); οὐκ ἠδυνήθησαν = they were unable (9:40; as Mark 9:18).

## Doubts for the peer

- 9:4: the καί between "stay there" and "leave" is linked to "until" (BSB recasts "and from there depart"). The alternative is `s` for καί and `t until`.
- 9:12: "As" on the second δέ (the δέ joining the clauses), with the first δέ `s`. The publisher had "As" on the first δέ.
- 9:18: the καί before ἐπηρώτησεν is linked to the "and" before "the disciples", because BSB moves the coordination into the "as" clause. The alternative is `s` plus `t and`.
- 9:52: the καί before πορευθέντες is linked to the relative "who" (publisher row kept). The alternative is `s` plus `t who`.
- 9:58 and 9:62: τήν = His / his (publisher rows kept, article as possessive, as Mark 9:18). Matthew 8:20, the identical verse, has `t His` with τήν `s`; one of the two should probably be aligned.
- 9:26: ἐν, τῇ and δόξῃ are linked to BSB's repeated "in the glory" as well as to "in His glory". The alternative is `t` for the repeated words.
- 9:29: ἐγένετο … ἕτερον = changed, with `t became` for the clothes clause, where ἐγένετο is understood. Linking ἐγένετο to "became" too is the alternative.
- 9:36: ἐσίγησαν = "kept this to themselves" kept as an idiom (publisher row), while "with them" after "was present" is now `t`.
- 9:53: οὐκ = refused, ἐδέξαντο = to welcome (split, following the negatives convention). Joining them as "refused to welcome" is the alternative.
- 9:39: μετὰ ἀφροῦ = "so that he foams at the mouth" as one group; "so that" could be `t`.
