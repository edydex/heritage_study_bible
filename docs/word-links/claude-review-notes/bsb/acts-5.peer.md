## Changes (4 verses)

- 5:24: ἱεροῦ = "of + temple guard" (with a note on the title) → ἱεροῦ =
  "of + temple", `t guard` ("BSB adds guard; the Greek says 'the captain of
  the temple'"). ἱεροῦ means "temple". "guard" explains the title, and no
  Greek word in the phrase means it. This also matches the reviewed Acts 4:1,
  which has the same phrase ὁ στρατηγὸς τοῦ ἱεροῦ and the same BSB wording.
- 5:12: πάντες = "the believers": link kept, and the note now ends "the
  link follows N1904, and BSB's Greek differs by one word in this verse".
  The EVIDENCE line shows a one-word difference (1 N1904 word unmatched, 1
  publisher word not in N1904), so rule 9 asks for a note. No BSB English
  word was left without an owner, so the record doesn't show which word it
  is, and the note does not say.
- 5:8: ἡ = "she" had no note → "In ἡ δέ the article serves as the pronoun
  she." This uses the same wording as the author's 5:33 and 5:41 notes. A
  reader tapping "she" would otherwise see only an article.
- 5:22: the note on ἀπήγγειλαν = "with the report" said "rendered returned
  with the report", but "returned" belongs to ἀναστρέψαντες. It now says
  "rendered with the report".

Every other line of the author's file is unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- 5:39/5:40 verse division: **agree, kept**. The record shows a real
  division difference: 5:39 has 3 N1904 words unmatched and 0 extra
  publisher words, and 5:40 has 0 unmatched and 3 extra. The draft left
  "At this, they yielded to Gamaliel" without an owner. The mechanics follow
  the reviewed Luke 7:18-19 and 24:45-46: `m 14 15 16 > 5:40` in 5:39 and
  `g @5:39` in 5:40. δέ = "At this" follows the convention for a plain
  connective. ἐπείσθησαν = "they yielded". αὐτῷ = "to Gamaliel" is honest:
  the `t` rule for names covers a name the Greek gives only in a verb ending
  or a participle. Here a Greek pronoun is present, and reviewed precedent
  links a pronoun to the name BSB substitutes for it, with a note. Examples:
  Acts 4:1 αὐτῶν = "Peter and John", Luke 22:4 αὐτόν = "Jesus", and more
  than 30 rows with "The pronoun … is rendered by the name".
- 5:12 πάντες = "the believers": **agree, kept** (note extended above).
  BSB's English has no "all". "the believers" is its explicit subject, and
  πάντες is the Greek's explicit subject ("they all"). It is a pronoun-like
  substantive rendered by a noun naming its referent, like 5:33 οἱ = "the
  Council members". `s` + `t` would split a real rendering. διὰ τῶν χειρῶν
  `s` is right: BSB makes "the apostles" the subject of "performed", so
  nothing in the English means "hands" or "through".
- 5:33 "resolved": **agree, kept**. The draft left "they resolved"
  without an owner, and the record shows 1 N1904 word unmatched. So the
  difference sits at this verb, and the EVIDENCE line supports the reading
  note. The review note ("BSB's Greek text differs at this verb; resolved
  still fits ἐβούλοντο") does not name the publisher's word. βούλομαι with
  an infinitive ("intend, plan") is rendered correctly by "resolved", so
  the link stays (rule 9, same meaning).
- 5:4 one οὐχί = both "not"s: **agree**. A single οὐχί heads one Greek
  question whose two verbs (ἔμενεν, ὑπῆρχεν) are joined by καί. BSB splits
  the question in two and repeats the negative, so the second "not" is the
  same word understood again.
- 5:27 one αὐτούς = both "them"s: **agree**. In ἀγαγόντες αὐτοὺς
  ἔστησαν the one pronoun is the object of the participle and the main
  verb. This is the same pattern as the reviewed Mark 15:46 (καθελὼν αὐτὸν
  ἐνείλησεν, where "it" with "wrapped" is linked to αὐτόν). The supplied
  objects in 5:6, 5:10 and 5:40 stay `t`, correctly. In 5:6 and 5:10 the
  pronoun belongs to an earlier finite clause, after which a new καί clause
  begins with no object. In 5:40 BSB uses "them" in place of the noun τοὺς
  ἀποστόλους, not a repeat of it. In 5:30 "Him" also stays `t`: it is a
  resumptive object after "whom" (ὅν), and a group "whom … Him" would
  read oddly.
- 5:10 καί (16) = "and" after "came in": **agree**. The Greek is
  [εἰσελθόντες … εὗρον] καί [ἐξενέγκαντες ἔθαψαν]. BSB's second "and"
  ("carried … and buried") joins ἐξενέγκαντες to its main verb inside the
  second half, which is `t` by convention. BSB's first "and" is the only
  English coordinator spanning the καί's boundary.
- 5:7 μὴ εἰδυῖα = "unaware of": **agree**. One English word holds the
  negative, so it cannot be split.
- 5:7 καί = "also": **agree, kept**. καί can mean "also", nothing else
  in the English renders it, and reviewed precedent links καί to "also" in
  this way ("The καί joining the clauses is rendered also"). The other
  "also"s of 5:1 and 5:9 rightly stay `t`, because no Greek word stands
  there.
- 5:27 καί = "where": **agree, kept**. BSB turns the καί clause into a
  where-clause, and reviewed Luke 8 does the same ("BSB turns this καί into
  where"). The note says so.
- 5:38 "their" for ἡ … αὕτη: **agree, kept**. BSB swaps the determiner
  "this" for "their" with the same reference, and the article as possessive
  is the convention. τὸ … τοῦτο `s` is right because one "their" covers
  both nouns.
- 5:21: γερουσίαν = "assembly of the elders" is right, because γερουσία is
  itself the council of elders. τῶν υἱῶν Ἰσραήλ = "of Israel" is a joined
  unit, which keeps "of" off the lone article. Both agree.
- 5:31: τῷ = "to" with Ἰσραήλ follows the indeclinable-name convention.
  Agree.
- Readings: the 5:1 Sapphira and 5:35 "of Israel" spelling notes are
  supported. In each, the draft left that English without an owner, and
  the record shows exactly one word unmatched. 5:28 (0 unmatched, 1 extra
  publisher word, no ownerless English) correctly claims nothing. For 5:12,
  see the change above.
- Brief rules checked across the chapter. No lone article carries "of the"
  or "to the" (the only article groups are "the", a possessive, a pronoun,
  or 5:31 τῷ with Ἰσραήλ). Supplied copulas are `t` (5:8 ×2, 5:9, 5:32 "so
  is", 5:34, 5:42).

## Still uncertain

- 5:17 Ἀναστάς = "They went out" (publisher row, kept). The singular
  participle has the high priest as subject, and BSB's plural "They" covers
  the whole group. It is loose, but it is not wrong enough to change.
- 5:22 "at the jail" on ἐν τῇ φυλακῇ with "there" `t`. BSB moves the
  phrase to "on arriving", and the reverse arrangement (phrase = "there",
  "at the jail" `t`) would also be defensible. Kept as the author has it.
- 5:12: the record does not show which word differs (most likely the form
  of "all"). The note therefore stays general.
