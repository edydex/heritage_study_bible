# BSB Matthew 18: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 34 of 34 verses (18:11 is in neither N1904 nor the BSB text).
18:13 and 18:21, done by an earlier reviewer, were checked like every other verse.

Check after the changes: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 34, "problems": 0}`.

16 changes in 14 verses: 14 link changes and 2 note-only cleanups.

## Changes

Supplied "and" between a participle and a finite verb (the Greek has no
conjunction there, so the English "and" is `t`, not part of the verb):

- 18:1: λέγοντες = "and asked" → asked, plus `t and`.
- 18:8: βληθῆναι = "and#2 be thrown" → be thrown, plus `t and#2` (ἔχοντα βληθῆναι
  has no conjunction).
- 18:9: βληθῆναι = "and#1 be thrown" → be thrown, plus `t and#1` (same reason).
- 18:15: ἔλεγξον = "and confront" → confront, plus `t and` (ὕπαγε ἔλεγξον has no
  conjunction).
- 18:21: εἶπεν = "and asked" → asked, plus `t and` (after the participle προσελθών).
- 18:28: ἔπνιγεν = "and began to choke" → began to choke, plus `t and`
  (after κρατήσας). The καί before κρατήσας stays `s`, as the author had it.
- 18:29: παρεκάλει = "and#0 begged" → begged, plus `t and#0` (after πεσών).
- 18:30: ἔβαλεν = "and had + thrown" → had + thrown, plus `t and` (after ἀπελθών).
- 18:31: διεσάφησαν = "and#1 recounted" → recounted, plus `t and#1` (after ἐλθόντες).
- 18:32: λέγει = "and declared" → declared, plus `t and` (after προσκαλεσάμενος).

Other link changes:

- 18:6: μύλος = millstone, ὀνικός = "a large" → μύλος = a + millstone,
  ὀνικός = large. The English "a" is the millstone's determiner; it does not
  render ὀνικός (of a donkey).
- 18:17: εἰπόν = "tell it" → tell, plus `t it`. εἰπόν has no object; this
  matches the author's own handling of supplied objects (on him, his debt, me).
- 18:21: προσελθών + αὐτῷ jointly = "came to Jesus" → προσελθών = came,
  αὐτῷ = to Jesus (with a note). αὐτῷ is the addressee of εἶπεν, not part of
  "came", and the participle does not render "to Jesus".
- 18:25: ἐκέλευσεν = "ordered that" → ordered, plus `t that`. "that" is an
  English complementizer; the Greek has an accusative and infinitive.

Note-only cleanups (links unchanged):

- 18:13: four long notes shortened to one plain sentence each (γένηται εὑρεῖν,
  αὐτῷ = that one, ὅτι not rendered, supplied sheep).
- 18:21: dropped the note on ἐμέ = me (obvious link, and it described the
  publisher's rows rather than the text). Shortened the notes on
  ἀδελφός + αὐτῷ = brother and on the unrendered καί.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 18:6, ἵνα = to#1 only, with "to#2 be drowned" on καταποντισθῇ: **agree.**
  The second "to" is the English infinitive form of the second verb. Linking
  both to ἵνα would also work but is not more accurate.
- Partitive "of" on τῶν in 18:28, but "of these" on τούτων in 18:6, 18:10 and
  18:14: **agree.** The genitive marks the whole phrase, so either anchor is
  defensible. The publisher's "of his" on αὐτοῦ (18:28) was the real error,
  and it is fixed.
- 18:22, ἕως = just: **agree.** "not just seven times" is BSB's way of putting
  "not up to seven times". Leaving the second ἕως as `s` is right.
- 18:9, τήν = the, τοῦ = of in "the fire of hell": **agree.** The head
  article goes to the English head article and the genitive article to "of".
  Each word the reader taps still shows the right Greek word.
- 18:24, "owing" on ὀφειλέτης (debtor + owing): **agree.** ὀφειλέτης means
  "one who owes", and the genitive of the amount depends on it.
- 18:2, αὐτό as `s` while 18:21 links αὐτῷ jointly with "brother": **agree,
  both kept.** In 18:21 αὐτῷ's role (object of forgive) survives on "brother".
  In 18:2, ἔστησεν (he set it) becomes intransitive "to stand", so αὐτό's
  role as object has no English counterpart.
- "and" bundled with a verb after a participle: **changed** in all ten places
  (listed above).
- "tell it" (18:17): **changed** to tell + `t it`.

## Still uncertain

- 18:28: the καί before κρατήσας could instead be linked to the English
  "and" before "began to choke" (καί coordinates ἔπνιγεν with εὗρεν). I kept
  `s` for καί and `t` for "and", as other reviewed chapters do.
- 18:32: "You wicked" stays on πονηρέ. "You" marks the vocative, which both
  Δοῦλε and πονηρέ carry, so either anchor is defensible.
- 18:26: "fell on his knees before him" splits πεσών = fell and
  προσεκύνει = on his knees. That is loose but acceptable, so it is unchanged.
- 18:2: "Jesus" is `t` as supplied by English. The Byzantine text adds
  ὁ Ἰησοῦς here, but BSB normally supplies the name for clarity, so the note
  was not changed to describe a textual difference.
