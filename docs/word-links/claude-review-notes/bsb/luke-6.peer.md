# BSB Luke 6: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 49 of 49 verses (6:1-49). I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line
against N1904, the displayed BSB, the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json` and the reviewed
parallels Mark 2:23-3:19, Matthew 5-7, 10:2-4, 10:24 and 12:1-14. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 49, "problems": 0}`.

Reading claims: the publisher record has a `partial` entry for 6:14, 6:17,
6:36, 6:45 and 6:46 only. I mapped the publisher's groups onto N1904 to see
which English words are left unmapped:

- 6:14: "John" is the only unmapped English word, and 17 of 18 N1904 words
  match out of an equal word count. That fits the "spelled differently"
  note, which does not state the publisher's spelling.
- 6:36: the publisher has 9 Greek words to N1904's 8, all 8 N1904 words
  match, and its καθώς maps to "as" alone. "just" is the only unmapped
  English word, so the publisher linked it to its extra word. The author's
  `t just` with a reading reason is right.
- 6:45: the publisher has 30 Greek words to N1904's 29, all 29 match, and
  the first "his" (good treasure of his heart) is the only unmapped English
  word. The author's `t his#0` with a reading reason is right.
- 6:17 and 6:46: all N1904 words match and the counts are equal. The author
  correctly claims no reading there.

## Changes (3 verses)

- 6:40: τόν `s` + `t his#0` → τόν = "his#0" (note: "The article τόν serves
  as the possessive."). This restores the publisher row. It makes the
  chapter consistent: 6:1 has ταῖς = "their" and 6:29 has τόν = "your". It
  also follows the most common treatment in the reviewed files. I count 59
  single articles linked to a possessive in the reviewed Gospel files,
  including every case in Luke 1, 2 and 4 (Luke 1:29, 2:28, 2:44, 4:40),
  most of Mark and Matthew 18-27. Only about 20 use `s` + `t`, mostly the
  older Matthew 4-15 files (including the parallel Matthew 10:24), plus
  Luke 15:12. This chapter therefore differs from Matthew 10:24.
- 6:30: παντί "to everyone who" → "to everyone"; αἰτοῦντι "asks" → "who
  asks". The relative "who" renders the participle, not πᾶς. The author
  made the same fix in 6:40 (πᾶς = everyone, κατηρτισμένος = who is fully
  trained). The publisher row had folded "who" into πᾶς in both verses.
- 6:38: καλόν "A good" → "good"; μέτρον "measure" → "A + measure". English
  "a" goes with the noun, as the author did in 6:17 and 6:43. I also added a
  note to δώσουσιν = "will be poured": "δώσουσιν (they will give) is
  rendered will be poured." The link itself is unchanged.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 6:1, 6:29 and 6:40, the article as a possessive: settled by linking all
  three (see Changes). ταῖς = "their" (6:1) and τόν = "your" (6:29) are
  kept. τήν = "one" (6:29) is also kept. It is the publisher row, and the
  article set against τὴν ἄλλην is the Greek for "the one … the other",
  which BSB renders "one … the other".
- 6:13 "to Him" on προσεφώνησεν: kept. The προσ- verb means "call to
  oneself". Every reviewed parallel links "to Him" to the verb:
  προσκαλεσάμενος/προσκαλεῖται = called + to Him in Mark 6:7, 7:14, 8:1,
  8:34, 12:43 and Matthew 10:1, 15:10, 15:32.
- 6:15-16, `t son`: kept. Mark 3:18 and Matthew 10:3 link "son" to the
  article (τὸν/ὁ τοῦ Ἁλφαίου), and Luke 3 links "the son" to τοῦ. In both
  places the genitive name carries only "of Alphaeus". Luke 6:15-16 has no
  article, so "of Alphaeus"/"of James" stay on the genitive as in those
  parallels, and "son" is English-only. The links follow BSB's "son", not
  the alternative "brother of James".
- 6:47, Πᾶς = "he": kept. BSB's "he … who comes" renders the pendent "Πᾶς
  ὁ ἐρχόμενος" ("everyone who comes"). ἐστίν has no expressed subject of
  its own. Making Πᾶς `s` would leave it unlinked while its English stands.
- 6:4, καί 7 = "and" before ate and καί 14 = "and" before gave: kept. καί 14
  stands between ἔφαγεν and ἔδωκεν, which is where BSB's "and gave" falls.
  Each καί keeps one English "and" in BSB's reordered list.
- 6:42, ἐκβάλω = "me take + out", ἐν = "of" (and later ἐν = "from"): kept.
  Matthew 7:4-5 has ἐκ τοῦ ὀφθαλμοῦ, where ἐκ = "out of"/"from". Luke has
  ἐν, so the only "out" in the Greek is the ἐκ- of ἐκβάλω, and "out" belongs
  to that verb. "of your eye" is BSB's form of the location phrase ἐν τῷ
  ὀφθαλμῷ σου, so its head preposition goes to ἐν. The same holds for
  "from" in the last clause. Making ἐν `s` would put "of" under a verb that
  means "cast out".

## Other points checked and kept

- 6:23 and 6:26, κατὰ τὰ αὐτά: the publisher's transferred groups had
  αὐτά = "their" and αὐτῶν = "in the same way", which is a swap. The
  author's fix (κατὰ τὰ αὐτά = in the same way, αὐτῶν = their) is right in
  both verses.
- 6:45, the three "his": `t his#0` (reading, above). "treasure#1 of his
  heart" is `t`, because N1904 (and the publisher) have only τοῦ πονηροῦ in
  the second clause. The publisher row had folded the whole phrase into
  πονηροῦ. The final αὐτοῦ is `s` ("the mouth"). The publisher has that
  word too, but its groups give it no English, and τό = "the".
- 6:36 "just" and 6:45 the first "his": the reading notes say only "BSB
  follows a reading with an extra word here", and the publisher's spelling
  is not stated (above).
- 6:31: ἄνθρωποι = "them", αὐτοῖς = "to others", as in Matthew 7:12. Each
  Greek word keeps its own grammatical role (subject of ποιῶσιν, object of
  ποιεῖτε).
- 6:8 εἰς τὸ μέσον = "among us" and τῷ … ἔχοντι = "with", as in Mark 3:3.
  6:4 οὐκ … εἰ μὴ μόνους = "only", as in Mark 2:26. 6:41-42 οὐ = "fail" and
  σοῦ/ἰδίῳ = "your own", as in Matthew 7:3-5.
- Negatives and auxiliaries (6:3, 6:29, 6:30, 6:37, 6:39, 6:43, 6:44, 6:46),
  article + participle units, the "a" + noun links and every remaining `t`
  (Jesus, them, it, are, is, that, his, there, was there, to you) were
  checked and kept.

## Still uncertain

- 6:3: τοῦτο is `s`. τοῦτο ὅ = "what" ("that which") would also be
  defensible.
- 6:38: ᾧ = "with the" (publisher row) is kept. "with" could instead go to
  the dative μέτρῳ, as Matthew 7:2 has ᾧ = "the" (there ἐν carries "with").
  I kept the row because ᾧ is dative too, so a change would only be taste.
- 6:15-16: the publisher put "son" inside the genitive group. That reading
  of the genitive of relationship would also be acceptable.
- 6:42: `s` for ἐν (with "out of" on ἐκβάλω) remains the alternative to
  ἐν = "of".
