# BSB Acts 12: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 25 of 25 verses (12:1-25), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 1 verse (12:3), three linked edits. The other 24 verses are kept
exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 12:3 ἦσαν … ἡμέραι: before ἦσαν = "during" and ἡμέραι = "Feast" →
  after ἦσαν ἡμέραι joined = "during". "During the Feast" says "in the days
  of the Feast", so "during" carries both "it was" and "days"; ἡμέραι means
  "days", not "Feast".
- 12:3 ἀζύμων: before "of Unleavened Bread" → after "Feast of Unleavened
  Bread" (the publisher's own row, restored). The plural τὰ ἄζυμα is the
  festival's name, and reviewed precedent puts "Feast of Unleavened Bread"
  on ἀζύμων wherever no word for "feast" stands in the Greek (Mark 14:1,
  14:12; Matthew 26:17). "Feast" goes on its own word only where the Greek
  has ἑορτή (Luke 22:1). Luke 22:7 (ἡμέρα = "day") does not apply, because
  BSB has no "day" here.
- 12:3 "the" before Feast: before `t` (reading reason) and τῶν `s` → after
  τῶν = "the", as in Mark 14:12 and Matthew 26:17. English needs "the" before
  this name whichever reading is followed, and N1904's τῶν (the article of
  τὰ ἄζυμα) supplies it. The extra word in the publisher's Greek (EVIDENCE: 0
  unmatched, 1 extra) is still reported, now in the note on ἦσαν ἡμέραι:
  "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here" (the Luke 22:7 wording). The
  extra word most likely goes with ἡμέραι, which the joined "during" now
  covers.

## Points checked (kept)

- 12:3 ἦσαν: BSB's English here is "…to seize Peter during the Feast of
  Unleavened Bread". There is no "This was", so no English "was" can carry
  ἦσαν, and "during" is the only fair rendering for it.
- 12:6 Ὅτε = "before" (note) and ἤμελλεν = "was" ("was to"): "before" fills
  Ὅτε's slot as the conjunction, and the note says that Ὅτε together with
  ἤμελλεν gives "the night before … was to". This is kept as it is: ἤμελλεν
  has its own "was", and joining the two would only move the same claim.
- 12:6 ἐκείνῃ `s`, τῇ = "the", νυκτί = "On … night": kept. Reviewed
  precedent where BSB turns "that X" into "the X" mostly puts "the" on the
  article and makes the demonstrative `s` (2 Thessalonians 1:10, Matthew
  9:31, 22:10, 24:38, 25:7). Only Hebrews 11:15 does otherwise. "the" on
  ἐκείνῃ would also have been defensible.
- 12:10, 12:15, 12:16 "When"/"when" on the connective that joins the two
  clauses: confirmed. In each verse BSB's when-clause contains a finite
  verb (προῆλθον; διϊσχυρίζετο; εἶδαν), so the participle cannot carry the
  subordination. This matches John 1:37 and 10:12. The first connective is
  rendered or left `s` as BSB has it: 12:10 καί `s` (new sentence), 12:15 δέ
  = "But", 12:16 δέ = "and".
- 12:20 ὁμοθυμαδὸν … παρῆσαν = "they convened": confirmed. "Convene" (come
  together) covers both "with one accord" and "were present". τρέφεσθαι =
  "depended … for food" and ἀπό = "on" (the source): confirmed. The
  publisher had "depended" on ἀπό, which was wrong.
- 12:14 ἀπὸ τῆς χαρᾶς = "so overjoyed that", with "she was" `t`: confirmed.
  The causal "from joy" becomes a result clause, so the phrase works as one
  unit.
- 12:8 Περιβαλοῦ = "Wrap … around you": confirmed. The prefix περι-
  ("around") and the middle voice ("yourself") belong to the verb, not to
  ἱμάτιον.
- 12:11 one ἐκ = "from" + "from": confirmed. ἐκ governs both χειρός and
  προσδοκίας, and BSB repeats the word it understands again.
- Readings, checked against the publisher record for each verse: 12:2 and
  12:12 (1 unmatched / 1 extra: John), 12:19 (1/1: Caesarea), 12:25 (3/3:
  John, the συν- compound, ἐξ/"to"), 12:24 (1/1: "of God" for τοῦ Κυρίου,
  bundled `s` + `t` as in John 4:1), 12:21 (0/1: extra word, mentioned in
  the `t and` reason). Every verse that has a reading claim also has a
  differing EVIDENCE line, and none of the notes states the publisher's
  Greek wording or spelling.
- Brief rules: supplied names for a verb ending are `t` (Herod 12:3, 12:4,
  12:20, 12:23; Peter 12:8, 12:9, 12:17; "the angel" 12:8). Greek words that
  BSB moves into a new role are linked (12:21 πρὸς αὐτούς = "the people",
  12:22 δῆμος = "they"). Supplied copulas are `t` (12:14 "she was", 12:22
  "This is"). No lone article carries a preposition anywhere in the chapter.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 12:10 / 12:16 / 12:15 "when" on the joining καί/δέ: agree (see above).
- 12:6 ἐκείνῃ `s`: agree; both options are defensible, and precedent leans
  to the author's choice.
- 12:13 θύραν τοῦ πυλῶνος = "outer gate" joined: agree. "Door of the
  gateway" becomes "outer gate" as a whole, so neither noun maps cleanly to
  one English word.
- 12:20 ὁμοθυμαδόν joined with παρῆσαν: agree.
- 12:18 "as to" `t`: agree. No Greek word means "as to", and τί = "what"
  is enough.

## Still uncertain

- 12:3: what the publisher's extra word is cannot be checked in the repo.
  If it is the article before ἡμέραι, BSB's own alignment may have put "the"
  on it. The meaning-based link to τῶν is still correct for N1904.
- 12:17 ταῦτα `s` ("Send word"): "word" could be read as rendering ταῦτα,
  but leaving it `s` is defensible, so it is kept.
- 12:12 ἦσαν = "were", with "had" on συνηθροισμένοι: the pluperfect "had"
  comes partly from ἦσαν. Kept, because the participle is where "had
  gathered" is normally linked.
