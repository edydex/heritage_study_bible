# BSB 2 Corinthians 5: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 21 of 21 verses (5:1-21). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording, and
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft and the
publisher record in `bsb-word-links/2-corinthians.json`. No KEEP blocks in
this chapter. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 21, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 6 verses. Four are link changes (5:1, 5:5, 5:9, 5:17). Two only
change a note (5:2, 5:10). The other 15 verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 5:1: αἰώνιον "an eternal" → "eternal"; οἰκίαν "house" → "an + house".
  English "a/an" goes with its noun, even when an adjective stands between
  them. This follows the brief, 2 Corinthians 4:17 (αἰώνιον = "eternal",
  βάρος = "an + weight") and more than 140 reviewed "a + noun" rows.
- 5:5: αὐτὸ τοῦτο "this very purpose" → αὐτό = "very", τοῦτο = "this", and
  "purpose" `t` ("English supplies the noun; the Greek has the neuter
  'this very thing'"). No Greek word means "purpose". It is the noun English
  supplies for the neuter pronoun, just as "tent" is supplied for τούτῳ in
  5:2. Treating both the same way keeps 5:2 and 5:5 under one principle.
  Romans 9:17, reviewed in this campaign, has the same phrase εἰς αὐτὸ
  τοῦτο and makes "purpose" `t` (its peer confirmed this). It also links
  αὐτό and τοῦτο separately, as the publisher rows did here. The
  Ephesians 6:22 and Colossians 4:8 rows that the author cited come from
  an older batch, and they say themselves that no purpose noun is printed.
- 5:9: ἐκδημοῦντες "away from" with "it" `t` → "away", with "from it" `t`
  ("English supplies the body as what we are away from; this verse does
  not name it"). The verse has no preposition, so "from" only introduces
  the supplied object. In the same chapter the "from" after this verb has
  its own Greek word: 5:6 ἐκδημοῦμεν = "are away" with ἀπό = "from", and
  5:8 ἐκδημῆσαι = "to be away" with ἐκ = "from". The parallel is now
  exact. The verbs carry "are here" and "away", and the supplied
  complements "in this body" and "from it" are both `t`.
- 5:17: καινή "a new" → "new"; κτίσις "creation" → "a + creation" (the
  same a/an rule as 5:1). Galatians 6:15 (καινή = "a new") is an older
  exception to the majority practice.
- 5:2 (note only): τούτῳ keeps "this" and "tent" stays `t`. I removed the
  `g` note "points back to the tent of verse 1". It said the same thing as
  the `t` reason ("English names the tent that τούτῳ refers to"), but stated
  BSB's reading of ἐν τούτῳ as a fact about the Greek. Some take it as "in
  this respect". The `t` reason correctly attributes it to the English.
- 5:10 (note only): the κομίσηται note now gives the verb's sense as
  "receive back, be repaid". That shows why "due" is part of the verb's
  rendering (see below).

## Points settled (author doubts and caller questions)

- 5:2 "tent" `t` and 5:5 "this very purpose": "tent" `t` is right. The
  reviewed Pauline chapters make a noun that English adds to name a
  demonstrative's referent `t` (Romans 15:28 "this service", 1 Corinthians
  9:15 "these rights", Matthew 26:42 "this cup"). "purpose" is the same
  kind of supplied noun, so it is now `t` too (see Changes). Only the
  generic noun that the pronoun's own gender gives ("this man", "these
  things") rides with the pronoun.
- Possessives and nouns inside verb paraphrases (2 Corinthians 1:22
  principle): a possessive that refers to the verb's own subject may stay
  with the paraphrase. A supplied noun may stay only if it renders the
  verb's own meaning. Each case passes:
  - 5:10 κομίσηται = "may receive his due": kept. In judgment contexts
    κομίζομαι means "receive what is due, be repaid" (Colossians 3:25,
    Ephesians 6:8), so "due" is part of the verb's meaning, and "his"
    refers to the subject ἕκαστος. τά `s` stays. BSB gives "the things"
    once, and the publisher put it on ἅ. That fits "for (πρός) the things
    (ἅ) done (ἔπραξεν)". Moving it to τά would be a matter of taste.
  - 5:4 βαρούμενοι = "under our burdens": kept. βαρέω means "weigh down,
    burden", so "burdens" renders the verb's meaning, and "our" refers to
    the subject (we).
  - 5:20 παρακαλοῦντος = "were making His appeal": kept. "appeal" renders
    παρακαλέω. "His" is God, the subject of the genitive absolute. "were"
    is the English auxiliary after "as though", not a person word.
  - 5:13 ἐξέστημεν = "we are out of our mind" and σωφρονοῦμεν = "we are of
    sound mind": kept, because "mind" is part of what both verbs mean.
- 5:9: ἐνδημοῦντες = "are + here" is right. "here" renders the verb's
  sense "be at home, be present". It is not an added place word, and it
  pairs with "away" for ἐκδημέω. "we" `t` (participle) and "in this body"
  `t` are right. "from" now goes with "it" (see Changes).
- 5:21: confirmed word by word with only factual notes. τὸν … γνόντα =
  "Him#0 who + knew", μή = "no", ἁμαρτίαν = "sin#0" / "sin#1", ὑπέρ = "on +
  behalf", ἡμῶν = "our", ἐποίησεν = "made", ἡμεῖς = "we", γενώμεθα =
  "might become" (the modal is on the finite subjunctive). "God" `t` (the
  subject is given only by the verb ending and the context) and "to be" `t`
  (supplied infinitive) are right. There are no interpretive notes. 5:20
  ὑπέρ = "on behalf" / Χριστοῦ = "of Christ" follows the same pattern.
- 5:15: "should" on ζῶσιν (the finite subjunctive), with μηκέτι = "no
  longer": right. 5:15's ἀπέθανεν = "He died" is finite, so "He" may stay
  with the verb.
- Person words are off the participles in 5:3, 5:4, 5:6 (three of them),
  5:9, 5:11, 5:12, 5:14 and 5:19. Every "we/He" from a person ending now
  sits on a finite verb or is `t`. "when/while/since/because" and the
  English auxiliaries ("are", "has") stay with the participle, as in the
  reviewed chapters.
- The negatives are split: 5:3 οὐ = "not" (εὑρεθησόμεθα = "we#1 will + be
  found"), 5:4 οὐ = "not" (θέλομεν = "we#2 do + wish") and 5:12 οὐ = "not"
  (συνιστάνομεν = "We are + commending"). 5:12 καὶ μή = "rather than" is a
  contrastive unit, not a negative on a verb, so joining them is fine.
- 5:4 καὶ γάρ = "So": kept. The reviewed chapters link γάρ to the English
  connective BSB uses, including "So" (1 Corinthians 8:11), "Now" and
  "Yet", and nothing else owns "So". This is unlike 2 Corinthians 4:18,
  where "So" had no Greek connective at all. 5:1 γάρ = "Now", 5:6 καί =
  "although" (reviewed καί = "though/although/yet") and 5:8 δέ = "then"
  are kept on the same basis.
- 5:16: the second νῦν `s` is right. "no longer" fully renders οὐκέτι, and
  joining νῦν would over-claim. "once" `t` (added adverb) is right. κατὰ
  σάρκα = "in this way" is kept, because the English pro-phrase stands for
  the repeated Greek phrase.
- 5:5 ὁ δούς = "has given" with "and" `t`: right. The Greek has no καί,
  and the 5:5 record shows 15 of 15 words matched with 0 publisher words
  outside N1904, so no extra word is involved.
- Readings: the publisher record has `partial` only for 5:1, 5:4, 5:5 and
  5:9. In each, sourceWords = matchedSourceWords = publisherGreekWords
  (21, 23, 15, 10), so the differences are only in order or form. No
  reading is claimed anywhere in the file, and no note states the
  publisher's spelling.
- Brief rules swept across the chapter. No "of the"/"to the"/"in the"
  sits on a lone article. 5:4 τῷ = "this" and τό = "our" follow the
  article-as-demonstrative and article-as-possessive practice. Every
  supplied copula is `t`: 5:13 "it is" twice, 5:17 "is#0" and "he is",
  5:18 "is", 5:21 "to be". The supplied objects and frames are `t`: 5:11
  "what it means", "it#1", 5:20 "you", 5:9 "from it". There is no added
  "there". The added adverb "once" is `t`. The supplied "and" in 5:5 is
  `t`.

## Still uncertain

- 5:1: the publisher's "the" stays on τοῦ σκήνους, with ἡ `s`. BSB's "the
  earthly tent" begins inside the ἡ phrase (ἡ ἐπίγειος … οἰκία), so ἡ =
  "the" would be just as defensible. I did not change a row that is not
  wrong.
- 5:9: the publisher's εἶναι = "to" / εὐάρεστοι = "please" could instead be
  one unit, εὐάρεστοι εἶναι = "to please". I kept it.
- Cross-chapter: εἰς αὐτὸ τοῦτο is now `t` for "purpose" in Romans 9:17
  and here, but still links "this very purpose" in Ephesians 6:22 and
  Colossians 4:8. Romans 13:6 links αὐτὸ τοῦτο = "work". Galatians 6:15
  keeps καινή = "a new". These older rows could be harmonized later. I
  did not change them.
