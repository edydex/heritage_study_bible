Parallels checked: 4:1-42 and 4:46-54 have no Synoptic parallel. The healing

## Publisher rows corrected

"When", "While" or "Since" was on the wrong word:

- 4:47: "When" moves from οὗτος to the participle ἀκούσας (When + heard).
  οὗτος → he.
- 4:51: "while" moves from αὐτοῦ to the genitive absolute participle
  καταβαίνοντος (while + was + on the way). ἤδη had carried "was"; it now
  carries only "still".
- 4:6: δέ carried "Since". BSB turns "there was Jacob's well …; so Jesus …
  sat" into a since-clause, so "Since" now goes on the inferential οὖν and δέ
  is `s`.
- 4:27: the opening Καί carried "Just then". Now ἐπὶ τούτῳ ("at this") →
  Just then, and καί is `s`.

Negatives split off verbs:

- 4:14: οὐ μή … εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα → never, as in Mark 11:14. διψήσει → will +
  thirst.
- 4:48: οὐ μή → never, and πιστεύσητε → you will + believe.
- 4:15: μή → not, and διψῶ → I will + get thirsty.
- 4:22: οὐκ → not, and οἴδατε → you do + know.
- 4:38: οὐχ → not, and κεκοπιάκατε → have + worked for.
- 4:9: συνχρῶνται → do + associate with.
- 4:35: ὑμεῖς carried "Do you". Now λέγετε → Do + say and ὑμεῖς → you.
- 4:32: οὐκ carried "nothing about". Now οὐκ → nothing and οἴδατε → know +
  about.

English-only words that a Greek word had claimed (now `t`):

- Names: "Jesus" in 4:16 (λέγει) and 4:43 (ἐξῆλθεν).
- Subjects: "his son" (4:52), "one" (4:20, δεῖ → must).
- Added explanation: "Jews" (4:20). ὑμεῖς → you.
- Objects: "Him" and "from her" (4:27), "something" (4:31).
- Copulas and predicates: "is" (4:24, which Θεός had carried), "was" (4:54,
  which Τοῦτο had carried), and "He" in "am He" (4:26, as in Mark 13:6 and
  Luke 21:8).
- Other: "So" (4:30), "now" (4:38, which ὑμεῖς had carried), "his" (4:36,
  which μισθόν had carried), "great" (4:45).
- 4:45: πάντα had carried "all the great things". Now πάντα → all and ὅσα →
  the … things.
- 4:54: the article ὁ carried "that". It is now `s`, and "that" is `t`.
- 4:2: καίτοιγε carried "although it was" and αὐτός carried "who". Now
  καίτοιγε → although, the cleft "it was … who" is `t`, and the intensive
  αὐτός is `s` (as in John 2:24).

Other wrong anchors:

- 4:9: σύ carried "can You" and ὤν carried "You are". Now σύ → You (first),
  ὤν → are ("You are a Jew"), and αἰτεῖς → can You ask for.
- 4:12: καί carried "as did". Now καί → as, and ἔπιεν → drank + did, because
  the verb is understood again.
- 4:17: Καλῶς carried "correct to say". Now Καλῶς → correct and εἶπες → You +
  to say. "are" is `t`.
- 4:18: ἔχεις carried "now". Now νῦν → now.
- 4:23: νῦν carried "has" and ἐστιν carried "come". Now νῦν → now and ἐστιν →
  has + come. The article τούς carried "as these". Now τοιούτους → such as
  these and τοὺς προσκυνοῦντας → to worship.
- 4:32: εἶπεν carried "He". In ὁ δέ the article now → He, and εἶπεν → told.
- 4:36: the article ὁ carried "Already". ἤδη is printed at the end of N1904
  4:35, so it is linked across the verse division: `m` in 4:35 and
  `g @4:35 27 = Already` in 4:36.
- 4:39: τόν carried the "the" of "the woman's". Now τῆς → the, with woman's,
  and τόν is `s`. τὸν λόγον … μαρτυρούσης ("the word … testifying") →
  testimony.
- 4:42: πιστεύομεν carried "now". Now Οὐκέτι ("no longer") → now + not only,
  and πιστεύομεν → We + believe.
- 4:50: λόγῳ carried "at His word". Now ἐπίστευσεν → took + at (the idiom
  "took … at His word"), τῷ → His and λόγῳ → word.
- 4:52: ἑβδόμην carried "at the seventh". Now ὥραν → at + hour and ἑβδόμην →
  the seventh. κομψότερον ἔσχεν → had recovered.

Joined units (rule 3):

- Article + participle: 4:10 ὁ λέγων → asking; 4:13 ὁ πίνων → who drinks;
  4:23 τοὺς προσκυνοῦντας → to worship; 4:24 τοὺς προσκυνοῦντας → His
  worshipers; 4:25 ὁ λεγόμενος → called; 4:26 ὁ λαλῶν → who speak; 4:34 τοῦ
  πέμψαντος → of Him who sent; 4:36 the reaper / the sower / the reaper; 4:37
  ὁ σπείρων → sows and ὁ θερίζων → reaps.
- Answered/said pairs: ἀπεκρίθη … καὶ εἶπεν → one verb (4:10, 4:13, 4:17).
- With ἄν: ἄν + aorist → would have (4:10, twice), and ὃς ἄν → whoever
  (4:14).
- Particles and phrases: Ἐὰν μή → Unless (4:48); καὶ γάρ → for (4:23); Ἐν τῷ
  μεταξύ → Meanwhile (4:31); ἐν ᾗ → when (4:52).
- Other: ἀπῆλθεν πάλιν → returned (4:3); τοῦ Ἰακώβ → Jacob's (4:6); ἐκ τῆς
  Σαμαρίας → Samaritan (4:7); εἰς τὴν ἑορτήν → there (4:45).

## Readings (only where EVIDENCE shows a difference)

- 4:1 (2/2): "Jesus" is `t` and ὁ Κύριος is `s`. BSB's Greek probably has
  Ἰησοῦς for Κύριος. "John" is linked, probably spelled Ἰωάννης.
- 4:4, 4:5, 4:7: "Samaria" is linked, probably spelled Σαμαρείας. The second
  extra word in 4:5 is probably τῷ before Ἰωσήφ; it changes no link.
- 4:9 (3/3): the three Samaritan words were the only unmatched publisher
  tokens, probably spelled Σαμαρῖτις, Σαμαρίτιδος and Σαμαρίταις. They are
  linked with a spelling note.
- 4:11 (0/2): "the woman" is `t`. BSB's Greek probably has ἡ γυνή after λέγει
  αὐτῷ.
- 4:17 (1/2): εἶπες is probably εἶπας, and there is probably an added αὐτῷ
  after εἶπεν. The verb is linked with a spelling note. The added word has no
  English.
- 4:24 (0/1): "Him" is `t`. BSB's Greek probably has αὐτόν after
  προσκυνοῦντας. The publisher's draft left "Him" unowned, which suggests its
  link.
- 4:29 (1/1): probably ὅσα for ἅ. ἅ is `s` (as in 4:39), and "ever" stays with
  ἐποίησα.
- 4:31: "Rabbi" is linked with a spelling note.
- 4:34: ποιῶ is probably ποιήσω. It is linked → do with a same-verb note.
- 4:39, 4:40: "Samaritans" is linked with a spelling note.
- 4:51 (0/1): "his" in "his servants" is `t`. BSB's Greek probably has αὐτοῦ
  after δοῦλοι. The article οἱ is `s`.
- 4:53 (0/1): BSB's Greek probably has ἐν before ἐκείνῃ. There is no English
  effect.

## Notable decisions

- 4:5: τῷ → "to", because the dative article marks the indeclinable Joseph,
  as in Luke 1:55.
- 4:10: Δός → "for". BSB turns the quoted "Give me a drink" into "asking you
  for a drink", and μοι is `s`.
- 4:11 and 4:12: the article τό → "this" ("this living water", "this well"),
  with a note. This follows the anaphoric-article precedent in Mark.
- 4:14: ἀλλά → "Indeed" is kept as the publisher had it.
- 4:35: καί → "until" ("four months, and the harvest comes" → "four months
  until the harvest"). ἔρχεται is `s`, since "until" does not mean "comes".
  ἰδού is `s`.
- 4:37: ἐστίν in "one is the sower" is `s`, because BSB says "One sows".
- 4:45: οὖν → "Yet" is kept, with a note on the contrast with 4:44. εἰς τὴν
  Γαλιλαίαν is `s`, because BSB says only "He arrived".
- 4:47: πρός is `s`, and αὐτόν → Him, which is the object of "begged" in
  BSB. γάρ is `s`, because BSB turns the γάρ clause into "who was about to
  die". "who" stays with ἤμελλεν as its built-in subject.
- 4:53: ἐκείνῃ → "very" ("this was the very hour"), and "this was" is `t`.
- 4:54: πάλιν is `s`, because it is redundant with δεύτερον.

## Doubts for the peer

- 4:2: the intensive αὐτός is `s` and the cleft "it was … who" is `t`. The
  other option is αὐτός → "it was + who", if the peer reads the cleft as BSB's
  rendering of the emphasis.
- 4:6: οὖν → "Since" (by analogy with the When-on-καί rule). The other option
  is οὖν `s` and "Since" `t`.
- 4:50: ἐπίστευσεν → took + at, τῷ → His, λόγῳ → word, and ὃν εἶπεν αὐτῷ is
  `s`. The other option is to keep the publisher's λόγῳ → "at His word" and
  make τῷ `s`.
- 4:39: λόγον and μαρτυρούσης are joined → testimony. The other option is
  λόγον → testimony, with the participle `s`.
- 4:24: τοὺς προσκυνοῦντας → "His worshipers" and "Him" is `t` (reading). If
  the extra word in BSB's Greek is what "His" renders, then "His" should be `t`
  and "Him" the English object.
- 4:27: "from her" in the first question is `t`. The other option is αὐτῆς →
  her + her, if μετ’ αὐτῆς is read with both questions.
- 4:53: ἐκείνῃ → "very". It could instead be ἐκείνῃ → "this + very".
- 4:52: "at" moved from ἑβδόμην to ὥραν. This is a small change and could be
  kept as the publisher had it.
