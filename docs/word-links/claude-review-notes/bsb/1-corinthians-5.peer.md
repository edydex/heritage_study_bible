## Answers to the author's doubts

- 5:8 ζύμῃ: kept as first ζύμῃ `s`, "bread#0" `t`, second ζύμῃ =
  "leavened". BSB reads "not with the old bread, leavened with malice and
  wickedness". "bread" is not a word for leaven, so it does not render ζύμῃ;
  this is a correct `s` + `t`, not a split pair. "leavened" does render the
  second ζύμῃ: "leaven of malice" becomes "leavened with malice", the same
  meaning in another part of speech. Joining both ζύμῃ to "leavened" would
  over-claim, because "leavened" is attached to "with malice".
- 5:1 οὐδέ = "intolerable even": kept (publisher row, with the note). The
  Greek clause has no verb, and the negative of οὐδέ survives in English
  only as the in- of "intolerable". Making "intolerable" `t` would tell the
  reader that the negative is English-only, which is false. The note says
  the negative is carried by "intolerable".
- 5:2 οὐχί + ἐπενθήσατε = "Shouldn’t you + have been stricken with grief":
  kept joined. "Shouldn’t" holds both the negative and the modal, and the
  modal belongs to the finite verb, so the contraction cannot sit on οὐχί
  alone. This matches the reviewed handling of modal/auxiliary
  contractions: Matthew 18:33 "Shouldn’t", Mark 12:24 "Aren’t you
  mistaken", John 12:5 "wasn’t + sold", Luke 17:8 "won’t he tell", Mark 9:28
  "couldn’t". James 4:1 "Don’t" on οὐκ alone is different: its "do" is a
  dummy auxiliary and the Greek question has no verb.
- 5:4 "am with you" `t`: kept. "am" is a supplied copula and "with you" has
  no Greek word. BSB does not repeat "assembled", so this is not a word
  understood again.
- 5:10 πάντως `s`, "I was" and "including" `t`: kept. πάντως means
  "altogether/at all"; "including" means something else, so the
  publisher's πάντως = "including" over-claimed.
- 5:3 ἐγώ = all four "I": kept. ἐγώ is an explicit pronoun and the subject
  of κέκρικα and of each participle, which is the Acts 22:3 pattern.
  Participle-only "I"s would be `t` only if there were no explicit pronoun.
- 5:12 "business" `t`: kept. τί is "What"; "business" is a supplied head
  noun, and "is it" is a supplied copula.

## Points checked and confirmed

- 5:3: μέν … δέ = "Although" with γάρ `s` (John 10:41); the publisher's γάρ
  = "Although" was wrong, since γάρ means "for". οὕτως `s` (the publisher's
  "who" was wrong); τὸν … κατεργασάμενον = "the one who did", with "on"
  joined to κέκρικα ("pronounced judgment on"). "from you", "with you" and
  "and" `t` (Colossians 2:5).
- 5:10: ἐπεὶ … ἄρα = "In that case" (ἐπεί "since/otherwise" with ἄρα
  "then"); ἐκ + ἐξελθεῖν = "to leave" ("go out of" = leave); τοῦ (18) =
  "this" (an article rendered "this", as in 1 Corinthians 3:12, Acts 25:22).
- 5:11: ἐάν τις = "anyone who" (John 8:52); "but" `t`, since the Greek has
  no connective before ᾖ; "With" joins συνεσθίειν (the συν- of "eat with",
  Luke 15:2) and is no longer on the lone article τῷ.
- 5:13: the Deuteronomy quotation is linked word by word: ἐξάρατε =
  "Expel", τόν = "the", πονηρόν = "wicked man", ἐξ = "from among", ὑμῶν
  αὐτῶν = "you" (the reflexive pair as one unit). δέ and ὁ `s`.
- 5:7: the Passover terms are linked word by word with no interpretive
  notes: ἄζυμοι = "unleavened" (the note is only about word order), πάσχα =
  "Passover lamb" (Luke 22:7), ἐτύθη = "has been sacrificed", νέον = "new",
  φύραμα = "a + batch". "really" `t` is an added adverb.
- 5:2: ἵνα = "and" kept with its note (Luke 20:14, Philippians 1:10). BSB
  turns the purpose clause into a second thing "you" should have done.
  Also kept: ὁ … πράξας = "the man who did"; τὸ ἔργον `s` with τοῦτο =
  "this"; μέσου = "fellowship".
- Lone articles: none carries "of the"/"to the"/"in the". 5:4, 5:5 and
  5:10 put the case word on the noun; 5:12-13 τούς = "those" are articles
  used as pronouns. Supplied copulas (5:1 "is", 5:6 "is", 5:12 "is it"),
  "that there is" (5:1) and the supplied objects or nouns ("the church",
  "business") are `t`.

## Still uncertain

- 5:2 ἵνα = "and": the reviewed precedent is split (Luke 20:14 and
  Philippians 1:10 link it; Ephesians 4:29 and 5:27 use `s` + `t`). It is
  kept because BSB's "and" still carries the clause link. ἵνα `s` with
  "and" `t` would also be defensible.
