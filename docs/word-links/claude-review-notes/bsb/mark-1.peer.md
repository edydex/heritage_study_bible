## Changes (1 verse)

- 1:31: τῆς = "her", χειρός = "by the hand" → τῆς = "the",
  χειρός = "by" + "hand", plus `t her#1` = "English names the person whose
  hand is taken; κρατήσας τῆς χειρός has no pronoun." Why: the English
  has its own "the" in "by the hand", and that "the" renders τῆς (rule 5).
  The old row linked the article to "her", so tapping "her" showed a Greek
  "the". "her" names the person grasped, and the Greek leaves her unstated
  ("taking hold of the hand"). This is different from τά = "their" in 1:18
  and 1:19: there the English has no "the", so the article's possessive
  force is the only Greek source for "their".

Nothing else was changed. The author's other corrections of publisher rows
are right. These include the supplied "Jesus" and "The people" moved to
`t`, the `t and` lines after participles, ἐξῆλθεν = came out with ἐξ αὐτοῦ
`s` (1:26), ἔννυχα λίαν = while it was still dark (1:35), μηκέτι /
δύνασθαι split (1:45), and τόν = son (1:19), which avoids a split pair.

## Textual claims

- 1:25 `t He said` (λέγων): **correct.** The publisher's Greek has 11
  words against 10, and all 10 N1904 words match. So there is exactly one
  extra word. N1904 has no verb of saying in this verse.
- 1:27 `t one another` and αὐτούς `s` (πρὸς ἑαυτούς): **correct.** N1904
  has 23 words, 21 matched, against 24 publisher words. The two unmatched
  N1904 words are συνζητεῖν (spelled συζητεῖν) and αὐτούς. The three
  publisher-only words are συζητεῖν, πρός and ἑαυτούς.
- 1:8: the publisher has 11 words against 10, all matched. The extra word
  is ἐν before Πνεύματι, as the author's note says.
- 1:1, 1:4 and 1:40 also have one extra publisher word, but it adds no
  English word, so no claim is needed. 1:5, 1:6, 1:9, 1:14, 1:16, 1:17,
  1:19, 1:29, 1:32 and 1:42 differ only by spelling (same word count). The
  other verses have publisher Greek identical to N1904, and none of their
  notes claims a different reading.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 1:27, "one another": **agree with `t`.** The words render πρὸς ἑαυτούς,
  which N1904 lacks. Linking them to συνζητεῖν because of its συν- prefix
  would hide that difference. "began to ask" stays on συνζητεῖν.
- 1:8, "with" on Πνεύματι: **agree with the link.** The plain dative is
  instrumental and needs "with" in English, just as ὕδατι = "with water"
  in the same verse. A `t` would claim that nothing in N1904 stands behind
  "with", and that is false. The note already mentions the publisher's ἐν.
- 1:5, one "all of" for πᾶσα and πάντες: **agree.** The one English "all"
  covers "Jerusalem and the countryside of Judea", so it stands for both
  Greek words. An `s` on πᾶσα would wrongly say it is untranslated.
  `t People` and `t from` are also right: BSB turns the Greek subjects
  into the places people came from.
- 1:41, αὐτοῦ = "His": **keep, but uncertain** (see below).
- 1:37, καί = "when": **agree.** Putting "when" back on εὗρον would
  over-claim the verb. An `s` on καί with a `t` on "when" would be a split
  pair.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 1:41: τὴν χεῖρα αὐτοῦ ἥψατο can also mean "touched him", with αὐτοῦ
  as the genitive object of ἥψατο. Mark often puts that object before
  ἅπτομαι (3:10; 5:30–31; 8:22; 10:13), and Matthew 8:3 and Luke 5:13
  read ἥψατο αὐτοῦ. The reviewed Matthew 8:3, which has the same English,
  links αὐτοῦ = "the man" and puts "His" under `t`. Kept here because the
  publisher's own row and the plain word order support "His hand". If it
  is changed, the result is αὐτοῦ = "the man" and `t His`.
- 1:35: the καί between ἐξῆλθεν and ἀπῆλθεν sits inside "slipped out",
  and the English "and" is `t`. Kept, because that "and" joins "got up"
  (ἀναστάς) to "slipped out", not the two merged verbs. Linking καί =
  "and" is the alternative.
- 1:22 `t The people` versus "people brought" (1:32) and "people came"
  (1:45), which are linked with the verb. The indefinite "people" stands
  for the impersonal third-person plural, as in Matthew 14:35, while
  "The people" names a definite group. Both treatments are defensible.
- 1:6 ἔσθων = "His food was" is a free recast, but it is not wrong.
