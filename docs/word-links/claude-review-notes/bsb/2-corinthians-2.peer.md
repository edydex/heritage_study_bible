# BSB 2 Corinthians 2: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 17 of 17 verses (2:1-17), every line of every verse, starting from
the author's final file. No KEEP blocks. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 17, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 5 in 3 verses (4 link changes, 1 note-only change). The other 14
verses are confirmed as the author left them.

## Changes

- 2:9 "My": `t` → linked to ἔγραψα ("My + writing"). The author's own reason
  ("the person is in the verb ἔγραψα") names a Greek owner, so `t` left a
  split pair. "My purpose in writing" is the first person of the finite
  verb, and person words go on the finite verb. Precedent: John 8:41
  ἔχομεν = "Our + is".
- 2:9 "would": `t` → linked to ἐστε ("would + be"). "would" covers both
  "stand the test" and "be obedient", and ἐστε is the only finite verb in
  the clause. Modal words go on the finite verb, even a present εἰμί:
  1 Corinthians 7:14 ἐστιν = "would be", John 6:64, and 2:3 in this chapter
  (ἐστιν = "would share"). τὴν δοκιμήν = "stand the test" is unchanged.
- 2:10 note only: on ᾧ = "If + anyone", "The relative ᾧ ("to whom")" →
  "The general relative ᾧ ("to whomever")". This states why a relative can
  carry "If".
- 2:16 death clause: ἐκ = "of", θανάτου = "death", θάνατον = "demise", εἰς
  `s`, "and" `t` → ἐκ θανάτου εἰς θάνατον = "of death and demise" as one
  unit. ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν is joined as one unit, and the two clauses are
  parallel. The ἐκ … εἰς idiom is joined the same way in Romans 1:17 (ἐκ
  πίστεως εἰς πίστιν = "by faith from start to finish"). BSB does not
  translate either clause word for word: it renders the death clause with
  two nouns and "and", and gives the two ζωή one "life". A word-by-word life
  clause would need ἐκ ζωῆς `s` and εἰς alone = "brings".
- 2:16 "that": taken out of the life unit and made `t` ("English supplies a
  relative frame for the phrase ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν"). The unit is now
  ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν = "brings life". A relative "that" with no Greek
  relative is `t`, as with "that comes" in Romans 1:5, 1:17 and 4:13, and
  "that leads" in 1 John 5:16. "brings" stays linked because it renders the
  "to (life)" of the idiom.

## Author's doubts

- 2:10 "If" on ᾧ: keep. ᾧ … χαρίζεσθε is a general relative clause
  ("whomever you forgive"), and BSB renders it as a condition: "If … anyone".
  "If" is on ᾧ alone, not on τι. τι is `s` because BSB has no "anything" in
  this clause. "forgive#1" (I also forgive) is χαρίζεσθε understood again
  with κἀγώ (John 15:4 rule). "him" stays `t` as a supplied object.
- 2:16: settled as above (both clauses joined as units). "we are" `t` is
  confirmed: the clause has no verb, and ἐσμέν stands in 2:15. A
  cross-verse link would only move a supplied copula.
- 2:3 "as I did" on τοῦτο αὐτό: keep (publisher row, with αὐτό added).
  "as I did" is BSB's phrase-level rendering of the object "this very
  thing". If "I" became `t`, "as … did" would still claim τοῦτο αὐτό, and
  the "I" cannot link to ἔγραψα a second time (only an explicit pronoun may
  link to a repeated "I").
- 2:9 "would": moved to ἐστε (see Changes).
- 2:2 and 2:3 "those whom"/"those who": keep. BSB turns the generic
  singular ὁ λυπούμενος into a plural, and the note says so. ὧν is a
  relative with its antecedent left implied ("from those of whom").

## Points confirmed

- 2:1: δέ = "So" with the note "BSB's Greek text has a different
  connective here". EVIDENCE gives 1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher
  word not in N1904. All 14 English words are matched by the publisher, yet
  "So" has no N1904 row, so the publisher linked "So" to the differing
  word. "So" is a normal rendering of δέ, so it is linked under rule 9. The
  note does not name the publisher's word. "visit" on τὸ … ἐλθεῖν ("to make
  + visit") and ἐν λύπῃ = "painful" are right: the coming is the visit.
- 2:4: ἀγάπην … ἣν ἔχω = "I love" is honest ("have love" = "love"; the
  relative is absorbed). In the JSON, τήν is already `s` with the standard
  reason. Only the note's gloss quotes the article. εἰς ὑμᾶς = "you" works
  like ἐξ ἐμοῦ = "I" in 2:2.
- 2:9: εἰς τοῦτο = "purpose", ἵνα = "to", γνῶ = "see", τὴν δοκιμήν = "stand
  the test" and ὑμῶν = "you" claim nothing extra. "in", "you" (object of
  writing), "was" and "and" are rightly `t`.
- 2:11: "of" belongs on ἀγνοοῦμεν ("we are + unaware of"). νοήματα is
  accusative, so "of" only completes "unaware".
- 2:17: "for profit" on καπηλεύοντες is right, because profit is part of
  what καπηλεύω means. ἐξ = "with" is right, and ὡς (12) is `s`. The
  εἰλικρινίας note "Same word, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text" is
  supported: EVIDENCE shows 1/1, and "sincerity" has no N1904 row although
  all 32 English words are matched. The review file does not name the
  spelling.
- Rule checks across the chapter:
  - Lone articles carry only "the": 2:6 ἡ, τῶν; 2:12 τό; 2:14 τήν, τῆς;
    2:17 τόν.
  - No participle carries a person: 2:3 ἐλθών ("my" `t`) and πεποιθώς
    ("I" `t`); 2:12 Ἐλθών ("I" `t`); 2:13 ἀποταξάμενος, with "I" on
    ἐξῆλθον.
  - Supplied words are `t`: copulas (2:2, 2:6, 2:9, 2:16 twice), objects
    (2:5 "it", 2:7 "him", 2:9 "you", 2:10 "him"), possessives (2:3 "my",
    2:8 "your"), "there" in 2:13 and "make" in 2:3.
  - Connectives rendered by a different connective are linked: 2:1 δέ
    "So" and 2:13 ἀλλά "So".
  - The chapter has no μή questions.

## Still uncertain

- 2:9 "My" on ἔγραψα: `t` is also defensible, since "My" grammatically
  modifies "purpose". I linked it because its only possible Greek owner is
  the verb's first person.
- 2:16 units or word by word: both are honest. I chose units for
  parallelism and to follow Romans 1:17.
- 2:5 ἀπό = "to" and μέρους = "some degree" are kept as separate links, as
  in Romans 15:15 and 15:24. "all of you" puts "of" on πάντας in 2:3 but on
  ὑμᾶς in 2:5. Both publisher rows are acceptable, so they were not
  harmonized.
