# BSB Mark 16: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 21 (16:1-20, plus N1904's shorter ending at 16:99, which BSB
does not print). No verse was already reviewed. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 21, "problems": 0}`.

57 of the 262 publisher `g` rows changed, in 17 verses. 3 of them only gained
an unlinked Greek token, with the English unchanged (ἀνατείλαντος τοῦ in
"just after sunrise", 16:2; πρός in "one another", 16:3; γάρ in "in their
fear", 16:8). The other 54 were corrected, split or joined. Matthew 28:1-10
(reviewed) was followed where the wording is parallel.

## Publisher rows corrected

"When/As/After" on the wrong word:

- 16:1: the opening καί carried "When". The genitive absolute διαγενομένου now
  takes When + was over; καί is `s`.
- 16:5: the opening καί carried "When". The participle εἰσελθοῦσαι now takes
  "When they entered"; καί is `s`, and εἰς is `s` (absorbed in "entered").
- 16:11: κἀκεῖνοι carried "And when they". Now κἀκεῖνοι = And + they, and
  ἀκούσαντες = when + heard.
- 16:14: αὐτοῖς carried "as they". Now ἀνακειμένοις = as + were eating, and
  αὐτοῖς = they.

A supplied "and" joining a participle to the main verb is now `t`: 16:1 ("and
anoint"), 16:8 ("and ran away"), 16:10 ("and told"), 16:13 ("and reported"),
16:15 ("and preach"), 16:20 ("and preached"). In 16:20 the "and" before "the
Lord" (on the article τοῦ) is also `t`: it introduces the genitive absolute,
recast as a finite clause.

Negatives and auxiliaries on the wrong word:

- 16:6: Μή = "Do not" → not; ἐκθαμβεῖσθε = Do + be alarmed (as Matthew 28:5).
- 16:8: εἶπαν = "they did not say" and οὐδενί = "a word to anyone". Now
  οὐδέν = not + a word, οὐδενί = to anyone, εἶπαν = they did + say.
- 16:13: ἐπίστευσαν = "but they did not believe", οὐδέ = "either". Now
  οὐδέ = but + not + either, ἐπίστευσαν = they did + believe.
- 16:14: ἐπίστευσαν = "they did not believe" → οὐκ = not, verb = they did +
  believe.
- 16:18: βλάψῃ = "it will not harm" → οὐ μή = not, βλάψῃ = it will + harm.
- 16:1: ἐλθοῦσαι = "they could go" → go; "they could" moves to the
  subjunctive ἀλείψωσιν (they could + anoint).

English-only words that a Greek word had claimed (now `t`):

- Subjects: Jesus (16:9 ἀναστάς, 16:11 ζῇ, 16:12, 16:14 ἐφανερώθη);
  "the women" (16:8).
- Objects: "it" (16:11, 16:13, 16:20 "accompanied it"), "them" (16:14
  "rebuked them for", 16:20 "worked through them").
- Others: "day" (16:2 μιᾷ, 16:9 πρώτῃ, as Matthew 28:1); "the body of"
  (16:1, αὐτόν = Jesus); "their" with χεῖρας (16:18).

Other changes:

- 16:6: ὁ (δέ) = he, as the subject pronoun; λέγει = said (as Matthew 28:9).
- 16:11: the passive ἐθεάθη ὑπ’ αὐτῆς ("He was seen by her") was all on
  ἐθεάθη as "she had seen Him". Now ἐθεάθη = had seen Him, ὑπ’ αὐτῆς = she.
- Article + participle joined (rule 3): 16:6 τὸν ἐσταυρωμένον = who was
  crucified; 16:10 τοῖς … γενομένοις = those who had been; 16:14 τοῖς
  θεασαμένοις = those who had seen; 16:16 ὁ πιστεύσας = Whoever believes and
  ὁ … ἀπιστήσας = whoever does not believe; 16:17 τοῖς πιστεύσασιν = those
  who believe.
- 16:18: καλῶς ἕξουσιν joined as one idiom = they will be made well.

## Notable decisions

- Different readings, only where EVIDENCE allows. The publisher TSV is not in
  the checkout, so the words were inferred from the EVIDENCE counts and the
  BSB English:
  - 16:2: μνῆμα = tomb, noting BSB's Greek μνημεῖον (same meaning).
  - 16:4: ἀνακεκύλισται = had been rolled away, noting BSB's Greek
    ἀποκεκύλισται (same meaning).
  - 16:18: "with their hands" is `t`: BSB follows the reading (καὶ) ἐν ταῖς
    χερσίν, which N1904 lacks (4 extra publisher words).
- 16:99, N1904's shorter ending: `x targetAbsent`. All 33 tokens are `s` with
  one reason: BSB does not print the shorter ending of Mark found in N1904.
  The bracket characters in 16:9 "[[Ἀναστὰς" and 16:20 "σημείων.]]" are part
  of the tokens and are linked normally.
- Kept publisher rows, with a note: 16:1 ἡ (τοῦ Ἰακώβου) = the mother, as
  Mark 3:18 τόν = son; 16:4 καί = But and γάρ = even though; 16:12
  πορευομένοις = along; 16:20 τόν = His (article with possessive force);
  16:11 ἠπίστησαν and 16:16 ἀπιστήσας keep "did/does not believe" (the
  negation is inside the verb).
- 16:8: εἶχεν γὰρ αὐτάς is `s`. BSB recasts "trembling and amazement seized
  them" as "trembling and bewildered"; τρόμος and ἔκστασις are linked.
  ἀπό is `s`, because "the tomb" is the object of "left".
- 16:19: μὲν οὖν and the article of the infinitive (τὸ λαλῆσαι) are `s`.

## Doubts for the peer

- 16:20: the καί between συνεργοῦντος and βεβαιοῦντος is `s`, and "and"
  before "the Lord" is `t`. I judged them different (BSB drops the inner καί
  and adds a clause coordinator), but a peer may prefer καί → that "and".
- 16:8: "not" is on οὐδέν rather than οὐδενί. Joining both as one
  negative-concord group is the alternative.
- 16:1: αὐτόν = Jesus with "the body of" as `t`. Keeping the whole phrase on
  αὐτόν, as the publisher did, is the alternative.
- 16:16: ὁ πιστεύσας joined, although the one article also governs
  βαπτισθείς; Matthew 10:39-41 kept ὁ = Whoever on its own.
- 16:17 "in new" (καιναῖς) and 16:15 "to every" (πάσῃ) keep the dative's
  "in/to" on the adjective, as the publisher did; it could move to the noun.
- 16:2: "just" is kept inside "just after sunrise" on the genitive absolute;
  it could be `t`.
