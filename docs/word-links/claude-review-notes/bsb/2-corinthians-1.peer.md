# BSB 2 Corinthians 1: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 24 of 24 verses (1:1-24). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording, and
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 24, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 2 verses (1:14, 1:17). The other 22 verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 1:14: καύχημα = "boast of" + "boast of" and ἐσμεν = "us" → καύχημα ἐσμεν
  joined = "boast of us" + "boast of". BSB recasts "we are your boast" as
  "you may boast of us". Linking the copula ἐσμεν on its own to "us" made a
  verb stand for a pronoun. Making it `s` with "us" `t` would split a pair,
  because the "we" of ἐσμεν is exactly the "us" that is boasted of. The
  joined unit treats copula and predicate noun as one verbal idea, as 1:7
  does with κοινωνοί ἐστε = "you share" (and 2 Corinthians 13:5 with
  ἀδόκιμοί ἐστε). The repeated "boast of" in the comparison stays with the
  unit; it is understood again across a change of person (ἐσμεν → ἐστε).
  ὑμῶν = "you", ὑμεῖς = "you" and ἡμῶν = "we" stay linked as role-reversed
  pronouns for the same people. ὑμεῖς gained a short note. "may", "will"
  and "already" stay `t`.
- 1:17: ᾖ παρ’ ἐμοί all `s` and "I really mean" `t` → παρ’ ἐμοί = "I"
  (the speaker, "with me"); ᾖ `s`; "really mean" `t`; "say" stays `t`.
  The old `s` reason said BSB "recasts" the phrase as "say … I really
  mean". That describes a rendering while leaving it unlinked. The "I" is
  the same person as ἐμοί, in a different role, as ἡμῶν → "we" in 1:8 and
  1:14. "say" and "really mean" do not mean "be", so ᾖ correctly stays
  `s`.

## Points settled (author doubts and caller questions)

- 1:9 (ἐν ἑαυτοῖς = "felt"): kept. "We had the sentence of death in
  ourselves" means in our own minds, and BSB (like NIV and ESV) expresses
  that inward sense with "felt". This is a shifted rendering of the same
  meaning, not a word of different meaning, so `s` + `t` would hide a real
  rendering. Joining ἐν ἑαυτοῖς ἐσχήκαμεν would blur the clear split.
  ἐσχήκαμεν = "we were under" ("we have had") is right, and the publisher's
  reversed rows were wrong.
- 1:11: kept. BSB reads "Then many will give thanks on our behalf for the
  favor shown us in answer to their prayers". ἐκ πολλῶν προσώπων ("from
  many persons") together = "many", the subject. εἰς ἡμᾶς ("toward us") =
  "shown us" fits the idea of "toward". διά = "in answer to", and the
  second πολλῶν = "their": "the prayers of many" told by pointing back
  to the many. "prayers" `t`, since the Greek has no word for prayer here
  and this is no parallel clause. "for" belongs to the active recast of
  the passive εὐχαριστηθῇ ("give thanks for"), and ἵνα = "Then" is a
  connective rendered by another connective.
- 1:18 ("as surely as" on πιστός, ὅτι `s`): kept. This matches Romans
  14:11 (ζῶ = "As surely as + live", ὅτι `s`). ὅτι introduces what is
  sworn and has no English word in BSB.
- 1:17 καί = "when": kept. It is a connective rendered by another
  connective joining the two answers. ἃ βουλεύομαι = "my plans" with the
  second βουλεύομαι = "do I make": kept, as the honest split of "what I
  plan, do I plan".
- 1:6 (ἐν `s`, "in you" `t`): kept. The Greek ἐν governs ὑπομονῇ ("in
  endurance"). BSB's "in" governs a supplied "you" and makes "patient
  endurance" the object, so "in" does not have ἐν's role.
- 1:1 ἐν ὅλῃ = "throughout" (in the whole of): confirmed. The publisher
  had it on οὖσιν, which was wrong.
- 1:24 ὑμῶν = "your" (joy): confirmed. ὑμῶν follows τῆς χαρᾶς, and "with
  you" is BSB's supplied partner of "fellow workers".
- 1:20 "is spoken" `t`: confirmed. The clause has no verb.
- 1:12 "sincerity" note: confirmed. EVIDENCE shows 1 N1904 word unmatched,
  and the publisher left εἰλικρινίᾳ and "sincerity" unlinked, so that is
  the word in question. The note ("Same word, spelled differently in BSB's
  Greek text") does not name the publisher's spelling. No other reading is
  claimed. 1:14 and 1:17 differ only in order or form, and none is
  claimed there.
- 1:23 ἐμήν = "my" (from "upon my soul"): kept. BSB folds ἐπὶ τὴν ἐμὴν
  ψυχήν into "as my witness", so "my" carries the possessive.
- 1:22 "His" inside σφραγισάμενος ("placed His seal"): kept. It is part of
  BSB's periphrasis for the middle "sealed" (sealed as His own), not a
  possessive added to a Greek noun. It matches 1:10 "placed our hope" for
  ἠλπίκαμεν.
- 1:16 "on my way" inside δι’ … διελθεῖν and προπεμφθῆναι: kept, as an
  idiom for "pass through" and "be sent on". "I wanted", "then" and "have"
  stay `t`.

Also checked and agreed: every lone article now carries only "the", an
article used as a possessive, or `s`; "of"/"to"/"in"/"for"/"by" sit on
the nouns (1:1, 1:3, 1:6, 1:7, 1:11, 1:24). No supplied copula or frame
sits on a Greek word. Person and modal words sit only on finite verbs
(1:4, 1:9, 1:13, 1:23). Participles carry no person (1:7 "we", 1:17
"I", 1:21 "He" are `t`). μήτι is `s` (1:17).

## Still uncertain

- 1:14: the joined καύχημα ἐσμεν unit is the least bad option, not a
  clean one. A reader who taps the second "boast of" also sees ἐσμεν,
  although the copula understood there would be ἐστε.
- 1:10: "us" after each ῥύσεται stays `t` as a supplied object. Linking
  the repeats to the one stated ἡμᾶς (as a word understood again) is a
  defensible alternative.
- 1:11: "prayers" (second) `t`, not understood again from δεήσει. That is
  a judgment about whether διὰ πολλῶν implies prayers.

## Coordinator adjustment at registration

- 1:10: ἡμᾶς now links to all three "us" (one object shared by the three
  deliver verbs, the Acts 6:12 / 1 Corinthians 4:14 rule), settling the open
  point above; the two `t us` lines are removed.
