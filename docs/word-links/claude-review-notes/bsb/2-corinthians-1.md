Conventions followed: 1 Corinthians 1:2 for "To + church" with the article
= "the" (1:1). Romans 15:6 and 1 Corinthians 1:7-8 for τοῦ `s` and "of +
Lord", with ἡμῶν = "our" (1:3, 1:14). Ephesians 1:3 and 1 Peter 1:3 for
"Blessed" with "be" `t` (1:3). Romans 1:13 and 1 Thessalonians 4:13 for οὐ
θέλομεν (οὐ = "not", θέλομεν = "We do + want") (1:8). Acts (several) for
"province of" `t` (1:8). Romans 14:11 for an oath formula carrying "as
surely as" (1:18). Romans 1, Romans 15:13, Ephesians 1 and Philippians 1
for εἰς τό + infinitive = "so that" (1:4). Acts 10:25 for an articular infinitive's τοῦ joining its
infinitive (1:8 τοῦ ζῆν). 1 Corinthians 1:8 for a relative ὅς = "He"
(1:10). README rule 3 for article + participle units. The brief for
supplied objects, copulas, adverbs, "that" and participle subjects (`t`),
μήτι `s`, and words understood again.

## Publisher rows corrected

Lone articles carrying case or other words (the article gets "the" or `s`,
the case word goes to the noun):

- 1:1 τῇ "To the" → "the"; ἐκκλησίᾳ → "To + church".
- 1:6 τῶν "of the" → "the"; παθημάτων → "of + sufferings".
- 1:7 τῶν "in our" and τῆς "in our" → "our" (article used as a
  possessive); "in" goes to παθημάτων and παρακλήσεως.
- 1:8 τοῦ "of" → τοῦ ζῆν = "of life" (articular infinitive).
- 1:11 τῇ "by" → "your" (possessive article); δεήσει "your prayers" →
  "by + prayers".
- 1:20 τό "are" → `s`, "are" `t` (verbless clause).
- 1:24 τῆς "for" → `s`; χαρᾶς → "for + joy"; τῇ "by" → `s`; πίστει → "by
  + faith".

- 1:13 "us" off ἐπιγνώσεσθε; "ability" off ἀναγινώσκετε.

## Notable decisions

- The key terms are linked word by word with no interpretive notes:
  παράκλησις/παρακαλέω = "comfort"/"comforts"/"comforted",
  θλῖψις = "troubles"/"trouble"/"hardships", θλιβόμεθα = "we are
  afflicted", πάθημα = "sufferings", ἀπόκριμα = "sentence", ἐπαγγελίαι =
  "the promises", ἀρραβών = "as a pledge", σφραγισάμενος = "placed His
  seal".
- Names linked despite spelling: Τιμόθεος/Τιμοθέου = "Timothy",
  Σιλουανοῦ = "Silvanus", Ἀχαΐᾳ = "Achaia", Ἀσίᾳ = "Asia", Μακεδονία =
  "Macedonia", Ἰουδαίαν = "Judea", Κόρινθος = "Corinth".
- Articles used as possessives keep the possessive: 1:1 ὁ = "our", 1:7
  τῶν/τῆς = "our", 1:11 τῇ = "your", 1:22 τοῦ = "His".
- 1:17: μήτι `s` (question marker); ἄρα and οὖν `s`; καί between the
  answers = "when"; ᾖ παρ’ ἐμοί `s`, with "say" and "I really mean" `t`.
- 1:20: αὐτῷ = "Christ" (pronoun rendered by the name); δι’ ἡμῶν = "our"
  ("our Amen"); διὸ καί keep the publisher's "so" and "And".
- 1:19: δι’ ἡμῶν `s`, since BSB names the preachers only once ("by me and
  Silvanus and Timothy").
- 1:12: γάρ = "And"; μαρτύριον = "testifies" (noun recast as a verb).

## Doubts for the peer

- 1:9 (hardest): ἐν ἑαυτοῖς = "felt" and ἐσχήκαμεν = "we were under" (the
  publisher had them the other way, with "we were under" on the reflexive).
  The alternative is to join ἐν ἑαυτοῖς ἐσχήκαμεν = "felt + we were
  under".
- 1:14: the recast "we are your boast … you are ours" → "you may boast of
  us … we will boast of you" is split as καύχημα = "boast of" (twice),
  ὑμῶν = "you", ἐσμεν = "us", ὑμεῖς = "you", ἡμῶν = "we". Joining
  καύχημα ὑμῶν ἐσμεν as one unit is the alternative.
- 1:11: second πολλῶν = "their" and "prayers" `t`; ἐκ πολλῶν προσώπων
  joined = "many"; εἰς ἡμᾶς = "shown us". `s` + `t` for πολλῶν/"their" is
  the alternative.
- 1:18: "as surely as" on πιστός (Romans 14:11) with ὅτι `s`; linking
  ὅτι = "as surely as" is the alternative.
- 1:17: καί = "when" (connective by another connective); `s` + `t` is the
  alternative. ἃ βουλεύομαι = "my plans" with the second βουλεύομαι = "do
  I make"; the alternative is ἃ βουλεύομαι `s`.
- 1:6: ἐν `s` and "in you" `t`; keeping ἐν = "in" with only "you" `t` is
  the alternative.
- 1:23: ἐμήν = "my" ("my witness", from "upon my soul"); the alternative
  is ἐπὶ τὴν ἐμὴν ψυχήν all `s` and "my" `t`.
- 1:22: "His" kept inside σφραγισάμενος ("placed His seal"), as BSB's
  rendering of the middle participle; "His" `t` is the alternative.
- 1:16: "on my way" kept inside δι’ … διελθεῖν and προπεμφθῆναι (as
  "sent on their way" elsewhere).
