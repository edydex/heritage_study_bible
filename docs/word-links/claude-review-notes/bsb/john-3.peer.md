# BSB John 3: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 36 of 36 verses (3:1-36). I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line
against N1904, the displayed BSB, the publisher rows in the draft, and the
publisher record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/john.json`
(its `partial` counts). I also compared the reviewed purpose-infinitive
precedents (Mark 6:37, 13:27, 15:43; Matthew 25:19; Luke 9:54) and the
μονογενής precedent (1 John 4:9).
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 36, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

Four verses changed: two links, two notes.

- 3:4: καί = "to", γεννηθῆναι = "be born" → γεννηθῆναι = "to be born"
  (the publisher's row), καί `s` ("The καί is not expressed; BSB turns the
  second infinitive into the purpose to be born."). Why: γεννηθῆναι is itself
  an infinitive, so "to" is its English infinitive marker. καί does not mean
  "to". The publisher row was correct, and the reviewed chapters keep the
  purpose "to" with the verb and leave καί `s` (Mark 6:37 "to give", Mark
  13:27 "to gather", Luke 9:54 "to consume").
- 3:23: καί = "to", ἐβαπτίζοντο = "be baptized" → ἐβαπτίζοντο = "to be
  baptized" (the publisher's row) with the note "The coordinated imperfect
  ἐβαπτίζοντο ("and were being baptized") is rendered as the purpose to be
  baptized.", καί `s`. Why: this is the same convention as Matthew 25:19
  (συναίρει = "to settle") and Mark 15:43 (ᾐτήσατο = "to ask for"), where
  a finite verb becomes a purpose infinitive. The "When" convention covers
  only a καί that BSB turns into a temporal clause.
- 3:16 note on οὕτως: "οὕτως means "thus, in this way"; English so can carry
  that sense." → "οὕτως means "so" or "in this way"." Why: the old note
  glossed only the manner sense and said BSB's "so" carries it. That quietly
  takes a side in the manner/degree question. The new gloss is neutral. The
  link itself (οὕτως = so) is unchanged.
- 3:18 `t but` reason: "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here; N1904
  joins …" → "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here, rendered but;
  N1904 joins …". Why: the record for 3:18 is 22/22/23 (one extra publisher
  word, none missing), and the publisher's 30 English words are all linked.
  "but" is the only one with no N1904 owner, so the publisher linked it to
  its extra word. This matches the Luke 24:48 wording and still does not name
  the word.

## Points checked and kept

- 3:26: τὸν Ἰωάνην. Ἰωάνην = "him" (with a spelling note), τόν `s`, and
  `t John’s disciples`. Kept. "him" is the object of "came to", the role and
  referent of πρὸς τὸν Ἰωάνην. "John’s disciples" names the subject, which
  the Greek gives only in the verb ending of ἦλθον (taken from 3:25). It is
  the reverse of 3:2 (αὐτόν = "Jesus"). The record agrees: 3:26 is 27/25/27,
  and the draft's unowned English words are exactly "him" and "Rabbi" for
  the two spelling-variant words. So the publisher too linked "him" to the
  name. Its "John’s disciples came" on ἦλθον over-claimed, and the author
  rightly made that `t`.
- 3:29: Ὁ ἔχων … ἐστίν = "belongs to". Kept. BSB turns "the one who has the
  bride is the bridegroom" into "the bride belongs to the bridegroom". The
  "having" and the identifying "is" both go into "belongs to". The other
  choices, ἐστίν `s` or a lone article carrying "belongs to" (the publisher's
  row), are worse. The `t` lines are all right. "the" before bridegroom: the
  predicate νυμφίος has no article. "and#1": it only joins the participle
  phrase ὁ ἑστηκὼς καὶ ἀκούων to the main verb χαρᾷ χαίρει. "is#1": the
  supplied copula of "That joy is mine". "and#2": BSB splits one clause in
  two.
- 3:29: διά = "to hear". Kept from the publisher with its note. "rejoices
  because of the bridegroom's voice" is unidiomatic English, and "overjoyed
  to hear his voice" is BSB's way of giving the cause. So "to hear" as a
  phrase renders the causal διά. Splitting it into "to" plus `t hear` would
  leave "to" as a bare infinitive marker linked to a preposition. Neither is
  perfect, and the publisher's row is not wrong, so it stays.
- 3:27: Οὐ … οὐδὲν ἐὰν μή = "only what". Kept. The four words are one
  negative-exception idiom ("not … anything unless"). BSB turns it into the
  positive "only what". If οὐδέν ("nothing/anything") were split off to
  "what", a tap on "what" would show a word meaning "nothing". The reading
  note ("BSB's Greek has a same-meaning variant in this phrase") is
  supported. The record is 17/15/18. One unmatched word and one extra are
  John's name, which leaves one N1904 word replaced by two publisher words,
  and οὐδέν is the only candidate. The note does not name the form.
- ἄνωθεν (3:3, 3:7, 3:31): the notes give both senses ("again, anew" / "from
  above"), say what BSB chose, and cross-reference. They do not say which
  sense John means. Kept.
- 3:16, 3:18 μονογενής: "one adjective meaning only or unique, rendered one
  and only". This is the lexical gloss used in 1 John 4:9. It makes no claim
  about "begotten" or about doctrine, and "one adjective" explains why three
  English words go to one Greek word. Kept.
- αἰώνιος (3:15, 3:16, 3:36): ζωήν = life and αἰώνιον = eternal are separate
  links with no note. That is right.
- 3:18 `t but`: a different-reading claim is allowed here (EVIDENCE differs),
  and "but" has no N1904 word. Only the reason's wording changed (above).
- 3:34 `t God`: N1904 has no subject for δίδωσιν, and the record has no
  `partial` entry for 3:34, so the publisher's Greek is the same. The reason
  ("English names the subject; the Greek verb δίδωσιν has no stated subject
  here") does not say whether the giver is God or the Son. Kept. The
  publisher's "God gives" on δίδωσιν over-claimed.
- 3:30: δεῖ = must#0 + must#1 (understood again), ἐλαττοῦσθαι = decrease.
  Kept. The publisher's "must decrease" on the infinitive over-claimed, and
  the convention lets a verb understood again link to each English repeat.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 3:4 and 3:23 καί = "to": changed; see above.
- 3:26: agree with Ἰωάνην = "him"; see above.
- 3:27: agree with the four-token group; see above.
- 3:3, 3:5 οὐ = no, τις = one, γεννηθῇ = he is born: agree. "no one" is "not
  + anyone", so οὐ gives "no" and the indefinite τις gives "one". "he" is the
  resumed subject in the English protasis and stays with the verb (rule 4).
  The publisher row is not wrong, so it stays.
- 3:29 διά = "to hear": kept; see above. πεπλήρωται = "it is now complete"
  with οὖν `s`: agree. "now" gives the perfect's present state. ESV keeps both
  "Therefore" (οὖν) and "now", so "now" does not render οὖν.
- 3:28 μαρτυρεῖτε = "can testify": agree. "can" is BSB's modal rendering of
  "you bear witness" (you are in a position to testify), and the publisher
  row is not wrong.
- 3:25 περί = "over the issue of": agree. It is one prepositional rendering
  with no other Greek word.

## Reading claims checked

`partial` records exist only for 3:2, 3:18 and 3:23-28. Every reading claim
is in one of them. The spelling notes (Rabbi 3:2, 3:26; John 3:23-27) match
the unmatched counts. The 3:24 count (8/7/9) shows that the name is the
unmatched word, so the John notes rest on the record. No claim is made for
the extra publisher word in 3:23, 3:24 or 3:28, and none is needed.
