## Publisher rows corrected

English words with no Greek counterpart that had been folded into a Greek
word (now `t`):

- Participle "and": 11:3 "and said" (λέγοντες, joined to 11:2), 11:4 "and
  explained", 11:20 "and began speaking", 11:21 "and turned"; the unlinked
  11:28 "and" (stood up and predicted) is `t` for the same reason.
- Supplied names and objects: 11:24 and 11:25 "Barnabas" (verb ending
  only); 11:26 "him" twice (εὑρών and ἤγαγεν have no object); 11:30 "their
  gifts" (ἀποστείλαντες has no object).
- BSB additions: 11:5 "its" (four corners, as Acts 10:11), 11:17 "the work
  of" (κωλῦσαι τὸν Θεόν = hinder God), 11:26 "back" (had been on εἰς),
  11:28 "that" (had been on Πνεύματος; the Greek has an infinitive clause).
- 11:2 "believers": οἱ ἐκ περιτομῆς is "those of the circumcision"; οἱ =
  the, ἐκ περιτομῆς = circumcised, "believers" `t`.

Words on the wrong Greek word:

- Lone articles carrying a preposition: 11:6 τῆς "of the" and τοῦ "of the";
  11:16 τοῦ "of the"; 11:20 τόν "about the"; 11:22 τῆς "of the"; 11:23 τήν
  "of" and τῇ "with"; 11:24 τῷ "to the"; 11:29 τοῖς "to the". The articles
  keep "the" where English has it; the prepositions go on the nouns (11:23
  τῇ προθέσει: πρόθεσις = with all, τῆς = their as a possessive article).
- "When/As" on a connective: 11:15 "As" moved from δέ to ἐν τῷ with the
  infinitive (Luke 10:38, 11:27); 11:18 "When" moved from δέ to the
  participle ἀκούσαντες; 11:22 "When" moved from δέ to the καί between the
  two clauses (convention for "When …, …"); 11:23 "When" moved from the
  relative ὅς (now "he") to παραγενόμενος.
- Understood verbs on καί: 11:15 καί carried "He had fallen" and 11:17 καί
  carried "He gave". The repeated verbs now link to their stated verbs
  (ἐπέπεσεν = fell + had fallen; ἔδωκεν = gave + gave), "He" is `t`, and καί
  ("also") is `s`.
- 11:5 ἦλθεν had "it came right down to": ἄχρι now has "right down to".
- 11:6 ἀτενίσας κατενόουν ("having gazed, I observed") = I looked + closely;
  "closely" had been on ἥν and "and saw" on κατενόουν, leaving καί and εἶδον
  unlinked.
- 11:9 Ἅ = anything + that; κοίνου had carried "Do not call anything
  impure" (now Do + call + impure, μή = not), as in Acts 10:15.
- 11:10 πάλιν = back (was drawn + up stays on ἀνεσπάσθη).
- 11:17 δυνατός had "to"; "to hinder" is now on κωλῦσαι and δυνατός is `s`.
- 11:19 ἕως = as far as (had been folded into "traveled"); μηδενὶ … εἰ μὴ
  μόνον ("to no one except only") jointly = only.
- 11:23 Θεοῦ = of God (τήν and τοῦ `s`).
- 11:24 Πνεύματος = of the + Spirit, Ἁγίου = Holy, as Acts 6:5 and Luke 4:1.
- 11:26 χρηματίσαι = were + called, πρώτως = first.
- 11:29 τις = his (εὐπορεῖτο = ability).

Negatives split off verbs: 11:8 οὐδέποτε = nothing + ever, with the verb
has + entered; 11:9 μή = not, with Do + call + impure.

## Readings

- 11:11 Καισαρίας: spelled differently in the publisher's Greek (probably
  Καισαρείας); linked to "Caesarea" with a note.
- 11:16 Ἰωάνης: spelled differently (probably Ἰωάννης); linked to "John".
- 11:20 Ἕλληνας: the publisher's Greek has a different word (probably
  Ἑλληνιστάς); BSB's "Greeks" renders N1904 Ἕλληνας, so it is linked with a
  note that does not name the other word.
- 11:22 the publisher's Greek has an extra word (probably διελθεῖν); "to"
  is linked to ἕως ("as far as"), with a note.
- 11:23 the publisher's Greek has an extra word (probably ἐν before τῷ
  Κυρίῳ); "in" is linked to Κυρίῳ with the John 12 note (same meaning; the
  dative alone gives "in").
- 11:28 ἐσήμαινεν: a different form of the verb in the publisher's Greek
  (probably aorist ἐσήμανεν); linked to "predicted" with a note.
- 11:18 is flagged as differing with 0/0 unmatched words, so no reading is
  claimed.

## Notable decisions

- Acts 10 parallel (10:11-16, checked against the Acts 10 author's saved
  work): σκεῦός τι = something, καταβαῖνον `s` (the descent is rendered
  once, by being let down), τέσσαρσιν = four, ἀρχαῖς = by + corners with
  "its" `t`, Ἅ = anything + that, σύ `s`, ἐπὶ τρίς joined.
- 11:11 καὶ ἰδοὺ ἐξαυτῆς jointly = Just then (καὶ ἰδού = Just then is the
  Gospels convention; ἐξαυτῆς "at once" is the core of "Just then").
- 11:11 ἦμεν ("we were") stays linked to "I was staying" with a note. The
  EVIDENCE line shows only one unmatched word here (the Caesarea spelling),
  so no reading is claimed.
- Scene openers: 11:1 δέ = soon (as Mark 4:37 καί = Soon), 11:19 οὖν =
  Meanwhile (μέν `s`), 11:26 δέ = So.
- 11:20 Ἦσαν and οἵτινες are `s`: BSB turns the "there were … who" clause
  into the main clause.
- 11:26 τε (not τε … καί) is `s`: BSB starts a new sentence. 11:21 τε =
  and, since BSB renders it.
- 11:15 "had fallen" is linked to ἐπέπεσεν in full: English pluperfect
  renders the aorist here, so "had" is not treated as an added tense.
- Kept publisher rows "a great"/"a large"/"a good" on the adjective (as
  reviewed Acts 6 and Luke 8), "the#2 Holy" on Ἁγίῳ in 11:16, and τόν =
  the for "the man's home" in 11:12 (as Matthew 13:55).

## Doubts for the peer

- 11:6 ἀτενίσας κατενόουν as one group "I looked + closely": an alternative
  is ἀτενίσας = closely, κατενόουν = I looked.
- 11:19 the four-token group for "only"; an alternative is μόνον = only
  with μηδενί and εἰ μή `s`.
- 11:29 τις = his: could instead be `s` (absorbed by each/his).
- 11:23 "in" linked to Κυρίῳ under a same-meaning reading note, rather than
  `t` for the extra word.
- 11:17 δυνατός `s` rather than joined with κωλῦσαι as "to hinder".
