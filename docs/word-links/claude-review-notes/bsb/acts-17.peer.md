# BSB Acts 17: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 34 of 34 verses (17:1-34), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
N1904, BSB's English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`. No KEEP blocks.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 34, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 13 changes in 11 verses (17:2, 17:5, 17:6, 17:11, 17:15, 17:17,
17:23, 17:25, 17:30, 17:31, 17:33). The other 23 verses are kept exactly as
the author left them. Seven of the changes undo publisher wording the author
had kept, where BSB adds an English word that reviewed Acts, Luke, John and
Mark chapters put in `t`.

## Changes

- 17:2 πρὸς αὐτούς: before πρὸς αὐτούς = "the synagogue" → after αὐτούς =
  "the synagogue" (the publisher's own row) and πρός `s` ("πρός ("to") is
  absorbed into went into"). "went into" already carries the direction, so
  πρός adds nothing to "the synagogue". Same pattern as 17:15 πρός and 17:26
  ἐπί in this chapter, and as 17:33 below.
- 17:5 "hoping": before ἐζήτουν = "in search of" + "hoping" → after ἐζήτουν
  = "in search of", "hoping" `t` ("English supplies hoping to carry the
  infinitive clause; ἐζήτουν is already rendered in search of"). BSB renders
  the clause twice. "in search of" renders what ζητέω means. "hoping" is a
  different verb (expectation, not seeking), so it is not the same word
  understood again. αὐτούς = "Paul and Silas" + "them" is kept (see doubts).
  Only its note now says which is which.
- 17:6 "other": before τινας = "some other" → after τινας = "some", "other"
  `t` ("English adds other to set the brothers apart from Jason"). Same as
  Acts 5:29, 9:39, 16:25, Luke 13:2, 17:17, Mark 9:14.
- 17:6 "now": before πάρεισιν = "have now come" → after πάρεισιν = "have" +
  "come", "now" `t` ("English adds now; the Greek has the plain present
  πάρεισιν"). Same as Acts 2:33, 9:14, 10:34, 13:32, John 9:15. "now" does
  not render the adverbial καί ("also") either, so καί stays `s`.
- 17:11 "to see": before εἰ = "to see if" → after εἰ = "if", "to see" `t`
  ("English supplies a verb of seeing to introduce the indirect question
  with if"). Same as Mark 3:2, 11:13, Luke 6:7, 14:28.
- 17:15 ὡς τάχιστα: before ὡς = "as", τάχιστα = "soon as possible" → after
  ὡς τάχιστα jointly = "as soon as possible" (rule 3). ὡς with a superlative
  is the idiom "as … as possible", so the second "as" belonged to ὡς as much
  as the first did.
- 17:17 "Gentiles": before τοῖς σεβομένοις = "God-fearing Gentiles" → after
  "God-fearing", "Gentiles" `t` ("BSB adds Gentiles to identify the
  God-fearers; the Greek says only the worshipers"). Same as Acts 13:16 and
  13:26.
- 17:23 "now": before καταγγέλλω = "now proclaim" → after "proclaim", "now"
  `t` ("English adds now to mark the contrast; the Greek has the plain
  present καταγγέλλω"). Same convention as 17:6.
- 17:25 "else": before πάντα = "everything else" → after πάντα =
  "everything", "else" `t` ("English adds else; the Greek says all
  things"). Same as Mark 9:38.
- 17:30 "Although": before μέν = "Although", ὑπεριδών = "overlooked" →
  after ὑπεριδών = "Although" + "overlooked" (participle note), μὲν οὖν
  jointly `s` ("not separately expressed; Although belongs to the participle
  ὑπεριδών"). The Although-clause is the participial clause. Acts 13:36 is
  the same construction (Δαυεὶδ μέν … ὑπηρετήσας): there μέν is `s` and
  "when" sits on the participle. Acts 3:13 and 13:28 also put "even
  though"/"though" on the participle. John 10:41 has μὲν … δέ = "Although",
  but there is no participle there.
- 17:30 "earlier": before χρόνους = "of earlier times" → after χρόνους =
  "of" + "times", "earlier" `t` ("English adds earlier, setting those times
  against now; the Greek says the times of ignorance"). χρόνους means
  "times". The idea of "earlier" comes from the contrast with τὰ νῦν.
- 17:31 "of this": before πίστιν = "proof of this" → after πίστιν =
  "proof", "of this" `t` ("English adds of this, referring to the coming
  judgment; the Greek has only πίστιν"). "this" is a supplied object (the
  "this" convention, as Acts 15:23).
- 17:33 ἐκ μέσου αὐτῶν: before ἐκ μέσου αὐτῶν jointly = "the Areopagus" →
  after μέσου αὐτῶν = "the Areopagus" (note: "their midst" is rendered the
  Areopagus, the meeting Paul had addressed, 17:22), ἐκ `s` ("ἐκ ("out of")
  is absorbed into left"). See the doubts below for why the link stays.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- **17:30 ἀπαγγέλλει vs "commands"**: the author's form is confirmed:
  ἀπαγγέλλει `s`, "commands" `t`, "He" `t`. The EVIDENCE line shows one
  N1904 word with no match. The publisher group that held "He", "now" and
  "commands" was dropped, so the differing word is in that group (the
  author's guess, probably παραγγέλλει, stays in the author note only). With
  an infinitive, ἀπαγγέλλω means "declare/announce that they should": BSB
  itself says "declared that they should repent" in Acts 26:20. That is not
  "command", so under rule 9 it is a different meaning and is not linked.
  "He" stays `t`. It is the subject of the English verb "commands", which has
  no N1904 word. God is named in the first clause, as Acts 13:36 does it
  ("David … he fell asleep"). τὰ νῦν = "now" is right.
- **17:30 "Although" on μέν or on ὑπεριδών**: moved to ὑπεριδών (above).
- **17:5 double links**: "hoping" is now `t` (above). αὐτούς = "Paul and
  Silas" + "them" is kept. In Greek αὐτούς is the object of προαγαγεῖν, and
  "bring them out" gives it that same role and meaning. BSB also moves
  αὐτούς into a new role as the people searched for and names them, and a
  Greek pronoun moved into a new role is linked. Both English words refer to
  the same two men, so neither repeat over-claims.
- **17:5 καί between the participles = "and"**: kept. It is the καί inside
  the participle chain προσλαβόμενοι καὶ ὀχλοποιήσαντες, and BSB's "and"
  closes that series (Matthew 26:26, Acts 13:16, 14:19). καί (4) = "So" is
  also right: a connective rendered with a different connective.
- **17:33**: the lead raised `s` for the Greek and `t` for "the Areopagus".
  I decided against that. In 17:22 BSB renders ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ Ἀρείου Πάγου as
  "in the meeting of the Areopagus", so "their midst" here is that same
  meeting. "the Areopagus" is the place Paul leaves, which is the role μέσου
  αὐτῶν has in Greek, and it names the same thing. Using `s` and `t` would
  make a split pair, and the publisher also linked μέσου to "the Areopagus".
  What was wrong was ἐκ: its "out of" is in "left" (ἐξῆλθεν), not in "the
  Areopagus", so it is now `s`.
- **17:2 πρὸς αὐτούς**: αὐτούς = "the synagogue" kept (the publisher's row).
  This is the same metonymy as 17:33: "to them", the Jews of 17:1, becomes
  the place where they met. πρός is now `s` (above). Using `s` and `t` here
  would also be a split pair.
- **17:4 / 17:12 οὐκ ὀλίγ- joined = "quite a few"**: confirmed. A litotes is
  one unit, as Acts 14:28 οὐκ ὀλίγον = "a long". οὐκ does not mean "quite".
- **17:27**: confirmed. Θεόν = "Him" (it is the object of seek in both
  languages). "God intended that" `t`: "God" is the subject of BSB's
  supplied frame and refers back to the subject of ἐποίησεν (17:26). That is
  a supplied name, not Θεόν in a new role. αὐτόν = "Him" + "Him" (object of
  both reach out for and find) and εἰ ἄρα γε = "perhaps" are right.
- **Frames and supplied words**: confirmed `t` for 17:3 "he declared" (ὅτι
  `s`, as Acts 9:20), 17:18 "They said this" (ὅτι = "because"), 17:7
  "named" (no ὄνομα, as Acts 10:5), "that" before an infinitive or
  participle clause (17:7, 17:16, 17:26, 17:29), the copulas 17:2 "was",
  17:19 "is", 17:22 "are", the place words 17:10 and 17:21 "there", the
  supplied objects 17:15 "him", 17:19 "him" ×2, 17:32 "him", and 17:18
  "Paul" (verb ending). The participle "and" lines (17:9, 17:11, 17:19,
  17:22, 17:24, 17:26, 17:34) have no καί, so `t` is right. τε in τε … καί
  is `s` (17:10, 17:14). The connectives that BSB renders with a different
  connective are linked (17:5 καί = So, 17:14 τε = but, 17:20 οὖν = and,
  17:21 ἤ = and). No "of the" or "to the" sits on a lone article.
- **Readings**: each is supported by its EVIDENCE line, and no note states
  the publisher's Greek wording. In 17:18 (Ἐπικουρίων) and 17:34
  (Ἀρεοπαγείτης), the partial record shows one word swapped for one, and
  the English "Epicurean"/"Areopagite" lost its publisher link, so the
  spelling notes are right. In 17:7 the same evidence holds, and "defying"
  lost its link, so the differing word is in ἀπέναντι … πράσσουσιν. The
  "different form" note is careful enough. In 17:19 "So" lost its link, so
  the differing word is the connective, and "So renders either" is right.
  In 17:22 the publisher has one extra word, but all 25 English words are
  matched, so rightly no reading is claimed.
- **17:28 quotations**: confirmed. Both quoted lines are linked word by word.
  Τοῦ = "His" (the article as a pronoun in the poet's line). γάρ and καί of
  that line are `s`.

## Still uncertain

- 17:5 "hoping": ζητέω with an infinitive is BSB's "were intent" (Luke
  19:47) and "got ready" (Acts 16:10), and those are linked. A reviewer who
  reads "hoping" as BSB's rendering of that use could link it again. I kept
  `t` because BSB has already used ἐζήτουν for "in search of".
- 17:33 and 17:2: if the project later decides that a place-for-people
  metonymy should be `s` and `t`, both verses should change together.
- Left folded as rendering choices, not additions: 17:8 "greatly"
  (ἐτάραξαν), 17:16 "deeply" (παρωξύνετο), 17:23 "this inscription"
  (ἐπεγέγραπτο), 17:26 "one man" (ἑνός), 17:7 "there is" (εἶναι), and 17:18
  "Some of them" on τινες (as Acts 7:24 and Mark 14:65, though Luke 24:18
  and Mark 3:14 use `t`).

## Coordinator adjustment (consistency pass, 2026-11-24)

- 17:23: the first-person "I" that BSB supplies for a participle clause
  is now `t`; a participle has no person, and the finite verb keeps its own
  "I". This aligns with Acts 22:20 and 23:27–30.
