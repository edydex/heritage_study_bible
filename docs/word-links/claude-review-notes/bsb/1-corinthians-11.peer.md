# BSB 1 Corinthians 11: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 34 of 34 verses (11:1-34). There are no KEEP blocks. Every `g`, `s`
and `t` line was checked against the Greek, BSB's English, the publisher rows
(drafts) and the publisher record for each verse in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/1-corinthians.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 34, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

Three changes in three verses (11:7, 11:21, 11:24). Every other verse in the
peer JSON is identical to the author's.

- 11:7: ὑπάρχων = "since he is" → "since + is"; "he" is now `t` ("A
  participle has no person; English supplies the subject"). The note now
  reads "rendered since … is". The author kept a third-person subject on the
  participle, citing Luke 18:36 and Ephesians 1:20. The Pauline files make
  that subject `t`, though, including earlier chapters of this book:
  1 Corinthians 7:22 ("he"), 7:29-30 ("they"), 9:9 ("it"), and Romans 2:14,
  4:19, 7:2-3. I found no reviewed 1 Corinthians or Romans verse that keeps
  "when/since he" on a participle. The book should follow one rule.
- 11:24: εὐχαριστήσας = "when He had given thanks" → "when + had given
  thanks"; "He#0" is now `t`, for the same reason. "He#1 broke" stays on
  the finite ἔκλασεν. The Gospel accounts give no counter-precedent. There
  BSB turns εὐχαριστήσας into the finite "gave thanks", which has no
  pronoun (Luke 22:19, Mark 14:23). "He took" on λαβών (Luke 22:19, Matthew
  26:27) renders a participle as a main verb, not as a subordinate "when"
  clause.
- 11:21: μέν = "While" with δέ `s` ("δέ answers μέν; BSB marks the contrast
  with While") → μέν … δέ joined = "While" // μέν … δέ ("on the one hand …
  on the other") marks the contrast, rendered While. The old `s` reason
  itself said that "While" renders the contrast δέ completes, so δέ was half
  of a split pair. 1 Corinthians 5:3 and John 10:41 join μέν … δέ =
  "Although" in the same way. ὃς … ὃς stay "one" / "another".

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points to settle

- 11:17: every English word has an honest owner, so the author's rows stay.
  In(παραγγέλλων) the following(Τοῦτο) instructions(παραγγέλλων) I
  have(ἐπαινῶ) no(οὐκ) praise to offer(ἐπαινῶ), because(ὅτι) your
  gatherings(συνέρχεσθε) do(`t`) more harm(εἰς τὸ ἧσσον) than(οὐκ … ἀλλά)
  good(εἰς τὸ κρεῖσσον). BSB recasts "not for the better but for the worse"
  as "more harm than good". "than" is the only English word that carries
  the not-this-but-that contrast, so it is a different connective for the
  same connective and is linked, not `s`/`t`. The comparative degree of
  ἧσσον is in "more". "your gatherings" turns the finite verb into a noun.
  Its "your" comes from the verb's person ending, so the link is honest.
  "do" is the light verb of BSB's new clause and has no Greek owner. The
  publisher had put it in "do more harm" on ἧσσον, which gave a verb to a
  prepositional phrase. `t` is right.
- 11:16: τοιαύτην `s` with "other" `t` is confirmed. "No such custom" and
  "no other practice" do not mean the same thing: "such" points back to the
  practice just disputed, while "other" excludes anything besides Paul's
  practice. That is an interpretive shift, not a shifted rendering. "Other"
  does not render τοιαύτην, so there is no split pair. "do" in "nor do the
  churches" is `t`: it is an English stand-in for the understood "have",
  not a repeat of the content word, as in 1 Corinthians 9:5 ("as do the
  other apostles", `t`).
- 11:22: confirmed against BSB's wording in the publisher record ("Don’t
  you have your own homes … Shall I praise you for this? No, I will not!").
  μή is `s` as a question marker and γάρ is `s`. "Don’t" is on οὐκ, since
  the contraction cannot be split; this matches 1 Corinthians 9:4-5 and
  6:16. "your own" is `t`. In the last clause οὐκ = "not" (the negative
  goes on "not"), and ἐπαινῶ = "I will": the verb stands in that clause, so
  person and modal go on it and "praise" is understood, as with ἔγνωσαν =
  "they had" in 1 Corinthians 2:8. "No" is an added answer word with a
  different role from "not", so it is `t`. That is not a split pair,
  because οὐκ is linked.
- 11:34: confirmed. εἰς κρίμα means "resulting in judgment", so "result in"
  on εἰς is honest. συνέρχησθε is a finite subjunctive in a ἵνα clause, not
  a participle, so "when#0" has no Greek word and is `t`. "it will" is the
  subject and auxiliary of BSB's new clause, and that clause has no Greek
  verb behind it, so `t`. "about" stays on λοιπά, following the
  preposition-on-the-noun convention, and τά = "the".
- 11:23-26 (words of institution): compared with the registered Luke
  22:19-20, Matthew 26:26-28 and Mark 14:22-24. The identical Greek has
  identical links:
  - τοῦτο ποιεῖτε εἰς τὴν ἐμὴν ἀνάμνησιν (11:24, 11:25) = this / do / in /
    τήν `s` / ἐμήν = "of Me" / remembrance, as in Luke 22:19.
  - Τοῦτό … ἐστιν τὸ σῶμα = This / is / τό `s` / body, as in Matthew
    26:26.
  - ὡσαύτως = "In the same way", τὸ ποτήριον = the / cup, μετά = after, τὸ
    δειπνῆσαι = "supper", and "He took" `t` (the verb carried over from
    11:23), as in Luke 22:20. The extra καί is `s`.
  - Τοῦτο τὸ ποτήριον ἡ καινὴ διαθήκη (τό `s`, ἡ = "the") and ἐν τῷ …
    αἵματι (τῷ `s`), as in Luke 22:20. 11:25 has its own ἐστίν, so "is"
    links to it.
  - "it" after broke (11:24) and after drink (11:25) is `t`, as in Luke
    22:19 and Matthew 26:26.

  The notes are grammatical only; none is interpretive.
- 11:10: confirmed. ἐξουσίαν = "authority" only. "a sign of" is BSB's
  interpretive addition and is `t`.
- 11:19: the publisher's γάρ = "indeed" and καί = "And" are kept. BSB often
  renders γάρ as "Indeed" in reviewed files (Luke 6:44, Mark 9:41, Romans
  2:14, 1 Corinthians 9:10). The reverse is defensible but not clearly
  better, so rule 10 keeps the publisher row.
- 11:5: τῇ ἐξυρημένῃ = "her head were shaved" is kept. It matches how the
  chapter treats the covering and shaving words, which carry the head or
  hair BSB names: κατακαλύπτεται "cover her head", κείρασθαι "have her hair
  cut", ἀκατακάλυπτον "with her head uncovered". ξυράω is the usual verb for
  shaving the head.
- Readings: the publisher record has no `partial` key for any of the 34
  verses, so the publisher's Greek equals N1904 throughout. No note claims a
  different reading, and no note mentions BSB's Greek text.

## Still uncertain

- 11:5: "head" inside τῇ ἐξυρημένῃ could instead be `t` as a supplied noun.
  I kept it for consistency with 11:6 and 11:13.
- 11:4: the publisher split κατά = "covered" / ἔχων = "with" is kept with
  its notes. Joining κατὰ … ἔχων = "with … covered" would also be honest.
- 11:20: ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό is `s`, with "come together" on the participle. That
  is BSB's standard rendering of συνέρχομαι (11:18, 11:33, 11:34). Acts
  4:26 instead gives "together" to ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό after συνήχθησαν.
- 11:26: τό = "this" before cup is kept as the article's demonstrative use,
  as the publisher had it. `s` + `t` would be the stricter alternative.
- 11:11: the note on χωρίς says "rendered independent of", but "of" is
  linked with the genitive noun. This is harmless and was left as is.
