# BSB Matthew 23: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 38 of 38 verses

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 38, "problems": 0}`.

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB
(23:1–13, 23:15–39; 23:14 is absent from N1904 and empty in this BSB text).
Textual claims were checked against the publisher's own Greek, as recorded
in the publisher-row statistics of `bsb-word-links/matthew.json` at `HEAD`.

## Changes (1 verse)

- 23:21: τῷ κατοικοῦντι = "the One who dwells" and αὐτόν = "in it" →
  τῷ κατοικοῦντι = "the One who dwells in" and αὐτόν = "it". Why:
  κατοικέω takes a direct object ("inhabit"), so the "in" of "dwells in"
  renders the verb, not the pronoun. αὐτόν alone is "it". This matches how
  the author linked "in" of "let in" to εἰσελθεῖν in 23:13. The note now
  says the participle is transitive ("inhabiting").

Nothing else was changed. The other publisher corrections in the author note
(23:3, 23:4, 23:7, 23:9, 23:13, 23:18, 23:23, 23:27, 23:30, 23:32, 23:37,
23:39) are right.

## Textual claims

- 23:4 "burdensome" `t`: confirmed. The publisher's Greek has 22 words
  against 20 in N1904. That is the two words of καὶ δυσβάστακτα. N1904's
  single καί joins the verbs ("and lay"), so linking it to "and" is right.
- 23:26 "and dish" `t`: confirmed. The publisher's Greek has 18 words
  against 15, which is καὶ τῆς παροψίδος.
- 23:38 "desolate" `t`: confirmed. The publisher's Greek has 7 words against
  6, which is ἔρημος.
- 23:7, 23:8, 23:23 also show one unmatched N1904 word. These are spelling
  differences only (Ῥαββεί/Ῥαββί, ἀφεῖναι/ἀφιέναι), not textual claims.
  23:17 (ἁγιάσας) and 23:25 (ἀκρασίας) match the publisher's Greek exactly,
  so BSB's present "makes" in 23:17 is a translation choice, not a variant.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 23:7: **agree.** "the title of ‘Rabbi’ by which they are addressed": "by
  which" is the means ("the title by which one is called"). It is not the
  agent ὑπό "by men", which BSB drops. So: καλεῖσθαι = they are addressed,
  Ῥαββεί = Rabbi, ὑπὸ τῶν ἀνθρώπων `s`, "the title of" and "by which" `t`.
  The publisher rows (καλεῖσθαι = the title, ὑπό = by, ἀνθρώπων = which they
  are addressed) were mechanical and wrong. This is not a split pair, because
  the English "by" does not render ὑπό.
- Vocative "You": **kept on the vocative noun.** I checked all reviewed
  Matthew chapters. "You"/"O you" stays with the vocative in 3:7 (You brood),
  6:30, 7:5 (You hypocrite), 8:26, 14:31, 16:8, 18:32 (You wicked) and 22:18
  (You hypocrites). It is `t` only in 5:22, 12:34 and 15:7. 25:26 (author)
  also keeps it, "as in Matthew 23". Moving it to `t` would make 23 disagree
  with the identical phrase in 22:18 and 7:5, and with "O Jerusalem" in
  23:37. The English "You" works as the vocative marker, like "O". If the
  project wants one rule, 5:22, 12:34 and 15:7 are the ones out of line.
- 23:16, 23:18 "by his oath" `t`: **agree.** ὀφείλει means "he is obligated";
  no Greek word means oath. Linking "oath" to ὀφείλει would teach a wrong
  gloss. This matches "of the sin" (23:32) and "shedding" (23:30).
- 23:4 τῷ δακτύλῳ αὐτῶν: **agree with the current split.** δακτύλῳ = a
  finger, τῷ and αὐτῶν `s`, "to lift" `t`. "lift" is the English idiom's
  verb, not a rendering of the dative. The current links let "finger" show
  δάκτυλος.
- 23:34 ἀπό … εἰς = in + after, with each πόλις = town: **agree.** Neither
  preposition maps to one English word, so the correlative pair is the
  honest unit. Keeping each "town" on its own πόλις is more useful than one
  four-word group.
- 23:31 "the sons", 23:35 "all the righteous blood": **agree.** Where Greek
  has no article, the English "the" stays with its noun or adjective. This is
  the chapter's general practice ("a son" 23:15, "a hen" 23:37).

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- The project-wide rule for the vocative "You" (see above).
- 23:28: καί "also" is `s`, and οὕτως alone = "In the same way". οὕτως καί
  as one group would also be defensible.
- 23:33: ἀπό is `s`, with "escape" on φύγητε. φύγητε ἀπό could also be one
  group.
- 23:35: τοῦ (of τοῦ δικαίου) = "of". This follows the chapter's habit of
  linking a genitive article to "of". The alternative is "of" with Ἅβελ, which
  is indeclinable, plus τοῦ `s`.
