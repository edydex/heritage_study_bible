## Changes (1 link, 3 notes)

- 3:1: `s 1` (δέ) + `t One afternoon` → `g 1 = One afternoon // BSB renders
  the δέ that opens this new episode as One afternoon, a setting drawn from
  the ninth hour.` This restores the publisher row. Reviewed chapters link
  BSB's episode openers to the connective that opens the episode, also when
  the English carries a time word the Greek lacks: Luke 11:14 Καί = "One
  day", Luke 1:11 δέ = "Just then", Luke 15:25 / 23:10 δέ = "Meanwhile".
  No reviewed chapter has a scene opener as `t` next to an unlinked opening
  connective. Here δέ `s` + "One afternoon" `t` is that split pair. The
  ninth hour stays linked on its own (ἐνάτην = ninth, ὥραν = hour + hour),
  so no Greek word is claimed twice.
- 3:7 (note only): the note on χειρός = by + hand named κρατέω, but the
  verb here is πιάσας (πιάζω). Now: "The genitive χειρός names the part
  grasped; English adds by."
- 3:9 (note only): added a note to the kept καί = When: "BSB turns this
  καί-clause into a When-clause leading into 3:10."
- 3:22 (reason only): `t For` said "the Greek has none", but μέν stands
  there. Now: "English adds a causal connective linking Moses' words to
  3:21; the Greek has only μέν, which does not mean for."

## Answers to the author's doubts and the open points

- 3:15 δέ `s` + "but" `t`: **agree, kept**. BSB reads "You killed the
  Author of life, but God raised Him from the dead". The δέ of τὸν δὲ
  Ἀρχηγόν contrasts the Author of life (killed) with the murderer (3:14,
  released). BSB's "but" contrasts the killing with God's raising, which
  the Greek gives only as the relative clause ὃν ὁ Θεὸς ἤγειρεν. It is a
  different contrast, so it is not a split pair. The publisher's "but God"
  on Θεός over-claimed.
- 3:13 ὅν = Him + Him: **agree, kept**. ὅν is the grammatical object of
  both coordinated verbs, παρεδώκατε and ἠρνήσασθε. Reviewed precedent:
  1 Peter 1:8 and 1 Timothy 6:16 ὅν = Him + Him. The third "Him" (release
  Him) is rightly `t`: ἀπολύειν stands in a genitive absolute, and ὅν is
  not its object.
- 3:1 hour + hour on ὥραν, ἐνάτην = ninth: **agree, kept**. BSB reads "at
  the hour of prayer, the ninth hour". This matches Matthew 20:5 (ὥραν =
  hour + hour, "One ὥραν serves both ordinals"). The only change in 3:1
  is δέ (above).
- 3:9 opening καί = When: **agree, kept** (publisher row). The connective
  that actually joins the clauses is the δέ of 3:10, which may not take a
  cross-verse link. With καί `s` and "When" `t`, the verse would have a
  split pair, because "When" is the usual shifted rendering of a narrative
  καί (Mark 11:15, Mark 8:22). 3:10 δέ stays `s`, and its reason already
  says BSB joins the clause with When.
- 3:11 στοᾷ = walkway + Colonnade: **agree, kept**. In τῇ στοᾷ τῇ
  καλουμένῃ Σολομῶντος the name is only "Solomon's". "Colonnade" is στοά
  understood again in the name. John 10:23 also has στοᾷ = Colonnade with
  Σολομῶνος = Solomon's, and the publisher itself had Colonnade on στοᾷ.
  Putting Colonnade on Σολομῶντος would claim a meaning the genitive name
  does not have.
- 3:18 God / He: **agree**. Θεός = God, προκατήγγειλεν = He foretold,
  ἐπλήρωσεν = has fulfilled, "saying that" `t`.
- 3:16 cleft: **agree**. "It is … that" is `t`, and "comes" is `t` (the
  article ἡ = that). τὸ ὄνομα αὐτοῦ = Jesus' name, the subject of
  ἐστερέωσεν, which BSB moves into the cleft.
- 3:22 "For" off μέν: **agree** (reason refined above). μέν anticipates the
  δέ of 3:24. It is not causal, so `s` + `t` is not a split pair.
- 3:5 ὁ = the man, 3:11 αὐτοῦ = the man, 3:12 αὐτόν = this man: **agree**.
  These match reviewed ὁ δέ rows (John 9:15, Mark 1:45, Matthew 22:12).
- 3:4 Peter + Peter on Πέτρος: **agree**. The publisher's "said Peter" on
  εἶπεν over-claimed. 3:17 ἐπράξατε = you acted + did and 3:20 ὅπως ἄν =
  that + that also agree with the understood-again rule (Colossians 4:8,
  Ephesians 6:22).
