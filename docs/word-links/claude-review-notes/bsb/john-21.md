# BSB John 21: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: all 25 (21:1-25). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`. A `totext --all` round trip
of the output gives the same decision lines.

There is no Synoptic parallel. The conventions come from the reviewed John files:

- Μετὰ ταῦτα is joined as one unit: Later here, as Afterward in 5:14.
- The case word goes on the noun, never on a lone article: to + disciples (11:7, 6:12).
- A supplied copula is `t` (4:54, 14:10, 15:5).
- Article + participle units are joined (15:5, 16:5).
- οὐ + ἴσχυον "unable" is joined (Luke/Mark precedent).
- With a temporal participle, "When/As soon as" goes on the participle and the connective is `s`.
- "son" with a bare genitive is `t` (Luke 6:15-16), and Ἰωάνου = of John with the spelling note (1:42).
- πάλιν beside δεύτερον is `s` (4:54).
- ἐπὶ τὸ στῆθος → against, with τὸ στῆθος `s` (13:25).
- τί πρὸς σέ is "what" + `t is that` (Matthew 27:4).

## Publisher rows corrected

**Names and objects folded into Greek words (now `t`)**

- 21:15, 21:16, 21:17 (twice each in 16 and 17), 21:19: "Jesus replied/asked/told/had asked", "Jesus said" and "Peter would glorify" are now the verb alone. The `t` reason is that English supplies the name.
- 21:1 "He made Himself known": `t Himself#1`.
- 21:6 "you will find some": `t some`. "they cast it there": `t it there`. "to haul it in": αὐτό = it, ἑλκύσαι = to haul + in.
- 21:7 "he had removed it": `t it`.
- 21:13 "gave it": `t it`.
- 21:18 "lead you": `t you#7`. "do not want to go": `t to go`.
- 21:20 "following them": `t them`.
- 21:8 "came ashore": ἦλθον = came, `t ashore`. "full of" sat on the article τῶν; it is now `t full`, with ἰχθύων = of fish and τῶν `s`.
- 21:3 "So they went out": `t So`. The verse has no οὖν, and its EVIDENCE line says the text equals N1904.
- 21:11 "It was full": μεστόν = full, `t It was`.
- 21:14 "This was" and "the third time that": τοῦτο = This and τρίτον = the third time, with `t was` and `t that` (as 4:54).
- 21:20 "He was the one" was on καί. It is now `t`, and καί ("also") is `s`.
- 21:23 "but only": ἀλλ’ = but, `t only`.
- 21:25 "I suppose that": `t that`.

**Connectives that carried the participle's "When / As soon as / after"**

- 21:7 οὖν "As soon as": ἀκούσας = As soon as + heard, οὖν `s`.
- 21:19 καί "And after": καί = And, εἰπών = after He had said.
- 21:21 οὖν "When": ἰδών = When + saw.
  - This also fixes a swap. Πέτρος had "he" and ἰδών had "Peter saw". Now Πέτρος = Peter and λέγει = he asked.

**Negatives split off verbs**

- 21:4 οὐ = not, with did + recognize.
- 21:11 not, with was + torn.
- 21:23 three times: not, with would + die / did + say / he would + die.
- 21:18 οὐ was unlinked. It is now not, with you do + want.
- 21:6 οὐκέτι ἴσχυον is joined as "they were unable", following precedent, because English fuses the negative into "unable".

**Articles that carried prepositions, nouns or "a"**

- 21:1 "to the" → the, with to + disciples.
- 21:6 "of the" → the, with of + boat. τῶν "of" is now `s`.
- 21:8 "in the" → the, with in + boat.
- 21:12 "of the" → the, with of + disciples.
- 21:25 "for the" → the. "for" moved to would have space for.
- 21:2 οἱ "the sons" → the, with `t sons` (as Matthew 4:21).
- 21:17 τό "a" → `s`. "a third time" goes with τρίτον, as the publisher already did in the second occurrence.

**Wrong owners and joins**

- 21:7 ἐκεῖνος had "the". The article ὁ now has "the", and ἐκεῖνος is `s`. ἦν … γυμνός is joined as "he had removed". ἔβαλεν ἑαυτόν is joined as "jumped".
- 21:4 πρωΐας ἤδη γινομένης is joined as "Early in the morning". Before, only πρωΐας had it, and ἤδη and γινομένης were unlinked.
- 21:9 ἀπέβησαν εἰς τὴν γῆν = they landed (Mark 6:53).
- 21:11 εἰς τὴν γῆν = ashore (Luke 5:11).
- 21:11 ἑκατὸν πεντήκοντα τριῶν is joined as "153".
- 21:8 πηχῶν διακοσίων is joined as "a hundred yards", with the second ἀπό `s`.
- 21:10 νῦν = just. It was folded into "you have just caught".
- 21:11 καί "but even" → but. "even with" now goes on the concessive genitive absolute ὄντων.
- 21:19 ποίῳ = the kind of + by which, and θανάτῳ = death. Before, θανάτῳ had "the kind of death".
- 21:20 "and saw" → saw, with `t and`.
- 21:20 ἐστιν = is, and ὁ παραδιδούς = going to betray. Before, it was "is going to" + betray, with the article unlinked.
- 21:20 στῆθος "against Jesus" → ἐπί = against and αὐτοῦ = Jesus, with τὸ στῆθος `s`.
- 21:24 ὁ μαρτυρῶν and ὁ γράψας are joined. "them down" → ταῦτα = them, and "down" goes with has written.
- 21:25 καθ’ "all of them" → καθ’ ἕν = all of. ἅτινα = them.
- 21:2 ὁ λεγόμενος is joined as "called".

## Notable decisions

- **21:15-17:** ἀγαπάω (ἀγαπᾷς twice) and φιλέω (φιλῶ three times, φιλεῖς twice) are each linked word by word to the "love" that BSB prints. There is no note about the two verbs.
- **Variant notes**, following the EVIDENCE lines:
  - Ἰωάνου (21:15-17) has the same-name spelling note (probably Ἰωάννου).
  - προβάτιά → sheep (21:16, 21:17) has "another form of this word, same meaning" (probably πρόβατα).
  - 21:4 has a "differs slightly, same sense" note on the joined genitive absolute (probably γενομένης).
  - 21:11 "So" is `t` "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here" (probably οὖν).
  - The extra publisher words in 21:1, 5, 12, 13 and 14, and the third extra word in 21:17, touch no English word: probably an article with Ἰησοῦς, or a connective BSB does not render. Nothing was claimed for them.
- **Vocatives:** Παιδία = Children and Κύριε = Lord. No "You"/"O" stands before them in this chapter. Σίμων Ἰωάνου: Simon + `t son` + of John.
- **21:5:** Ἰησοῦς = He, with a note (BSB has just named Jesus; compare 1:42 "who").
- **21:8:** ἀλλά = "only" is kept as a shifted rendering ("not far … but about"), so it is not a split pair. In 21:23 "but" already renders ἀλλά, so "only" there is `t`.
- **21:13:** "He did the same with" is kept on ὁμοίως ("likewise") as the publisher had it, now with a note.
- **21:23:** the publisher's οὖν = "Because of" and οὗτος = "this" were kept. οὗτος ὁ λόγος can be read as "this saying of Jesus", which BSB recasts as "Because of this, the rumor".

## Doubts for the peer

- **21:1 `t Himself#1` and 21:18 `t you#7`:** the pronoun object is understood again from the parallel clause (ἑαυτόν, σε). I treated them as English-supplied objects. Linking them as repeats (the 15:2/15:4 "understood again" pattern) would also be defensible.
- **21:23:** should οὗτος be `s`, with οὖν = "Because of this"? See above.
- **21:8 `t ashore`:** "came ashore" for ἦλθον could be read as one idiomatic rendering.
- **21:11:** "of large" stays on μεγάλων, as the publisher had it. "of" could equally go with ἰχθύων.
