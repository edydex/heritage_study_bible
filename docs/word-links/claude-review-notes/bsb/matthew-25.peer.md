Every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line was read against N1904 and the displayed

## Changes (3 verses)

- 25:3: τάς = "their" → τάς `s` (article), `t their` (reading difference).
  The publisher's Greek has 12 words against N1904's 11, and "their" is linked
  there to the extra word: BSB's text reads τὰς λαμπάδας αὐτῶν. "their"
  renders that αὐτῶν, not the article, so linking τάς invented a link
  (rule 9). This is not a split pair: in BSB's own text the article is
  untranslated, as in 25:1 and 25:7.
- 25:22: the `t who had received` reason said N1904 lacks λαβών and the
  Byzantine text adds it, which reads as a reading difference. BSB's text has
  no λαβών either: the publisher linked "who had received the" to τά, and its
  one extra Greek word carries no English (the bracketed δέ of the modern
  editions). Reason now: English supplies the participle from 25:16 and 25:20;
  the Greek has only ὁ τὰ δύο τάλαντα. The ὁ = The servant note was adjusted
  the same way. Links unchanged.
- 25:41: the `t His` reason ("N1904 has no αὐτοῦ after εὐωνύμων") also implied
  a reading difference. The publisher's text has none there either. Its one
  extra word is οἱ before κατηραμένοι, which carries "you who". Reason now:
  English supplies the possessive; the Greek has only ἐξ εὐωνύμων. The
  κατηραμένοι = you who are cursed note now records that BSB's text has οἱ.
  The link stays, because an anarthrous participle used in address is still
  rendered "you who are cursed".

## Answers to the author's doubts and the coordinator's points

- 25:9 "No": kept as `t`, with Μή ποτε = or. N1904 prints μή ποτε as two
  words throughout (it is WH spelling of μήποτε, "lest"), and the reviewed
  chapters always join it (4:6, 7:6, 13:15, 13:29, 15:32 = "or"). ποτε alone
  never means "or", and the "lest" sense comes from μή, so Μή = No with
  ποτε = or would split one conjunction. "No" is the refusal BSB supplies for
  the elliptical construction. The Greek has no Οὔ here; compare 13:29, where
  Οὔ is printed and linked. The publisher also had "or" on its single word
  Μήποτε. Its οὐ = No was wrong, because οὐ μή is the emphatic "not".
- 25:15/25:16 Εὐθέως: correct. N1904 ends 25:15 with "ἀπεδήμησεν. Εὐθέως".
  The publisher's Greek has 19 words in 25:15 and 14 in 25:16, so BSB's text
  begins 25:16 with εὐθέως, where BSB renders "at once". The `m 19 > 25:16`
  line and `g @25:15 19 = at once` are right.
- 25:15 τάλαντα = talents + talents + talent: kept. The noun is understood
  with δύο and ἕν in the same verse, and the same pattern is used elsewhere
  (25:2 were + were, 25:33 His + His, Matthew 8 our + our). In 25:17 and
  25:18 the noun is `t` because it is understood from another verse.
- 25:24 δὲ καί = Finally: kept. "Finally" stands where BSB has "also" in
  25:22 (καί). It marks the last servant in the series, so the δέ transition
  and the additive καί together are a fair unit. δέ = Finally with καί `s`
  would also be defensible; this is not an error. The publisher's καί = "and"
  was wrong, because that "and" joins the participle προσελθών.
- 25:3 τάς = their: changed (see above). The extra word is αὐτῶν (the modern
  text), not the Byzantine ἑαυτῶν, but either way N1904 lacks it.
- 25:22 "who had received": not a reading difference for BSB (see above).
- "The servant who had received" joins (25:16, 18, 20, 24): correct. Each
  joins the article with its own participle (λαβών, εἰληφώς), and "servant"
  is BSB's word for the substantival article, as rule 3 keeps "one".
- 25:44 τότε = And: kept. The only other candidate, καί, is rendered "too",
  so `s` + `t And` would split a pair.
- 25:30 ἐκεῖ = where there: kept. It is the publisher's row, and it matches
  the parallel 8:12, 13:42, 13:50 and 22:13 (only 24:51 splits it).
- 25:17 ὁ = the one + `t with`, against 25:22 ὁ = The servant +
  `t who had received`: consistent. In each verse the article stands for the
  person, and the English adds the link that the elliptical Greek leaves
  unstated.
- 25:26 "You wicked": kept linked with Πονηρέ (vocative "You" is linked with
  the vocative word).
- 25:6 `t him`: confirmed as a reading difference. The publisher's Greek has
  one extra word, which carries "him" (αὐτοῦ).

## Still uncertain

- 25:24 δὲ καί = Finally against δέ = Finally with καί `s`: both are
  defensible.
- 25:41 κατηραμένοι = "you who are cursed": "you who" renders BSB's οἱ, which
  N1904 lacks. It is kept on the participle because the vocative participle
  alone carries that sense, but `t you who` would be the strict alternative.
- The identity of the publisher's extra Greek words (αὐτῶν in 25:3, δέ in
  25:22, οἱ in 25:41, αὐτοῦ in 25:6) is inferred from word counts and from
  which English words have no N1904 owner. The publisher's Greek table itself
  was not available offline.
