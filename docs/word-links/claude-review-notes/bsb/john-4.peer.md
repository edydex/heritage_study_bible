# BSB John 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 54 of 54 verses (4:1-54), every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line.
Final check: `{"versesOk": 54, "problems": 0}`.

Reading claims were checked against the publisher records in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/john.json`. Only 4:1, 4:4,
4:5, 4:7, 4:9, 4:11, 4:17, 4:24, 4:29, 4:31, 4:34, 4:39, 4:40, 4:51 and

those verses, and each fits its counts.

## Changes (6 links in 5 verses)

- 4:5: τῷ → "to" becomes υἱῷ → "to + son", and τῷ is `s`. In N1904 the
  article goes with υἱῷ, not with the name. The old note said it marked
  Joseph, which is not true of this text. In John 2:5 and 2:8 "to" also goes
  on the dative noun.
- 4:10: Δός → "for" becomes ὁ λέγων … Δός → "asking + for". On its own,
  "for" does not mean "give". Together, "asking … for" does render "the one
  saying, Give". μοι stays `s`.
- 4:28: λέγει → "said to" becomes λέγει → "said" and ἀνθρώποις → "to +
  people". A verb should not carry the dative's "to". This matches John 2:5
  (λέγει … τοῖς διακόνοις → said / the / to + servants).
- 4:39: τῶν → "of the" becomes τῶν → "the" and Σαμαρειτῶν → "of +
  Samaritans". The spelling note is kept.
- 4:42: τῇ → "to the" becomes τῇ → "the" and γυναικί → "to + woman".
- 4:42: τοῦ → "of the" becomes τοῦ → "the" and κόσμου → "of + world".

The last three changes follow the rule that a lone article does not carry a
preposition. The rest of the chapter, and John 1-3 (for example 1:29 "of +
world"), already do this.

## Points settled

- 4:6 "Since" on οὖν: **keep.** "There was Jacob's well; so (οὖν) Jesus sat
  by it" states a real inference. BSB marks the same link on the other clause
  ("Since … was there, Jesus … sat"). "Since" is an honest rendering of οὖν,
  just as "When" goes on a καί between clauses. δέ `s` is right.
- 4:35/36 ἤδη → "Already": **keep the cross-verse link.** N1904 4:35 has 28
  tokens and ends with ἤδη (token 27). Neither 4:35 nor 4:36 has a `partial`
  record, so BSB's Greek is the same word for word: ἤδη is in Greek 4:35 too.
  The 4:36 Greek (18 tokens) starts with ὁ θερίζων, while BSB's English 4:36
  starts with "Already". The publisher had ἤδη unlinked and gave "Already the"
  to ὁ. This is a real verse-division difference, so `m 27 > 4:36` and
  `g @4:35 27 = Already` are right.
- 4:50: **keep the author's version.** ἐπίστευσεν → "took + at" (the
  idiom for "believed"), τῷ → "His", λόγῳ → "word", and ὃν εἶπεν αὐτῷ `s`. The
  publisher's λόγῳ → "at His word" would put "at" and "His" on a noun that
  means neither. Ἰησοῦς → "Jesus" as the object of the idiom is fine.
- 4:2: **keep.** καίτοιγε → although, the cleft "it was … who" is `t`, and
  the intensive αὐτός is `s`. This matches John 2:24 (αὐτός `s`) and earlier
  files that treat a cleft "it was" as `t`.
- 4:1: **keep.** "Jesus" is `t` and ὁ Κύριος is `s`, because a name does
  not render the title "Lord". Jude 1:5 is handled the same way. The record
  shows 2 N1904 words unmatched and 2 publisher words not in N1904. The draft
  left Κύριος, Ἰωάνης, "Jesus" and "John" unowned, which fits.
- 4:11 reading: **keep.** The record has all 18 N1904 words matched and 2
  extra publisher words. The draft left "the woman" unowned. So `t` "the woman"
  with a reading reason is right.
- 4:24 reading: **keep.** The record has 1 extra publisher word and none
  unmatched. The publisher linked τούς → "His" and left "Him" unowned, so its
  extra word is the one rendered "Him". "His worshipers" stays on τοὺς
  προσκυνοῦντας (article used as a possessive), and "Him" is `t` with the
  reading reason.
- 4:51 reading: **keep.** The record has 1 extra publisher word, and "his"
  (in "his servants") was unowned in the draft. So `t` "his" with a reading
  reason is right, and οἱ is `s`.
- 4:14 οὐ μή … εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα → "never": **keep.** Matthew 21:19 joins Οὐ
  μηκέτι … εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα → "never + again" in the same way. διψήσει → "will +
  thirst".

## Answers to the other author doubts

- 4:39: **keep** λόγον and μαρτυρούσης joined → "testimony". Linking λόγον
  alone would show "word" for "testimony" and leave out the participle that
  "testi-" comes from.
- 4:27: **keep** "from her" `t`. μετ’ αὐτῆς ("with her") goes with λαλεῖς.
  "From" is not μετά, so αὐτῆς should not carry it.
- 4:53: **keep** ἐκείνῃ → "very" and "this was" `t`. In BSB's sentence
  "this" is the subject and "the very hour" renders ἐκείνῃ τῇ ὥρᾳ.
- 4:52: **keep** ὥραν → "at + hour" and ἑβδόμην → "the seventh". This fits
  4:6 ("the sixth").

## Still uncertain

- 4:29 and 4:39: "ever" stays inside "I ever did" (ἐποίησα), as the
  publisher had it. Earlier files sometimes fold "ever" into a verb, as in
  "has ever seen". In 4:29 the record shows BSB's Greek differs at ἅ, but
  BSB's English has "ever" in 4:39 too, where ἅ matches. So "ever" does not
  show which reading BSB follows.
- 4:10: "asking + for" joins a content verb (Δός) into a group. The other
  option is Δός `s` and "for" `t`.
- 4:17: εἶπες is linked with the note "same word, spelled differently". The
  record shows this one N1904 word unmatched. The exact form in BSB's Greek is
  not in the repository.
