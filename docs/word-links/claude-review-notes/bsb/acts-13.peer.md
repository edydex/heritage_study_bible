# BSB Acts 13: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 52 of 52 verses (13:1-52), every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line,
against the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the
publisher record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 52, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 4, in 3 verses (13:13, 13:14, 13:34). The other 49 verses are kept
exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 13:13 δέ (Ἰωάνης δέ): before δέ `s` and "where" `t` → after δέ = "where"
  (the publisher's own row, restored), note "BSB joins John's leaving to
  Perga with where in place of δέ, as Acts 10:24". Here "where" stands in
  the connective's slot, so it is a connective rendered by a different
  connective, not an added word. Reviewed Acts 6:13 (τε = where) and 10:24
  (δέ = where, kept by that chapter's peer review) link it. Only Acts 7:15
  does the opposite, so the publisher row was not wrong (rule 10).
- 13:14 καί (before ἐλθόντες): before καί `s` and "where" `t` → after καί =
  "where" (the publisher's row, restored), for the same reason and with the
  same precedents. The "and" before "sat down" stays `t`: it joins the
  participle ἐλθόντες to ἐκάθισαν, and there is no καί at that point.
- 13:14 ἐλθόντες εἰς = "they entered": added "BSB's Greek has a different
  form of this verb here, with the same meaning" (rule 9). EVIDENCE shows 1
  unmatched and 1 extra word. The publisher record keeps Πισιδίαν =>
  "Pisidian" as a matched pair, so the author's guess (a different form of
  Πισιδίαν) cannot be right. The only English the publisher's mapped rows
  leave without an owner is "they entered", so its Greek word is the one not
  in N1904. The note names no publisher spelling.
- 13:34 μέλλοντα ὑποστρέφειν εἰς: before one group = "to see" (note: in the
  wording of the psalm) → after `s` 7 8 9 ("BSB uses the psalm's wording see
  decay (verse 35) …") and `t` "to see" ("BSB borrows see from the psalm
  quoted in verse 35; the Greek says 'about to return to' decay"). "See"
  means neither "be about to" nor "return to". Linking three Greek words to
  an English word that means none of them breaks the rule against linking
  Greek content words to an English word that does not mean them. Because
  "see" does not render those words, the `s` + `t` pair is not a split pair.
  I found no reviewed precedent that links a borrowed word like this. The
  publisher's row (μέλλοντα alone = "to see") was also wrong. μηκέτι = "never"
  and διαφθοράν = "decay" stay linked.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 13:34: changed, as above.
- 13:42 αὐτοῖς `s` / "them" `t`: confirmed. λαληθῆναι αὐτοῖς means "be spoken
  to them", the hearers who make the request. BSB's "them" is the object of
  "urged", Paul and Barnabas. The referents differ, so this is not a split pair.
- 13:14 διελθόντες … παρεγένοντο = "traveled inland": kept. BSB merges the
  two verbs into one verb phrase. "Traveled … to" carries παρεγένοντο εἰς
  ("arrived at"), so leaving the main verb `s` would be less accurate.
- 13:17 τὸν λαόν = "them + people", ὕψωσεν = "made + into a great": kept.
  "Them" is the object of "made" and so stands for τὸν λαόν. "People" is the
  same word repeated. Neither word over-claims.
- 13:26 ἐν ὑμῖν = "you": kept. BSB moves the pronoun into the address. A
  Greek pronoun in a new role is linked. The author's note says that "among"
  is dropped.
- 13:15 "please" `t` and 13:16 εἶπεν = "began to speak": kept.

## Other points checked (kept)

- 13:38-39: this is a real verse-division difference. The publisher record
  for 13:38 has 23 N1904 words, 13 matched and 13 publisher words, so the
  publisher's 13:38 ends at καταγγέλλεται. Its 13:39 has 16 words for
  N1904's 6. Tokens 14-22 `m` > 13:39 and linked there with `g @13:38` use
  the same mechanics as Acts 5:39-40 and Luke 24:45-46. The UKRK review also
  moves 13:38's end into 13:39. The καί (13) is among the 10 moved words, but
  BSB's 13:39 has no "and", so `s` in 13:38 loses nothing. "Through" = ἐν:
  the one unmatched 13:39 word is ἐν, because the publisher's 13:39 has two
  ἐν. No reading is claimed.
- 13:3 and 13:16 καί = the series "and": these follow the participle-"and"
  convention. A καί inside a participle chain that BSB's "and" closes is
  linked to it (Matthew 26:27, Acts 14:19). Acts 8:30 has no καί in that slot,
  because its καί joins the finite verbs ἤκουσεν/εἶπεν, so its "and" is `t`.
  Every other participle "and" in the chapter (13:8, 13:10, 13:11, 13:14,
  13:29, 13:43, 13:51) has no καί there and is `t`.
- 13:44 Θεοῦ `s`, "of" and "Lord" `t`, τοῦ = "the": EVIDENCE shows 1
  unmatched and 1 extra word. The publisher's "of the Lord" row is dropped,
  and "Lord" cannot render Θεοῦ, so Θεοῦ is the unmatched word. The notes
  give only N1904's wording. "The article is common to both readings" follows
  from the count (only one word differs) and names no spelling.
- 13:20 "the" `t` and 13:33 "their" `t`: EVIDENCE shows 0 unmatched and 1
  extra word, and in each verse that English word was the only one left
  without an owner. The reading claims are supported. 13:33 ἡμῶν = "us" stays
  linked, because no N1904 word is unmatched.
- Spelling notes (13:4 Seleucia; 13:5, 13:13, 13:24, 13:25 John; 13:6
  Bar-Jesus; 13:16 of Israel; 13:21 Kish and Benjamin, 2 unmatched/2 extra;
  13:28 Pilate): in each verse the counts match, and the draft left exactly
  that name and its English without an owner. "Spelled differently" also
  covers a different case form such as the one in 13:6.
- Old Testament quotations: 13:22 (1 Sam 13:14/Ps 89:20), 13:33 (Ps 2:7,
  following Hebrews 1:5 and 5:5), 13:34 (Isa 55:3), 13:35 (Ps 16:10, following
  Acts 2:27), 13:41 (Hab 1:5) and 13:47 (Isa 49:6) are linked word by word
  and correctly.
- Lone articles: every single-article row that carries more than "the"
  matches a convention. Possessives: 13:3, 13:16, 13:17, 13:25, 13:31, 13:32,
  13:51. Pronoun or relative: 13:9 who, 13:51 they. Demonstrative: 13:49
  that. Case word with an indeclinable name: 13:22 τοῦ Ἰεσσαί, 13:23 τῷ
  Ἰσραήλ. None carries a preposition with a declinable noun, and none
  carries is/are/was/were.
- τε: in 13:1 both τε belong to τε … καί and are `s`. In 13:4, 13:46 and
  13:52 τε is the clause connective and is linked ("and", "Then", "And").
- 13:37 ἤγειρεν = "raised from the dead" follows reviewed Acts 2:24.

## Still uncertain

- 13:8 "that is what" and 13:47 "this is what" keep "is" inside the οὕτως
  group, as Matthew 18:35 and 1 Peter 3:5 do. Luke 24:46 instead links
  οὕτως = "This + what" with "is" `t`. Precedent is split, so I kept the
  author's rows.
- 13:13/13:14 "where": precedent is split (Acts 7:15 against 6:13 and
  10:24). I restored the publisher rows because the majority and the most
  recent reviewed Acts precedent link it.
- 13:34 "blessings" stays on πιστά (the publisher's row). The head
  substantive is τὰ ὅσια, but "blessings" supplies the noun for both neuter
  plurals, so I did not count the row as wrong.
- 13:42 λαληθῆναι = "to continue" is a loose rendering. It still names the
  same act of speaking the message, unlike the borrowed "see" in 13:34, so
  I kept it.
- 13:22 "son" `t` (τόν `s`): Matthew 10:2-3 put "son" on the article.
  Matthew 4:21 and Acts 1:13 use `t`, and the convention bars a noun on a
  lone article, so I kept `t`.
- The 13:14 reading note rests on the evidence counts and the unowned
  English. The publisher's Greek table is not in the repo.
