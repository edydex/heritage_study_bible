# BSB 2 Corinthians 3: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 18 of 18 verses (3:1-18). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording, and
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft. No KEEP
blocks in this chapter. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 18, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 5 verses (3:5, 3:9, 3:15, 3:17, 3:18). Two are link changes
(3:5, 3:15); three only reword a note (3:9, 3:17, 3:18). The other 13
verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 3:5: ὡς `s` and "that#1" `t` → ὡς = "that#1", with a note. λογίσασθαί
  τι ὡς ἐξ ἑαυτῶν is "to reckon anything as from ourselves". BSB recasts
  the "as" complement as a that-clause ("to claim that anything comes from
  us"). That is what Acts 17:22 does: ὡς δεισιδαιμονεστέρους ὑμᾶς θεωρῶ,
  "I see that … you are very religious", with ὡς = "that" and the supplied
  "are" `t`. "that" does ὡς's job here, so `s` + `t` split a pair.
  "comes#0" stays `t`.
- 3:15: ἀλλ’ = "And even" → ἀλλ’ = "And"; "even" `t`. ἀλλά is a connective
  rendered by another connective ("And"). The emphatic "even" has no Greek
  word: there is no καί, and ἀλλά on its own means "but". The reviewed
  chapters make such an "even" `t`: Jude 1:9 (δέ = "But", "even" `t`),
  1 Peter 4:15 and Acts 28:21 ("or even", only ἤ). Only ἀλλὰ καί joins as
  "Even" (Luke 16:21).
- 3:9 (note only): ἡ = "the#0" keeps its link. The note "BSB follows a
  reading with this article in another case" → "BSB's Greek text has a
  different form of this article". EVIDENCE supports a difference here: 1
  N1904 word is unmatched, and the publisher's "the#0" is unlinked. It
  does not show which form the publisher's word has, so the review file
  should not name the case.
- 3:17 (note only): "there is" stays `t`. The reason now says that
  English adds "there is" to answer "where", and that the Greek has no
  ἐκεῖ and no verb. The old reason, "existence verb", did not cover the
  place word "there".
- 3:18 (note only): τὴν αὐτήν stays `s`. Its reason "BSB renders 'the same
  image' as 'His image'" sounded like a link. It now reads: τὴν αὐτήν ("the
  same") has no English counterpart; BSB says "His image" instead of "the
  same image".

## Points settled (author doubts and caller questions)

- 3:18 τὴν αὐτήν `s` + "His" `t`: confirmed. This is a word of different
  meaning, not a split pair. Here αὐτήν agrees with εἰκόνα in the
  attributive position ("the same image"). As a feminine accusative it
  cannot be the possessive "his" (that would be αὐτοῦ). "His" is BSB's
  reading of whose image is meant. The phrase can also mean "all into one
  and the same image". The publisher's αὐτήν = "His" over-claimed.
  ἀπὸ δόξης εἰς δόξαν = "with#1 intensifying glory" as one group:
  confirmed. Romans 1:17 joins ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν = "by faith from
  start to finish" the same way.
- 3:10, the five tokens ἐν τούτῳ τῷ μέρει εἵνεκεν = "in comparison to":
  kept. BSB reads "has no glory now in comparison to the glory that
  surpasses it". The publisher's own rows put "in" on ἐν and "comparison
  to" on μέρει, so BSB's "in comparison" draws on ἐν … μέρει ("in this
  respect"). εἵνεκεν is the preposition that governs τῆς … δόξης, as "to"
  governs "the glory". No split gives each English word a true match:
  "to" alone does not mean "because of", and `s` for ἐν τούτῳ τῷ μέρει
  would contradict the publisher's alignment. "once", "now" and "it" stay
  `t`.
- 3:5 ὡς: linked (see Changes).
- 3:3 "is" on φανερούμενοι ("is clear"): kept. The participle is the
  clause's verb, and "is clear" renders its passive meaning ("being made
  manifest"). It is not a copula supplied to a verbless clause. The
  reviewed chapters keep "is" with a participle that BSB makes a
  predicate ("is able", "is distressed", "is ready"), as Romans 5:1 keeps
  "have been justified" on Δικαιωθέντες. Only the person, "It", is
  supplied, and it stays `t`.
- 3:9 περισσεύει … δόξῃ = "glorious#1 + is": kept. "abounds in glory" →
  "is … glorious" is one predicate, so "is" alone would claim that
  περισσεύει means "be". Joining a verb with the word that completes it
  matches Matthew 5:20 (περισσεύσῃ πλεῖον = "exceeds") and 1 Thessalonians
  4:1.
- 3:12 χρώμεθα παρρησίᾳ = "we#1 are + bold": kept for the same reason
  ("we use boldness" → "we are bold"). πολλῇ = "very"; "we#0" on the
  participle Ἔχοντες stays `t` (Romans 5:1).
- 3:13 πρὸς τό = "to", μή = "keep + from": confirmed. This follows
  1 Corinthians 10:6 (εἰς τό = "to", μή = "keep + from"). "We are" `t`,
  with καί `s`.
- 3:15 κεῖται ἐπί = "covers": kept. "lies on" → the transitive "covers",
  and ἐπί has no other English word. "even": now `t` (see Changes).
- 3:8 μᾶλλον = "even more": kept, unlike 3:15. μᾶλλον itself means "all
  the more", and John 19:8 and Acts 22:2 link μᾶλλον = "even more".
- 3:17: N1904 has οὗ … ἐλευθερία with no ἐκεῖ and no verb, and EVIDENCE
  shows the publisher's Greek is the same. So "is#1" and "there is" are
  both `t`. οὗ = "where" stays linked.
- Readings: only 3:6 and 3:9 differ by EVIDENCE (1 unmatched word each).
  The 3:6 note ("Same verb, spelled differently in BSB's Greek text") is
  suitably soft. The 3:9 note is now softened too. Neither review-file
  note names the publisher's word.

## Still uncertain

- 3:10: a reviewer could still prefer εἵνεκεν = "in comparison to" with
  ἐν τούτῳ τῷ μέρει `s`. I kept the joined group because the publisher's
  rows tie "in comparison" to ἐν … μέρει.
- 3:11 ἐν δόξῃ = "the glory of": "of" could instead go on τὸ μένον ("of
  that which endures"). I kept the author's choice, because the relation
  that "of" expresses comes from ἐν, and τὸ μένον is nominative.
- 3:18 εἰκόνα = "into + image" (the preposition for the accusative of
  result is on the noun, not on μεταμορφούμεθα): kept, in line with
  putting case prepositions on the noun.
