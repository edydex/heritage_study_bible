## Answers to the author's doubts

- 10:24 τό = "good#0" and τό = "the good": kept. The rule is: an article
  with no noun of its own (used as a pronoun) may carry the English noun
  BSB supplies for it; an article that belongs to a noun in the Greek never
  carries an extra noun or genitive. 1 Corinthians 2:11 (τά = "the
  thoughts", τοῦ `s`, ἀνθρώπου = "of man") follows it, and so do Romans 8:5
  (τά = "the things"), 1 Corinthians 2:14 and 7:32-34 (τά = "the affairs",
  "the work"). 1 Corinthians 4:5 does not conflict with it: there τῶν is
  the article of καρδιῶν, so "men's" has no Greek owner and is `t`. 10:24
  has no noun anywhere in the verse, so τό carries "good". When the Greek
  does state the noun and BSB repeats it, the repeat goes to the noun, not
  the article: 10:33 σύμφορον = "good#0 + good#1", τό = "the"; 10:29
  συνείδησιν = "conscience#0", both τήν `s`. The three verses fit one rule.
- 10:1-3 "they/They" on the finite verbs, πάντες = "all": confirmed. Person
  goes on the finite verb (Mark 14:23). The three verses now agree. 10:4
  πάντες `s` is right because English does not repeat "all"; a link to
  10:3 would cross a verse boundary that is not a versification difference.
- 10:6 εἰς τό = "to", μή = "keep + from", εἶναι ἐπιθυμητάς = "craving":
  confirmed. "keep … from" is how BSB says the negative purpose clause
  ("so that we would not be cravers"). It renders μή, not an added action.
  The peer for John 18:28 accepted μή = "avoid" on the same grounds (also
  2 Timothy 2:14 μή = "to avoid"). Romans 13:5 is different: there "to
  avoid" was put on διά, which means "because of", not a negative.
  Joining εἶναι to ἐπιθυμητάς is more honest than εἶναι `s`: "craving"
  renders "to be cravers" as a whole. ἡμῶν `s` is right ("us" belongs to
  ἡμᾶς).
- 10:11 πρός = "as#1", νουθεσίαν = "warnings", ἡμῶν = "for us":
  confirmed. "as" gives πρός's sense "with a view to". "for" in "warnings
  for us" renders the objective genitive ἡμῶν. The publisher's row put
  "for" on πρός and "as" on the noun, which over-claimed.
- 10:20 ἀλλά = "No + but", ὅτι `s`: confirmed. Reviewed chapters link a
  "No" that rejects what came before to ἀλλά (1 Corinthians 2:7, Acts 2:16,
  John 12:27, Romans 2:29, 8:37). ὅτι means "that", not "but", so the
  publisher's ὅτι = "but" was wrong. ἃ θύουσιν joined = "the sacrifices"
  is right: ἅ is a relative pronoun, not an article. The second θύουσιν =
  "are offered" (voice change) is fine.
- 10:33 "For" on ζητῶν with "am" (ζητῶν = "For + am + seeking") and "I#2"
  `t`: confirmed. This matches the registered 1 Corinthians 9:19 and 9:21
  (ὤν = "Though + am", "I" `t`). "For" is BSB's reading of how the
  participle relates to the main verb, as "Though" is there. The swap of
  πάντα = "in all" and πᾶσιν = "everyone" is correct: πᾶσιν is the dative
  of the people pleased, and πάντα is the accusative "in all things".
  "I#1 do" is `t`.
- 10:17 οἱ πολλοί = "who + many", "are#0" `t`, ἐσμεν = "we#0 + are#1":
  confirmed (the note was reworded, see above). A lone article cannot carry
  "who are", and a supplied copula is `t`.

## Other points checked

- 10:29: συνείδησιν = "conscience#0" (the actual noun); "should" goes on
  the finite κρίνεται ("should + be determined"), not on μου. Both
  confirmed.
- 10:28: one διά governs both ἐκεῖνον τὸν μηνύσαντα and τὴν συνείδησιν, so
  linking it to both "for the sake of" phrases is right: same role and
  same meaning. "then", "it" and "you#1" are `t` (added adverb, supplied
  objects). Moving "food" to ἱερόθυτον is defensible, since the
  substantive means "sacrificial meat". Τοῦτο = "This".
- 10:22: "to#1 jealousy" belongs to παραζηλοῦμεν ("provoke to jealousy"),
  not to Κύριον. ἤ `s` and question μή `s` follow the question-marker rule.
  "Are#1 we" goes on ἐσμεν.
- 10:12 "he is standing firm" on the infinitive under δοκῶν, and 10:13 τοῦ
  δύνασθαι = "so that you can", both confirmed. An infinitive with an
  understood subject follows 1 Corinthians 3:18, 8:2 and Galatians 6:3, and
  articular τοῦ + infinitive = "so that they may …" in Acts 26:18 and
  Hebrews 11:5. The participle "no person" rule does not apply.
- 10:1 ὅτι = "that#0 + that#1": kept. This is not an added "that": ὅτι
  governs both clauses, and BSB repeats it in the same role. This matches
  the registered 1 Corinthians 3:16.
- 10:4 ἡ = "that#1" (an anaphoric article: "that rock", meaning the one
  just named): kept. The demonstrative renders the article, so it is not
  supplied.
- Brief rules: supplied copulas (10:7 "were", 10:13 "what#0 is" and
  "is#1", 10:17 "there is" and "are#0", 10:26 "is") are `t`. Added objects
  (10:4 "them", 10:13 "it", 10:28 "it" and "you#1", 10:27 "to#0 a meal",
  10:30 "in the meal") and added adverbs (10:21 "too" twice, 10:28 "then")
  are `t`. Modals and person are on finite verbs (10:8-9 "We should",
  10:12 and 10:24 "should", 10:29 "should", 10:33 "may"). No "of the" or
  "to the" sits on a lone article or number (10:8, 10:11, 10:16, 10:18 and
  10:31 were all fixed by the author). Different connectives are linked
  (10:32 καί = "whether", "or"; 10:5 ἀλλά = "Nevertheless"; 10:11 δέ =
  "Now", "and"; 10:31 οὖν = "So").
- Old Testament quotations, checked word by word: 10:7 (Exodus 32:6). ὁ =
  "The", λαός = "people", Ἐκάθισεν = "sat down", φαγεῖν = "to#0 eat", πεῖν
  = "to#1 drink", the two καί = "and", ἀνέστησαν = "got up", παίζειν =
  "to#2 indulge in revelry". 10:26 (Psalm 24:1): τοῦ = "the#0", Κυρίου =
  "Lord's", ἡ γῆ = "The earth", τὸ πλήρωμα = "the#1 fullness", αὐτῆς =
  "thereof", "is" `t`. Both are correct.

## Still uncertain (kept, not changed for taste)

- 10:18 κατὰ σάρκα = "people" (publisher row, with a note). "the people of
  Israel" is BSB's way of saying "Israel according to the flesh". `s` +
  `t` would also be defensible.
- 10:25 and 10:27 διά = "of" ("questions of conscience"), and 10:13 σύν =
  "when" ("when you are tempted"). Both are publisher rows kept with notes.
  The English word is a weaker function word with the same relation, not a
  different meaning, so I did not change them to `s` + `t`.
- 10:13 "bear" `t` (complement of δύνασθε left understood, as in 1
  Corinthians 3:2). The later ὑπενεγκεῖν is rendered differently ("stand up
  under") and stands in another clause, so the "understood again" rule does
  not clearly apply.
