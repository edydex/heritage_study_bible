## Changes (1 verse)

- 6:12: `g 13 = him#0` + `t him#1 = English supplies the object of
  brought.` → `g 13 = him#0 + him#1 // The one object αὐτόν serves both
  verbs; BSB repeats him.` In συνήρπασαν αὐτὸν καὶ ἤγαγον the one stated
  object αὐτόν is understood again with ἤγαγον, and BSB repeats it ("seized
  him and brought him"). The reviewed precedent is the same: Luke 20:12
  τοῦτον = "him#0 + him#1" ("wounded him and threw him out"), Luke 20:15
  αὐτόν = "him#0 + him#1" ("threw him out … and killed him"), John 19:1
  τὸν Ἰησοῦν = "Jesus + Him". As a `t`, the second "him" looked like an
  English addition, but it renders the Greek object a second time.
  "Stephen" after "confronted" stays `t`: ἐφίστημι is intransitive (with a
  dative when it has a complement: Acts 4:1, 17:5), so the accusative αὐτόν
  is not its object. This differs from Luke 20:10, where the transitive
  δείραντες shares αὐτόν ("the servant + him").

No other row changed. I checked each of the author's corrections to the
publisher rows and agree with all of them.

## Points checked

- 6:4 ἡμεῖς `s`: **agree, kept**. "ourselves" is the reflexive object that
  English "devote" needs. It is not the emphatic pronoun. BSB writes it
  where the Greek has no pronoun: Colossians 4:2 Τῇ προσευχῇ προσκαρτερεῖτε
  = "Devote yourselves" (on the verb) and Acts 2:42 "They devoted
  themselves". If ἡμεῖς were rendered, it would give "we ourselves" or "but
  we". BSB runs 6:3-4 as one sentence ("We will appoint … and will devote
  ourselves"), so the contrastive ἡμεῖς is absorbed into the We of 6:3. The
  verse divisions are the same, so no cross-verse link. "will devote
  ourselves" stays on προσκαρτερήσομεν.
- 6:9 καί = "including": **agree, kept as a link with its note**. In BSB's
  reading the Cyrenians and Alexandrians belong to the Freedmen synagogue.
  The καί between Λιβερτίνων and Κυρηναίων adds those groups to the
  synagogue's members, and "including" says the same thing as a relation of
  membership. This is the same meaning, shifted, like the connective turned
  into a relative in 6:6 and 6:13. It is not a different meaning like "and"
  for ὅταν, so `s` + `t` would make a split pair.
- 6:6 καί = "who" and 6:13 τε = "where": **agree**. Luke 15:15 (καί = "who",
  "a citizen …, who sent him") has the same structure as 6:6. Luke 8:5 and
  Mark 14:16 (καί = "where") match 6:13, where BSB attaches the τε clause
  to "the Sanhedrin" of 6:12.
- 6:11 εἰς and 6:13 κατά = "against#0 + against#1": **agree**. One
  preposition governs both nouns. The reviewed precedent is Luke 1:79 (ἐν =
  "in#0 + in#1") and John 20:17 (πρός = "to#2 + to#3"). The publisher's
  "against God" on Θεόν and "against the" on τοῦ over-claimed.
- 6:5 Πνεύματος = "of the + Spirit", Ἁγίου = "Holy": **agree**. Same as Luke
  4:1. The noun has no article, so "the" goes with the noun.
- 6:14 ὁ Ναζωραῖος = "of Nazareth": **agree**. Same as John 18:5, 18:7 and
  Luke 18:37. Joining the two tokens removes the lone article carrying
  "of".
- 6:2 οὐκ ἀρεστόν = "unacceptable": **agree**. It is one English word for
  the negative and the adjective, so the tokens cannot be split.
- Readings, checked against the `partial` counts:
  - 6:5: 1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904. πλήρη was
    the token the publisher could not link, so the same-meaning note on
    "full" is right.
  - 6:9: 1 unmatched and 1 not in N1904. συνζητοῦντες was the only content
    word the publisher left unlinked, so the spelling note is right.
  - 6:13: 0 unmatched and 1 extra publisher word (21 against 20). "this"
    `t` with "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here" is right.
  - 6:3: 0 and 0 (18/18/18). No reading is claimed there, which is
    correct; δέ = "Therefore" is a translation choice.
  - None of the notes states the publisher's Greek wording.
- Brief rules: the supplied copulas in 6:3 "to be", 6:8 "who was" and 6:15
  "was" are `t`. No "of the"/"to the"/"in the" sits on a lone article (6:4,
  6:7, 6:9, 6:13 and 6:14 were all fixed). The only other repeated English
  words are prepositions or nouns that render their own Greek words (6:4
  "to … to", 6:5 "of … of", 6:7 "of … of").

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 6:9 "including": see above, **kept**.
- 6:9 "They began to argue" on συνζητοῦντες: **agree, kept**. No Greek word
  is left for "began". It is BSB's ingressive rendering of the participle
  once it becomes a new sentence, so it belongs to the verb's phrase. The
  6:1 "began" is different because there it comes from ἐγένετο.
- 6:2 "in order to wait on" on διακονεῖν: **agree, kept**. BSB recasts the
  subject infinitive as purpose. "in order to" is how the infinitive is
  rendered, not a separate Greek word left out. Reviewed precedent folds a
  purpose recast into the verb in the same way (1 Thessalonians 2:4 ὡς …
  ἀρέσκοντες = "in order to please"). As a `t`, "in order" would wrongly
  suggest an English addition with no Greek basis.
- 6:1 ἐγένετο = "began", γογγυσμός = "to grumble": **agree, kept split**.
  Both are content words, each with its own English: the inceptive "arose"
  of ἐγένετο, and the grumbling of the noun. Joining them would hide which
  Greek word gives the grumbling.

## Still uncertain

- 6:12 "Stephen" (`t`) after "confronted": it could be argued that the
  referent of αὐτόν is understood with ἐπιστάντες, as in Luke 20:10. I kept
  `t` because ἐφίστημι takes no accusative object.
- 6:5 ὁ = "This" (an anaphoric article on the publisher row) was kept. It
  is a determiner, not a copula, preposition or noun.
