# BSB 2 Corinthians 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 18 of 18 verses (4:1-18). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording, and
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft and the
publisher record in `bsb-word-links/2-corinthians.json`. No KEEP blocks in
this chapter. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 18, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 2 verses (4:1, 4:6). 4:6 is a link change; 4:1 only rewords a
note. The other 16 verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 4:6: ἔλαμψεν = "made + shine#1" → ἔλαμψεν = "shine#1"; "made" `t`.
  λάμπω is intransitive in this verse ("light will shine", "who shone")
  and everywhere else in the NT. God is the one who shines, and the verb
  has no object. BSB makes God the causer ("made His light shine"), and
  that causative is an English addition, like "His light". The reviewed
  chapters link "made" only when the Greek verb is itself causative
  (1 Corinthians 3:6 ηὔξανεν = "made + grow", Acts 5:27 ἔστησαν =
  "made + stand"). They make a "make" that English adds `t` (John 15:2,
  2 Corinthians 2:3, James 3:3). The note now says that BSB recasts "He
  shone" as "made His light shine".
- 4:1 (note only): ἔχοντες keeps "since + has given". Its note said "is
  recast as since God has given", but "God" is not in this group (it is
  `t`). It now reads: the causal participle ἔχοντες ("since we have") is
  recast from the giver's side as since … has given.

## Points settled (author doubts and caller questions)

- 4:1: kept as the author has it. "since" introduces the clause whose verb
  is "has given", so it belongs to the causal participle, as in 3:12
  (Ἔχοντες = "since + have"). "has given" is the same fact as "having this
  ministry", told from the giver's side. It is not a word of different
  meaning. The reviewed chapters link such converse recasts with a note
  (1 Peter 1:3 ἀναγεννήσας = "has given + new birth", 1 Corinthians 2:12,
  3:10). The publisher also linked "has given" to ἔχοντες. If "has given"
  were `t`, ἔχοντες would show only "since". "us" stays `t` (a participle
  has no person). καθὼς ἠλεήθημεν = "in + mercy" as one unit is an honest
  claim: "as we received mercy" is rendered "in His mercy", and John 1:23
  joins καθὼς εἶπεν = "in the words of" the same way. Neither Greek word
  carries "God" or "His". The alternative (καθώς = "since") would take
  "since" away from the clause it introduces.
- 4:6: "Let" on λάμψει: confirmed. The future works as a command, and
  jussive "let" goes on the verb. "Jesus" `t` with the reading reason:
  confirmed. EVIDENCE shows 27 publisher Greek words against 26 N1904
  words, all matched. The draft left both "of" and "Jesus" without an
  owner, so they sat on the extra publisher word. Moving "of#4" onto
  Χριστοῦ (the N1904 genitive) is right. "His", "light#1" and "give us"
  stay `t`. πρός = "to" is kept, since "to" carries the purpose.
- 4:17: confirmed. EVIDENCE shows 16 publisher Greek words against 15
  N1904 words, all matched. The draft left "our" without an owner, so
  "our" `t` with the reading reason is supported. The τό and τῆς articles
  correctly do not carry it. Joining καθ’ ὑπερβολὴν εἰς ὑπερβολήν =
  "far beyond comparison" matches 3:18 (ἀπὸ δόξης εἰς δόξαν) and Romans
  1:17. The publisher's rows split the phrase so that "far" and "that is"
  sat on καθ’ alone, and εἰς ὑπερβολήν had no link. "that is" `t` (a
  supplied relative clause with a copula), "an" with βάρος and "and" `t`
  are right.
- 4:16: confirmed. εἰ καί = "Though"; the first ἀλλ’ `s`; the second
  ἀλλ’ = "yet". N1904 has ἄνθρωπος only once (ὁ ἔσω ἡμῶν has no noun), so
  "self#1" is the noun understood again: ἄνθρωπος = "self#0 + self#1". ὁ
  (12) `s` is right, because an article may not carry a noun. Keeping
  ἡμέρᾳ καὶ ἡμέρᾳ as three rows (καί = "by") is acceptable.
- 4:12: confirmed. ζωή = "life" only. ἐνεργεῖται = "is#0 at work + is#1
  at work": the verb is understood again in the verbless second clause.
  "is" here belongs to the verb's rendering, not to a supplied copula.
- 4:14: confirmed. ἡμᾶς = "us#0 + us#1" (one object serves both verbs).
  "in His presence" `t` (it names a possessor the Greek does not). "will"
  goes on ἐγερεῖ.
- 4:8-10: consistent. "We" `t` in 4:8 and 4:10. The passive "are hard
  pressed" stays on θλιβόμενοι, as in 1 Corinthians 4:12-13 ("When +
  are#0 vilified", "we#0" `t`). 4:9 has no supplied words. "may" goes on
  φανερωθῇ, and καί = "also".
- 4:13: confirmed word by word. Ἐπίστευσα = "I#0 believed", διό =
  "therefore#0", ἐλάλησα = "I#1 have spoken" (person on finite verbs).
  καί (13) = "also" with ἡμεῖς = "we". The second καί (17) keeps the
  publisher's "and". This is acceptable but not certain: in the Greek that
  καί means "also" (διὸ καί), and BSB turns it into a coordinator.
- 4:2 "ways" inside τὰ κρυπτά = "secret + ways": kept. A substantive
  adjective carries its English head noun, as in 1 Corinthians 1:28 (τὰ
  ἐξουθενημένα = "despised things") and Romans 1:20 (ἀόρατα = "invisible
  qualities").
- 4:7 "to show" `t` with ἵνα = "that": kept. No Greek verb means "show".
  Philippians 4:10 makes "to show it" `t`. ἡ = "this#1" follows reviewed
  practice for an article rendered "this".
- 4:18 "So" `t`: kept. The genitive absolute gives the condition for 4:17.
  BSB's "So" makes it a result instead, which is a different relation.
  "fix our eyes" as one rendering of σκοπούντων matches Hebrews 12:2
  ("Let us fix our eyes" on ἀφορῶντες).
- 4:15 διά = "to#0": kept with its note. Here "extending through more and
  more people" and "to more and more people" describe the same spread. No
  reading is claimed: EVIDENCE has 0 unmatched and 0 extra Greek words.

## Still uncertain

- 4:13 καί (17) = "and" (see above). `s` for καί plus "and" `t` would be
  the stricter option.
- 4:15 διά = "to#0". `s` plus "to#0" `t` would be the stricter option if
  "to" is judged too far from "through".
