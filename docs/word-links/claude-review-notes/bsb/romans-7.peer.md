# BSB Romans 7: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 25 of 25 verses (7:1-25), every `g`/`s`/`t`/`m` line, starting from
the author's final file. No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`.

6 changes in 4 verses (7:3, 7:8, 7:13, 7:18). All other author rows are kept.

## Changes

- 7:3: τοῦ … εἶναι "and + is" → "is"; "and" → `t` ("English joins the result
  clause with and; the Greek has an articular infinitive and no καί"). The
  τοῦ of a result infinitive does not mean "and"; an English "and" before an
  articular infinitive is `t` in 1 Thessalonians 4:6 and Hebrews 10:2, and the
  same holds for an "and" that only joins a participle or infinitive clause
  (Acts 8:30, 14:22).
- 7:8: πᾶσαν "every kind" → "every kind of"; ἐπιθυμίαν "of covetous desire" →
  "covetous desire". "of" belongs to BSB's quantifier, not the accusative noun
  (as πάσῃ = "every kind of" in reviewed Romans 1:29).
- 7:13: ἁμαρτία (ἡ ἁμαρτία) "sin" → "sin + it"; `t it` removed. ἡ ἁμαρτία is
  the explicit subject of κατεργαζομένη, so the `t` reason "a participle has no
  person" was not true here. BSB names the noun in its purpose clause and
  resumes it with "it" in the noun's own clause: the pattern of reviewed
  Romans 4:16 (ἐπαγγελίαν = "promise + it").
- 7:18 (three lines):
  - τὸ κατεργάζεσθαι "to do + carry … out" → "carry … out"; "to do" → `t`
    ("English supplies the verb that θέλειν leaves implied"). In BSB's wording
    "to do" is the complement of "desire" (θέλειν), the same English supplement
    the chapter marks `t` after "want" in 7:15, 7:16 and 7:19. κατεργάζεσθαι's
    own role (what is not present to me) is "carry … out", so "to do" is not a
    second rendering with the same role.
  - καλόν "good" → "good + it"; `t it` removed. τὸ καλόν is the object of
    κατεργάζεσθαι, exactly the slot "it" fills in "carry it out"; BSB moved the
    noun into its first clause. "English supplies the object" was not true.
  - μοι "I" → "I + I"; `t I#2` removed. The author's own reason said μοι is
    understood in the second clause; an explicit pronoun understood again may
    take the repeated "I" (Acts 22:3), as the chapter already does for με (7:11),
    αὐτό (7:17, 7:20) and ἐγώ (7:25).

  In the publisher rows σύνφημι (7:16) and ἐνοικοῦσα (7:17) are the unlinked

- Lone articles: none carries a case word, copula or noun.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 7:18: "to do" is θέλειν's complement in BSB's wording → `t` (changed). "I" in
  "I cannot" → μοι understood again (changed).
- 7:3: "and" `t` is the better choice (changed), per the precedents above.
- 7:6: "to" `t` kept. Putting it on ἀποθανόντες or on ἐν ᾧ would make a Greek
  word seem to mean "to"; it belongs to the antecedent the Greek omits.
- 7:13: link ἡ ἁμαρτία to "it" as well (changed).
- 7:11: με = "me + me" kept (explicit pronoun, object of both verbs). Same for
  κατεργάζομαι / αὐτό understood again in 7:17 and 7:20.
- 7:15, 7:16, 7:19: "to do" `t` kept. θέλω takes the relative ὅ as its object;
  the Greek does not repeat the main verb there.
- 7:20: ἐγώ kept with ποιῶ. "ὃ οὐ θέλω | ἐγὼ τοῦτο ποιῶ" is a natural parse
  and sets up "οὐκέτι ἐγώ" in the next clause; reading it with θέλω is also
  possible, but the publisher's row is not wrong.
- 7:10: εἰς = "meant to bring" / "brought" with "was" and "actually" `t`: kept.
- 7:23: μου = "me", μέλεσιν `s` kept. Joining would put the content word
  μέλεσιν ("members") on an English word that does not mean it.

## Still uncertain

- 7:3 and 7:25 ἄρα = "then", οὖν = "So" (publisher rows) are the reverse of
  reviewed Romans 5:18 (ἄρα = "So", οὖν = "then"). Both particles mean
  "so/then", so neither is wrong; left as is.
- 7:18 and 7:13 rest on reading BSB's "it" as the resumed Greek noun. A
  reviewer who prefers one link per noun could keep "it" `t`, but then the
  reason must say BSB resumes the noun, not that English supplies the
  word.
