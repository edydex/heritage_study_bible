# BSB Romans 4: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: all 25 (4:1-25). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Final
check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`.

106 of the 344 publisher `g` rows changed, in 24 of the 25 verses (4:22
unchanged apart from its resolved καί). EVIDENCE says the publisher's Greek
differs in 4:1, 4:5, 4:11 and 4:19, but with no unmatched N1904 word; the
extra publisher word in 4:11 (probably καί before αὐτοῖς) and in 4:19
(probably ἤδη) has no English word in BSB, so no different reading is
claimed and there are no cross-verse links.

No Gospel parallel. The quotation in 4:3 and 4:22 follows Galatians 3:6 and
James 2:23 (ἐλογίσθη = "it was credited", αὐτῷ = "to him", εἰς = "as");
Romans 1 for εἰς τό + infinitive joined as one purpose/result marker (1:11),
"that comes" supplied (`t`, 1:17), "That is why" for a connective (1:15), and
`s` wording for articles; Matthew 5 / Luke 6 for the beatitude copula
(`t`); Acts for "that" before an infinitive (`t`); the brief's rules on
lone articles, participles, negatives, supplied copulas and "there".

## Publisher rows corrected

Lone articles carrying a preposition or verb (article keeps "the" or `s`,
the preposition goes to the noun or verb):

- 4:3 ἡ "does the" → "the", "does + say" on λέγει; 4:6 τόν "of the" → "the",
  "speaks + of" on λέγει (English verb takes "of", like Romans 1:16
  "ashamed of"); 4:6 τοῦ "of the" → "the", "of + man"; 4:11 τῆς "of the" →
  "the", "of + righteousness"; 4:11 τῆς "by" → `s`, "by faith" on πίστεως;
  4:12 τοῖς "in the" → "the", "in + footsteps"; 4:12 τῆς "of the" → "the",
  "of + faith"; 4:17 τούς "to the" → "the", "to" with "who gives life to".
- Related moves: 4:19 and 4:20 τῇ "in his" → "his" (article as possessive),
  "in + faith" on πίστει; 4:19 "of" from ἑαυτοῦ to σῶμα ("of + body": the
  "of" joins decrepitness to body, ἑαυτοῦ is only "his"); 4:19 "of" from
  Σάρρας to μήτρας ("of + womb", Σάρρας = "Sarah's"); 4:13 τῷ Ἀβραάμ: "to"
  moved to the article (indeclinable name).

Article + participle units joined (rule 3): 4:4 τῷ ἐργαζομένῳ "of the
worker" (the article had "of" alone); 4:5 τῷ μὴ ἐργαζομένῳ "to the one who
does + work" (μή = "not"), τὸν δικαιοῦντα "Him who justifies"; 4:11 τῶν
πιστευόντων "who believe"; 4:12 τοῖς στοιχοῦσιν "who + walk"; 4:17 τοῦ
ζωοποιοῦντος "the + who gives life to"; τὰ … ὄντα "what + does + exist";
4:18 τὸ εἰρημένον "had been told"; 4:24 τοῖς πιστεύουσιν "for us who
believe", τὸν ἐγείραντα "Him who raised". Articular infinitive 4:13 τὸ …
εἶναι "that + would be"; εἰς τό joined in 4:11 ("So then", "in order

## Notable decisions

- Theological terms are linked word by word with no interpretive notes:
  λογίζομαι "credit(ed)/count", δικαιοσύνη "righteousness", πίστις
  "faith", ἐπαγγελία "promise", νόμος "law", χάρις "grace" (4:4 "a gift"
  with a short gloss note), περιτομή / ἀκροβυστία "circumcision /
  uncircumcision" (rendered "the circumcised", "uncircumcised", "are not
  circumcised", "before/after" with brief gloss notes).
- 4:6 kept the publisher split καθάπερ = "likewise", καί = "And".
- 4:19 kept "since he was" on ὑπάρχων: the brief's participle rule is about
  first/second person, and "he" is the participle's own subject (Abraham).
- 4:3 τῷ before Θεῷ `s` (Galatians 3:6); James 2:23 joined it with Θεῷ.

## Doubts for the peer

- 4:9 τῷ Ἀβραάμ linked to both "Abraham's" and "to him": the dative
  recipient is "to him", the name is "Abraham's". Alternative: "Abraham's"
  only, with "to him" `t`.
- 4:16 "Abraham's offspring": the article τῷ could be read as a possessive
  and linked (majority convention for "his/their"), but a name is more than
  the article says, so I left it `s` + `t`. Also ἐπαγγελίαν = "promise +
  it" with "comes" `t`; the stricter option is ἐπαγγελίαν = "it" only.
- 4:10 περιτομῇ ὄντι joined as "his circumcision"; alternatively ὄντι =
  "his" alone. 4:14 οἱ = "those who", ἐκ = "by", "live" `t`; Galatians 3:7
  joined οἱ ἐκ as "those who have".
- 4:8 "against him" `t`: οὗ could be argued to carry it too, but it is the
  same relative already rendered "whose", in a different role.
