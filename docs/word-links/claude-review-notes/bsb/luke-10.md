# BSB Luke 10: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 42 (10:1-42). No verse was kept from an earlier review (the
draft had no KEEP blocks). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 42, "problems": 0}`.

126 of the 640 publisher `g` rows changed. 18 of them only gained unlinked
Greek tokens, with the English unchanged (for example πρὸς αὐτούς = them
10:2, ἐν μέσῳ = among 10:3, ἣν ἄν = Whatever 10:5, εἰ μή = except 10:22, καὶ
ἰδού = One day 10:25, ἀποκριθεὶς … εἶπεν = replied 10:41). The other 108 rows,
in 34 verses, were corrected, split or moved. Parallels checked: Matthew
9:37-38 (harvest), Matthew 10:7-16 and 10:40, Mark 6:8-11 and Luke 9:3-5
(sending), Matthew 11:21-27 (woes, thanksgiving), Matthew 13:16-17 (blessed
eyes), Matthew 22:35-40 and Mark 12:29-31 (great commandment).

## Publisher rows corrected

Words supplied by English that had been folded into a Greek word (now `t`):

- Copulas: 10:2 "harvest is", "are few"; 10:7 "is worthy"; 10:23 "Blessed
  are" → harvest, few, worthy, Blessed; `t is`/`t are` (as Matthew 9:37,
  10:10, 13:16).
- "Jesus" on a verb or participle: 10:21 ἠγαλλιάσατο, 10:23 στραφείς, 10:28
  εἶπεν, 10:40 ἐπιστᾶσα ("She came to Jesus") → `t Jesus`.
- Objects: 10:22 "to reveal Him" (as Matthew 11:27), 10:26 "do you read it",
  10:30 "took up this question", 10:32 "saw him", 10:34 "went to him" (as
  Mark 1:31), 10:35 "gave them" → `t Him/it/this question/him/them`.
- 10:30: ἐκδύσαντες "stripped him" and αὐτόν "him#1" (after "beat") were
  shifted; αὐτόν = him#0, and the English repeats "him" after beat and
  leaving (`t him#1`, `t him#2`). πληγὰς ἐπιθέντες joined = beat.
- 10:11: ἀπομασσόμεθα "we wipe off as a testimony" → we wipe off; `t as a
  testimony` (N1904 has only the dative ὑμῖν, "against you"; the phrase is
  in Luke 9:5, not here). τοῦτο "of this" → this; γινώσκετε = be sure of.
- 10:27: the second "Love" sat on σου ("Love your"); now Ἀγαπήσεις = Love +
  Love (the verb understood again) and σου = your.
- "and" joining a participle to the main verb (now `t`): 10:10 "and
  declare", 10:17 "and said", 10:23 "and said", 10:30 "and said", 10:34
  "and bandaged", 10:35 "and gave", 10:40 "and said".

Negatives and auxiliaries split:

- 10:4: μηδένα "anyone" + "Do not greet" → not + anyone; ἀσπάσησθε = Do +
  greet (as Luke 5:14). 10:7 "Do not" + "move around" → not; Do + move
  around. 10:10 "they do not welcome" → not; they do + welcome. 10:20 "do
  not" + "rejoice" → not; do + rejoice.
- 10:24: "did not see it", "did not hear it" → οὐκ = not; did + see/hear;
  ἅ = what + it (as Matthew 13:17).
- 10:40: "do You not care" → οὐ = not, μέλει = do + care, σοι = You.
- 10:42: "will not be taken away from" → οὐκ = not; will + be taken away;
  αὐτῆς = from her.

Prepositions and "the" on a lone article; "a" with its noun; rule 3:

- 10:2 τοῦ "of the", 10:19 τοῦ "of the", 10:35 τῷ "to the" → the; the
  preposition moved to the noun (of + harvest, of + enemy, to +
  innkeeper). 10:7: τοῦ "of" → `s`; μισθοῦ = of + wages (as Matthew 10:10).
  10:5: τούτῳ "to this" → this; οἴκῳ = to + house. 10:12: ἐκείνῃ "for
  that" → that; πόλει = for + town (as Matthew 10:15).
- 10:8, 10:10: ἣν "a" and the verb "If you enter" → ἣν ἄν = If/if (as Mark
  6:11 ὃς ἄν = If anyone), πόλιν = a town, εἰσέλθητε = you enter.
- Article + participle/infinitive joined: 10:8 τὰ παρατιθέμενα = whatever is
  set before; 10:11 τὸν κολληθέντα = that clings; 10:13 αἱ γενόμεναι = that
  were performed; 10:16 ὁ ἀκούων, ὁ ἀθετῶν (twice), τὸν ἀποστείλαντα; 10:19
  τοῦ πατεῖν = to tread; 10:23 οἱ βλέποντες = that see; 10:35 τῷ
  ἐπανέρχεσθαι = return; 10:36 τοῦ ἐμπεσόντος = to the man who fell; 10:37
  Ὁ ποιήσας = The one who showed; 10:38 ἐν τῷ (+ infinitive) = As.

Other words on the wrong Greek word:

- 10:35: ἐγώ carried "my" and με carried "I". ἐγώ is the subject of
  ἀποδώσω and με the subject of the infinitive, so ἐγώ = I and με = my.
- 10:42: ὀλίγων ("few") carried "thing" and ἤ ("or") carried "only". Now
  ἑνός = one thing, ὀλίγων … ἤ is `s`, "only" is `t` (see doubts).
- 10:3: ἀποστέλλω "I am sending you out" → I am sending + out; ὑμᾶς = you.
- 10:9: αὐτῇ "who are there" → ἐν αὐτῇ = there; `t who are`.
- 10:21: ἔμπροσθεν "in Your sight" → in + sight; σου = Your (as Matthew
  11:26).
- 10:22: καί (second) "and no one knows" → and; οὐδείς = No one + no one,
  γινώσκει = knows + knows (Luke does not repeat them, unlike Matthew
  11:27's οὐδέ … τις).
- 10:7: αὐτῇ "the same" → same; τῇ = the. 10:31: ἐκείνῃ "the same" → same;
  τῇ = the.
- 10:26, 10:27, 10:29, 10:37: the article of ὁ δέ now carries the subject
  (Jesus, He, he, the expert in the law) and εἶπεν keeps only the verb
  (replied, answered, asked).
- 10:36: εἰς "of" and the participle "who fell into the hands" → εἰς =
  into, τούς = the, λῃστάς = hands of robbers (note).
- 10:38: δέ "As" → `s`; ἐν τῷ = As.
- 10:40: "by all the" + "preparations to be made" → περί = by, πολλήν = all,
  διακονίαν = the preparations to be made.
- 10:1: "ahead of Him" on πρό → πρὸ προσώπου = ahead of, αὐτοῦ = Him.
- 10:35: "for any additional" + "expense" → ὅ τι ἄν = for any,
  προσδαπανήσῃς = additional expense.

## Notable decisions

- Readings, only where EVIDENCE shows a different publisher text:
  - 10:1 and 10:17: "seventy-two". ἑβδομήκοντα = seventy and `t two` with
    "BSB follows a reading with an extra word here; N1904 has seventy"
    (probably ἑβδομήκοντα δύο). 10:1 ἀνὰ δύο is linked to "two by two"; the
    other extra publisher words claim nothing.
  - 10:21: `t in#0` (BSB follows a reading with an extra word here;
    probably ἐν before τῷ Πνεύματι). "Jesus" is a plain `t`: the counts allow
    only one extra word.
  - Same word in another spelling or form, linked with a note: 10:7
    ἔσθοντες (eating), 10:13 Χοραζείν, 10:15 Ἅιδου, 10:19 ἀδικήσει (probably
    a subjunctive), 10:30 Ἱερειχώ, 10:32 Λευείτης, 10:33 Σαμαρείτης, 10:40
    εἰπόν. 10:32's second extra publisher word (probably γενόμενος) has no
    unowned English, so no reading is claimed.
- Parallels kept consistent where the Greek is the same: Matthew 9:37-38
  (μέν `s`, ὅπως = to), Matthew 11:21-23 (Woe to you, they would have
  repented with ἄν joined, μή = No), Matthew 11:25-26, Matthew 13:17, Matthew
  22:37 (Κύριον = the Lord, τόν `s`). Where I differ from older reviewed
  rows it is for the brief's rules: no preposition on a lone article
  (10:2 τοῦ θερισμοῦ, against Matthew 9:38) and article + participle joined
  (10:13 αἱ γενόμεναι, 10:16, against Matthew 11:21 and 10:40).
- Scene and clause frames: καὶ ἰδού = One day (10:25). καί = when (10:30,
  BSB's "was going down … when he fell"); 10:33 καί = when (the καί-joined
  clauses become "when …, he looked"), with ἰδών = he looked at, `t him#1`,
  `t and`. 10:38 δέ = where (BSB's relative clause).
- 10:19: οὐδέν = Nothing; the emphatic οὐ μή is `s` ("Nothing" carries the
  negation; the English has no "never").
- 10:30: the καί between the participles ἐκδύσαντες and πληγὰς ἐπιθέντες is
  linked to the series "and" before "went away" (note).
- Idioms linked with a note: υἱὸς εἰρήνης = a man of peace (10:6), τὰ παρ’
  αὐτῶν = whatever you are offered (10:7), ἐφ’ ὑμᾶς = you after "is near"
  (10:9), τῇδε ἦν = She had (10:39), μετ’ αὐτοῦ = him (showed him mercy,
  10:37), ὑπολαβών = took up (10:30), περιέπεσεν = he fell into the hands
  (10:30, publisher row kept).
- Pronoun rendered as a name: αὐτός = Jesus (10:38).

## Doubts for the peer

- 10:42: EVIDENCE says the publisher's Greek equals N1904 (ὀλίγων δέ ἐστιν
  χρεία ἢ ἑνός, "few things are needed, or one"), but BSB's English "only one
  thing is necessary" matches the shorter reading. No reading is claimed;
  ὀλίγων … ἤ is `s` and "only" is `t`. The publisher had ὀλίγων = thing and
  ἤ = only, which do not mean those words.
- 10:30: καί (between the participles) = "and#1" before "went away". The
  alternative is καί `s` and "and" `t`, as for an "and" that joins a
  participle to the main verb; I linked it to avoid a split pair, but the
  scopes differ.
- 10:36: λῃστάς = "hands of robbers". The alternative is λῃστάς = robbers
  with "hands of" `t`; I kept it close to 10:30, where "the hands" rides on
  the verb.
- 10:1 and 10:17: "seventy" linked, "two" `t` with a reading reason. A peer
  may prefer ἑβδομήκοντα `s` and "seventy-two" `t`; I linked "seventy"
  because that part is the same in both texts.
- 10:21: "in#0" as a reading. The N1904 dative τῷ Πνεύματι can itself mean
  "in the Spirit", so a link "in + Spirit" is possible; I followed rule 9
  because the counts show exactly one extra publisher word.
- 10:40: πολλήν = "all" and διακονίαν = "the preparations to be made" are
  free renderings, kept as links with notes rather than `s` + `t`.
