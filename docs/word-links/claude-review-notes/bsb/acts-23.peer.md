# BSB Acts 23: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 35 of 35 verses (23:1-35), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`
(23:6, 23:8, 23:20, 23:23, 23:30 and 23:33 have a `partial` record).
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 35, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 2 verses. 23:24 has one link change (ἐπιβιβάσαντες and "for"),
with a reworded note on τὸν Παῦλον. 23:20 has a note change only. The
other 33 verses are kept exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 23:24 ἐπιβιβάσαντες: before `g 4 = for` ("ἐπιβιβάσαντες … is rendered
  for Paul: the mounts are for Paul to ride") → after `s 4` ("BSB has no
  verb for ἐπιβιβάσαντες ("having set him on them"); mounts for Paul only
  implies it") and `t for` ("English supplies for (mounts for Paul); the
  Greek has no preposition here"). BSB's wording is "Provide mounts for
  Paul to take him safely to Governor Felix". It has no "to ride", "set
  on" or "mount" verb. "for" is a preposition that means neither "having
  set on" nor "mounting". The author's note even explains "for" by "to
  ride", which is not in BSB. That is an over-claim, not a split pair: the
  English word means something else, so `s` + `t` is correct (brief: the
  ὅταν/"and" rule). The other owners stay: κτήνη = mounts, παραστῆσαι =
  Provide, ἵνα = to#0, διασώσωσι = take … safely, and τὸν Παῦλον = "Paul +
  him". τὸν Παῦλον is the one accusative object shared by the participle
  and its main verb in one clause, so it links to both, as λαβὼν περιέτεμεν
  αὐτόν (16:3) and Acts 9:25 do. Its note now reads "τὸν Παῦλον, the one
  object of ἐπιβιβάσαντες and διασώσωσι, is rendered Paul and again him".
- 23:20 ὡς μέλλων note: "BSB's Greek text has a different form of the
  participle with the same sense" → "BSB's Greek text differs slightly
  here, with the same sense". The partial record (24 N1904 words, 23
  matched, 24 publisher words) shows that one N1904 word is unmatched.
  μέλλων is the likely one, because the publisher rows leave it without a
  link, but the record does not say which word it is or how it is
  spelled. So the note no longer says what form BSB's Greek
  has. The link stays: "on the pretext" renders the sense of ὡς μέλλων in
  either form.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- **23:24 "for"**: changed (see above). "for" alone does not mean
  ἐπιβιβάσαντες. The honest owners are listed above.
- **23:27, 23:28, 23:30 first-person "I"**: confirmed, and applied
  consistently. Under the person rule, "I/we/you" from a person ending go
  on the finite verb and never on a participle. Each letter verse has one
  "I" on its finite verb: 23:27 ἐξειλάμην = "I#0 + to#1 rescue", 23:28
  κατήγαγον = "I#1 brought + down", 23:30 ἔπεμψα = "I#1 sent". 23:30 I#0
  belongs to the explicit pronoun μοι, which BSB turns into a subject. The
  extra "I" of a participle clause is `t` each time: μαθών "For I had
  learned", βουλόμενος "since I wanted", παραγγείλας "I also instructed".
  The letter has no ἐγώ, so 22:3 does not apply. There, one explicit Ἐγώ
  is the subject of the whole sentence and links to each repeated "I".
  Second-person "you" follows the same rule: 23:3 you#2 is on the finite
  κελεύεις, not on the participle παρανομῶν. Third-person pronouns on
  participles stay by the Acts 16 convention: 23:16 "he went", 23:18 "He
  has", 23:23 "he called", 23:25 "he wrote", 23:33 "they delivered", 23:35
  "he#1 ordered". Linking the extra "I" to the finite verb as well (I + I)
  was not taken, because "I had learned" is not what ἐξειλάμην says.
- **23:33 "and"**: confirmed. εἰσελθόντες καὶ ἀναδόντες … παρέστησαν is a
  participle chain. BSB subordinates the first participle ("When … arrived")
  and puts its only coordinator before the main verb ("and presented"), as
  in 18:21 (peer). So καί (5) = "and". The καί before τὸν Παῦλον is the
  adverbial "also" (Paul as well as the letter). It does not mean "and" and
  stays `s`.
- **23:10 him#1**: confirmed `t`. ἄγειν τε is a second infinitive joined by
  τε, and its object is left understood. This is the 16:39 (peer)
  situation, not one object shared by a participle and its main verb, so
  αὐτόν links only to him#0.
- **23:13 "of them … involved in"**: confirmed. The number τεσσεράκοντα
  means only "forty". The article + participle οἱ … ποιησάμενοι ("those who
  made") is the Greek subject that BSB recasts as "of them … involved in",
  so it owns those words (rule 3). ταύτην = this, τήν `s`.
- **23:3 vocative and σε**: confirmed. Τύπτειν = strike, σε = you#0 (the
  object after strike). The vocative τοῖχε = "you#1 + wall" (an English
  "you" before a vocative goes with the vocative noun), and κεκονιαμένε =
  whitewashed. "here" and "yourself" `t`: the Greek has no place word, and
  σύ is already "You" in the first clause.
- **23:16 παραγενόμενος `s`, 23:18 joined "took", 23:31 ἀναλαβόντες `s`**:
  kept. "went into" renders εἰσελθὼν εἰς. "took … to" fits both παραλαβών
  and ἤγαγεν. "brought" is the plain rendering of ἤγαγον, and "taking
  along" has no English word.
- **23:30 "their case"**: kept `t` with the reading reason. The partial
  record (21 N1904 words, all matched, 22 publisher words) shows one extra
  publisher word. The publisher's own rows link every N1904 word but not
  "their case", which is consistent with that extra word being its owner.
  The note says only "a reading with an extra word".
- **Caesarea (23:23, 23:33)**: kept. Each partial record shows one N1904
  word unmatched and one publisher word extra. In both verses the
  publisher rows leave Καισαρίας / Καισαρίαν without a link. The note "Same name, spelled differently in BSB's Greek
  text" is the approved wording.
- **23:6, 23:8**: no reading is claimed, as the author decided. Every
  English word has an N1904 owner. In 23:6 the publisher leaves "I" of "I
  am on trial" off κρίνομαι, but in N1904 the person ending of κρίνομαι
  owns it.

Other rows I checked closely and kept: 23:1 and 23:26, where the dative
"in"/"To" sits on the first content word of the phrase, never on the
article; 23:6 "in the resurrection" on ἀναστάσεως with καί `s`; 23:27
ἀναιρεῖσθαι = "to kill him", a passive turned active that keeps its
patient (5:26, 16:36); 23:27 him#1 `t`, since the object τὸν ἄνδρα τοῦτον
is already rendered "This man"; 23:28 ἐνεκάλουν = against with τήν =
their; 23:30 ἔσεσθαι = "there was", an existential εἶναι, unlike the place
word "there".

## Still uncertain

- 22:5 (Acts 22 author file, not yet peer-reviewed) links the ending of
  ἐπορευόμην to two "I"s ("I#0 + I#1 was on my way"), and there is no ἐγώ
  in that verse. That is the I + I option this chapter does not use. One
  of the two chapters should be brought in line. Older registered rows
  (10:29 "when I was invited", 17:23 "as I walked around") still keep a
  first-person "I" on a participle, which the current person rule
  replaces.
- 23:27 him#1: a case can be made for τὸν ἄνδρα τοῦτον = "man + him#1"
  (the Greek role of τὸν ἄνδρα is the object of ἐξειλάμην). I kept the
  author's `t` because "This man" is BSB's rendering of that noun phrase.
