# BSB Acts 7: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 60 of 60 verses. 7:55 is Codex's KEEP block: I checked it but did not
edit it (comments below). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 60, "problems": 0}`.

I made 7 changes in 7 verses: 5 change links and 2 only reword a note. I
confirmed everything else in the author's file, including the reading claims.

## Changes (verse: before → after, why)

- 7:6: ἀλλοτρίᾳ = "a strange", γῇ = "land" → γῇ = "a + land", ἀλλοτρίᾳ =
  "strange". English "a" goes with the noun. The author already did this in
  7:30 ("of a + bush"), and this publisher row was missed.
- 7:12: ἀκούσας = "When + heard that" → "When + heard", with "that" `t`.
  The Greek has no ὅτι. The fact heard is given by the participle ὄντα, so
  English "that" is supplied. Reviewed rows give a "that" `t` when the Greek
  uses an infinitive or participle.
- 7:18: note only. The ᾔδει note said "knew nothing of", but "of" sits on τὸν
  (indeclinable Ἰωσήφ). The note now says "knew nothing".
- 7:20: τρεῖς = "For three", μῆνας = "months" → μῆνας = "For + months",
  τρεῖς = "three". The accusative of the noun gives the length of time. This
  is the reviewed convention ("for + years/months/days", e.g. John 5:5).
- 7:36: τεσσεράκοντα = "for forty", ἔτη = "years" → ἔτη = "for + years",
  τεσσεράκοντα = "forty". This is the same convention as 7:20. Also, the
  number is indeclinable and carries no case.
- 7:46: note only, on the `t` for "for". The old reason said "for" marks "the
  dative of BSB's different noun (God)". That states a grammatical fact about
  the publisher's Greek, which is not in the repo. It now reads: "English
  for goes with the noun after it, which BSB reads differently; N1904's noun
  here, οἴκῳ ("house"), is not linked."
- 7:53: εἰς = "by", διαταγάς = "ordained", ἀγγέλων = "angels" → εἰς
  διαταγάς = "ordained", ἀγγέλων = "by angels". In "ordained by angels",
  "by" introduces the angels as the ones who acted. That comes from the
  genitive ἀγγέλων, not from εἰς ("as/into"). This settles the author's 7:53
  doubt.

## Points I was asked to settle

- **7:46 (οἴκῳ / "God", "for")**: confirmed. EVIDENCE shows 1 N1904 word
  unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904. The publisher's own row skips
  οἴκῳ and links τῷ = "for the", so a different noun with a different meaning
  is supported: `s` for οἴκῳ and `t` for "God". "For" cannot honestly sit
  anywhere else. On τῷ it would break the lone-article rule. On οἴκῳ it would
  show "house" behind BSB's "God". No other Greek word means "for". So `t`
  is right, and only its note needed softening (above). No note in the file
  names the publisher's Greek word.
- **7:38 (ὑμῖν / "to us")**: confirmed. EVIDENCE shows 1 word unmatched and 1
  extra, and the publisher's row has no link for ὑμῖν. "You" and "us" differ
  in meaning, so `s` + `t` is right. The notes give only N1904's word.
- **7:59**: confirmed. Putting "While" on ἐπικαλούμενον matches Acts 1:10:
  there the ὡς of the Greek subordinate clause is linked to BSB's "when",
  which BSB places before the other clause. Στέφανον = "him + Stephen" is
  honest. Unlike John 5:16, where the moved "Jesus" is the subject of a
  different verb and is `t`, here the participle ἐπικαλούμενον agrees with
  Στέφανον. BSB's "Stephen appealed" renders "Στέφανον ἐπικαλούμενον"
  directly, and "him" is the same Στέφανον as the object of the stoning. This
  matches John 11:31, where a Greek name is linked to the English word in its
  new role (Μαριάμ → "she"). Both English words render the one Greek noun,
  which has both roles in the Greek.
- **7:8**: confirmed. ἐγέννησεν owns all three "became the father of",
  including the bare "of#3". The second and third clauses leave the verb
  unstated, so these are words understood again. The articles before the
  names are `s` (Matthew 1:2). τοὺς = "the" in "of the twelve patriarchs".
- **Lone articles**: I scanned the chapter by script. No group made of
  articles alone carries more than "the", except allowed cases: an article
  used as a pronoun (7:2 ὁ = Stephen, 7:25 οἱ = they), an article used as a
  possessive (7:13, 7:14, 7:17, 7:20, 7:27, 7:54, 7:60), and indeclinable
  names (7:9 and 7:18 τὸν Ἰωσήφ "of", 7:13 τῷ Φαραώ, 7:17 τῷ Ἀβραάμ, 7:40
  τῷ Ἀαρών, 7:43 τοῦ Μολόχ). I spot-checked 7:2, 7:8, 7:13, 7:30, 7:34,
  7:41, 7:42, 7:44, 7:46, 7:52 and 7:60. In each, the preposition now sits
  on the noun.
- **Readings**: every claim matches the counts in acts.json. 7:43 Rephan is
  1 word unmatched and 2 extra (spelling plus "your"). 7:13 is 1 unmatched
  and 2 extra: the same meaning is linked, and the second extra word is not
  claimed. 7:22 has 1 extra word, linked with a note. 7:19 "our" and 7:25
  "his" each have 1 extra word and are `t`. 7:3 and 7:10 each have 1 extra
  word that BSB does not render, and rightly nothing is claimed. No note
  states the publisher's exact wording or spelling.
- **OT quotations**: I checked each one word by word: 7:3 (Gen 12:1), 7:6-7,
  7:27-28 (Exod 2:14), 7:32-34 (Exod 3), 7:37 (Deut 18:15), 7:40 (Exod
  32:1), 7:42-43 (Amos 5:25-27) and 7:49-50 (Isa 66:1-2). Only 7:34 "send …
  back" needed thought (below).
- **Brief rules**: supplied copulas are `t` (7:32 am, 7:35 is, 7:47 it was,
  7:49 is/is/will … be). Words understood again are linked to the word the
  Greek states: 7:6 ὅτι, 7:8 ἐγέννησεν, 7:38 μετά, 7:51 ἀντιπίπτετε =
  "did". The only τε (7:26) is `s`.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 7:59: confirmed, as above.
- 7:18 ἄχρι οὗ = "Then": kept. BSB turns the "until" clause into a new
  sentence, and "Then" carries the same time link. `s` + `t` would leave the
  obvious source of BSB's "Then" unlinked.
- 7:37 ὁ = "the same": kept. "Same" brings out the identifying article (cf.
  Acts 1:11 οὗτος = "This same"). It is not a copula, preposition or noun.
- 7:9 "him" and 7:45 "it#0" `t`: kept. These objects are left out in the
  Greek and the English supplies them, like 7:39 and 7:58. That is different
  from 7:59, where Στέφανον is written once and has both roles.
- 7:17 τῆς = "His", ἧς ὡμολόγησεν `s`, "for … to fulfill" `t`: kept.
- 7:29 καί = "and" (moved), ἐν = "to": kept. Same meaning in a new place, so
  this is not a split pair.
- 7:43 καί = "Therefore", 7:52 καί = "even": kept. 7:53 "you who" on οἵτινες
  kept, since the relative stands for the "you" of 7:52. "By" moved, as
  above.
- 7:44 "with them" `t`, ἦν = "had": kept.

## Still uncertain

- 7:34 ἀποστείλω = "will send + back" follows Acts 1:11 "will come back".
  "Back" could also be `t`, since ἀποστέλλω alone does not say "back". I
  left it as is.
- 7:55 KEEP (Codex, not edited): ὑπάρχων πλήρης = "Stephen + full" folds a
  supplied name into the group. Under this chapter's convention, "Stephen"
  would be `t` (as in 7:1 and 7:24). Its note already says the name is
  supplied. Two of its notes are wordy but correct.
- 7:12 "heard that": I followed the reviewed rule that
  English "that" without a Greek ὅτι is `t`. Linking "that there was" to ὄντα
  would also be defensible.
