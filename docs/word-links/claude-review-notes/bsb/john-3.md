## Publisher rows corrected

Articles and pronouns carrying words they do not mean:

- 3:1: τῶν "of the" → the; Ἰουδαίων = of + Jews. 3:29: τοῦ "of the" → the; νυμφίου = of + bridegroom.
- 3:18: Θεοῦ "of God’s" → God’s; Υἱοῦ = of + Son (the "of" is the genitive of "the Son", not of God).
- 3:29: Ὁ "belongs to" → Ὁ ἔχων … ἐστίν = belongs to (BSB recasts "the one who has the bride is the bridegroom"). 3:34: ὁ "whom" → `s`; ὅν = the One whom.
- 3:12: τά "things" (×2) → τὰ ἐπίγεια = earthly things, τὰ ἐπουράνια = heavenly things; ὑμῖν "you about" (×2) → you, with "about" on the verb.
- 3:21: αὐτοῦ "what he" → he; τὰ ἔργα = what + has done; "it" (anticipatory) `t`.

Supplied English words folded into Greek words (now `t`):

- 3:2: διδάσκαλος "You are a teacher" → a teacher; ἐλήλυθας = You + who has come; `t are` (BSB recasts "You have come from God as a teacher").
- 3:8: ἐστίν "it is" → is; πᾶς "with everyone" → everyone; `t it`, `t with` (Greek "so is everyone born of the Spirit").
- 3:31: λαλεῖ "speaks as one" → speaks; `t as one`.
- 3:34: δίδωσιν "God gives" → gives; `t God`. The Greek verb has no stated subject (N1904 and, per its EVIDENCE line, the publisher's Greek lack ὁ Θεός here).
- 3:25: Ἰουδαίου "a certain Jew" → a + Jew; `t certain` (no τις).
- 3:26: ἦλθον "John’s disciples came" → came; `t John’s disciples`.
- Supplied "the" with an anarthrous noun (as Matthew 3:11, 3:16, Luke 13:22): 3:5 "the Spirit", 3:23 "the water", 3:29 "the bridegroom".
- 3:29: ἐμή "is mine" → mine, `t is`; πεπλήρωται "and it is now complete" → it is now complete, `t and`; χαίρει "and is overjoyed" → χαρᾷ χαίρει = is overjoyed, `t and` (the "and" only joins the participle phrase to the main verb).

Negatives and auxiliaries split off verbs:

- 3:7 μή = not, θαυμάσῃς = Do + be amazed. 3:8 οὐκ = not, οἶδας = you do + know. 3:10 οὐ = not, γινώσκεις = you do + understand. 3:11 οὐ = not, λαμβάνετε = you people do + accept. 3:12 οὐ = not, πιστεύετε = you do + believe.
- 3:16 μή = not, ἀπόληται = shall + perish. 3:17 οὐ "did not" → not, ἀπέστειλεν = did + send. 3:20 οὐκ "does not" → not, ἔρχεται = does + come. 3:36 οὐκ = not, ὄψεται = will + see.
- 3:18: all three negatives split (is + condemned; whoever + does + believe; he has + believed). 3:24: οὔπω "had not yet" → not yet; ἦν = had + been.

Connectives and prepositions on the wrong word:

- 3:14: καί "Just" → `s`; καθώς = Just as.
- 3:4: καί → to (BSB turns "enter … and be born" into "to be born"); 3:23 the same for "to be baptized".
- 3:8: ἔρχεται "it comes from" → it comes; πόθεν = where + from.
- 3:11: ὅ "of what", "to what" → what; "of"/"to" moved to the verbs (we speak + of, we testify + to), as "testified about" in 3:26.
- 3:25: ἐκ "between" and μετά "and" → ἐκ … μετά = between + and (one recast).
- 3:29: οὖν "That" → `s`; αὕτη = That. τήν "the" → `s`; τοῦ = the (of "the bridegroom’s").
- 3:30: δεῖ understood again: δεῖ = must + must; ἐλαττοῦσθαι = decrease.
- 3:31: ἐκ "to" and ἐστιν "belongs" → ἐκ … ἐστιν = belongs to.
- 3:32: τοῦτο "to" → ὃ … τοῦτο = what; μαρτυρεῖ = He testifies + to.
- 3:2: ταῦτα "the" → `s`; τά = the.

Article + participle units joined (rule 3): 3:13, 3:15, 3:16, 3:18, 3:20, 3:21, 3:31 (×3), 3:33, 3:36.

## Readings

- Only the EVIDENCE lines of 3:2, 3:18, 3:23-28 show publisher Greek different from N1904.
- Spelling only, linked with a note: Rabbi 3:2, 3:26 (probably Ῥαββί); John 3:23, 3:24, 3:25, 3:26, 3:27 (probably Ἰωάννης).
- 3:18: "but" `t` with the reading reason (one extra publisher word, probably δέ).
- 3:27: same-meaning variant, linked with a note (probably οὐδὲ ἕν for οὐδέν).
- 3:23, 3:24: one further extra publisher word each, probably the article ὁ before the name; nothing in English depends on it, so no claim.
- 3:28: one extra publisher word, probably a second recitative ὅτι; no English counterpart, no claim.

## Notable decisions

- ἄνωθεν: linked to "again" in 3:3 and 3:7 with a note that it can also mean "from above"; linked to "from above" in 3:31 with a cross-reference. No claim about which sense John intends.
- μονογενής (3:16, 3:18): one adjective = "one and only", noted as "only, unique". No "only begotten" or etymology claim.
- αἰώνιος (3:15, 3:16, 3:36): ζωήν = life and αἰώνιον = eternal kept as separate links, no note.
- 3:16: οὕτως = so, noted as "thus, in this way" without settling manner vs degree. τόν = His (possessive article), second τόν `s`.
- 3:8: πνεῦμα = wind, noted that it means both wind and spirit.
- 3:26: τὸν Ἰωάνην = him (spelling note); BSB moves the name into "John’s disciples", which is `t`.
- 3:27: Οὐ … οὐδὲν ἐὰν μή joined = "only what" ("cannot receive anything unless" said positively).
- 3:6: τὸ γεγεννημένον = born (×2); σάρξ = Flesh, ἐστιν = is (BSB's "Flesh is born of flesh").
- 3:10: τοῦ Ἰσραήλ joined = Israel’s (indeclinable name).
- 3:11: λαμβάνετε = you people (plural ending), kept from the publisher with a note.
- 3:29: διά = to hear (causal "because of"), kept from the publisher with a note.
- 3:17: κρίνῃ = condemn and σωθῇ = save (passive made active), noted.

## Doubts for the peer

- 3:4 and 3:23: καί = "to". In 3:4 γεννηθῆναι is itself an infinitive, so "to" could instead go with it and καί be `s`.
- 3:26: Ἰωάνην = "him" with `t John’s disciples`. The alternative is linking the name to "John’s" and `t him`.
- 3:27: the four-token "only what" group. The alternative is Οὐ … ἐὰν μή = only and οὐδέν = what.
- 3:3, 3:5: kept οὐ = no, τις = one, γεννηθῇ = he is born. τις could instead be "he" (the subject of γεννηθῇ), with "one" on δύναται.
- 3:29: διά = "to hear"; "hear" has no Greek verb here, so `t hear` is possible. Also πεπλήρωται = "it is now complete": "now" may echo οὖν rather than the perfect; I kept οὖν `s`.
- 3:28: μαρτυρεῖτε = "can testify" kept; "can" is English modality.
- 3:25: περί = "over the issue of" kept as one phrase.
