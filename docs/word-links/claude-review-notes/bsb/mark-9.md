## Notable decisions

- Different reading: only 9:42 "in Me" is `t`, because BSB follows εἰς ἐμέ, which N1904 lacks. In the other verses where EVIDENCE shows a difference (9:2, 9:4, 9:5, 9:10–14, 9:16, 9:38, 9:43), it is only spelling or form: Ἡλείας, Ἰωάνην/Ἰωάνης, Ῥαββεί, συνζητέω, συνλαλέω, σκανδαλίσῃ. These words are linked normally.
- Pronouns that BSB renders as names or nouns are linked: Jesus (9:11, 9:15, 9:20, 9:28), the boy’s (9:21), the boy (9:20), the child (9:36).
- An article that serves as a pronoun, possessive or demonstrative is linked: ὁ/οἱ δέ → He/he/they (9:12, 9:21, 9:32, 9:34); τούς → his (9:18); the publisher's anaphoric article → this is kept (9:10, 9:32).
- ἵνα with the subjunctive → to (9:9, 9:18, 9:30), and εἰ → to (9:42), following Matthew 18:6.
- Kept from the publisher, with a note: καί → There (9:2); οὐ → any (9:3); ἄλαλον → that makes him mute (9:17); εὐθύς → As soon as (9:15); καί → or and ἵνα → trying (9:22); ταχύ → turn around (9:39).
- Left unexpressed (`s`): λέγοντες (9:11); ἐπί (9:12); πρὸς αὐτόν (9:20); ἐστὶν ὡς (9:21); the quoting article τό (9:23); αὐτοῦ after out (9:25); ἀποκτανθείς after "They will kill Him" (9:31); λαβών (9:36); ἐστιν in "there is no one who" (9:39). The usual items are also `s`: the recitative ὅτι and the addressees αὐτῷ/αὐτοῖς.

## Doubts for the peer

- 9:38: N1904, and by the evidence count also the publisher's Greek, has the clause twice: ὃς οὐκ ἀκολουθεῖ ἡμῖν … ὅτι οὐκ ἠκολούθει ἡμῖν. BSB renders it once. I linked the relative clause (4 tokens) to "else" (the publisher had ὅς → else) and the ὅτι clause to "because he does not accompany us". The alternatives are: `s` the relative clause and make "else" `t` (a split pair); or link the relative clause to "he does not accompany us", whose present tense fits ἀκολουθεῖ.
- 9:2: I kept the publisher's καί → There. Matthew 17:2 (an earlier review) has `s` καί + `t There`.
- 9:34: πρὸς ἀλλήλους is joined to "had been arguing", and "of them" is `t`. The alternative is πρὸς ἀλλήλους → of them.
- 9:3: the launderer clause is recast. οἷα … οὕτως → brighter than; οὐ → any (from "such as no launderer could"); στίλβοντα … λίαν → radiantly.
- 9:6: BSB reverses the two γάρ clauses. The first γάρ → For and the second → so … that. Swapping them is also defensible.
- 9:23: τό, which quotes the father's "If You can", is `s`. It could instead be joined with εἶπεν → echoed.
- 9:39: I kept καί → and, although BSB's "and" sits inside "turn around and speak evil".
- 9:17: ἄλαλον → "that makes him mute" is kept as one phrase. The alternative is ἄλαλον → mute with `t that makes him`.
