# BSB John 6: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 71 of 71 verses (6:1-71), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, in four sections
(6:1-20, 6:21-35, 6:36-59, 6:60-71). Each one was compared with the
publisher draft rows and with the reviewed parallels (Matthew 14, Mark 6,
Luke 9). Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 71, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 4 verses, 8 lines. Everything else in the author file is kept.

## Changes

- **6:7** ἀρκοῦσιν "would + buy enough" → "would + enough"; "buy" is now `t`
  ("English supplies the buying implied by the genitive of price Διακοσίων
  δηναρίων"). ἀρκοῦσιν means "are sufficient" and has no idea of buying. The
  buying comes from the genitive of price ("bread worth two hundred
  denarii"), but "buy" is an English verb and δηναρίων is a noun already linked
  to "denarii", so `t` is the honest choice. Matthew 20:2 sets the precedent:
  "to pay" is `t` for the price phrase ἐκ δηναρίου. Mark 6:37 differs because
  it has a real verb of buying (ἀγοράσωμεν = "Should we + spend").
- **6:10** τὸν ἀριθμόν `s` + "of them" `t` → ἀριθμόν = "of them" (note: the
  accusative τὸν ἀριθμόν "in number" is rendered "of them" after the count),
  τόν `s`. This restores the publisher row. "about five thousand of them" is
  BSB's way of saying "about five thousand in number": both phrases tie the
  numeral to the men. It is a shifted rendering, not an addition. Luke 22:3
  makes a similar shifted link (ἀριθμοῦ = "one"). Rule 10: the publisher row
  was not wrong.
- **6:17** opening καί `s` + "and#0" `t` → καί = "and#0" (note). That was a
  split pair. The καί coordinates κατέβησαν (6:16) and ἤρχοντο; ἐμβάντες is
  the participle. In BSB's "went down…, got into a boat, and started across",
  the "and" before the last verb is that καί. The author linked the same
  structure this way in 6:11 (καί = "and" before "distributed"). The
  participle-"and" convention does not apply because a real καί is there.
- **6:65** Διὰ τοῦτο = "This is why" (one group) → Διά = "why", τοῦτο =
  "This", and "is" is `t` ("English adds the copula in 'This is why'"). Rule 7
  says a supplied "is" is `t`. Reviewed Matthew 13:13 handles the same Greek
  phrase διὰ τοῦτο → "This is why" this way. 1 John 3:8 and Hebrews 11:2 join
  "This is why" for different Greek phrases, so the precedent is mixed, and
  rule 7 decides.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- **6:10**: changed. See above.
- **6:7** αὐτοῖς = "for + of them", ἕκαστος = "each": kept. ἕκαστος means only
  "each". "of them" names the group that αὐτοῖς refers to, and "for" is the
  dative with ἀρκοῦσιν. ἵνα keeps "to" and λάβῃ keeps "have". The
  publisher's ἕκαστος = "each of them" folded αὐτοῖς's English into another
  word.
- **6:22**: kept. συνεισῆλθεν … εἰς = "had + boarded + with": "with" comes from
  συν-, and both εἰσ- and εἰς are inside "boarded". τὸ πλοῖον = "it" is correct.
  ἄλλο belongs in the "only" group: οὐκ ἄλλο … εἰ μή ("no other … except") is
  the exclusive construction that English turns into "only". πλοιάριον =
  "boat" and ἕν = "one" stay separate. The publisher's αὐτοῦ = "with His" was
  correctly fixed.
- **6:36** ὅτι `s` + "as" `t`: kept. "as" introduces a comment clause and does
  not mean "that", so this is not a split pair.
- **6:11** ὁμοίως καί = "and": kept. "likewise also" adds the fish to the
  same action, and BSB expresses that by joining the fish to the loaves.
- **6:65**: changed to the split form. See above.
- **6:67** Μή `s`: kept. "Do" is the English question auxiliary, which any
  question needs. By convention it stays with the verb (θέλετε = Do + want).
  The particle's "expecting no" nuance is not rendered.

## Points checked without change

- **6:1** θαλάσσης = "Sea#0 + Sea#1": kept. τῆς Τιβεριάδος is a second
  genitive on the same head noun, and English repeats that head as "Sea". This
  is the noun understood again, like a repeated verb. τῆς (before Τιβεριάδος)
  = "the#2" fits that reading (the [Sea] of Tiberias), and "that is" is `t`.
- **Readings**: checked against the `partial` records in `john.json`. 6:2
  (14 source / 13 matched / 14 publisher) and 6:25 (12/11/12) each have one
  substituted word. In 6:2 ἑώρων = "they saw" is linked with a note about a
  same-meaning verb. In 6:25 Rabbi has the spelling note. 6:29 (17/17/18),
  6:52 (15/15/16) and 6:66 (15/15/16) each have one extra publisher word. 6:29
  needs no claim. In 6:52 τήν = "His" fits the article-as-possessive
  convention and rule 9's same-meaning case. In 6:66 μαθητῶν = "of + disciples"
  (partitive genitive) likewise. No note states the publisher's spelling.
  6:58 has no reading difference ("the manna" `t`, οἱ = "your"), which is
  correct.
- **Parallels**: 6:20 matches Mark 6:50 (ὁ = Jesus, ἐγώ = I, εἰμι = It is,
  μή = not, φοβεῖσθε = do + be afraid). "about five thousand" matches Matthew
  14:21 and Luke 9:14.
- The article + participle joins, the negative splits (6:17, 6:27, 6:35, 6:36,
  6:37, 6:64), the "When" moves (6:5, 6:14, 6:19, 6:25), and the case words
  taken off articles (6:12, 6:33, 6:51, 6:53, 6:54) are all correct.

## Still uncertain

- 6:10: `s` + `t` and the restored link are both defensible. I chose the link
  because it was the publisher row and it is a shifted rendering.
- 6:45: "Him" (after "learned from") is `t`. Πατρός = "Father + Him" would
  match the 6:1 "Sea + Sea" treatment. Kept `t`, because it is a pronoun and
  not a repeat of the word.
- 6:46: "only" is `t`. The publisher had οὗτος = "only He". The emphatic
  resumptive οὗτος carries some of that exclusiveness.
- 6:71: οὗτος = "Judas#1" (in BSB's concessive clause) while "he" stays with
  ἔμελλεν. Another option is οὗτος = "he" with "Judas#1" as `t`.
- 6:51: the article ὁ = "this" (article read as a demonstrative; publisher row
  kept).
