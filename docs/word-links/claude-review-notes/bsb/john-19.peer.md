# BSB John 19: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 42 of 42 verses (19:1-42), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, against N1904,
BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/john.json`. Final check:
`fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 42, "problems": 0}` (`check --target BSB` gives the
same).

## Changes (3 verses, 4 decisions)

- 19:18: `s 12` (δέ) + `t with#1` → `g 12 = with#1`, note "δέ is rendered
  with: with Jesus in the middle." This restores the publisher's row.
  μέσον δὲ τὸν Ἰησοῦν ("and Jesus in the middle") is BSB's absolute
  with-phrase. The reviewed John 8:9 does the same (καὶ ἡ γυνή … → "with the
  woman standing there", καί = with). The author's own 19:23 also links the
  καί that adds the tunic to "with". Leaving δέ `s` and "with" `t` made a
  split pair.
- 19:31 γάρ: `s 18` + `t and#0` → `g 18 = and#0`, note "γάρ ("for") is
  rendered and, since BSB states this explanation before the request it
  explains." This restores the publisher's row. In BSB's recast, "and" is the
  only connective that starts the γάρ clause ("and the next day was a High
  Sabbath"). The reviewed Luke 20:40 and Matthew 6:32 link γάρ = And/and in
  the same way. The `s` + `t` pair was a split pair.
- 19:31 ἐκείνου: `s 22` + `t next` → `g 22 = next`, note "ἐκείνου ("that"
  Sabbath) is rendered next, since BSB mentions this day before the Sabbath
  is named." "next" does the work of the demonstrative. It picks out which
  day: the Sabbath that follows the Preparation. BSB moved the clause before
  "during the Sabbath", so it could not write "that". The reviewed Luke
  20:35 links ἐκείνου ("that" age) to "to come" for the same kind of shift.
  The author's correct move of "the" to ἡ is kept.
- 19:14: τοῖς "to the" → τοῖς = the#3, Ἰουδαίοις = to + Jews (note "The
  dative τοῖς Ἰουδαίοις supplies to."). This follows the reviewed John
  convention (John 2:5, 2:8, 4:28, 6:12, 6:33) and the author's own fixes in 19:26
  and 19:27. There, "to" went to the noun and the lone dative article kept
  only "the". τοῦ = "for the" before the indeclinable πάσχα is kept: the
  case word goes on the article there.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the brief's points

- 19:19 / 19:23, ἔγραψεν and ἔλαβον `s`: **agree**. "posted" honestly renders
  ἔθηκεν ("put"). "had" is the causative, as in 19:1 had + flogged and
  Matthew 27:26 / Mark 15:15 ("he had + flogged"). "divided … into four
  parts" honestly renders ἐποίησαν τέσσερα μέρη ("made four parts"); "into"
  belongs to that recast. Every English word has an owner, and nothing is
  folded into ἔγραψεν or ἔλαβον. "wrote" and "took" have no English words, so
  joining them to "posted" or "divided" would over-claim. 19:39 is different:
  ἦλθεν … φέρων = brought is right there, because "brought" does mean "came
  bearing".
- 19:31 αὐτῶν `s` + `t the bodies`: **agree, kept**. αὐτῶν is the genitive
  possessor of τὰ σκέλη ("their legs"). BSB drops it ("the legs"). "the
  bodies" names the subject of ἀρθῶσιν, which in Greek is only the verb
  ending. The plural verb points to the men, not to the neuter τὰ σώματα
  (compare singular μείνῃ). So "the bodies" renders neither αὐτῶν nor σώματα,
  and this is not a split pair.
- 19:31 γάρ and ἐκείνου: changed (see above).
- 19:10 οὐ = refuse, λαλεῖς = Do You + to speak: **agree**. This is the
  reviewed John 10:26 split (οὐ = refuse, πιστεύετε = to believe). Keeping
  the negative as its own group follows the convention that negatives are
  split off verbs.
- "When" off οὖν: **agree**. 19:5 puts "When" on the καί between the two
  clauses (When-rule). 19:13 and 19:26 put When + heard / When + saw on the
  participles ἀκούσας and ἰδών. οὖν is `s` in all three. In 19:38, Μετὰ
  ταῦτα = Afterward and δέ `s` is right.
- 19:29 "there" `t`: **agree**. ἔκειτο is rendered "was sitting". "there"
  is a locative the Greek does not state.
- 19:15 one αὐτόν = Him + Him + Him: **agree**. One object serves Ἆρον ἆρον,
  σταύρωσον, as in the reviewed Luke 23:21.
- 19:36 "one" with Ὀστοῦν: **agree**. "one … bones" comes from the singular
  noun ("a bone"), and οὐ keeps "Not".
- New rules: no supplied copula is linked to an εἰμί in another clause
  (19:13 `t is`, 19:24 `t is what`, 19:42 `t it was`; 19:14 and 19:40 leave
  the unrendered ἦν/ἐστίν `s`). The repeated content words and objects are
  linked to the one Greek word (19:1 Jesus + Him, 19:6 Him + Him, 19:15).
  The repeated ἔχω in 19:10 is `s`, because BSB does not repeat "have".
- Parallels match: mockery (19:2 = Matthew 27:29: set + on, `t it`), flogging
  (19:1 = Matthew 27:26 / Mark 15:15), "Crucify … Him" (19:6 `t Him` where
  no αὐτόν stands; 19:15 = Luke 23:21), title (19:19 = Luke 23:38: ΤΩΝ = THE,
  ΙΟΥΔΑΙΩΝ = OF + JEWS), sponge (19:29 = Matthew 27:48 / Mark 15:36: `t it`,
  `t and` for the participle), burial (19:41 ἦν τεθειμένος = had + been laid
  as Luke 23:53; τῷ Πειλάτῳ joined = to Pilate in 19:21 as Matthew 27:58).
- Readings: every verse that names Pilate (19:1, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 15,
  19, 21, 22, 31, 38 twice) has the soft spelling note. In each of these
  `partial` records, exactly the Pilate words are unmatched. 19:11 (one
  extra publisher word), 19:23 and 19:32 (one differing pair each, no Pilate)
  and 19:24 (two extra) make no reading claim, and no English word lacks an
  N1904 owner there. 19:38 (two Pilate pairs and one extra word) makes no
  claim either. 19:5, 19:14 and 19:16 have no `partial` record, and "Pilate"
  is correctly `t` there.

## Still uncertain

- 19:23 ἐκ τῶν ἄνωθεν = from + top, δι’ ὅλου = in one piece + to bottom. The
  publisher had "top to bottom" on ἄνωθεν. Both are defensible, so I kept
  the author's version.
- 19:29 μεστόν (first) = "of" (publisher row, kept). The alternative is
  μεστόν `s` with "of" on ὄξους.
- 19:2 / 19:29 `t it` for the crown and the sponge follows Matthew 27:29 and
  27:48. The reviewed Mark 15:17 instead links "it" to στέφανον, so the
  precedent is mixed. 19:32 `t those` (the legs) is the same kind of case.
