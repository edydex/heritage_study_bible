# BSB 2 Corinthians 13: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 14 of 14 verses (13:1-14), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the N1904 Greek, the BSB English and each verse's EVIDENCE line. The author
reviewed 13:1-4 and 13:6-11. 13:5 and 13:12-14 are Codex KEEP blocks. I
checked them but did not edit them. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 14, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 2 verses (13:8, 13:10), 3 changes.

## Changes

- 13:8 οὐ δυνάμεθα: "we cannot" with "do" `t` → "we cannot do". This puts
  the publisher's "do" back. δύναμαι with an accusative object (here τι)
  means "be able to do something", so "do" is part of the verb's
  rendering, not a supplied verb. Reviewed precedent agrees: Mark 9:22
  (εἴ τι δύνῃ = "You can do") and Luke 12:26 (οὐδὲ δύνασθε = "you cannot
  do"). The note now says this.
- 13:10 διὰ τοῦτο: "This is why" → "This + why", with "is" `t`. "is" is a
  supplied copula. Most reviewed διὰ τοῦτο rows split it off: 1 Corinthians
  4:17, Mark 6:14, Matthew 14:2 and John 16:15 use "That + why", and
  John 6:65, 13:11 and Matthew 13:13 split the two words. Only
  1 Corinthians 11:30 and 1 John 4:5 keep "is" on the phrase. This also
  matches 13:1 in this chapter, where "This is" became τοῦτο = "This" with
  "is" `t`. The author's note cites 1 Corinthians 4:17 as precedent for the
  whole phrase, but that verse actually has "That + why" with "is" `t`.
  One-word connectives (διό, ὅθεν) = "That is why" are a different case and
  stay whole.
- 13:10 χρήσωμαι / κατά: "I will + my use of" / "in" → "I will + my use" /
  "in + of". The author was right to move "my use" off κατά: "use" renders
  χράομαι, and "my" comes from the verb's person ending (John 8:41 ἔχομεν =
  "Our + is"). But nothing required moving "of". In "my use of the
  authority", "of" ties "the authority" to "use", and in the Greek that
  relation is made only by κατὰ τὴν ἐξουσίαν. χράομαι takes a dative and has
  no "of" of its own. So "of" now stays on κατά with "in", as in the
  publisher's row (κατά = "in my use of"), minus the over-claimed "my use".

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 13:10 χρήσωμαι = "I will + my use of" with κατά = "in": partly kept.
  "I#2 will" and "my use" stay on the finite verb. The modal and the person
  word belong to the finite verb, and "my" from the person ending follows
  the John 8:41 pattern, so it does not over-claim. "of" moves back to κατά
  (see Changes). I checked BSB's exact wording: "so that when I am present I
  will not need to be severe in my use of the authority". "need to be" is
  correctly `t`: "need" adds a modal meaning that the Greek lacks, and "to
  be" is a copula for "severe", which BSB makes from the adverb ἀποτόμως.
  παρών = "when + am present" with "I" `t` is correct (participle person
  rule).
- 13:4 εἰς = "to" with "serve" `t`: kept. "serve" adds real lexical content
  (service), so it is a supplied verb. Putting it on εἰς would over-claim.
  13:3 εἰς = "in dealing with" stays as the publisher's row, because "in
  dealing with" works as an English compound preposition ("with regard
  to") and does not add a separate idea. This small difference is listed as
  uncertain below.
- 13:7 δέ = "even if": kept. BSB turns the δέ contrast into a concession.
  Reviewed precedent does the same with δέ: John 7:6 "Although",
  1 Corinthians 15:6 "though", Romans 6:17 "though". The "if" reflects the
  hypothetical subjunctive ὦμεν, which still depends on ἵνα. It does not add
  a new condition word. "even if" is the connective rendered as a
  concession, not a word with a different meaning, so it is not a split pair
  and needs no `s` + `t`. ὡς … ὦμεν = "appear" ("be as") is correct. So are
  ἀδόκιμοι = "to have failed" and δόκιμοι = "to have stood the test", which
  are parallel inside the same verse.
- 13:2 "my" `t` in "in my absence": kept. A participle has no person.
  ἀπών = "in + absence" and "my" `t` follow the rule. The difference from
  Philippians 1:27 is logged here: that older review keeps ἀπών = "in my
  absence" whole. It also puts "I" on the participle ἐλθών ("I come"). Both
  break the current participle-person rule, so they are queued fixes for
  Philippians, not precedent. I did not change Philippians.
- 13:11 "one another" `t` for the middle παρακαλεῖσθε: kept. The Greek has
  no reciprocal pronoun, and a supplied object is `t`. In the reviewed files,
  every BSB "one another" is linked to ἀλλήλων/ἑαυτῶν or to a joined idiom
  that contains the reciprocal idea (Philippians 3:17 συνμιμηταί). None is
  linked to a bare middle verb.

## Other points checked

- 13:1: δύο = "two" (no "of" on the number) and μαρτύρων = "of +
  witnesses" are correct. τοῦτο = "This" with "is" `t` is correct (John
  21:14). The Deuteronomy 19:15 quotation is linked word by word, exactly as
  in Matthew 18:16: ἐπί "by", στόματος "the testimony", δύο "two",
  μαρτύρων "of + witnesses", καί "or" (with note), τριῶν "three",
  σταθήσεται "must be established", πᾶν "Every", ῥῆμα "matter".
- 13:4 is confirmed:
  - The first καὶ γάρ is split: γάρ = "For", καί = "indeed" (Romans 15:3).
    The second καὶ γάρ is joined as "And" (2 Corinthians 5:2).
  - "are" moved from ἡμεῖς to ἀσθενοῦμεν, giving "we" + "are weak".
  - "though" `t` is correct (no concessive word in the Greek).
- Joined units are confirmed: τοῖς προημαρτηκόσιν = "those who sinned
  earlier", ἔλθω εἰς τὸ πάλιν = "I#3 return", the second καὶ γάρ = "And",
  διὰ τοῦτο (joined, now without "is"), and τὸ αὐτό = "one" with φρονεῖτε =
  "be of + mind".
- 13:7 ποιῆσαι = "will + do" is accepted. It is the content of a prayer
  with an accusative subject, and "will" cannot belong to εὐχόμεθα (Acts
  26:29 γενέσθαι = "may become").
- 13:9 εὐχόμεθα = "our prayer is for" is accepted as one rendering of the
  finite verb. The Greek clause has a finite verb, so "is" is not a supplied
  copula in a verbless clause. John 8:41 ἔχομεν = "Our + is" is the
  precedent.
- Readings: only 13:4 has a `partial` record (25/25/25 source words, 0
  unmatched either way), so the difference is form or order only. No
  reading is claimed anywhere, and none should be.
- Verse division: N1904 13:1-11 match BSB verse for verse, so the author's
  verses need no cross-verse links. The KEEP blocks handle the 13:12-14
  split. 13:5, 13:12, 13:13 and 13:14 in the final file are identical to
  `scripts/original-languages/reviewed-bsb/2-corinthians-13.json`, including
  key order.
- No "of/to/in" sits on a lone article or a number. No supplied copula sits
  on a Greek word that lacks one. No person word sits on a participle.
- KEEP blocks (note only, no real error):
  - 13:5 joins ἀδόκιμοί ἐστε as "you + fail the test", while 13:6-7 link
    ἀδόκιμοι alone. That is acceptable.
  - Its long notes spell "Cant" without the apostrophe. This is cosmetic.

## Still uncertain

- 13:3 εἰς = "in dealing with" vs 13:4 εἰς = "to" + "serve" `t`. Both are
  defensible, but they treat the supplied verbal idea differently.
- 13:10 κατά = "in + of": "of" matches κατά's relation to the authority.
  "in" mainly joins "severe" to "my use". Making "in" `t` would be the
  strictest alternative.
- 13:6 ἐσμέν = "have" with ἀδόκιμοι = "failed the test" (finite auxiliary
  for finite copula) is kept. Joining the two, as 13:5 does, would also be
  acceptable.
