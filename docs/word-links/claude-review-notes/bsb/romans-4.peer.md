# BSB Romans 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 25 of 25 verses (4:1-25), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek (N1904), the BSB wording, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the
publisher record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/romans.json`.
There were no KEEP blocks. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 25, "problems": 0}`.

3 changes in 3 verses. The rest of the author's work is confirmed.

## Changes

- 4:10: `g 3 = after#0` + `g 4 5 = his circumcision` →
  `g 3 4 5 = after#0 his circumcision` ("ἐν περιτομῇ ὄντι ("being in
  circumcision", said of Abraham) is rendered after his circumcision.").
  ἐν means "in", not "after". The "after" comes from the whole phrase "in
  the state of circumcision". The other three members of the verse already
  join ἐν with its noun (ἐν ἀκροβυστίᾳ = "before" twice, ἐν περιτομῇ =
  "after"), so now all four are handled the same way. οὐκ stays "not".
- 4:11: `g 21 22 = are not circumcised` → `g 21 22 = not circumcised`,
  plus `t are = English supplies a copula.` "are" is a supplied copula with
  the recast phrase δι’ ἀκροβυστίας. The author already made the same "are"
  `t` in 4:12 ("who not only are circumcised"), and "he was still" is `t`
  earlier in this verse.
- 4:19: `g 12 = since he was` → `g 12 = since + was` (note "The participle
  ὑπάρχων ("being") is rendered since … was."), plus `t he#1 = A participle
  has no person; English supplies the subject.` A participle has no person,
  in the third person too. This follows Romans 1:21-22 (γνόντες,
  φάσκοντες, "they" `t`), and also 4:11 ("he was still" `t`) and 4:18 ("he"
  `t` with εἰρημένον) in this chapter. The finite κατενόησεν keeps "he
  acknowledged".

## Answers to the author's doubts and the review questions

- **4:9 τῷ Ἀβραάμ = "Abraham's + to him": kept.** BSB moves the name to
  the faith ("Abraham's faith") and keeps the dative role as "to him". "to
  him" has the Greek word's own role (the one credited). "Abraham's" is that
  same word, named, in a new role BSB gives it. Both English words refer to
  the one person, so neither over-claims. This is the Acts 17:5 precedent
  (αὐτούς = "Paul and Silas" + "them": "a Greek pronoun moved into a new
  role is linked"). Making either one `t` would leave a word that does
  render τῷ Ἀβραάμ with no Greek behind it. ἐλογίσθη = "was credited"
  without "to him" is right. ἡ `s` is right too, because the possessive
  comes from the name.
- **4:10**: changed (above). "Was it" / "It was" `t`: agree. The question
  and answer are verbless in Greek, and "was" is not "credited" repeated
  (only an auxiliary, not the content word), so the "understood again" rule
  does not apply. ὄντι is now inside the joined phrase. It is not linked to
  "his" alone, because a participle is not a possessive.
- **4:16 ἐπαγγελίαν = "promise + it", "comes" `t`: agree.** The verbless
  Διὰ τοῦτο ἐκ πίστεως clause has the promise as its understood subject.
  BSB names it there and resumes with "it" where the Greek noun stands
  (subject of εἶναι βεβαίαν). The two English words have the same role and
  meaning, so this is a word understood again. **"Abraham's" `t`, τῷ `s`:
  agree.** The article-as-possessive convention covers "his/their/your"
  that the article implies. It cannot supply a name. Ἀβραάμ later in the
  verse is the genitive of "faith of Abraham", which is a different role,
  so it cannot carry "Abraham's" either. εἰς τό = "may#1" is kept. Putting
  "may be" on εἶναι (as 4:11 puts "might be credited" on λογισθῆναι) and
  εἰς τό `s` is equally defensible, but the author's link is not wrong.
- **4:17: confirmed.** The two ὄντα are now right: τὰ … ὄντα = "what +
  does + exist", μή = "not", ὡς ὄντα = "into being", "yet" `t`. Θεοῦ = "God
  + God" is an appositional repeat with the same role and referent. κατέναντι
  = "in the presence of" only. "He is our father" `t`: this is a repeat
  across verses, not a verse-division difference.
- **4:18 ἔσται = "shall + be", σου = "your"; 4:24 μέλλει = "will",
  λογίζεσθαι = "be credited", "righteousness" `t`: confirmed.** The modal
  sits on the finite verb in both.
- **4:23 ὅτι `s`, "the words" `t`: confirmed.** "the words" is a noun BSB
  supplies as subject of "were written". It does not mean "that", so this
  is not a split pair. Same as ὅτι `s` with the colon in 4:17.
- **4:7-8 (Psalm 32): confirmed word by word.** Μακάριοι/μακάριος =
  "Blessed", with "are" / "is" `t` (verbless beatitude). ὧν = "they whose"
  (a headless relative includes its antecedent). ἀφέθησαν = "are forgiven",
  ἐπεκαλύφθησαν = "are covered", καί `s` (comma), both αἱ `s`. οὗ = "whose".
  οὐ μή = "never", λογίσηται = "will + count", ἁμαρτίαν = "sin", Κύριος =
  "the Lord". "against him" `t`: οὗ is already rendered "whose", and the
  extra pronoun has a different role.
- **4:14 οἱ = "those who", ἐκ = "by", "live" `t`: kept.** The article is
  used as a pronoun, so "those who" may sit on it. Galatians 3:7 joined οἱ
  ἐκ as "those who have", but there BSB has no separate preposition. Here
  "by" renders ἐκ directly.
- **4:8 "against him" `t`: agree** (above).

## Readings

None claimed: agree. EVIDENCE marks 4:1 and 4:5 as differing with 0
unmatched and 0 extra words, so the Greek wording is the same. In 4:11 and
4:19 the record shows one publisher Greek word beyond N1904 (30 vs 29, 20 vs
19), and every N1904 word is matched. BSB's English has no word for either
extra word, so nothing is linked or claimed. 4:1 κατὰ σάρκα `s` ("This BSB
wording leaves κατὰ σάρκα untranslated") describes the English shown here,
which has no "according to the flesh". It is a statement about the English,
not a reading claim. No note states the publisher's Greek wording.

## Still uncertain

- 4:16 εἰς τό = "may#1" versus "may be" on εἶναι (see above). Either is
  honest.
- 4:12 ἐν ἀκροβυστίᾳ = "before + was circumcised" keeps "was" in the group,
  while the stative "are" in 4:11-12 is `t`. "before he was circumcised" is
  an event clause BSB builds from the noun, not a copula with an adjective,
  so I left it. A stricter reviewer could make "he was" `t`.
- 4:6 καθάπερ = "likewise", καί = "And" (publisher split) is kept. The καί
  is really "also", which "likewise" partly covers. It is not clearly wrong.
