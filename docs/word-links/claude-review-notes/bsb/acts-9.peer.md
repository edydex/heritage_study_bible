## Changes (5 verses: 3 links, 2 notes)

- 9:39: `g 13 = him#1 + him#2 // αὐτῷ serves both stood around and
  showing; BSB repeats him.` → `g 13 = him#1` and `t him#2 = English
  supplies whom the clothing was shown to; αὐτῷ is the dative of
  παρέστησαν (stood around), and ἐπιδεικνύμεναι has no dative of its own.`
  αὐτῷ is the dative that the intransitive compound παρέστησαν needs ("stood
  beside him"). The participle ἐπιδεικνύμεναι has its own stated objects
  (χιτῶνας καὶ ἱμάτια) and no dative of the person. The middle voice may
  even mean the widows showed clothes they were wearing. The person shown is
  clear from context, but αὐτῷ does not fill that role. This is the
  distinction drawn in the Acts 6:12 peer review: a pronoun is linked twice
  only when it has the same role with both verbs, as αὐτόν does in 9:25
  (object of both λαβόντες and καθῆκαν), which stays `him#0 + him#1`.
- 9:27: `g 25 = Damascus + that city` and `t to#2` → `g 25 = that city //
  Δαμασκῷ is rendered that city, since BSB has already named Damascus with
  the road.` and `t to#2 Damascus = English names where the road led; the
  Greek says only on the road and names Damascus in the later clause (that
  city).` The Greek road clause (πῶς ἐν τῇ ὁδῷ εἶδεν τὸν Κύριον) has no
  Damascus, and Δαμασκῷ is not understood again in it. Δαμασκῷ belongs only
  to the boldness clause, where BSB renders it "that city". The publisher's
  row also linked "that city" to Δαμασκῷ; the error was folding "to
  Damascus" into ὁδῷ. Linking the moved name a second time would show
  Δαμασκῷ as the road's destination, which the Greek clause does not say.
- 9:7: `g 6 = stood there` → `g 6 = stood` and `t there = English adds
  there; εἱστήκεισαν means only stood.` The adverb of place is English-only.
  Reviewed precedent treats the added "there" the same way: Acts 4:14
  ("standing there", `t there`), Luke 5:17, John 18:5, John 19:29.
- 9:37 (note only): `t her body` reason "N1904 has no object for washed" →
  "N1904 has no object for washed and placed". In N1904 neither verb has an
  object, and the extra word in BSB's text would be the object of both
  verbs, not only of λούσαντες.
- 9:12 (note only): `t his#0` reason now reads "N1904 has no article or
  pronoun with hands, and BSB's Greek text has extra words in this verse".
  The old note did not say why "his" has no N1904 word. The publisher record
  links "In a vision" and "his" only to its own extra words (3 publisher
  words not in N1904). The note still does not name those words.

No other row changed. I checked each correction the author made to the
publisher rows and agree with all the others.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 9:5 εἶπεν = asked + replied: **agree, kept**. ὁ δέ Ἐγώ εἰμι … has no verb.
  The verb of saying is understood from the εἶπεν stated in the same verse,
  so "replied" links to it as a word understood again (rule 7). Precedents
  link the repeat even when BSB uses a different English verb: John 18:23
  ἐλάλησα = "I said + I spoke", Acts 3:17 ἐπράξατε = "you acted + did".
  "Saul" stays `t` (a name supplied for a verb ending), and ὁ = He.
- 9:11 "told" `t`, πρὸς αὐτόν = him: **agree, kept**. The verbless ὁ δὲ
  Κύριος πρὸς αὐτόν depends on the εἶπεν of 9:10. A link across verses is
  only for real verse-division differences, so "told" is English-only. The
  same applies in Acts 5:9 (`t replied`). Both verses therefore follow the
  same-verse limit on understood-again links. The publisher's πρός = told
  over-claimed. "him" renders αὐτόν, and πρός belongs with it.
- 9:39 αὐτῷ = him#1 + him#2: **changed** (see above).
- 9:27 Δαμασκῷ = Damascus + that city: **changed** to "that city" only,
  with "to Damascus" `t` (see above).
- 9:21 "hasn’t he come" on ἐληλύθει: **agree, kept**. The contraction
  cannot be split. The negative of the one Οὐχ governs both halves of the
  question, and the note says so.
- 9:22 τοὺς κατοικοῦντας = the + living: **agree, kept**. In N1904 the
  article goes with the participle, and the English "the Jews living" is
  definite because of it. The publisher's "the" points to an extra word in
  its own text, but no reading needs to be claimed: the link uses N1904's
  own article.
- 9:8 εἰσήγαγον = they led, χειραγωγοῦντες = by the hand: **agree, kept**.
  The finite verb gets the finite English verb, and the participle gets the
  manner it adds (χειρ-, "by the hand"). εἰς = into keeps the motion of
  εἰσ-.

## Other points checked

- 9:3 "As" on τε, ἐγένετο `s`, Ἐν = on, τῷ πορεύεσθαι = his journey:
  **agree, kept**. BSB's "As Saul drew near to Damascus on his journey,
  suddenly …" makes the first of the two τε-joined clauses subordinate. By
  the καί → When convention, "As" goes on the connective between the clauses
  (compare Acts 3:9 καί = When). Reviewed Luke uses temporal "As/While" for
  ἐν τῷ + infinitive (Luke 18:35, 24:4, Acts 2:1). Here BSB renders that
  ἐν τῷ πορεύεσθαι as "on his journey", so Ἐν already has its own English
  word. ἐγένετο is `s` as in every reviewed ἐγένετο δέ / καὶ ἐγένετο frame
  (Luke 2:1, 18:35, 24:4, Acts 5:7). This τε is a lone connective, not the τε
  of τε … καί, so the `s` rule for τε … καί does not apply. 9:32 is
  consistent: "As" goes on the participle διερχόμενον.
- 9:14 ὧδε = here, ἔχει = he is + with, "now" `t`: **agree**. The clause has
  a real verb, ἔχει, which BSB recasts as "is … with authority". This is a
  rendering of ἔχει, not a supplied copula. ὧδε is linked, so "now" `t` is
  not a split pair.
- 9:11 ἰδού `s`: **agree**. BSB has no word for it ("for he is praying"), so
  there is no split pair. γάρ = for.
- 9:20 ὅτι `s` + "declaring" `t`: **agree**. "declaring" is a verb of saying
  and does not mean "that". The recitative ὅτι is `s` as in reviewed Luke
  12, Luke 19, John 1 and Acts 3:22. This is a legitimate `s`/`t`, not a
  split pair.
- 9:36 λέγεται = is, διερμηνευομένη = translated as: **agree, kept**. This
  follows John 1:41 (ἐστιν = is, μεθερμηνευόμενον = translated as). The
  finite passive λέγεται carries the finite "is", and the note gives its
  sense ("is called"). Joining both as "is translated as" (like John 1:38's
  joined "means") would also be defensible, but the split is not wrong.
- Readings, checked against each `partial` record (and the words the
  publisher's groups leave unlinked):
  - 9:12: 3 publisher words not in N1904. The publisher leaves "In a
    vision" and "his" unlinked to N1904, so `t` with a reading reason is
    right for both.
  - 9:37: 1 extra word, and the publisher links every English word. "her
    body" is therefore tied to that extra word, and the reading `t` is right.
  - 9:30, 9:31, 9:36, 9:40: one N1904 word is unmatched and one publisher
    word is extra. In each verse the unmatched N1904 word is Καισάριαν,
    Σαμαρίας or Ταβειθά, with "Caesarea", "Samaria" or "Tabitha" unlinked by
    the publisher. That confirms the "Same name, spelled differently in
    BSB's Greek text" links.
  - 9:15, 9:22, 9:27 (one extra word) and 9:38 (0/0): rightly no reading
    claimed.
  - No note states the publisher's exact Greek.
- Brief rules: no lone article carries a preposition. Supplied copulas are
  `t` (9:10 "am"). Supplied names for verb endings are `t` (9:5, 9:20,
  9:26, 9:27, 9:28, 9:34, 9:41, 9:43). Pronouns rendered by a name are
  linked (9:3 and 9:17 αὐτόν, 9:7 αὐτῷ, 9:18 αὐτοῦ, 9:22 οὗτος = Jesus).
  The τε of τε … καί is `s` (9:2, 9:15 first τε, 9:24, 9:29). 9:24 "city
  gates" on πύλας matches Hebrews 13:12 ("city gate" on πύλης).

## Still uncertain

- 9:11 ἐπί = to, ἐν = on (publisher rows, kept). BSB makes the house the
  destination and puts it "on" the street, so each preposition's English
  word now has the other noun as its object. The alternative (ἐπί = on, "to"
  `t`, ἐν `s`) is no cleaner, so I kept the rows with their notes.
- 9:36 ἐποίει = always and ὧν `s` (publisher row, kept). "always" carries
  the habitual imperfect of ὧν ἐποίει rather than the word "do". A strict
  reviewer could make ἐποίει `s` and "always" `t`.
- 9:32 πρός = "to visit": the English "to" is an infinitive marker, but
  the phrase as a whole renders going to the saints. Kept.
