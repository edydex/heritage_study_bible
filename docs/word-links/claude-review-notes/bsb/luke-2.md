# BSB Luke 2: author review (Claude)

Verses reviewed: 52 (2:1-52). No verse was marked KEEP, so none was kept
unchanged from an earlier review. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 52, "problems": 0}`. Luke 2 has no Synoptic parallel; Mark and
Matthew reviews were followed for the shared conventions (narrative ἐγένετο,
articular infinitives, negatives, supplied subjects and objects).

135 of the 689 publisher `g` rows changed, in 41 verses. 30 of them only
gained a Greek token that the draft had left unowned, with the English
unchanged (for example ἐκ joining ἀπό in "from", 2:4; ἥτις with καλεῖται =
"called", 2:4; φόβον μέγαν with ἐφοβήθησαν = "they were terrified", 2:9;
πρὶν ἢ ἄν = "before", 2:26; ὅπως ἄν = "so that", 2:35; ὀγδοήκοντα τεσσάρων =
"eighty-four", 2:37). The other 105 were corrected, split or joined.

## Publisher rows corrected

"When/While/After" on the wrong word:

- 2:6: Ἐγένετο carried "While". ἐν τῷ (+ infinitive) now = While; Ἐγένετο δέ
  is `s` (narrative frame).
- 2:15: ἐγένετο carried "When". ὡς now = When; καὶ ἐγένετο is `s`.
- 2:17: δέ carried "After". The participle ἰδόντες now = After they had seen;
  δέ is `s`.
- 2:43, 2:45, 2:48: the opening καί carried "When". It now goes to the
  genitive absolute τελειωσάντων (2:43) or the participles εὑρόντες (2:45) and
  ἰδόντες (2:48); καί is `s`.
- 2:2: ἐγένετο carried "to take place while". "while" now goes to the genitive
  absolute ἡγεμονεύοντος (while + was governor); ἐγένετο = was + to take
  place.

Negatives split off the verb:

- 2:26: μή = "that he would not" → not; ἰδεῖν = that he would + see.
- 2:37: ἀφίστατο = "never left" → οὐκ = never, ἀφίστατο = left.
- 2:45: εὑρόντες = "they could not find Him" → μή = not; εὑρόντες = When +
  they could + find.
- 2:49: ᾔδειτε = "Did you not know" → οὐκ = not, ᾔδειτε = Did you + know.
- 2:50: συνῆκαν = "did not understand" → οὐ = not, συνῆκαν = did + understand.
- 2:43: ἔγνωσαν = "were unaware He had stayed" → οὐκ ἔγνωσαν = were unaware
  (the negative is inside "unaware").

English-only words that a Greek word had claimed (now `t`):

- Subjects: "He went there" (2:5), "His parents" (2:22), "the angel" (2:20,
  passive ἐλαλήθη), "Jesus’ parents" (2:39), "His parents" (2:48).
- Objects: "it" (2:18), "the Child" after "seen" (2:17), "Him" (2:21 given Him,
  2:22 present Him, 2:45 find Him), "He had stayed" (2:43).
- Copulas and framing: "will be" (2:12), "was" (2:25 righteous, 2:37 a widow),
  "which was" (2:20), "named" (2:36), "then" and "but" (2:37), "and" joining
  the participle οὔσῃ (2:5), "in" in "Nazareth in Galilee" (2:4).

Explicit pronouns that a neighbouring word had absorbed:

- 2:5: αὐτῷ = to him (was inside "pledged to him in marriage").
- 2:6: αὐτούς = they (was inside εἶναι "they were").
- 2:7: αὐτόν = Him#0 (was inside "She wrapped Him in swaddling cloths").
- 2:10: ὑμῖν = you (was inside "I bring you good news").
- 2:17: αὐτοῖς = they#2; the passive τοῦ λαληθέντος = had received.
- 2:46: αὐτούς = them#1 (was inside "asking them questions").

Articles or adjectives carrying a preposition (rule 5):

- 2:1 τὴν "of the"; 2:22 τῷ "to the"; 2:23 τῷ "to the"; 2:27 τοῦ "under the";
  2:41 τῇ "for the" and τοῦ "of the"; 2:42 τῆς "of the": each article now
  takes only "the", and the preposition goes to the noun (or, in 2:1, to the
  infinitive "that a census should be taken of").
- 2:2 τῆς "of" → `s`; "of Syria" = Συρίας. 2:22 τοῦ "of" → `s`; "of
  purification" = καθαρισμοῦ. 2:6 τοῦ "for" joined with τεκεῖν.
- 2:10 μεγάλην "of great" → great; "of" goes to χαράν. 2:13 οὐρανίου "of the
  heavenly" → heavenly; "of the" goes to στρατιᾶς. 2:32 σου "to Your" →
  Your; "to" goes to λαοῦ. 2:36 ἑπτά "for seven" → seven; "for" goes to ἔτη.
  2:44 αὐτόν "for Him" → Him; "for" goes to the verb (as 2:45). 2:48 σε "for
  You" → You; "for" goes to the verb.
- 2:49: the article τοῖς carried the noun "house". ἐν τοῖς now = in + house
  (ἐν τοῖς τοῦ Πατρός μου, "in the things of My Father").

Other corrections:

- 2:2: αὕτη "This was the" → This; πρώτη = the first.
- 2:4: ἀπό + ἐκ = from (BSB's one "from"); Γαλιλαίας = Galilee; πόλεως `s`.
- 2:5: οὔσῃ "and was expecting" / ἐγκύῳ "a child" → οὔσῃ = was, ἐγκύῳ =
  expecting a child.
- 2:15: εἰς "and gone into" → into; ἀπῆλθον = had left + gone (one verb,
  rendered twice), ἀπ’ αὐτῶν = them, "and" `t`.
- 2:21: τὸ ὄνομα (of "his name was called") had been linked to "the name" in
  the apposition. Now ἐκλήθη τὸ ὄνομα = was named, αὐτοῦ = He, and the neuter
  τὸ κληθέν = the name + had given.
- 2:25: ἄνθρωπος (11) carried "who". ὁ ἄνθρωπος οὗτος = who; καί `s`.
- 2:29: ἀπολύεις "You now dismiss" → You + dismiss; Νῦν = now.
- 2:35: καί "and" / δέ "as well" swapped: in καὶ … δέ, δέ joins the clause
  ("and") and καί means "also" ("as well").
- 2:37: ἕως "to the age of" → to; ἐτῶν = the age of.
- 2:38: αὐτῇ carried "she". αὐτῇ τῇ (ὥρᾳ) = at that (moment); ἀνθωμολογεῖτο
  = she gave thanks.
- 2:44: ὁδόν carried "they traveled on". ἦλθον = they traveled on; ἡμέρας
  ὁδόν = for a day.
- 2:47: ταῖς carried His#1. The one αὐτοῦ now = His + His; both articles `s`.
- Article + participle joined (rule 3): 2:5 τῇ ἐμνηστευμένῃ, 2:15 τὸ γεγονός,
  2:18 οἱ ἀκούσαντες and τῶν λαληθέντων, 2:33 τοῖς λαλουμένοις, 2:38 τοῖς
  προσδεχομένοις, 2:47 οἱ ἀκούοντες.

## Notable decisions

- Narrative ἐγένετο with no English counterpart is `s` (2:1, 2:6, 2:15,
  2:46). In 2:46 the publisher's καί = "Finally" is kept.
- Different readings, only where EVIDENCE allows. The publisher Greek is not
  in the checkout, so the words were inferred from the EVIDENCE counts and the
  BSB English:
  - 2:19: συνβάλλουσα = pondered, noting the publisher's spelling συμβάλλουσα
    (1 unmatched / 1 extra word; same verb).
  - 2:48: ζητοῦμεν = have been + searching for, noting the publisher's
    imperfect ἐζητοῦμεν (same verb, same meaning).
  - 2:51: "these" is `t`: BSB follows a reading with ταῦτα after ῥήματα
    (1 extra publisher word), which N1904 lacks.
  - 2:12: the extra publisher word (probably τό before σημεῖον) changes
    nothing; "a sign" = σημεῖον, and "will be" is `t`.
  - 2:21: EVIDENCE says "differs" with 0 Greek words unmatched or extra; the
    difference is on the English side only, so no reading is claimed. ἐν τῇ
    κοιλίᾳ ("in the womb") is `s` as a translation choice.
- Kept publisher connectives: καί = "Just then" (2:9), "But" (2:10, 2:50),
  "So" (2:16), "Then" (2:34, 2:51), "before" (2:44, with a note).
- Kept publisher renderings with a note where useful: οὗτος = this Child
  (2:34), εὐδοκίας = on whom His favor rests (2:14), αὐτός = Simeon (2:28),
  αὐτοῦ = the Child's (2:33) and the Child (2:38), ἐν (τῷ Πνεύματι) = Led by
  (2:27).
- 2:8: ἐν τῇ χώρᾳ τῇ αὐτῇ ("in the same region") = nearby, as one group;
  "residing in the fields" stays with ἀγραυλοῦντες.
- 2:36: ἀπὸ τῆς παρθενίας αὐτῆς ("from her virginity") is `s`; BSB leaves it
  untranslated.

## Doubts for the peer

- 2:43: ἐν τῷ (+ ὑποστρέφειν) is linked to "and", since BSB folds this
  second time clause into "When … and they were returning". The alternative is
  `s` for ἐν τῷ and `t` for "and".
- 2:17: τοῦ παιδίου τούτου = "Him" and "the Child" (object of "seen") is `t`.
  One could instead link παιδίου to "the Child" and make "Him" `t`.
- 2:25: ὁ ἄνθρωπος οὗτος ("this man") = "who", a referential rather than
  lexical rendering, kept because the publisher already linked ἄνθρωπος to
  "who".
- 2:21: τὸ κληθέν = "the name … had given" assumes the neuter refers back to
  ὄνομα; check whether ὄνομα should instead link to "name" in the apposition.
- 2:2: ἐγένετο = "was" + "to take place"; "was" could be a supplied copula
  (`t`) with ἐγένετο = "to take place" only.
