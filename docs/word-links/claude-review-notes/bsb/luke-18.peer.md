# BSB Luke 18: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 43 of 43 verses (18:1-43; no verse was kept from an earlier
review). I checked every `g`, `s` and `t` line against N1904, the displayed
BSB, the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/luke.json`, the publisher rows
in the draft, and the reviewed parallels Mark 10:13-34, 10:46-52, Matthew
19:13-30, 20:17-19 and 20:29-34 (plus Luke 15:26 and the "home" precedents
in Luke 1, 5, 8 and Mark 5, 7, 8). Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 43, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes

One link change and two note fixes. The rest of the author's file is
unchanged; a round trip of the final JSON differs from the author's file
only in these lines.

- 18:16: τῶν "to#2" → `s` (article); τοιούτων "such as these" → "to#2 such
  as these", with a note on the possessive genitive. Why: a lone article
  may not carry a preposition or case word (brief rule; the case word goes
  on the article only with indeclinable names). The author already applied
  this rule in 18:31-33 ("about + Son", "to + Gentiles", "on + day") and
  18:43 ("to God"), so 18:16 was the one outlier in the chapter. Mark 10:14
  and Matthew 19:14 still put "to" on τῶν; the reviewed Luke 2-15, 19 and
  21 files have no lone article carrying a case word.
- 18:5 note on ὑπωπιάζῃ: "is rendered "will ... wearing ... out" after
  "stop"" → "(literally "strike under the eye") means "wear out"; "she will"
  goes with this verb and "stop" with μή." Why: the old note left out "she",
  which is part of the group, and did not say where "stop" belongs.
- 18:7 note on οὐ μή: ""Will ... not"" → "The emphatic negative οὐ μή, in
  a question expecting the answer yes, is rendered "not"; "Will" goes with
  the verb." Why: the group is "not" only; "Will" is linked to ποιήσῃ, so
  the old note quoted a phrase the group does not hold.

## Reading claims

The publisher record has a `partial` entry for 18:5, 7, 11, 12, 21, 24, 30,
35, 40 and 43. I checked each claim against its counts.

- 18:12 (8 / 7 / 8 Greek words): one N1904 word unmatched and one publisher
  word not in N1904. The publisher's "pay tithes" did not transfer while
  every other English word did, so the difference sits in the verb slot.
  The same-meaning link with "BSB's Greek has another form of this verb" is
  right (rule 9, first bullet).
- 18:21 (8 / 8 / 9): one extra publisher word and the only untransferred
  English word is "my". `t my` with the reading reason is right and states
  no publisher spelling.
- 18:24 (18 / 18 / 20): two extra publisher words; the only untransferred
  English word is "sadness". `t sadness` with the reading reason is right.
  "the man's" stays on αὐτόν: BSB recasts "him … sad" as "the man's
  sadness", and αὐτόν is still the person seen.
- 18:30 (17 / 16 / 17): one word differs either way. "will fail to
  receive" did not transfer as a whole, which fits one publisher group
  holding the differing word. The verb slot is the likely place (modern
  critical texts have a compound of the verb here), and "will + receive"
  renders either form, so the same-meaning note on λάβῃ is right. If the
  differing word were οὐχί instead, "fail to" would still render it, so no
  link would change.
- 18:35: Ἱερειχώ = Jericho with "Same name, spelled differently in BSB's
  Greek text" is right; "Jericho" is the only untransferred English word.
- 18:5, 7, 11, 40, 43: Greek counts are complete (0 unmatched either way);
  only the English counts differ. No reading may be claimed, and none is.
  In 18:11 the word order that BSB's English follows ("stood by himself")
  is consistent with that; πρός = by and ἑαυτόν = himself are right.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 18:5: agree with all five links.
  - ἐρχομένη = "with her + requests": the participle ("coming", her
    repeated visits) is what BSB recasts as "with her … requests", and the
    note says so. Making "requests" `t` would leave "with her" on
    ἐρχομένη, which reads worse and still is not literal. Not an
    over-claim: "requests" renders the visits, nothing else in the verse.
  - εἰς τέλος = "perpetual": BSB takes εἰς τέλος as "continually"; joined
    idiom, right.
  - μή = "stop": μή with the present subjunctive negates continuing action;
    "stop" is exactly that negative. Same principle as the negatives split
    off elsewhere.
  - ἵνα = "Then": BSB turns the ἵνα clause into a new sentence and "Then"
    carries the purpose/result link. It is not an English-only word, so `s`
    ἵνα + `t Then` would be a split pair.
  - ὑπωπιάζῃ = "she will + wearing + out": right; only the note changed.
- 18:6: agree. τί … λέγει ("what … says") is what BSB turns into "the words
  of"; "of" marks the judge as the speaker, i.e. the subject of λέγει. The
  alternative (`t of`) would hide that. The article ὁ correctly keeps only
  "the#2".
- 18:7: agree. μακροθυμεῖ = "Will He continue to defer" (one verb, the
  present aspect in "continue"); ἐπ’ αὐτοῖς ("over them") = "their" is the
  people concerned; "help" has no Greek counterpart and is rightly `t`.
  "for" on ἐκλεκτῶν (the objective genitive) matches "of Man" on ἀνθρώπου.
- 18:14: agree. εἰς τὸν οἶκον αὐτοῦ = "home" (four tokens) matches the
  established precedent: Luke 1:23, 1:56 and 8:39 and Mark 5:19 join all
  four tokens; Luke 15:6 joins three. "home" means "to his own house", so
  it renders the whole phrase.
- 18:31-33: agree; see the 18:16 change, which brings the last outlier in
  line.
- 18:12 and 18:30: agree; see "Reading claims".
- 18:36: agree. εἴη τοῦτο = "was happening" matches Luke 15:26 (ἂν εἴη
  ταῦτα = "was going on"). The publisher's split put "happening" on τοῦτο
  ("this"), which it does not mean.

## Other points checked

- 18:30 οὐχὶ μή = "fail to" with λάβῃ = "will + receive": right and matches
  Mark 10:30 (ἐὰν μή = "fail to", λάβῃ = "will + receive"). The second ὅς
  is rightly `s` because BSB makes "no one who has left" (18:29) the
  subject.
- 18:15-17, 18-30, 31-34, 35-43 agree with the reviewed parallels wherever
  the Greek and English match (18:17 = Mark 10:15; 18:19-20 = Mark
  10:18-19; 18:24 = Mark 10:23; 18:41 = Mark 10:51). Luke's own
  differences (μαστιγώσαντες sharing "They will"; διά `s` in 18:31; ἵνα
  σιγήσῃ joined in 18:39 as in Matthew 20:31) are handled correctly.
- No split pairs, no lone article carrying is/was or a preposition (after
  the 18:16 fix), and no note states the publisher's spelling.

## Still uncertain

- 18:3: ἤρχετο = "who kept appealing" with λέγουσα `s`. "appealing" blends
  the repeated coming with the plea that λέγουσα introduces, so ἤρχετο +
  λέγουσα joined would also be defensible. The author's link with its note
  is accurate, so I left it.
- 18:25: "easier for" on εὐκοπώτερον (publisher, as Matthew 19:24) against
  Mark 10:25's "easier" + "for a camel". Both are accurate; not changed.
- 18:16: Mark 10:14 and Matthew 19:14 still put "to" on τῶν. If the series
  harmonizes those verses, they should follow the brief rule used here.
