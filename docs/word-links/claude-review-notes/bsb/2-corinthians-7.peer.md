# BSB 2 Corinthians 7: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 16 of 16 verses (7:1-16). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording, and
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft and the
publisher record in `bsb-word-links/2-corinthians.json`. No KEEP blocks in
this chapter. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 16, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 8, in 7 verses. Link changes in 5 verses (7:1, 7:9, 7:10, 7:11,
7:14). 7:3 and 7:8 only reword a note. The other 9 verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 7:11: each ἀλλά = "what" → `s`. πόσην = "what#0 + what#1" → πόσην =
  all eight "what"s. The "what" before each item renders πόσην ("how
  much"), which the Greek states once and BSB repeats in each parallel
  phrase. This is the rule for words understood again (John 15:2, Romans
  6:16 δοῦλοι). The ascensive ἀλλά ("indeed, yes", KJV "yea") has no
  English in BSB, which joins the list with commas. An exclamatory "what"
  does not mean "but/indeed", so this is not a split pair. The whole list
  is treated the same way, and no English word has two owners. πόσην also
  keeps the first "what" ("Consider what … has produced"). The Greek
  clause is an indirect exclamation after ἰδού ("see how much it
  produced"), and BSB's opening "what" stands for that same word.
- 7:1: μολυσμοῦ = "that defiles" → "defiles"; "that" `t`. The brief makes
  an added "that" `t`, and the reviewed chapters do this for a relative
  clause that English builds from a noun or phrase (Romans 5:14
  παραβάσεως, "that" `t`; 2 Corinthians 2:16 and 1 Corinthians 16:19,
  "that" `t`).
- 7:9: κατά = "that + had intended" → "had intended"; "that" `t`, for the
  same reason. Θεόν = "God" is unchanged. The note now says that κατὰ
  Θεόν is recast as "God had intended".
- 7:10: εἰς = "that leads to" (publisher row) → "leads to"; "that" `t`.
  Same rule. Romans 6:16 and 6:22 also link "leading to"/"leads to" on
  εἰς. Note only: the κόσμου note named τοῦ κόσμου although τοῦ is `s`.
  It now names κόσμου alone.
- 7:14: εἴ τι = "by anything" → "anything"; "by" `t`. τι is the
  accusative object of κεκαύχημαι ("I have boasted anything"). The "by"
  only joins that object to "embarrassed" and renders no Greek word or
  case. The publisher had already put "by" on τι.
- 7:3 (note only): the "you#2" note said "whom they live and die with",
  but the English subject is "we". It now reads: English supplies the
  object of with; the Greek verbs ("die with", "live with") name no one.
- 7:8 (note only): the "now" note said "the Greek has only βλέπω".
  EVIDENCE shows that BSB's Greek text has one word more than N1904 in this
  verse. The draft also left both βλέπω and "I now see" without an owner,
  so the publisher's row for "I now see" contained a word N1904 lacks. The
  note now says that N1904 has only βλέπω here, though BSB's Greek text has
  an extra word in this verse. It claims no link and names no word. (That
  word is probably the γάρ some editions print after βλέπω, but this is not
  stated in the review file.)

## Author doubts and caller questions

- 7:11 (author's hardest doubt): settled as above. The repeated "what"s
  belong to πόσην, and every ἀλλά is `s`. ἰδού = "Consider" is kept, not
  `s`. "Consider" renders ἰδού's call to look ("see, behold"); it is not
  an English addition. αὐτό `s` with τοῦτο = "this" is right ("this very
  thing" → "this"). τῷ = "this" with πράγματι = "in + matter" is right
  (1 Corinthians 5:10, 11:26), and no lone article carries "in".
  ἀπολογίαν = "eagerness to clear yourselves" stays one rendering of the
  noun.
- 7:4: confirmed. ὑπερπερισσεύομαι ("I overflow") = "my + overflows" and
  τῇ `s`. The first person of the verb becomes the possessor when BSB
  makes "joy" the subject, as with John 8:41 ἔχομεν = "Our + is". The
  τῇ of τῇ χαρᾷ is the ordinary dative article ("with the joy"), parallel
  to τῇ παρακλήσει. It is not a possessive article like the one in 7:8.
- 7:8: confirmed. ἐλύπησεν = "caused#1 + sorrow#1" and ὑμᾶς = "you#1"
  (the publisher had ἐκείνη = "caused", which was wrong). ἐκείνη ("that")
  is `s`, because "my" does not mean "that". The article ἡ = "my#1"
  matches τῇ = "my#0" in the same verse. "it#0", "it#1" and "now" are `t`.
  εἰ καί = "Although" and εἰ καί = "but only" as concessive units are
  acceptable. The first pair stays εἰ = "if", καί = "Even", because BSB
  has a word for each.
- 7:9: confirmed. Θεόν = "God". μηδενί = "not#1 + any way", ἐν = "in",
  ζημιωθῆτε = "were#1 + harmed" (the negative is in μηδενί). εἰς = "led +
  to" is kept, with "you#1" `t` (supplied object). This matches εἰς =
  "leads to" in 7:10 after the change above. "And" `t` is right: EVIDENCE
  shows no extra publisher word in 7:9 (order or form only).
- 7:15: ὡς = "as" confirmed, with "that" and "were" `t`. The BSB English
  in the record reads "when he remembers that you were all obedient as you
  welcomed him with fear and trembling". (The wording "as he remembers …,
  receiving him …" is not the text of this record.) BSB's "that" begins the
  clause that recasts the noun phrase τὴν πάντων ὑμῶν ὑπακοήν ("the
  obedience of you all"). The ὡς clause ("how you received him") is BSB's
  "as you welcomed him". So ὡς belongs on "as", and the publisher's ὡς =
  "that" took the complementizer of the noun recast.
- Participle subjects: confirmed `t` in 7:1 ("we", ἔχοντες), 7:5 ("we#1",
  θλιβόμενοι), 7:7 ("He", ἀναγγέλλων) and 7:15 ("he",
  ἀναμιμνῃσκομένου). "When" sits on the participle in 7:5 (genitive
  absolute ἐλθόντων = "when + arrived", with ἡμῶν = "we#0") and in 7:15.
  καὶ γάρ = "For" in 7:5 is right. The publisher's Καί = "when" took the
  participle's "when".
- 7:5: ἐν παντί = "from every direction" joined is kept. The publisher
  had ἐν = "from", but ἐν does not mean "from", and BSB renders the idiom
  as a whole. In 7:11, ἐν = "In" stays split because the English keeps
  "In".
- 7:3: confirmed. The spelling note on συνζῆν = "live" is supported.
  EVIDENCE shows 1 unmatched N1904 word and 1 publisher word not in N1904.
  The draft left only συνζῆν and "we live" (among content words) without
  an owner, so the publisher's "we live" row was the one with the
  unmatched word. The note does not state the publisher's spelling. "we"
  (infinitives have no person), "this" and "you#0" (supplied objects) are
  `t`, and εἰς τό = "so + that#1" follows 2 Corinthians 1:4.
- 7:14: confirmed, no reading. Every English word is covered by a
  surviving publisher row, so the extra publisher word had no English.
- No note names or spells the publisher's Greek. The only reading notes
  are 7:3 (same verb, spelled differently) and 7:8 (extra word, no link).

## Still uncertain

- 7:11: one Greek word (πόσην) now carries eight English words. This is
  correct under the rule for words understood again, but the registry
  reviewer may prefer the first "what" ("Consider what") `t` as a
  supplied object. I kept it on πόσην because BSB's indirect exclamation
  takes the place of πόσην's clause.
- 7:7: ᾗ `s` (BSB's contact clause "the comfort he had received") is
  kept. Joining ᾗ with παρεκλήθη = "he had received" would also be
  defensible.
