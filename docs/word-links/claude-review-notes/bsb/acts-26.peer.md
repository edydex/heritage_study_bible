# BSB Acts 26: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 32 of 32 verses (26:1-32), every `g`, `s` and `t` line, against
the Greek, the BSB English, each verse's EVIDENCE line and the publisher
record in `public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json`.
Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 32, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 5 verses (26:4, 26:5, 26:11, 26:12 links; 26:9 one note). The
other 27 verses are kept exactly as the author left them.

## Changes

- 26:4 μὲν οὖν: before οὖν = "Surely" with μέν `s` → after μὲν οὖν joined
  = "Surely" ("The particles μὲν οὖν ('so then, indeed') are rendered
  together as Surely"). The "So"/"So then" precedents (14:3, 19:38, 26:9)
  link οὖν because "so" is what οὖν means. "Surely" is not an inferential
  word. It is an assertion, and that force comes from μέν ("indeed") at
  least as much as from οὖν. So the particle pair owns it as one unit
  (rule 3), not οὖν alone.
- 26:5 "They": before προγινώσκοντες = "have known" with "They" `t` →
  after προγινώσκοντες = "They have known" (the publisher row). The person
  rule covers I/we/you only. A third-person subject pronoun on a participle
  rendered as a finite verb is the corpus convention (Acts 16 peer: 8:27,
  16:3, 16:19, 16:20, 16:30; Acts 23 peer: 23:16, 23:18, 23:33). The Acts
  23:27-30 rule that the author cited applies to the first person, and that
  note itself keeps third-person pronouns on participles. 26:7 "as they …
  serve" and 26:31 "On their way out" already follow the convention.
- 26:11 ἕως: before ἕως = "went", εἰς = "to#1" → after ἕως … εἰς joined =
  "to#1" ("ἕως … εἰς ('as far as to') is rendered to") and `t went`
  ("English supplies a verb of motion; ἕως ('as far as') marks only how far
  the persecuting reached"). "went" is a verb of motion. It does not mean
  "as far as", so it cannot carry ἕως (the brief's ὅταν/"and" case). ἕως
  εἰς is one compound preposition of extent, and BSB's "to foreign cities"
  renders it. The ἐδίωκον note drops "went" ("rendered I … to persecute").
  καί stays = "even".
- 26:12 "my": before πορευόμενος = "was on my way" → after = "was on +
  way", with `t my` ("English supplies my in on my way; πορευόμενος is a
  participle and has no person"). The author already made the "I" of this
  same participle `t`, and made the "my" of the participle ἐμμαινόμενος in
  26:11 `t`. The first-person "my" of the idiom needs the same treatment
  (as Acts 22:20 peer: "my" on the participle συνευδοκῶν is `t`). In 22:5
  and John 5:7, "on my way" stays whole because there it sits on a finite
  first-person verb.
- 26:9 note on `t too` only: before "English adds too; the Greek has no
  word for also here" → after "English adds too to convey the emphatic
  ἐγώ ('I myself'); the Greek has no word for also". Links unchanged (see
  below).

## Points raised and the author's doubts

- **26:11 ἕως = "went"**: changed (above).
- **26:30 τε = "along with"**: confirmed, kept. BSB's English is "Then the
  king and the governor rose, along with Bernice and those seated with
  them". καί (10) already owns "and#1". "along with" is a real additive
  connective at the exact point where ἥ τε Βερνίκη attaches the second pair
  to the first. Only τε can own it, and it means what τε does here
  ("and"). This is not the plain τε … καί = "X and Y" case, where τε has
  no English word. Making it `s` + `t` would create a split pair.
- **26:9 ἔδοξα ἐμαυτῷ / "too" / "I ought"**: confirmed, kept. ἔδοξα
  ἐμαυτῷ ("I thought to myself") is one expression of opinion, and "was
  convinced" renders it whole. ἐμαυτῷ means "to myself", not "too", so the
  publisher row was wrong. "I too" expresses the emphasis of ἐγώ (contrast
  with the hearers of 26:8). But "too" ("also") is not the meaning of any
  Greek word here, so `t` with the clarified note is more honest than
  "I#0 too" on ἐγώ. ἐγώ = I#0 stays. "I#1" before "ought" stays `t`: δεῖν
  is impersonal ("it was necessary") and carries no supplied subject
  (Acts 5:29, 20:35). "I#2 could" stays `t`.
- **26:2 μέλλων**: decided for the author's `s` + `t to#0 stand`.
  "to stand" is a verb BSB supplies for ἐπὶ σοῦ ("before you"). It does
  not mean "be about to", so the publisher row was an over-claim. When BSB
  does render μέλλω in this chapter, it has its own word "would" (26:22
  μελλόντων, 26:23 μέλλει). Here BSB has no word for it, and joining it
  into "to defend myself" would hide that. The joined form (as 1 Peter
  5:1) remains a tolerable alternative.
- **26:23 second εἰ `s`, "and#0" `t`**: confirmed. εἰ ("that, whether")
  does not mean "and". BSB states "that" once for both clauses and
  coordinates with "and", which no Greek word supplies.
- **26:26 λανθάνειν = "has escaped + notice", οὐ … οὐθέν = "none"**:
  confirmed. λανθάνω means "escape the notice of", so "notice" belongs to
  the verb, and αὐτόν = "his" is the object recast as a possessive. In οὐ
  πείθομαι … οὐθέν, the negative on πείθομαι and the οὐθέν together become
  BSB's positive "I am confident that none". Joining them is right, and
  πείθομαι = "I am confident" is not over-claimed.
- **Supplied participle subjects** (checked one by one): 26:5 "They" is
  changed (third person, above). 26:11 "I#0" (τιμωρῶν) and "my"
  (ἐμμαινόμενος): confirmed `t`, and the finite ἠνάγκαζον and ἐδίωκον keep
  their own "I". 26:12 "I" (πορευόμενος): confirmed `t`, and "my" now also
  `t`. 26:13 "as I was": confirmed `t`. κατὰ τὴν ὁδόν has no verb, and
  κατά = "on". 26:17 "I#0" (ἐξαιρούμενος): confirmed `t`. 26:22 "I#0"
  (τυχών) and "I#2" (λέγων): confirmed `t`, and ἕστηκα keeps "I#1 stand".
- **Shared objects**: 26:11 αὐτούς = them#0 + them#1 is confirmed. One
  accusative is the object of the participle τιμωρῶν and of the main verb
  ἠνάγκαζον in one clause (16:3, 9:25 pattern). them#2 is αὐτοῖς ("against
  them"). them#3 stays `t`, because ἐδίωκον has no object and the dative
  αὐτοῖς belongs to ἐμμαινόμενος (the 16:39 peer distinction). 26:21 με =
  me#0 + me#1 is confirmed. The one accusative serves the participle
  συλλαβόμενοι and the infinitive διαχειρίσασθαι under ἐπειρῶντο in one
  clause.
- **Damascus road 26:13-16 against reviewed 9:3-6 and 22:6-9**:
  consistent. φῶς = "a light" (9:3). "from heaven" = οὐρανόθεν (one word
  here). με = "me". τοὺς σὺν ἐμοὶ πορευομένους = "companions" with ἐμοί =
  "my" (22:9 οἱ σὺν ἐμοὶ ὄντες). εἰς / τήν / γῆν = to / the / ground (9:4).
  φωνήν = "a voice", λέγουσαν = "say". τῇ `s` with Ἑβραΐδι διαλέκτῳ = "in
  Hebrew" (22:2). Σαούλ = Saul#0 / Saul#1, τί = "why", με = "Me", διώκεις =
  "do you persecute" (9:4, 22:7). 26:15 matches 22:8: ἐγώ = I#0, εἶπα =
  asked, εἶ = "are You", Κύριε = Lord, Ἐγώ = I, εἰμι = am, ὅν = whom, σύ =
  you, διώκεις = "are persecuting", δέ `s`. ὁ = "the" with Κύριος = "Lord"
  differs from 22:10 only because BSB writes "the Lord" here and "He"
  there. 26:16 ἀνάστηθι = "get up" (9:6). 26:13 "About" `t` is right,
  because 22:6 has περί and this verse does not.
- **Readings**: confirmed none claimed. EVIDENCE shows differences in
  26:4, 26:21, 26:26 and 26:31 (publisher words not in N1904, none
  unmatched) and in 26:7 (0/0, order or spelling only). In each, the
  English is a normal rendering of the N1904 words. 26:4 and 26:21 "the
  Jews" sit on Ἰουδαῖοι like the anarthrous "the Jews" in 26:2 and 26:7.
- **26:13 "About"**: `t` confirmed (above).
- **26:24 ταῦτα = "At this stage", αὐτοῦ = "of Paul's", ἀπολογουμένου =
  "defense"**: kept. It is a recast genitive absolute. Each English piece
  sits on the Greek word it comes from, and αὐτοῦ is a pronoun BSB turns
  into the name (linked, not a supplied name).
- **26:16 με = "from Me"**: kept. EVIDENCE shows BSB's Greek matches N1904
  here, so με is present. BSB recasts the object "me" as "from Me", which
  is the pronoun in a new role (as 26:4 μου = "I").

## Other checks (no change)

- Lone articles: none carries of/to/in (26:6, 26:10, 26:12, 26:18, 26:19,
  26:20, 26:23, 26:32 all moved by the author). τοῖς = "to#0 those" in
  26:20 is a substantival article with no noun (as Matthew 25:34, Galatians
  3:22). It is not a lone article of a noun. 26:12 τῆς (attributive article
  after the anarthrous ἐξουσίας καὶ ἐπιτροπῆς) = "the" before authority is
  accepted.
- Participle "and" `t`: 26:1, 26:5, 26:11, 26:21 and 26:22 have no καί at
  the joint. 26:14's "and" joins the genitive absolute (12:13 pattern), with
  τε `s`.
- Supplied words `t`: 26:3 "you#1", 26:6 "my", 26:7 "God", "It is", "that",
  26:10 "is what", "my", "them", 26:14 "It is", 26:20 "that", "then#1",
  26:22 "here", 26:26, 26:29 "that", 26:28 "said", 26:29 "replied". All
  are correct.
- τε: 26:3, 26:20 (5), 26:22 (21) and 26:23 τε … καί are `s`. In 26:10 and
  26:16 (τε … τε), the second τε = "and". 26:20 τε (9) = "then#0" and 26:30
  τε (1) = "Then" are lone connective τε rendered by a connective. 26:22 τε
  (14) = "alike" ("small and great alike" renders "both … and").

## Still uncertain

- 26:9 "too": linking "I#0 too" to the emphatic ἐγώ is the alternative.
  It is defensible if one counts BSB's "too" as its way of expressing the
  emphatic pronoun. I kept `t` because "too" means "also".
- 26:2 μέλλων: joining μέλλων ἀπολογεῖσθαι = "to#1 defend myself" (as 1
  Peter 5:1) is the alternative to `s`.
- 26:22 οὖν = "But" and 26:10 / 26:26 adverbial καί = "And"/"and" are kept
  as connectives rendered by a connective. οὖν has a contrastive use
  ("however") that fits "But".
- 26:20 πᾶσαν = "to#1 everyone": πᾶσαν τὴν χώραν is accusative ("all the
  region"), so "to" is BSB's recast for the recipients. It is kept as one
  phrase. A stricter reviewer could make "to#1" `t`.
