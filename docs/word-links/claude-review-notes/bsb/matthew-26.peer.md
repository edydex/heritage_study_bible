# BSB Matthew 26: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 75 of 75 verses

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 75, "problems": 0}` (and `check
--target BSB` gives the same).

Every `g`, `s` and `t` line was read against N1904 and the displayed BSB, in
three passes (26:1–25, 26:26–50, 26:51–75). Textual claims were checked
against the publisher's own rows in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/matthew.json` at `340be73`.
Only five verses carry a `partial` record. In 26:25, 26:36, 26:49 and 26:74
one word differs in form (Ῥαββί, Γεθσημανί, εὐθέως). In 26:45 the publisher
has one extra word. Every other verse has publisher Greek identical to N1904,
and the author's notes make no reading-difference claim there.

## Changes (3 verses)

- 26:39: προσευχόμενος καὶ λέγων = "and prayed" → προσευχόμενος καὶ λέγων =
  prayed, `t and`. The "and" joins "fell" and "prayed". It is the English
  coordinator for a participle rendered as a finite verb, exactly as in 26:42
  and 26:44 ("went away and prayed"). It does not render the καί between the
  two participles. That καί stays inside the condensed unit, so this is not a
  split pair.
- 26:64: ὑμῖν = to + you, `t all of` → ὑμῖν = "to all of you" (the
  publisher's row). The `t` reason itself said "all of" marks that ὑμῖν is
  plural. Those words render the number of ὑμῖν, so they are not English-only
  (rule 7). This matches 26:27, where πάντες = "all of you".
- 26:66: ὑμῖν = "do you", δοκεῖ = think → ὑμῖν = you, δοκεῖ = do + think. The
  auxiliary belongs to the verb (rule 2; compare θέλεις = "do You want" in
  26:17 and χρείαν ἔχομεν = "do we need" in 26:65). Every other reviewed
  τί ὑμῖν/σοι δοκεῖ links it this way: 17:25, 18:12, 21:28 and 22:42.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 26:12 and 26:36: keep both. In 26:12 the word-for-word links are right:
  "body" is σῶμα and "Me" is με, and the note on σώματος explains BSB's move.
  In 26:36, αὐτῶν = His disciples and τοῖς μαθηταῖς = them follow BSB's
  noun-for-pronoun swap, and both notes say so. The two verses use opposite
  methods. Each is defensible, and both are the publisher's rows, so neither
  was changed.
- 26:39: changed (see above). The doublet stays one group, rendered "prayed",
  and "and" is `t`.
- 26:41: `t For` with μέν `s` is right. μέν is answered by δέ (but) and never
  means "for". The Greek has no γάρ, so this is not a split pair. The
  publisher's μέν = For was wrong.
- 26:47 "armed": `t` is acceptable. The publisher's μετά = "armed with" would
  also be fair, because "accompanied by" = μετ’ αὐτοῦ in the same verse is a
  similar explicitation. This is not an error, so it is unchanged.
- 26:47 "sent" and 26:57 "the house of": `t` is right. These are supplied
  content, not renderings of ἀπό and πρός.
- 26:64 "all of": changed (see above).
- 26:4: `t Him` is right. Ἰησοῦν = Jesus + Him would also be defensible,
  because the object is shared, but `t` matches 26:57 (`t Him` after
  ἀπήγαγον).
- 26:45: λοιπόν = still is right. "Are you still sleeping" is the usual
  rendering of καθεύδετε (τὸ) λοιπόν. The publisher's one extra word fits the
  modern editions' τὸ before λοιπόν, and "the disciples" (not "His
  disciples") rules out αὐτοῦ. No English depends on it, so leaving it
  unlinked is correct.
- 26:53: οὐ = "not" in "Are you not aware" is right. BSB moves the negative
  from "I cannot" to the main verb, and δοκεῖς = Are you + aware.

## Other points checked and left as they are

- Name links: names are linked to an explicit pronoun or article (26:16
  αὐτόν = Jesus, 26:23 ὁ = Jesus, 26:47 αὐτοῦ = Jesus) and are `t` where the
  Greek has only a verb ending (26:16 Judas, 26:20, 26:25, 26:40, 26:43
  Jesus). Linking avoids a split pair, so both treatments are right.
- Series "and" (26:26 and#0, 26:27): linking the καί between the two
  participles to BSB's list "and" is a fair positional rendering. The other
  options would leave καί `s` beside `t and`.
- 26:35 καί = And: the καί is adverbial ("also") in ὁμοίως καὶ πάντες, and
  BSB conveys that sense with "other" (`t`). Linking it to BSB's
  sentence-initial "And" is tolerable and was kept.
- 26:54 ὅτι = that, `t say`: BSB's "that" is a relative pronoun, but it
  stands for the ὅτι clause. Kept.
- 26:42 `t cup`: right. The publisher's Greek (= N1904) has no ποτήριον.

## Still uncertain

- 26:12 and 26:36 use different methods across BSB's reorderings (lexical in
  26:12, by role in 26:36). A later pass may want one convention.
- 26:22, 26:37 and 26:39 leave the clause-initial καί `s` and give BSB's
  medial "and" `t`. This follows the author's chapter-wide rule for
  participle "and". A strict reader could see each verse as a split pair.
