## Changes (3 verses: 2 link changes, 1 note-only)

- 10:16: αὐτά = "the children", with `t them` (after "blessed") →
  αὐτά = "the children" + "them"; the `t` is removed. In ἐναγκαλισάμενος
  αὐτὰ κατευλόγει, the single αὐτά stands between the participle and the
  main verb and is the object of both. Reviewed chapters link this pattern
  (participle + object + finite verb) to every English repeat: Matthew
  18:28 (κρατήσας αὐτὸν ἔπνιγεν), 27:2 and 28:17 (ἰδόντες αὐτὸν
  προσεκύνησαν). With the `t`, a reader who tapped "them" after "blessed"
  saw no Greek word, though αὐτά stands right before κατευλόγει. The
  publisher had linked αὐτά to this "them".
- 10:30: ἐὰν μή = "will fail to", λάβῃ = "receive" → ἐὰν μή = "fail
  to", λάβῃ = "will" + "receive" (with a short note). "will" gives the
  future meaning of the subjunctive verb. It is not part of the negated
  condition. This follows rule 2 and the chapter's own 10:15 (οὐ μή =
  "never", εἰσέλθῃ εἰς = "will" + "enter").
- 10:38 (note only): the βαπτίζομαι note said it is rendered "I will
  undergo". "I" is linked to ἐγώ, so the note now says "will undergo".
  The links are unchanged.

The author's 109 corrections of publisher rows are right. They include:
"When"/"As" moved from connectives to participles and genitive absolutes
(10:14, 10:17, 10:41, 10:46, 10:47); the participle "and" made `t`;
negatives taken out of verbs; ὁ/οἱ with δέ as He/They; 10:37 σου = "Your"
and ἡμῖν = "of us" (the publisher had σου = "of us"); 10:34 "flog Him" and
"kill Him" separated; and 10:40 ἀλλά no longer carrying "These seats
belong to those".

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 10:32 "As" on the middle καί: agree. Reviewed Mark verses put
  When/as on the καί that BSB turns into a subordinate clause (1:37, 2:2,
  5:15, 5:38). The opening δέ is a transition and stays `s`.
- 10:10 καί `s` + "When they were" `t`: agree. This is not a split pair.
  The Greek has no two clauses for καί to join. BSB turns the phrase εἰς
  τὴν οἰκίαν into a clause, and καί → When would show "and" under "When".
- 10:16: changed (see above).
- 10:30: changed (see above). "fail to" stays on ἐὰν μή.
- 10:34: agree. καί is `s`, and "who will mock" stays on the verb. "who" is
  the subject that the verb ending gives (rule 4). "Him" after "kill"
  stays `t`. Unlike 10:16, αὐτόν belongs to the μαστιγώσουσιν clause, each
  earlier verb has its own pronoun, and ἀποκτενοῦσιν is a separate finite
  verb after καί. Matthew 26:4 (κρατήσωσιν καὶ ἀποκτείνωσιν … "kill Him")
  uses `t` for the same pattern.
- 10:13 αὐτοῖς = "those" with "who brought them" `t`: agree. This matches
  Matthew 19:13. 10:13 has no `partial` record, so the publisher's Greek is
  N1904 here, and no different reading is claimed.
- 10:32, 10:49, 10:50 (opening καί/δέ `s` + `t` "and"): agree. Each "and"
  joins a participle that BSB makes a finite verb. It is not the
  connective.
- 10:21: agree. Ἕν = "one thing", ὑστερεῖ = "lack", "There is" `t`.
- 10:7 reading claim: verified. The record has sourceWords 10,
  matchedSourceWords 10, publisherGreekWords 16 and target 17/17. So the
  publisher has exactly six words that N1904 lacks, and every N1904 word is
  matched. καὶ προσκολληθήσεται πρὸς τὴν γυναῖκα αὐτοῦ is six words. The
  variant with τῇ γυναικὶ αὐτοῦ would be five. The `t` reason is right.
- Spelling-only verses (10:1, 10:35, 10:41, 10:46, 10:51): agree. In each,
  publisherGreekWords equals sourceWords, with only one or two N1904 words
  unmatched. That fits the spellings named, so linking those words is
  right.
- These verses have no `partial` record, so the publisher's Greek is N1904
  there: 10:6 "God", 10:14 "and", 10:37 the second "Your", 10:43 "shall
  be" and 10:44 ἐν ὑμῖν. They are correctly treated as English choices. No
  reading is claimed.
- English "the" before a noun with no Greek article: keep it with the
  noun, as the author did (10:1, 10:6, 10:13, 10:44). Reviewed BSB chapters
  do this about 130 times, for example Matthew 19:4 and 19:8 "the
  beginning". Without a reading difference, a `t` "the" appears only in
  Matthew 19:13 and Mark 3:8. Matthew 19:13 is the outlier, so Mark 10
  is not changed.

## Still uncertain

- 10:34: precedent for the understood object of a later verb is mixed.
  Matthew 21:39 links αὐτόν to "him" after a καί-joined ἀπέκτειναν, but it
  starts with a participle (λαβόντες αὐτόν). Matthew 26:4 uses `t`. I kept
  `t`.
- 10:37: the second "Your" could instead be linked to the first σου as one
  word serving both phrases. It is left `t`, and this is not an error.
- 10:25: "for" is on κάμηλον ("for a camel") but on ἤ ("than for").
  Matthew 19:24 puts it on εὐκοπώτερον and ἤ. This is publisher style and
  is left.
- 10:48: πολλῷ = "all", μᾶλλον = "the louder" (kept from the publisher).
  "all the" / "louder", or one unit "all the louder", would also work.
