Every `g`, `s`, `t` and `m` line was read against N1904 and the displayed

Textual claims were checked against the publisher's rows and word-count

## Changes (2 verses)

- 6:43: `s 7` (ἀπό "is not rendered") + `t of#1 bread` → `g 7 = of#1` and
  `t bread`. Why: this was a split pair. BSB says "broken pieces of bread
  and fish", so "fish" also depends on that "of". In the Greek, καὶ ἀπὸ τῶν
  ἰχθύων means "and (some) of the fish". The only English word for ἀπό is
  that "of". Only "bread" has no Greek word.
- 6:2: the reason for `t how can` changed from "English turns the Greek
  exclamation into a question" to "English supplies "how can"; the Greek
  question has no interrogative word or modal verb." Why: N1904 (like
  NA28) prints this clause as a question (… γινόμεναι;), not as an
  exclamation. The link was right; only the reason was wrong.

Nothing else was changed. The author's corrections of publisher rows are
right. These include the participle "and" lines; the negatives split from
their verbs; "When" moved from καί/δέ to the participle or genitive
absolute; prepositions moved from lone articles to the noun; τούτῳ = He and
πολλά = greatly (6:2, 6:20); ὃς ἄν, κατ’ ἰδίαν, λίαν ἐκ περισσοῦ and
ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν ἦλθον as units; αὐτοῦ = John’s in 6:28 (the publisher had τήν);
and πάντες = all with ἔφαγον/εἶδαν = they + verb (as Matthew 14:20).

  19 matched. Seven words are the moved clause. The eighth is the publisher's
  first καί: it could pair with N1904 καί(0) or καί(7), so it is not a

- 6:16 `t from the dead`: 11 against 11, 10 matched. The one difference is

- Same word, different form (one-for-one counts, linked normally): 6:6

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 6:27–28 (`g @6:28`, `m … > 6:27`, καί = who): **agree.** The counts above
  show a verse-division difference only. The method matches reviewed cross-
  verse moves (1 John 2:13–14, Philippians 2:7–8, 1 Peter 3:15–16). καί =
  who matches καὶ τὸ κοράσιον = who later in 6:28. `t and` (went and
  beheaded) matches the participle pattern.
- 6:39 ἀνακλῖναι: **agree**, and the record supports it (see above). The
  link is honest. It is the same verb. Even N1904's "commanded them that all
  recline" is fairly rendered "directed them to have the people sit". The
  note states the difference in voice.
- 6:2 ἡ = "this" against 6:35 `t This`: **agree.** In 6:2 "this" is the
  determiner of the same noun the article governs (ἡ σοφία). In 6:35 "This"
  is a new subject, and ὁ τόπος has become the predicate "a desolate place".
  Matthew 14:15 also uses `t This`.
- 6:2 `t how can` and διὰ τῶν χειρῶν … γινόμεναι = perform, αὐτοῦ = He:
  **agree** (reason corrected). Linking διὰ τῶν χειρῶν αὐτοῦ = He and
  γινόμεναι = perform would be equally honest. This is not an error.
- 6:12 `t the people` against 6:14 "people were saying": **agree.** An
  indefinite "people" as the subject of a verb is kept with the verb, as in
  Mark 1:32 and 1:45. A definite "the people" is `t`, as in Mark 5:14 and
  5:17.
- 6:20 αὐτοῦ = John’s + `t words`: **agree.** No Greek word means "words".
- 6:11 τόπος = place, ἐκεῖθεν = that: **agree** to keep the publisher's
  rows. τόπος is the only Greek "place" and has the same referent.
- 6:35 ἤδη = By now + already#0, καί = So: **agree.** The verse-opening καί
  cannot mean "By now", and the publisher's οἱ = "So the" was wrong. Both
  English adverbs express the one ἤδη.
- 6:37 `t all of` and 6:41 `t them` ("among them all"): **agree.** Neither
  verse has a Greek word for these. 6:37 has no `partial` key.
- εὐθύς: 6:25 At once, 6:27 without delay, 6:45 Immediately, 6:50 at once,
  6:54 As soon as. No "At this" occurs in this chapter.
- 6:26 καί = but follows the reviewed parallel Matthew 14:9.

## Still uncertain (left unchanged)

- 6:43 (my change): the author's reading (`s ἀπό`, `t of bread`) is also
  defensible. The publisher left ἀπό without a row.
- 6:6: κύκλῳ = around, περιῆγεν = He went (publisher). Linking περιῆγεν =
  went around, with κύκλῳ inside "from village to village", is also
  possible.
- 6:54 εὐθύς = As soon as: "As soon as" merges "immediately" with the "when"
  of the genitive absolute. Linking εὐθύς alone is acceptable.
- 6:34 and 6:55: the English "and" joins different verbs from the Greek καί
  (καί(5) and καί(5) are `s`, "and" is `t`). This is right, but readers may
  see it as a split pair.
- Small slip in the author note: it mentions "the second ἐνδύσησθε (6:9)",
  but 6:9 has only one ἐνδύσησθε. The JSON is right.
