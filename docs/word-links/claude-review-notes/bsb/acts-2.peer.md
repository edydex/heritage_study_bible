Checked: 47 of 47 verses (2:1-47), every `g`/`s`/`t` line, against N1904,
BSB's English and the publisher record in
`public/data/original-languages/bsb-word-links/acts.json` (2:1, 2:7, 2:9,

Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 47, "problems": 0}`.

## Changes (10, in 8 verses)

- 2:33: `g 7 = and#0` (τε) → `s 7` + `t and#0`. Reasons:
  - τε joins the two participles ὑψωθείς and λαβών ("having been exalted
    and having received"). BSB keeps ὑψωθείς as a fronted participle
    ("Exalted, then, …,") and makes λαβών finite ("He has received"). Its
    only "and" then joins has received (λαβών) to has poured out (ἐξέχεεν).
    That joins a different pair of words from the pair τε joins, so it is
    not τε moved to another place. It is the English "and" that joins a
    participle clause to the main verb, which the conventions make `t`.
  - Reviewed precedent: Matthew 27:48 (τε linking participles `s`, "has no
    separate English word"); τε … καί always has τε `s` (Acts 1:1, 1:8, Luke
    15:2, 23:12). The "and" of 2:46, by contrast, does join the two
    participles that τε joins ("continued to meet … and to break"), so τε
    = and stays there.
- 2:24: "from the dead" `t` → `g 3 = raised + from#0 the dead` (ἀνέστησεν),
  with a note. 2:24 and 2:32 are BSB's two expansions of the same verb
  ἀνέστησεν ("raised up"): "raised Him from the dead" and "raised this Jesus
  to life". The reviewed Gospels link "raised to life" to the verb (Matthew
  16:21, 17:23, 20:19, Luke 9:22), and "rise again" / "rose again" also
  stay with the verb (Mark 10:34 ἀναστήσεται, 1 Thessalonians 4:14 ἀνέστη).
  Now both verses follow one principle. Mark 6:16 (`t from the
  dead`) is a different case: there BSB repeats a phrase from 6:14. The
  publisher record for 2:24 has all 17 Greek words matched, so no reading is
  claimed.
- 2:24: "from" of "from the agony" was on λύσας (`releasing + from#1`) →
  `g 6 = from#1 + agony`. The publisher had "from the" on the article τάς.
  The brief's rule is that the article keeps "the" and the preposition goes
  on the noun. τάς = the#1 is unchanged.
- 2:14: `οἱ κατοικοῦντες = who dwell in`, `Ἱερουσαλήμ = Jerusalem` → `οἱ
  κατοικοῦντες = who dwell`, `Ἱερουσαλήμ = in Jerusalem` (the publisher's
  row, with notes). Reasons:
  - The same construction with Jerusalem as object is linked this way in
    the reviewed Acts 1:19 (τοῖς κατοικοῦσιν = who lived, Ἱερουσαλήμ = in
    Jerusalem) and Luke 13:4 (living / in Jerusalem). The Acts 4:16 review
    in progress does the same.
  - Matthew 23:21 (dwells in / it) has a pronoun object. It is the only
    reviewed exception and does not outweigh the same-book precedent.
  - The publisher row was not wrong, so rule 10 keeps it.
- 2:9: `οἱ κατοικοῦντες = residents of`, `Μεσοποταμίαν = Mesopotamia` → `οἱ
  κατοικοῦντες = residents`, `Μεσοποταμίαν = of Mesopotamia`. The publisher
  had "of" on the lone article τήν. Under the lone-article rule the
  preposition goes on the noun. This also matches 2:14 and 1:19 ("in
  Jerusalem"), "of Libya" and "from Rome" (2:10).
- 2:9 and 2:10: τε καί joined as `and` → τε `s` ("The τε of τε καί ('both …
  and') is rendered only by the following and."), καί = and. No reviewed
  chapter joins τε with καί. All 38 reviewed τε groups stand alone, and
  Acts 1:1 and 1:8 use exactly this reason. 2:11, where BSB has "both", keeps τε = both.
- 2:4: `ἐπλήσθησαν = they were + filled with`, `Ἁγίου = the#0 Holy` →
  `ἐπλήσθησαν = they were + filled`, `Ἁγίου = with the Holy`. The "with"
  renders the genitive of content. Every reviewed instance of this exact
  phrase puts it on the genitive (Luke 1:15, 1:41, 1:67 "with the Holy").
  So do the other verbs of filling (Luke 5:26, 6:11, John 2:7, 6:13), and
  2:28 here ("fill me with joy"). The author's split of "they were" off
  πάντες is kept.
- 2:36: `οἶκος Ἰσραήλ = Israel` (joined) → οἶκος `s`, Ἰσραήλ = Israel.
  "Israel" does not mean "house". Reviewed precedent: Matthew 10:6 (οἴκου
  `s`, Ἰσραήλ = of Israel).
- 2:46 (reason only): the first τε's reason "linking to the previous verse"
  → "The first τε of τε … τε ('both … and') is not separately expressed;
  the second τε is rendered and." The two τε pair the participles
  προσκαρτεροῦντες and κλῶντες. No link changed.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 2:24 / 2:32: changed (above). Both expansions now go with ἀνέστησεν.
- 2:9 / 2:14 κατοικέω: changed (above). The preposition goes on the place
  name, as in Acts 1:19 and Luke 13:4. Matthew 23:21 is not followed here.
- 2:29 / 2:37 Ἄνδρες ἀδελφοί = Brothers: **agree, kept**. This is the
  reviewed Acts 1:16 link. The other vocatives stay word for word: Ἄνδρες
  Ἰουδαῖοι = Men + of Judea (2:14) and Ἄνδρες Ἰσραηλεῖται = Men + of Israel
  (2:22). This matches Acts 1:11 (Ἄνδρες = Men, Γαλιλαῖοι = of Galilee).
  BSB has no "You"/"O" here.
- 2:36 οἶκος Ἰσραήλ: changed (above).
- 2:3 πυρός = "flames of fire": **agree, kept**. "flames of fire" is BSB's
  rendering of the genitive "(as) of fire". A tap on "flames" shows πυρός,
  which is not misleading, and the publisher row is not wrong.
- 2:45 πᾶσιν `s`, καθότι ἄν `s`, "who was in" on εἶχεν: **agree, kept**.
  BSB merges "to all, as anyone had need" into "with anyone who was in
  need". "anyone" is τις, "who" is the English relative for τις as subject
  of εἶχεν, and καθότι ("according as") has no English word.

## 2:42 (Codex KEEP, not edited)

No meaning error. The convention point the author noted stands. τῇ (token
3) alone carries "to#0 the" and τῇ (token 10) alone carries "to#2 the", so
a lone article carries a preposition. Later chapters would give each
article "the" and put "to" on its noun (διδαχῇ, κλάσει). The long notes on
`g 0 2` and `g 15 16` could also be shortened. Left for a later fix.

## Still uncertain

- 2:23 "Him#0" `t`: τοῦτον is the grammatical object of ἀνείλατε ("put Him
  to death") and is also rendered "He". Linking τοῦτον = He + Him#0 (as
  Acts 3:13 ὅν = Him + Him) would also be defensible. Kept as `t` because
  the reason is accurate.
- 2:6 τῇ = his, ἰδίᾳ = own: reviewed precedent is split (John 10:3-4
  article = his; Luke 10:34, John 15:19 ἴδιον = his/its own). The
  publisher row was kept.
