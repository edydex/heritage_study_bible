# BSB 1 Corinthians 4: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 21 of 21 verses (4:1-21). No KEEP blocks in this chapter. Every
`g`, `s` and `t` line was checked against the Greek, BSB's English and each
verse's EVIDENCE line. Final check: `fromtext` → `{"versesOk": 21,
"problems": 0}`.

## Changes

Four changes, in four verses.

- 4:5 τῶν = "men’s" → τῶν `s` (standard article reason); "men’s" `t`
  ("English supplies men’s to say whose hearts; the Greek has only the
  article"). The Greek is τὰς βουλὰς τῶν καρδιῶν, "the counsels of the
  hearts", with no ἀνθρώπων. The possessive-article convention covers an
  article rendered by a possessive pronoun ("his/their/your"), as with ὁ =
  "his" later in this verse. "men’s" adds a noun the Greek does not have,
  so tapping it should not show the article as if it meant "men". Same
  handling as Luke 22:51 ("man’s" `t`, "the Greek has only the article").
  καρδιῶν = "of + hearts" is unchanged.
- 4:14 "you#1" (after "warn") `t` → linked to ὑμᾶς, so ὑμᾶς = "you#0 +
  you#1" (note: shared object of both participles). In "Οὐκ ἐντρέπων ὑμᾶς
  … ἀλλ’ … νουθετῶν" the one ὑμᾶς is the object of both contrasted
  participles; νουθετῶν leaves it understood. That is a Greek owner, not a
  supplied object such as "it" in 4:7 and 4:12. Reviewed precedent does the
  same: 1 Corinthians 1:7, 1 Thessalonians 2:12, 2 Thessalonians 3:3,
  1 Timothy 1:12, 2 Timothy 4:18.
- 4:16 μιμηταί = "imitate" and γίνεσθε = "to" → μιμηταὶ … γίνεσθε joined =
  "to imitate" (note: "become imitators" is rendered to imitate). "to" does
  not mean "become", and γίνεσθε has no English of its own here. A word
  that BSB does not render must not carry an unrelated English word.
  Hebrews 6:12 does not apply: there γένησθε has its own English ("you will
  + be + will") because it also governs νωθροί. A noun or adjective with
  γίνομαι rendered by one English verb is joined in reviewed rows (Acts
  27:16 περικρατεῖς γενέσθαι = "to secure", Hebrews 6:4 μετόχους
  γενηθέντας = "who have shared", Acts 16:29). "to" stays in the group
  because the Greek imperative becomes an English infinitive after "urge",
  as in 4:14 ἐντρέπων = "to shame".
- 4:21 ἐν (8) = "in" and πνεύματι = "with#1 a + spirit" → ἐν = "in +
  with#1" (note: one ἐν governs both nouns), πνεύματι = "a#1 + spirit".
  "ἐν ἀγάπῃ πνεύματί τε πραΰτητος" has one ἐν governing both datives. The
  dative of πνεύματι comes from that preposition; it is not a free
  instrumental dative, so "with" belongs to ἐν. BSB renders the same ἐν as
  "with" in ἐν ῥάβδῳ in this verse. Reviewed precedent links a repeated
  preposition to its one ἐν (Matthew 3:11 "with + with", Luke 1:79 "in +
  in", 2 Thessalonians 2:13 "by + by"). This differs from 4:12, where
  χερσίν has no preposition and "with + hands" on the noun is right.

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 4:5 τῶν = "men’s": changed (see above).
- 4:12 κοπιῶμεν ἐργαζόμενοι = "We work hard": kept. Both words add
  meaning: ἐργαζόμενοι gives "work" and κοπιῶμεν gives the toil ("hard") and
  the person ("We"). Linking only κοπιῶμεν and making ἐργαζόμενοι `s` would
  leave a participle unrendered that BSB actually renders. A split
  (κοπιῶμεν = "We + hard", ἐργαζόμενοι = "work") would claim more precision
  than the English has. The joined unit is the honest choice.
- 4:14 "you" after "warn": changed to a link (see above).
- 4:15 "Even" on ἐάν: kept. ἐάν with ἀλλά in the main clause is concessive,
  and "even if" is a standard sense of ἐάν. γάρ `s` is right, because BSB has
  no "for" in this clause. The understood ἔχητε = "you#0 have + you#1 do +
  have#1" is also kept. The second clause (ἀλλ’ οὐ πολλοὺς πατέρας) leaves
  the same verb understood, with the same role (main verb with a direct
  object) and the same meaning ("have"). Only the mood changes (subjunctive
  to indicative), and the rule allows that. BSB's wording "you do not have"
  matches this: οὐ = "not" and the do-support stays with the verb. No
  English tense is added.
- 4:3 εἰς ἐλάχιστόν ἐστιν = "care very little": kept as one idiom. "care"
  does not render ἐστιν or εἰς on its own. The English recasts the whole
  predicate "it is a very small thing (to me)". The dative ἐμοί = "I" stays
  separate.
- 4:21 "with" on πνεύματι vs ἐν understood again: changed to ἐν (see
  above).

## Points the coordinator asked to settle

- 4:4 οὐδὲν ἐμαυτῷ σύνοιδα = "My conscience is clear": kept joined. BSB
  recasts "I am aware of nothing against myself" as a whole. A split
  (ἐμαυτῷ = "My", σύνοιδα = "conscience", οὐδέν = "clear") would link
  "clear" to "nothing" and "conscience" to a verb, which over-claims. γάρ
  `s` is right, because BSB has no "for".
- 4:11 τῆς ἄρτι = "this very": kept. "very" alone does not render ἄρτι,
  and article + adverb form one attributive unit ("the present"). This
  matches Romans 11:8 (τῆς σήμερον ἡμέρας = "this very day").
- Person words: confirmed. "we" before the participles in 4:12 (two) and
  4:13 is `t`. "you#5" for λαβών in 4:7 is `t`. "I" in 4:18 is on the
  explicit pronoun μου of the genitive absolute, not on the participle.
  τις = "they" (4:2), σε = "you" (4:7) and ὑμᾶς = "your" (4:15) render
  explicit Greek words and are right.
- 4:8 reading: confirmed. The publisher record has `partial` (17 source
  words, 16 matched, 17 publisher Greek words), and the EVIDENCE line shows
  one N1904 word unmatched and one publisher word not in N1904. The only
  English left without a publisher owner is "might be kings with", so the
  differing word is the last verb. The note ("Same word, spelled
  differently in BSB's Greek text") does not name the publisher's
  spelling. All other verses have no `partial` key, and no other note
  claims a reading.
- Other rules checked: no "of the"/"to the"/"in the" sits on a lone article
  (4:9 τῷ = "the", 4:13 τοῦ = "the", 4:20 τοῦ `s`). The supplied copulas
  are `t`: 4:5 "is", the six "are" in 4:10, 4:17 "is#0" and "which is",
  4:20 "is". 4:4 ἐστιν = "It is" renders a real copula in a cleft (Acts
  6:2, John 21:7). The added "that" (4:9), "whole" (4:9), "only" (4:19),
  "of you" (4:18), "a matter" (4:20), "wait" (4:5), "to go" (4:6) and the
  supplied objects "it" (4:7, 4:12) are `t`. Connectives that BSB renders
  with a different connective are linked: 4:2 λοιπόν = "Now", 4:3 ἀλλά =
  "In fact" and ἵνα = "if", 4:6 ἵνα = "Then", 4:19 καί = "and then".

## Still uncertain

- 4:13 πάντων = "of the world": kept as linked with its note. BSB swaps the
  pair: κόσμου = "earth", and "all" becomes "the world", in the sense of
  "everyone". An `s`/`t` split is arguable if "world" is judged a different
  meaning from "all".
- 4:19 τόν = "what#0", alongside λόγον = "are saying": kept as parallel to
  τήν = "what#1". Joining τὸν λόγον = "what … are saying" would also be
  defensible.
