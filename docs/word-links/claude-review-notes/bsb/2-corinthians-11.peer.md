# BSB 2 Corinthians 11: independent peer review (Claude)

Checked: 33 of 33 verses (11:1-33). I read every `g`, `s` and `t` line of
the author's final file against the Greek and BSB's exact wording. I also
compared each verse with the publisher's rows in the base draft, with the
EVIDENCE line and with the `partial` records in the publisher file. No KEEP
blocks in this chapter. Final check: `fromtext` →
`{"versesOk": 33, "problems": 0}`.

Changes: 3 verses (11:6, 11:20, 11:23), all link changes. The other 30
verses are unchanged.

## Changes

- 11:6: ἰδιώτης = "not#0 a polished + lacking", λόγῳ = "speaker" →
  ἰδιώτης = "not#0 + polished + lacking", λόγῳ = "a + speaker". "a" goes
  with its noun, and in "not a polished speaker" that noun is "speaker".
  BSB turns ἰδιώτης into the adjective "(not) polished" and λόγῳ into the
  head noun. This is the same move the author made in 11:2, where "a" left
  the adjectival Θεοῦ ("godly") for ζήλῳ ("with a + jealousy").
- 11:20: εἴ (6, 9, 12, 15) = "or#0" … "or#3" (publisher rows) → those four
  εἴ join the four repeated τις in one `s` line, and each "or" is `t`.
  εἰ means "if", not "or". BSB recasts the five "if anyone …" clauses as
  "anyone who … or … or …", so the repeated εἴ τις has no English words and
  the "or"s are English coordination. Reviewed files treat a repeated εἰ
  the same way: Acts 26:23 (second εἰ `s`, "and" `t`) and 1 Timothy 5:10
  (each εἰ `s`, list "and" `t`). It also matches this chapter's 11:26,
  where a repeated κινδύνοις is `s` and the list "and" is `t`.
- 11:23: θανάτοις = "of#2 death", "danger" `t` → θανάτοις = "danger of
  death" (the publisher's row, with the author's note). In the plural,
  θάνατοι means "deaths", that is, mortal perils. "danger of death" renders
  that one word and is not a noun BSB supplies. The author's own note
  already said θανάτοις "is rendered danger of death", which did not fit
  the `t`.

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- 11:1 καί `s` + "already" `t`: agreed. In ἀλλὰ καὶ ἀνέχεσθε, καί is
  "indeed/even". "already" adds a time sense that καί does not have, so it
  is not a shifted rendering. ἀνέχεσθε = "you are + doing that" and the
  second μου `s` are fine. "of" on the partitive ἀφροσύνης and μου = "my"
  are also fine.
- 11:6 "lacking" on ἰδιώτης: agreed. The second clause, ἀλλ’ οὐ τῇ γνώσει,
  leaves out its predicate, and that predicate is ἰδιώτης. "lacking" has
  the same role (predicate) and meaning ("unskilled, deficient") in the
  knowledge clause. Precedent: 1 Corinthians 4:15, where the verb left out
  of ἀλλ’ οὐ πολλοὺς πατέρας is linked again. ἀλλ’ (6) = "certainly": agreed.
  This ἀλλά opens the main clause after a concessive εἰ καί and carries
  the emphatic "yet, at least, certainly" sense. The second ἀλλ’ `s`:
  agreed (new sentence).
- 11:6 ἐν πᾶσιν `s`, "every way possible" on παντί: agreed. That is the
  publisher's row and it is not wrong, since "in every way possible" is the
  English intensive of "in every way". "possible" does not mean "in all
  things", so linking it to ἐν πᾶσιν would over-claim. "We" `t`
  (participle) and "this" `t` (object) are correct.
- 11:4: agreed on every point. ἄλλον = "a + other + than": "another" is
  "an other", and "than" is the complement word of English "other",
  linked the way "takes advantage of" and "boast of" are. ἕτερον = "a
  different + than" (Galatians 1:6). The three οὐκ are `s` because BSB's
  comparison replaces "whom … not"; that is a negative recast away, with
  an honest reason. The second "if" is on the one εἰ, which governs both
  conditions. It has the same role and meaning, like 2 Corinthians 6:7
  (one τῶν = "the#2 + the#3"). The publisher's ἤ = "or if" over-claimed. "and"
  (participle subject joined to κηρύσσει) and "it" are correctly `t`.
- 11:7 "Was" on ἐποίησα with "for me", "it" `t`: agreed. The clause has a
  Greek verb, and BSB recasts "did I commit a sin" as "Was it a sin for
  me". So "Was" renders ἐποίησα; it is not a copula supplied in a verbless
  clause.
- 11:10: confirmed against the registered 2 Corinthians 1:18. There "as
  surely as" sits on the oath predicate (πιστός) and ὅτι is `s`. Here the
  oath predicate is the fronted ἔστιν, so ἔστιν = "As surely as + is" and
  ὅτι `s` follow the same pattern. ἀλήθεια = "the truth" (no Greek article;
  "the" goes with its noun) is fine.
- 11:12 καθώς = "equals", ἡμεῖς = "our": agreed. εὑρεθῶσιν keeps "be
  regarded as" (the publisher's row). καὶ ποιήσω = "I will keep on doing"
  is also fine.
- 11:22: confirmed. κἀγώ = "So + I" and each "am" `t` (John 15:9;
  1 Corinthians 7:7-8). The copula must not be linked to εἰσιν, which is
  in another clause and another person. The Ἰσραηλεῖται note is softened
  correctly, and the EVIDENCE line and the `partial` record (9 of 10 words
  matched) support it.
- 11:23 "danger": changed (see above).
- 11:23-29 catalogue: checked item by item. 11:26: each κινδύνοις that BSB
  covers with the pair's single "danger" is `s`, and each list "and" with
  no καί is `t`. Linking "and" to κινδύνοις would make "and" mean
  "danger". "in" is on the dative κινδύνοις ("in danger") or on ἐν
  (city/country). "from" is on the genitive noun (rivers, bandits) or on
  ἐκ/ἐξ. "on"/"among" are on ἐν, and "my" ×2 and "I have been" are `t`.
  11:27: "in" is on κόπῳ (dative) and on ἐν (6, 13). ἐν (3, 10) is `s`
  under "without sleep/food", the καί are linked, and the two list "and"
  are `t`. 11:23: every ἐν = "in", and the adverbs are linked. 11:28:
  "for" is on ἐκκλησιῶν, "of" `t` (apposition), and ἡ (9) = "my". All
  correct.
- 11:24: kept. τεσσεράκοντα has no article. Its "the" goes with the head
  word, the substantival numeral, just as "the" goes with the anarthrous
  Ἰουδαίων in the same verse. "lashes" is a supplied noun (`t`). The join
  τεσσεράκοντα παρὰ μίαν = "the forty + minus one" is a numeral unit under
  README rule 3 and is not wrong. The publisher's split (παρά = "minus",
  μίαν = "one") would also be acceptable, so I did not change it.
- 11:29 "burn with grief" whole on πυροῦμαι: agreed. The figurative
  πυροῦμαι means burning with emotion.
- 11:30: confirmed. δεῖ = "must" and "I" `t` (an impersonal δεῖ carries no
  supplied subject; Acts 5:29, 20:35). τά = "the things", "of" on "boast
  of", and "that show" `t` (1 Corinthians 2:14) are also correct.
- 11:33: confirmed. The second διά `s` and "in" `t`. BSB's one "through"
  belongs to διὰ θυρίδος. "in the wall" only places the window, and "in"
  does not mean "through", so linking it would be a cross-wired link. This
  matches Acts 9:25, where "a window in" is `t`.

## Still uncertain

- 11:1 "already": a reviewer who reads "already" as BSB's freer rendering
  of the affirming καί could link it. I kept the author's `s` + `t`.
- 11:6 "a": with "a + speaker" the "a" sits on a dative of respect. The
  other view is to keep it with ἰδιώτης, the Greek predicate noun. I
  followed the rule that "a" goes with the English noun, as in 11:2.
