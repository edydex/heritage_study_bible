Checked: 30 of 30 verses (19:1–30, including Codex's earlier 19:22).

## Changes (6 verses: 4 link fixes, 2 note-only fixes)

- 19:3: πειράζοντες "and tested" → "tested"; "and" is now `t`. The English
  "and" joins "came" to a Greek participle made finite; it is not part of
  the verb. καί (between the two participles) stays `s`, because BSB renders
  λέγοντες as "by asking".
- 19:16: εἶπεν "and inquired" → "inquired"; "and" is now `t`. Greek has the
  participle προσελθών, which BSB makes finite ("came up … and inquired").
- 19:22 (Codex's verse): links unchanged. Four notes and the δέ reason were
  long or unclear ("The actual hearing participle…", "quantity-to-extent
  wording"); each is now one short factual sentence (ἀκούσας → "When …
  heard"; τὸν λόγον → "this"; periphrastic ἦν … ἔχων → "he had"; κτήματα
  πολλά → "great wealth"; "BSB does not render δέ here").
- 19:24: εἰσελθεῖν note "BSB renders it 'pass' and then 'enter'" →
  "N1904's one εἰσελθεῖν serves both clauses; other editions read διελθεῖν
  ('to pass') for the camel and repeat εἰσελθεῖν for the rich man." The old
  note implied BSB chose two English verbs for one Greek verb; BSB's "pass …
  enter" reflects a two-verb reading. The ellipsis link itself is kept.
- 19:25: λέγοντες "and asked" → "asked"; "and" is now `t` (participle made
  a second finite verb).
- 19:26: εἶπεν "and said" → "said"; "and" is now `t` (the participle
  ἐμβλέψας is made finite, "looked at them and said").

## Answers to the author's doubts

- 19:10, 19:3, 19:7 (αὐτοῦ, ἀνθρώπῳ, αὐτήν): agree. The Textus Receptus has
  all three, and the critical text has ἀνθρώπῳ (19:3) and [αὐτοῦ] /
  [αὐτήν] in brackets. N1904 lacks them. The hedged reasons are true.
  Keeping οἱ as `s` and "His" as `t` in 19:10 is right. BSB's "His" renders
  αὐτοῦ, not the article.
- 19:29: agree, no change. πολλαπλασίονα means "many times as much";
  linking it to "a hundredfold" would show the reader a wrong meaning. This
  is a different word in BSB's text (rule 9), not a shift in sense, so the
  `s`/`t` pair is not a split pair. "or wife" (ἢ γυναῖκα) as `t` is
  correct; N1904 lacks it.
- 19:26: agree. αὐτοῖς → "them" in "looked at them" avoids a split pair;
  BSB has moved the dative from "said" to "looked at".
- 19:14: agree, keep τῶν → "to" (genitive of possession, like τῶν → "of"
  elsewhere in the chapter). Moving "to" onto ἐστίν would also be defensible,
  but it is not an error.
- 19:24: agree, εἰς stays `s`. Joining it to εἰσελθεῖν would also attach it
  to "to pass".
- Kept publisher renderings ("some Pharisees", "only One", "renewal of all
  things", "the last"): agree. Each is a normal rendering of one Greek word,
  not an over-claim.

## Still uncertain

- 19:24: the base text BSB follows probably reads διελθεῖν … εἰσελθεῖν.
  The new note only says "other editions" (true at least of the Textus
  Receptus). A stricter rule-9 treatment would make "to enter" `t`. I kept
  the ellipsis link because N1904's single verb does govern the rich-man
  clause.
- 19:24: "easier for" and "than for" put the infinitive-subject "for" on
  εὐκοπώτερον and ἤ. It could instead go with κάμηλον and πλούσιον. Left
  as a matter of style.
- 19:5: "be united to" puts the dative "to" on the verb while τῇ is `s`.
  This is acceptable, but τῇ → "to" would also work.
