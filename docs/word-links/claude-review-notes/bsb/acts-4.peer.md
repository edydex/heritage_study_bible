## Changes (6 verses)

Lone articles that still carried "of the" (the rule: the article gets
"the", the preposition goes on the noun; none of these nouns is an
indeclinable name):

- 4:1: τοῦ = "of the", ἱεροῦ = "temple" → τοῦ = "the", ἱεροῦ = "of +
  temple" (captain of the temple).
- 4:8: τοῦ = "of the", λαοῦ = "people" → τοῦ = "the", λαοῦ = "of + people"
  (elders of the people).
- 4:26: τῆς = "of the", γῆς = "earth" → τῆς = "the", γῆς = "of + earth"
  (kings of the earth).
- 4:33: τοῦ = "of the", Κυρίου = "Lord" → τοῦ = "the", Κυρίου = "of + Lord"
  (resurrection of the Lord Jesus).

Supplied English folded into a Greek word:

- 4:9: ἀσθενοῦς = "who was lame" → ἀσθενοῦς = "lame", `t who was` (English
  turns the adjective into a relative clause; the Greek has no relative or
  copula). A supplied copula is `t`; reviewed precedent for an adjective
  made into a relative clause: Matthew 12:22, Luke 5 and Luke 7 (`t who
  was` / `t who were`).
- 4:32: πάντα = "everything they owned" → πάντα = "everything", `t they
  owned` (English adds it to make clear that everything means their
  possessions). πάντα means "all things"; nothing in the clause means
  "owned", and αὐτοῖς already has "they" (the first one). This matches the
  author's own 4:34 change ("their property" `t`).

## Answers to the author's doubts and the points raised

- 4:10 cleft: **agree, kept**. BSB writes "let this be known …: It is by
  the name of Jesus Christ …, that this man stands". The colon stands where
  ὅτι stands, so ὅτι `s` with the colon reason is right. The English "that"
  is not the "that" of a content clause: it is the cleft "that" after the
  relative clauses, in the exact place where the Greek resumes with ἐν
  τούτῳ ("by this one/by him") before οὗτος παρέστηκεν. The cleft fronts
  "by the name" and leaves its slot at "that", so ἐν τούτῳ = "that" is the
  honest link. "It is" is a supplied cleft frame and copula, so `t`.
  "this" in "let this be known", "then" and "but" stay `t`.
- 4:35: **agree, kept** (ἑκάστῳ = "to anyone", τις = "he", εἶχεν =
  "had"). The Greek says "it was distributed to each, as anyone had need".
  In BSB "to anyone" is the recipient, which is ἑκάστῳ's role (dative
  "to each"), and "he" is the subject of "had need", which is τις's role.
  The publisher's mapping left ἑκάστῳ with only "to" and gave τις the
  recipient phrase's "anyone", so the word for "each" lost its meaning and
  the subject τις sat on the recipient.
- 4:25 διά = "by + through": **agree, kept**. In διὰ Πνεύματος Ἁγίου
  στόματος Δαυείδ the one διά governs both genitives; στόματος has no
  preposition of its own, so "through" is διά understood again, not the
  genitive case.
- 4:21 διὰ … ὅτι = one "because": **agree, kept**. BSB merges "because of
  the people, for all were glorifying God" into "because all the people
  were glorifying God"; both causal words stand behind the one "because",
  while τὸν λαόν keeps "the people" and πάντες "all".
- 4:17 ἵνα μή = "to keep … from": **agree**. "to" gives the purpose and
  "keep … from" the negation; μηδενὶ ἀνθρώπων joined = "to anyone" (after
  "not") is also fine.
- 4:19 ἐνώπιον = "in + sight", Θεοῦ = "God’s": **agree**. ἐνώπιον is
  "in the sight of"; the genitive Θεοῦ is "God’s".
- 4:22 γεγόνει = "was", σημεῖον = "miraculously", ἰάσεως = "healed":
  **agree**. BSB rewrites "on whom this sign of healing had happened" as
  "who was miraculously healed"; the event verb gives "was", the sign
  noun gives the adverb, the healing noun gives the verb. τοῦτο `s` is right.
- 4:32 ἦν … κοινά = "shared", αὐτοῖς = "they": **agree** ("all things
  were common to them" → "they shared"). Only "they owned" changed (above).
- 4:8 "with the Holy" on Ἁγίου vs 4:31 "filled with" on the verb:
  **left as is**. Reviewed Luke 1:15, 1:41 and 1:67 do the same as 4:8, and
  4:31 is KEEP. It is a difference of convention, not an error.
- 4:24 "their voices" on φωνήν: **agree** (English determiner for the
  noun). οἱ = "the believers" also stays: an article used as a pronoun and
  rendered by a noun has reviewed precedent ("In ὁ δέ / οἱ δέ the article
  serves as the pronoun …; BSB says the man / the men").
- 4:31 (KEEP): no error found. The notes are wordy but correct; nothing
  was edited.

## Readings (checked against the `partial` records)

- 4:6, 4:13, 4:19 (John), 4:27 (Pilate), 4:36 (a Levite): each record has
  1 N1904 word unmatched and 1 publisher word not in N1904, and the draft
  listed that English name as unlinked. "Same name/word, spelled
  differently in BSB's Greek text" is correct and states no publisher
  spelling.
- 4:4: 0 unmatched, 1 extra publisher word, and the publisher's "the" (the
  number) was unlinked. `t the` with "N1904 has no article before ἀριθμός;
  BSB follows a reading with an extra word here" is correct and does not
  name the word.
- 4:30: 0 unmatched, 1 extra publisher word, and the publisher's first
  "Your" was unlinked. τήν = "Your" holds on its own (article used as a
  possessive with χεῖρα, the majority convention). The note "BSB's Greek
  text has one extra word here" is not required for that link, but rule 9
  allows it, the evidence supports it, and it tells the reader why the
  publisher's "Your" came from elsewhere. Kept.
- 4:18 and 4:28: 0 unmatched, 1 extra publisher word each. Every English
  word is accounted for from N1904 words or as supplied English, so no
  reading claim is needed. Correctly nothing claimed.

## Still uncertain

- 4:23 τοὺς ἰδίους: "their" on the article and "own people" on ἰδίους was
  kept (publisher row; John 1:11 τὰ ἴδια / οἱ ἴδιοι does the same). Acts 1:25
  instead put the possessive on ἴδιον, but there ἴδιον had lost "own"
  entirely, which is not the case here.
- 4:22 "was" on γεγόνει is a judgment on BSB's passive rewording; the
  alternative is "was + healed" on ἰάσεως with γεγόνει `s`.
