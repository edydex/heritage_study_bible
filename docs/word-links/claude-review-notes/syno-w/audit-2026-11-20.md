# SYNO-W audit, random sample of 160 verses (Claude)

Method: a uniform random sample of 160 of the 7,960 reviewed New Testament verses of the Russian Synodal (SYNO-W) links, seed 20261120.
Every `g`, `h`, `s`, `t` and `m` line was read against the Nestle 1904 Greek and the Russian text as given; TR (Scrivener) claims in reasons were checked from memory of the printed TR.
Only meaning was judged (the compiler already guarantees coverage); a script also checked that repeated Russian words (`#n`) are never assigned out of Greek order.

## Summary

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Verses checked | 160 (2,733 link lines: 2,175 `g`, 271 `s`, 236 `t`, 48 `h`, 3 `m`) |
| Verses with at least one real error (categories 1–4) | **11** |
| Category 1, wrong link | 2 (both on `h` sub-links; no wrong `g` link found) |
| Category 2, over-claim | 1 |
| Category 3, wrongly unexplained | 8 findings (12 Greek tokens) |
| Category 4, bad reason | 0 |
| Minor (debatable / inconsistent, not wrong) | 22 items in 24 verses |
| Clean verses (nothing to report) | 127 / 160 (79%) |
| **Estimated error rate** | **11 / 160 = 6.9%** of verses (95% Wilson interval 3.9–11.9%). Counting `g`-level errors only: 9 / 160 = 5.6%. At line level about 15 of 2,733 lines (~0.5%) are affected. |

Caution: this is one sample of 160 verses; the interval is wide, and most real errors found are low-impact (a missing link on a function word, not a wrong meaning).

## Findings (categories 1–4)

### 1 Corinthians

- **1 Cor 9:25 — cat. 3** — `s 9` ἵνα with `t для`; the `s` reason itself says Russian "uses a preposition and noun". Purpose ἵνα + subjunctive is rendered by purpose для + verbal noun; the dataset links ὅπως/ἵνα → "для того чтобы" elsewhere (1 Cor 1:29, 1 Jn 3:5). Fix: `g 9 = для // Purpose ἵνα with the subjunctive is rendered by для with the verbal noun получения.` and drop `t для`.
- **1 Cor 15:12 — cat. 3** — `s 10` ἐν with `t из#1`. ἐν ὑμῖν τινες "some among you" = "некоторые из вас"; the preposition has a clear counterpart. Fix: `g 10 = из#1 // ἐν "among" is rendered partitively.` and drop `t из#1`.

### 2 Corinthians

- **2 Cor 9:7 — cat. 3 (three pairs)** — `s 1` καθώς / `t по`, `s 6 9` ἐκ, ἐξ / `t с#0`, `t с#1`, `s 8` ἤ / `t и`. Each Greek word's own `s` reason names the Russian word that renders it. "μὴ ἐκ λύπης ἢ ἐξ ἀνάγκης" = "не с огорчением и не с принуждением" (under negation "or" becomes "and not"). Fix: `g 1 = по // καθώς "as (he decided)" is rendered "according to (the disposition)".`; `g 6 = с#0`; `g 9 = с#1`; `g 8 = и // Under the negation the disjunction is rendered "и не".`; drop the four `t` lines.

### Acts

- **Acts 20:18 — cat. 3** — `s 12` ἀφ’ with `t в#0`. ἀφ’ ἧς ("from which [day]") = "в который"; ἧς is already linked to который, but its governing preposition is left unlinked while the Russian preposition gets `t`. Fix: `g 12 = в#0 // ἀφ’ ἧς is rendered "в который" (the day on which).` and drop `t в#0`.
- **Acts 23:15 — cat. 2** — `g 2 = вы#0 + вы#1` over-claims. вы#1 is the subject of хотите, which renders ὡς μέλλοντας, an accusative plural agreeing with ὑμᾶς (13). It does not repeat ὑμεῖς (2), the subject of ἐμφανίσατε. Fix: `g 2 = вы#0` and `g 15 = вы#1 + хотите // Accusative plural μέλλοντας (agreeing with ὑμᾶς) supplies the subject of хотите.` (same convention as `g 9 = вы#1 + пророчествовали` in 1 Cor 14:5).
- **Acts 26:18 — cat. 1 (h-level)** — `h 4 = обратились` and `h 4 = чтобы`: both sub-links point at ἐπιστρέψαι, and the purpose marker τοῦ (3) gets none. чтобы renders the articular-infinitive purpose marker, as in 2 Cor 3:13 (`h 10 = чтобы`) and Heb 12:10 (`h 16 = чтобы`). Fix: `h 3 = чтобы` and `h 4 = обратились`.

### Ephesians

- **Eph 5:5 — cat. 1 (h-level)** — `g 14 5 = никакой + не` with `h 14 = никакой` and `h 14 = не`: никакой is assigned to οὐκ, and πᾶς (5) gets no sub-link. никакой renders πᾶς under negation, exactly as 1 Cor 1:29 does it (`h 3 = никакая`, `h 1 = не`). Fix: `h 5 = никакой` and `h 14 = не`.

### Luke

- **Luke 1:11 — cat. 3** — `s 1` δέ, whose reason says it "is expressed by a temporal sequencing adverb in Russian", together with `t тогда`. The reason contradicts the `s`. Fix: `g 1 = тогда // Narrative δέ is rendered "then".` and drop `t тогда`.
- **Luke 8:23 — cat. 3** — `s 5` κατέβη with `t поднялся`: the main predicate of the clause (λαῖλαψ κατέβη = "поднялся ... ветер") is left unlinked because the direction changes. Elsewhere sense-shifted verbs are linked (σώσει → исцелит, Jas 5:15; ἡγήσασθε → принимайте, Jas 1:2). The same applies at lower weight to `s 8` εἰς / `t На`. Fix: `g 5 = поднялся // The storm "came down"; Russian says the wind rose.`; `g 8 = На // εἰς "onto" the lake is rendered as location.`; drop both `t` lines.
- **Luke 10:1 — cat. 3** — `s 22` ἤμελλεν with `t хотел`. μέλλω "be about to / intend" is rendered "хотел"; the dataset links μέλλω in the same way elsewhere (Acts 23:15 μέλλοντας → хотите, Luke 24:21 μέλλων → должен, Rev 12:4 μελλούσης → надлежало). Fix: `g 22 = хотел // Imperfect of μέλλω ("was about to") is rendered as intention.` and drop `t хотел`.

### Matthew

- **Matt 13:22 — cat. 3** — `s 7` ἐστιν, whose reason says the copula "is expressed by a signifying relation in Russian", together with boilerplate `t означает = ... no separate equivalent word`. The two reasons contradict each other; означает renders the explanatory ἐστιν ("this is the one who hears"). Fix: `g 7 = означает // The explanatory copula of the parable interpretation is rendered "signifies".` and drop `t означает`.

## Minor items (debatable, not counted as errors)

1. **1 Cor 11:5** — τὸ (17) is `s` as an "unexpressed article", but "то же" renders τὸ αὐτό; better `g 13 16 17 18 = то же` with `h 17 = то`.
2. **1 Cor 12:14** — `s 0 1` καὶ γάρ plus `t же`, although the `s` reason says Russian "uses postpositive же". The logic shifts (explanatory → continuative), so leaving it unlinked is defensible, but `g 1 = же` matches the reason.
3. **1 Cor 9:18** — θήσω (`s 9`) and благовествую (`t`) are the two finite predicates of the purpose clause; a sense-shift link `g 9 = благовествую` is arguable.
4. **1 John 1:2** — καί / ибо left as `s`+`t` (logic changed); anaphoric τὴν (11) rendered by "сию" left as `s`+`t`.
5. **2 Cor 7:14** — ὅτι / Итак left as `s`+`t` (explanation → inference).
6. **Gal 3:10** — γάρ / а left unlinked; τοῖς (18) / "что" left unlinked, although Acts 4:24 links τὰ → что.
7. **Gal 4:13** — δι’ (cause) / в#0 (setting) left unlinked; defensible because Synodal reinterprets.
8. **John 5:44** — καί / а left unlinked; attributive τὴν (11) / которая left unlinked.
9. **Matt 5:28** — πρός (purpose) / с#0 (manner) left unlinked; ὁ (6) / кто left unlinked, although Matt 13:22 links ὁ → кто.
10. **Phil 3:15** — καί / же left unlinked.
11. **Acts 8:14, Mark 13:14** — attributive οἱ / "Находившиеся", "находящиеся" left as `s`+`t`, while Mark 1:36 links οἱ → бывшие.
12. **Acts 23:15** — τὰ (18) / "дело" left as `s`+`t`, while Eph 6:21 links τὰ κατ’ ἐμέ → обстоятельствах through `h 5`.
13. **John 5:13, Rev 12:4** — an article rendered by a demonstrative ("на том месте", "Дракон сей") is left as `s`+`t`.
14. **Acts 26:18** — `g 17 18 = и#1 + получили`: и#1 is a supplied coordinator, not a rendering of τοῦ. Cleaner: `g 18 = получили`, `s 17 = The second purpose marker shares the earlier чтобы.`, `t и#1`.
15. **Acts 10:10** — the `s 7` reason (δέ is "implicit in the Russian temporal connective") contradicts the `t Между тем` reason ("no separate written counterpart").
16. **Luke 17:3** — the `t же` reason does not mention that TR has δέ (ἐὰν δὲ ἁμάρτῃ εἰς σέ), although the same verse notes TR εἰς σέ.
17. **Luke 2:38** — the `t она` reason calls it a Russian supply; it is TR αὕτη (καὶ αὕτη αὐτῇ τῇ ὥρᾳ).
18. **Luke 13:13, Mark 5:42** — inchoative "стала" and "начала" are `t`, while John 1:10 groups `g 9 = начал быть`.
19. **Rev 13:10** — "сам" is `t` in the first clause, but "самому" is grouped with αὐτόν (`g 13 = тому самому`) in the parallel clause.
20. **Rev 12:4, Rev 13:10** — the article reasons carry a stray phrase: "At source-only12:18 they remain local explanations rather than transferred words."
21. **2 Pet 1:21** — `s 13 14` ἀπὸ Θεοῦ with `t святые Божии`. Θεοῦ is present in both N1904 and TR, so `g 14 = Божии` would match the 2 Cor 1:20 precedent (αὐτοῦ → Нем#1 across διά/ἐν); review rule 9 ("never invent a link" for a different reading) makes the non-link defensible.
22. **Matt 4:3** — `s 5` αὐτῷ with `t к Нему`. The same pronoun stands in a different position (after εἶπεν in N1904, after προσελθών in TR); by the 2 Cor 1:20 precedent `g 5 = Нему` + `t к` would be acceptable. Defensible as is.

## Patterns

1. **Split `s`/`t` pairs.** A Greek function word is marked `s` while the Russian word that renders it is marked `t`, often with the `s` reason itself naming that Russian word. This accounts for 8 of the 11 real-error verses. Examples: Matt 13:22 (ἐστιν / означает), 2 Cor 9:7 (καθώς / по, ἤ / и), Luke 1:11 (δέ / тогда).
2. **The same phenomenon is treated differently in different verses.** The reviewers made locally reasonable choices without a shared rule.
   - μέλλω is linked in Acts 23:15, Luke 24:21 and Rev 12:4, but not in Luke 10:1.
   - A substantivizing or attributive article is linked to the Russian relative or participle in Acts 4:24, Matt 13:22, Rev 12:4 and Mark 1:36, but not in Gal 3:10, John 5:44, Matt 5:28, Acts 8:14 and Mark 13:14.
   - Connectives whose logic changes: καί → но (Matt 13:22) and καί → а (2 Cor 3:13, Matt 22:21) are linked, while γάρ → а (Gal 3:10), καί → а (John 5:44) and καί → ибо (1 John 1:2) are not.
3. **Textual differences (TR vs N1904) are handled very well.** About 40 TR claims were checked (for example Luke 12:38 придет#0 / стражу#0, Rom 14:6 the extra day clause, 2 Cor 1:20 διό / δι’ αὐτοῦ, Heb 9:14 αἰωνίου / Святым, Rev 13:10 συνάγει) and none was false. The only weakness is the opposite direction: two TR words were described as Russian supplies (Luke 17:3 же = TR δέ, Luke 2:38 она = TR αὕτη).
4. **Articles.** The blanket reason "printed Greek articles are unexpressed" is also applied where Synodal renders an anaphoric article with a demonstrative (John 5:13 том, Rev 12:4 сей, 1 John 1:2 сию, 1 Cor 11:5 "то же"). This is harmless but loses a real correspondence.
5. **`h` sub-links in two-word groups.** Sometimes both `h` lines point at the same Greek token, so the other token gets no head (Eph 5:5, Acts 26:18). The correct pattern is used elsewhere (1 Cor 1:29, 2 Cor 3:13).
6. **Boilerplate `t` reasons can contradict the paired `s` reason** (Matt 13:22, Acts 10:10). A check that flags an `s` reason naming a Russian word that is itself `t` would catch most category-3 items automatically.
7. **Word order and repeated words are reliable.** No `#n` index was assigned out of Greek order in any verse, and no swapped neighbours were found. Examples: Rom 14:6 (16 repeated forms, all correct) and Luke 12:38 (придет#0/#1 and стражу#0/#1 correctly split between N1904 and TR).

Base-text note (outside the link categories): in Heb 9:13 ("кровь тельцов и волов") and John 20:29 ("потому что увидел;" without "Меня") the Russian differs from the common online Synodal text ([bible.by Heb 9:13](https://bible.by/verse-en/65/9/13/): "тельцов и козлов"; [bible.by John 20:29](https://bible.by/verse-en/43/20/29/): "увидел Меня"). Both readings match the project source `scripts/bible-sources/russyn_vpl.txt`, so the links are correct for the text as given. If that source is ever corrected, τράγων → козлов and με → Меня would need relinking.
